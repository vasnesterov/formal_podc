Analysing Snapshot Isolation — PODC 2016

Andrea Cerone, Alexey Gotsman. Analysing Snapshot Isolation. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933096. Formalized version: the extended version with the appendix proofs (§A, §B; no arXiv version); numbering follows it. The JACM 2018 version was consulted.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main results hold as stated: the dependency-graph characterisation of SI (Theorems 9 and 10, with Lemmas 12, 13, 15), the dynamic chopping criterion (Theorem 16, with Lemmas 17, 24, 26, 27) and its static version (Corollary 18), and the robustness criteria (Theorems 19, 22). The proof of Lemma 26 misses a case, closed by proving its two parts in the other order. A typo in Definition 20 (ExecSI instead of ExecPSI) makes Theorem 21 false as written; it holds for the intended definition.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Definition 20 defines HistPSI = {H | ∃VIS, CO. (H, VIS, CO) ∈ ExecSI} with ExecSI instead of the ExecPSI defined on the line above, which makes Theorem 21 false as written: the long-fork history of Figure 2(c) has a graph in GraphPSI but is not in HistSI (Examples.theorem21_literal_false). Read literally, HistPSI = HistSI, and Theorem 21 would then say that SI histories are exactly the histories of graphs in GraphPSI. With ExecPSI, Theorem 21 holds (theorem21), and no other result is affected. The JACM version has the same typo. We prove Theorem 21 for the intended definition although the paper cites it.

  • Some derived anti-dependencies S' —RW→ V' in the proofs of Lemmas 26 and 27 need S' ≠ V'; the excluded case is a short cycle forbidden by G ∈ GraphSI.

Typos

  • §6.1 refers to "the long fork anomaly in Figure 4"; it is Figure 2(c).

  • The proof of Theorem 10(i) says "Figure 3" for Figure 4.

🔧 Lemma 26 (transitivity of WW_splice(G))

The first part of the proof rules out S'' —WW→ T' with a cycle of DCG(G) (Figure 10(f)) that is in DCG(G) only if S'' ≉ T', and the case T ≈ S is not covered at that point.

Impact. Statement true; the proof only needs reordering (Chopped.Hyp.splice_ww_trans).

Details

The proof first shows T' —WW→ S'' and only then T ≉ S.

Repair

Prove the second part first (it does not depend on the first); then the cycle is in DCG(G).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. Purely combinatorial and declarative: no processes, failures, timing or randomness. Everything is finite-relational, and finiteness is never needed.

  • Transactions are lists of read(x,n)/write(x,n) operations in program order, with values in an arbitrary type. T ⊢ read(x,n), T ⊢ write(x,n) and INT are as in §2. A history (𝒯, SO) has an arbitrary SO in §§2–4 and §6; all results there hold for every SO, which is more general than the paper's union of total orders.

  • Abstract executions (𝒯, SO, VIS, CO) and pre-executions use the axioms of Figure 1 and the sets ExecSI, ExecSER, ExecPSI, HistSI, HistSER, HistPSI. max_CO is a predicate, and its definedness is part of EXT.

  • Dependency graphs follow Definition 6, with RW derived from WR and WW; graph(X) follows Definition 5. Cycles are closed walks that may repeat vertices. Edge adjacency is cyclic, so the last and first edges also count as adjacent.

  • Chopping (§5): a history is given by its sessions, i.e. lists of transactions in session order. splice(H) concatenates each session into one transaction and has empty SO. DCG(G), critical cycles and splice(G) follow §5 and equation (2).

  • Programs for Corollary 18 are abstracted by their numbers of pieces and the pieces' read/write sets. "Produced by P" means an injective session-to-program map in which the j-th transaction matches the j-th piece.

Not formalized. The equivalence of the axiomatic SI specification with the operational SI concurrency control, which the paper cites. Also not formalized: §B, i.e. the chopping criteria for serializability and parallel SI (Theorems 29 and 31, cited) and their comparison with Corollary 18, the static robustness analyses beyond Theorems 19 and 22, and most worked examples (Figures 4–6, 11–13).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The hypotheses are non-vacuous: Examples.lean contains concrete dependency graphs, including write skew in GraphSI \ GraphSER. The model is self-contained in this folder: Model.lean and Relations.lean with Mathlib only. The Crooks et al. model of Papers/PODC2017/SeeingIsBelieving/ (Model.lean, Adya.lean) overlaps: it also has transactions as operation lists and Adya's WR/WW/RW with SER/SI/PSI conditions, but uses unique values (a read returns its writer's id), no sessions, and a per-key version order in place of WW(x). A bridge (SiB histories as History with SO = ∅ and values Option ι, version order ↦ WW) would let the SI/PSI characterisations of the two papers be compared (theirs go through Adya's timestamp-based PL-SI); it does not exist yet. CSLib candidates: the relational helpers in Relations.lean (exists_strictTotal_extension, Acyclic) and the labelled-walk library CycleDefs.lean/Cycles.lean.
