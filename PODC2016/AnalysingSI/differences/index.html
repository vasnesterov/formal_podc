Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Analysing Snapshot Isolation — PODC 2016.

Paper: A. Cerone, A. Gotsman, Analysing Snapshot Isolation, PODC 2016, extended version with the appendix proofs (§A, §B). Numbering follows the PODC/extended version (the JACM 2018 version renumbers: Theorem 10 → 4.2, Theorem 16 → 5.2, …). The JACM version was consulted; it has the same definitions, proofs and the same slips.

Statement-level issues

  1. Definition 20 / Theorem 21 (typo, ✏️). Definition 20 defines HistPSI = {H | ∃VIS, CO. (H, VIS, CO) ∈ ExecSI} — with ExecSI, not the ExecPSI defined on the line above (checked on the rendered page, p. 9 of the extended version; the JACM version, Definition 5.14, has the same typo). Read literally, HistPSI = HistSI, and Theorem 21 (HistPSI = histories of graphs in GraphPSI) is false: the long-fork history of Figure 2(c) has a dependency graph in GraphPSI but is not in HistSI (see Examples.lean). The intent is evident (the text, the name, Theorem 22 and the proof of Theorem 21 all use ExecPSI), so we define HistPSI with ExecPSI and prove Theorem 21 for it (theorem21). Theorem 21 is cited from the authors' DISC 2015 paper, but it is a numbered theorem of this paper and is used for Theorem 22's interpretation.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. Lemma 26, transitivity of WW_splice(G): missing case (🔧). The proof first shows T' —WW→ S'' and only then T ≉ S. In the first part, case V'' —WW→ V', it excludes S'' —WW→ T' by the cycle T' —WW→ V' —SO⁻¹→ V'' —WW→ S'' —WW→ T' of Figure 10(f), which is a cycle of DCG(G) only if S'' ≉ T'; the case S'' ≈ T' (i.e. T ≈ S) is not covered. Repair (used in Lean): prove T ≉ S first — the paper's own second part does not use the first — after which the cycle is in DCG(G).

  2. Lemmas 26 and 27: implicit distinctness (✏️). Several anti-dependencies S' —RW→ V' are derived without checking S' ≠ V' (required by Definition 5), e.g. Figure 10(d). When S' = V' the configuration is itself a cycle S'' —SO→ S' —WR→ S'' excluded by G ∈ GraphSI; the Lean proof handles these cases (Hyp.no_ww_of_wr_wr).

  3. Theorem 10(i), construction of the total commit order. The paper adds unrelated pairs one at a time and re-solves the system (Lemma 15 with R = Rᵢ). We take one linear extension L of the least solution CO₀ (Szpilrajn, exists_strictTotal_extension) and apply Lemma 15 with R = L; this is the limit of the paper's iteration and needs no finiteness. Same idea, shorter proof.

  4. Lemma 13 assumes VIS and CO acyclic; we only assume CO acyclic (VIS ⊆ CO by (S3)).

  5. Lemma 24 is stated in the paper for "no two adjacent anti-dependency edges". Our lemma24 is generic in the adjacency predicate P and needs P a b → P c d → P a d (true for "both are RW"); for arbitrary P the generic statement is false (a figure-eight walk), which does not affect the paper.

Modelling choices

  1. Transactions (Definition 1) are lists of operations in program order (Txn = List (Op Obj V)); events are list positions, so events of different transactions are disjoint by construction. Values range over an arbitrary type V (the paper: ℤ). Transactions are not required to be nonempty (all results hold without it).

  2. Histories (Definition 2) for §§2–4 and §6 have an arbitrary session-order relation SO; Theorems 8, 9, 10, 19, 21, 22 hold for every SO (the paper requires a union of total orders on disjoint sets). For §5 (chopping) a history is given by its sessions (Chopped: sess s lists the transactions of session s in session order, sessions nonempty); SO and splice(H) are derived from it. Every finite history whose SO is a union of total orders is of this form up to renaming of transactions.

  3. max_R(A) is the predicate IsMaxBy R A a; the paper's convention "the use of max implicitly assumes it is defined" is built into EXT as an existential.

  4. Dependency graphs (Definition 6): RW is derived from WR, WW by a definition (DepGraph.RW), not a field; the sets GraphSER/SI/PSI include the conditions of Definition 6 (the paper lets G range over dependency graphs). graph(X) = G is equality of the tuples (𝒯, SO, WR, WW).

  5. Cycles (§A.5) are nonempty closed walks (vertices may repeat) given as lists of (label, target) pairs. "Two adjacent anti-dependency edges" is CycAdj: two cyclically consecutive edges, including last–first; a one-edge cycle has no adjacent pair (irrelevant: RW is irreflexive). If the paper's "cycle" means a simple cycle, Theorems 19 and 22 are unaffected: a closed walk with no two adjacent RW edges (resp. with at most one RW edge) splits at a repeated vertex into two shorter closed walks, one of which keeps the property (the argument of lemma24), so both readings give the same conditions.

  6. Critical cycles (§5): (i) no repeated vertex; (ii) some rotation of the label sequence starts with "conflict, predecessor, conflict"; (iii) for every rotation starting with an RW edge and every later RW edge, a WR or WW edge lies strictly between them (this covers both arcs between any two RW edges). IsCriticalIn is defined for any labelled graph and used for both DCG(G) and SCG(P). Reading (ii) cyclically is the natural reading for a cycle; reading (iii) on both arcs makes fewer cycles critical, so the hypothesis "no critical cycle" of Theorem 16 and Corollary 18 is weaker than under a one-arc reading, and the Lean theorems are at least as strong as the paper's.

  7. Theorem 16 is proved in the stronger form of §A.5: for the paper's splice(G) (equation (2)), splice(G) ∈ GraphSI (theorem16_splice); spliceability (theorem16) follows.

  8. Corollary 18. Programs are abstracted by kᵢ and the read/write sets Rⱼⁱ, Wⱼⁱ. "H can be produced by P" is: an injective assignment of programs to sessions, the j-th transaction of a session corresponds to the j-th piece of its program (j < kᵢ; a session may have fewer transactions than pieces), and every object read (resp. written) by an operation of the transaction lies in the piece's read (resp. write) set.

  9. Theorems 8 and 21 are cited by the paper (Adya; DISC 2015). We prove them (with preservation of the dependency graph, theorem8_soundness, theorem21_soundness), so Theorems 19 and 22 have their intended meaning in terms of histories.

Not formalized

  • The equivalence of the axiomatic definition of SI (Definition 4) with the operational SI concurrency control (cited from [10]).

  • §B: Theorems 29 and 31 (chopping under serializability and parallel SI, cited) and the comparisons of the three chopping criteria; the static robustness analyses of §6 beyond Theorems 19 and 22.

  • Most worked examples (Figures 4–6, 11–13); Examples.lean covers the anomalies of Figure 2 listed in the README.

Typos (no effect)

  • §6.1: "the dependency graph G₄ of the long fork anomaly in Figure 4" should be Figure 2(c).

  • Proof of Theorem 10(i): "in Figure 3 the transactions T' and T'' are unrelated" should be Figure 4.
