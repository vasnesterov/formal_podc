A Randomized Concurrent Algorithm for Disjoint Set Union — PODC 2016

Siddhartha V. Jayanti, Robert E. Tarjan. A Randomized Concurrent Algorithm for Disjoint Set Union. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933108. Formalized version: arXiv 1612.01514 v1 (the only version; a revised version of the conference paper). Numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Linearizability and wait-freedom (Theorem 3.4 and the early-termination variants of Section 6) are proved for all three versions of Find and every fixed order of the ids, after easy repairs of the proofs in Section 3. The paper's efficiency results (Sections 4–5) were not checked in Lean. The one step of them we examined, the Chernoff bound in the proof of Lemma 4.2, on which the O(log n) high-probability bound and part of Theorem 5.1 rest, is wrong, and its repair exists only on paper.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Lemma 4.2 (exponent h², checked in the arXiv text)

The proof claims the bound exp(−(c/2−1)²h²/c) "by a Chernoff bound", but Chernoff gives only exp(−(c/2−1)²h/c), linear in h, and the quadratic bound already fails for fair coins with c = 4, h = 6 (chernoff_step_fails).

Impact. We do not know whether the stated lemma is false; only its proof is broken. Corollaries 4.2.1 and 4.2.2, Theorem 4.3 and the case d < 1 of Theorem 5.1 inherit the gap. Corollary 4.2.1 and Theorem 4.3 survive with a larger constant c, and Theorem 5.1 can be repaired on paper with another threshold; not checked in Lean.

Details

The proof bounds the probability that fewer than h of c·h independent links are "good", each good with probability ≥ 1/2. Fair coins are allowed by Lemma 4.1: with c = 4, h = 6, fewer than 6 heads among 24 coins has probability 55455/2²⁴ ≈ 3.3·10⁻³ > e⁻⁹. The case d < 1 of the proof of Theorem 5.1 uses the quadratic form to show that there are very few "middle" nodes. With the paper's threshold 4h and the linear exponent, its charge is not O(m).

Repair

A threshold 7h repairs the proof of Theorem 5.1 (on paper; not formalized).

✏️ Slips

  • Proof of Lemma 4.1: "rank … never less than the rank of its parent" should be "never more".

  • Proof of Lemma 4.2: "fewer than h bad links" should be "good links".

  • Proof of Lemma 5.3: "depth 1 before the Find" should be "depth 2".

Typos

  • Algorithm 4, line 7: u.p should be u.parent.

  • Proof of Theorem 5.1: the two exponents h²/16 and h²/4 are inconsistent.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 3.1

🔧 Lemma 3.1 ("u, v and w are distinct"; same point in the proof of Lemma 3.3)

The proof's claim that u, v and w are distinct is false: a splitting Find can read v ← u.parent = u while u is a root, and if u is linked before the next read, it executes Cas(u.parent, u, w) with u = v (cas_same_node, a concrete 2-process execution).

Impact. Lemma true: this Cas always fails. The same execution gives a Find iteration in which u does not advance, contrary to a sentence in the proof of Lemma 3.3; this happens at most once per Find. Both lemmas are proved with this case added (lemma_3_1_union, waitFree).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model: any set of processes, deterministic programs, no crashes, an arbitrary (adversarial) scheduler. Each process runs one operation at a time, and every invocation is allowed.

  • Each atomic step is one read or one Cas on one parent pointer (casObject E a, initially a). Local computation is folded into the preceding access.

  • No randomness. The node ids are an arbitrary injective key : E → ℕ, so the random order of the paper is a special case. E is finite.

  • The object is DSU over E. Its state is the same-set relation, initially equality. sameSet answers membership; unite merges the two classes and returns a dummy true.

  • Find comes in three versions: Algorithm 1, two reads per iteration as written; and splitting with k + 1 tries (k = 0: Algorithm 4, k = 1: Algorithm 5).

  • Linearizability is CSLib's IsLinearization, and wait-freedom is CSLib's Impl.WaitFree.

Proof structure.

  • Linearizability. An invariant over (history, configuration, linearization) says that the abstract state of the linearization is the partition into trees of the current memory. Responses of SameSet = false are inserted retroactively, at a cut of the linearization recorded when Find(v) found its root; this uses IsLinearization.insert from Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/LinPoints.lean. The proof of Lemma 3.2 leaves two facts implicit; both are easy (linearizable):

    • When SameSet returns false, the answer is decided at the later test u = u.parent, but the operation is linearized earlier, at the last read of Find(v). One needs that u was a root already at that read: it was a root when Find(u) returned and is one at the test, and non-roots never become roots again. Linearizing at the test instead would be wrong, because v may have been linked into u's tree in between.

    • The link case needs that the larger node is not in the smaller root's tree. This holds because ids increase along parent pointers.

  • Wait-freedom. The potential is bound · Σₐ #{ids above a.parent} + Φ. The first term decreases at every successful Cas. Φ is a lexicographic local potential: positions of u, v (taken at their roots while a Find runs), then the phase, then the Find's progress. It decreases at every own step that leaves memory unchanged (Impl.waitFree_of_cfgPotential, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Potential.lean).

Not formalized. The randomized analysis of Sections 4–5 (Lemma 4.1 relies on Goel et al., SODA 2014), the O(h+1) step bound of Lemma 3.3, and the MakeSet remark.

The PODC version claims the expected work bound O(m(α(n, m/(np)) + log(np/m + 1))) (Theorem 5.1 of the arXiv version, stated in the PODC abstract) also for one-try splitting, and the authors later acknowledged that its proof is incorrect. The arXiv version says: "In the conference version of our paper [JT16], we claimed that Theorem 5.1 holds for one-try splitting, but our proof, essentially the proof of Theorem 5.1, is not correct for this algorithm." It proves only a weaker bound (Theorem 5.2, m/(np²) in place of m/(np)) and calls the original claim "an open problem" (Section 7). We could not consult the proceedings text and do not formalize Sections 4–5, so we neither confirm nor refute the claim.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: concrete valid executions (seqDemo_valid: Unite(0,1) then SameSet(1,0) returns true; seqDemo_false: SameSet(1,0) alone returns false) and cas_same_node, all evaluated by simp. The model comes from CSLib Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/{Model,History,LinPoints}.lean; the shared library Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/{History,Model,LinPoints,Potential}.lean provides the current operation instance curOp, Impl.run_pend_curOp, Impl.linearizable_of_run, IsLinearization.insert and Impl.waitFree_of_cfgPotential.
