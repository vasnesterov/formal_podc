Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of A Randomized Concurrent Algorithm for Disjoint Set Union — PODC 2016.

Paper: S. V. Jayanti, R. E. Tarjan, A Randomized Concurrent Algorithm for Disjoint Set Union, PODC 2016 (DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933108). Text used: arXiv 1612.01514 v1 (the only arXiv version), which describes itself as "a significantly revised version of the conference paper". Section and theorem numbers below are those of the arXiv version. We could not consult the proceedings text, so items about Sections 4–5 are labelled with the version they concern.

Modelling choices (no effect on the results)

  1. Shared-memory model. CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Model.lean): deterministic processes, every primitive access to a base object is one atomic step, local computation is folded into the continuation of the preceding access, returning a response is a separate step. Any set P of processes; every process may invoke any operation (allowed is True). No crashes. The paper's APRAM with Cas is this model.

  2. Base objects. One compare&swap object per element a, storing a.parent, initially a (CSLib casObject E a). The algorithms only use read and cas. Node ids are not stored in memory: they are a fixed function key : E → ℕ known to all processes (the paper's x.id, "fixed initially").

  3. Node order. The paper chooses the total order uniformly at random. Sections 3 and 6 (the only parts formalized as theorems) hold for every fixed total order, so the results are stated for an arbitrary injective key. Injectivity is necessary: with two equal ids, concurrent Unites could link two roots to each other.

  4. Sequential specification. The state of the DSU object is the relation "same set" (initially equality); unite x y merges the two classes (joinRel), sameSet x y answers membership. Unite has no result in the paper; here it returns the dummy response true.

  5. Algorithm 1 (Find without compaction) reads u.parent twice per iteration, once for the test u ≠ u.parent and once for u ← u.parent, as written. Linearization point: the last test.

  6. "Do twice" generalized. FindKind.split k tries every parent update k + 1 times; Algorithm 4 is split 0, Algorithm 5 is split 1. Similarly the early-termination algorithms (Section 6) are formalized for k + 1 tries, k = 1 being Algorithms 6/7 and k = 0 the one-try variant mentioned in the text.

  7. The local test u = v of line 7 (Algorithms 2, 3) and of line 4 (Algorithms 6, 7) is folded into the step that produced the new value of v (resp. u), or into the invocation. The linearization point "u = v*" of Unite is therefore the read that found v to be a root, i.e. the paper's linearization point of the last Find(v).

  8. Linearizability is CSLib's IsLinearization (Herlihy–Wing linearizability in the amended form, see wiki/shared-memory.md). Wait-freedom is CSLib's Impl.WaitFree: in every infinite execution, a process whose operation stays pending takes only finitely many steps.

Statements

  1. Lemma 3.1 is formalized twice. lemma_3_1_union is the paper's statement. The union forest uf α is computed along the execution: a step that turns a root a into a non-root with parent b adds the edge a → b. The theorem says that the union forest has the same roots as the parent forest, that ids increase along its edges, and that every parent is an ancestor in it. Every step either changes nothing, or links a root below a node with a larger id in another tree, or (in a Find) replaces a parent v by a proper ancestor of v in the union forest. lemma_3_1 is the same without the union forest: the new parent lies in the same tree and has a larger id than the old one.

  2. Lemma 3.2 is formalized as linearizability (linearizable, linearizable_with_state) with the paper's linearization points, plus the extra fact that the abstract state of the linearization is the partition into trees of the current memory.

  3. Lemma 3.3 is formalized as wait-freedom (waitFree). The step bound O(h + 1) per operation (h = height of the union forest) is not formalized. The potential used gives only a much weaker per-operation bound, and that bound is not stated.

  4. Theorem 3.4 = theorem_3_4 (linearizable ∧ wait-free, for all three versions of Find).

  5. Section 6 (Algorithms 6 and 7) has no numbered theorem; the paper presents them as implementations of SameSet / Unite with marked linearization points. Formalized as et_linearizable and et_waitFree (for k + 1 tries).

Findings in the paper

  1. Proof of Lemma 3.1: "u, v and w are distinct" is false (imprecision; the lemma holds). The proof claims that each Cas(u.parent, v, w) done in a Find has u, v, w distinct. With splitting, Find may read v ← u.parent = u while u is a root. If another process then links u, the Find reads w ← v.parent ≠ v and executes Cas(u.parent, u, w) with u = v. Such a Cas necessarily fails, because u is no longer a root and non-roots never become roots again, so nothing breaks. Lean: concrete 2-process execution cas_same_node; the case is handled in fRes_spec. The same execution gives a loop iteration of Find in which u does not move, which the proof of Lemma 3.3 ("each update of u in Find replaces u by a proper ancestor") overlooks. It can happen only once per Find, so the step bound is unaffected.

  2. Proof of Lemma 3.2, SameSet returning false (gap in exposition; statement true). The linearization point (the last read of Find(v)) precedes the test u = u.parent that decides the answer. The argument needs that u was a root at the linearization point. This holds because u was a root when Find(u) returned, is a root at the test, and non-roots never become roots again. The paper leaves this implicit. Linearizing at the test itself would be wrong, because v may have been linked into u's tree in between. In Lean this is a retroactive linearization point (IsLinearization.insert, invariant field QOK.chk).

  3. Proof of Lemma 3.2, link case (gap in exposition; statement true). "The smaller of u and v is a root just before the Cas. It follows that x and y are in different trees." This also needs that the larger node is not in the smaller root's tree, which holds because ids increase along parent pointers (Lemma 3.1). Used in same_update_link.

  4. Lemma 4.2: the Chernoff bound does not give the stated exponent (proof gap; arXiv version). The claimed bound is 1/exp((c/2 − 1)² h² / c). The Chernoff bound applied in the proof gives 1/exp((c/2 − 1)² h / c), linear in h. The quadratic bound fails in the model the proof reduces to: with each of the ch links good with probability exactly 1/2, which Lemma 4.1 allows, c = 4 and h = 6, the probability of fewer than 6 good links among 24 is 55455/2²⁴ ≈ 3.3·10⁻³ > e⁻⁹ ≈ 1.2·10⁻⁴ (chernoff_step_fails; the linear exponent is consistent with this case, chernoff_linear_ok). We do not know whether the statement of Lemma 4.2 is false for the actual union forest; only its proof is broken. Consequences. Corollaries 4.2.1–4.2.2 and Theorem 4.3 survive with the linear exponent: choose c larger (e.g. c ≥ 10 for 1/n² with h = lg n + 1). The case d < 1 of the proof of Theorem 5.1 uses the quadratic form ("the expected number of middle nodes is at most n/2^{h²/16}", total charge O(hpn/2^{h²/4}) = O(m)). With the linear exponent and the paper's threshold 4h for "low" nodes, the charge is O(h·m·(np/m)^{1−1/(4 ln 2)}), which is not O(m). Raising the threshold to c·h with (c/2 − 1)²/c > ln 2 (e.g. c = 7) restores O(m) and keeps the O(m log(1/d)) count of low nodes, so Theorem 5.1 is very likely still true. Not formalized beyond the numeric witness.

  5. One-try splitting in the PODC version (acknowledged by the authors; not checked by us). The arXiv version states that the conference version claimed the bound of Theorem 5.1 for one-try splitting and that the proof "is not correct for this algorithm". The arXiv version proves a weaker bound (Theorem 5.2, with m/(np²) in place of m/(np)) and leaves the original claim open. The probabilistic analysis is not formalized, so we neither confirm nor refute this.

  6. Typos and slips (none affects the formalized results):

    • Algorithm 4, line 7: Cas(u.p, v, w) should be Cas(u.parent, v, w).

    • Proof of Lemma 4.1: "the rank of a node is never less than the rank of its parent" should be "never more" (ranks are non-decreasing along parent pointers, x.r ≤ x.p.r, as stated just before).

    • Proof of Lemma 4.2: "there are fewer than h bad links among the ch links" should be "fewer than h good links" (a good link increases the rank, and the rank stays below h).

    • Proof of Theorem 5.1 (case d < 1): the middle-node bound is given as n/2^{h²/16} and the total charge as O(hpn/2^{h²/4}). The two exponents are inconsistent (see also item 17).

    • Proof of Lemma 5.3: "The Find on x can decrease one or more node depths only if x has depth 1 before the Find and 1 after" should be "depth 2 before and 1 after". A Find from a node of depth ≤ 1 changes nothing, and the subsequent bound 2^{i−2} via invariant (3) is for depth 2.

Not formalized

  1. Sections 4 and 5 (randomized analysis: Lemma 4.1, which relies on Goel et al. (SODA 2014), Corollaries 4.1.1–4.2.2, Theorems 4.3, 5.1, 5.2, 5.4, Lemma 5.3). These depend on the independence assumption (∗) and on properties (i)–(vi) of Goel et al., which the paper only cites. Only the numeric witness of item 17 is in Lean.

  2. The MakeSet remark (lock-freedom with an unbounded universe) and the remarks of Section 7.

  3. The O(h + 1) step bound of Lemma 3.3 (see item 11).
