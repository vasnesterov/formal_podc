Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed (2+ε)-approximation for weighted vertex cover — PODC 2016.

Paper: R. Bar-Yehuda, K. Censor-Hillel, G. Schwartzman, A Distributed (2+ε)-Approximation for Vertex Cover in O(log Δ/ε log log Δ) Rounds, PODC 2016, arXiv 1602.03713v2 (numbering follows it).

No false statements were found.

Statements

  1. Theorem 2.1 is proved in a stronger form (no issue). We compare w(S_δ) with the weight of every vertex cover, not only a minimum one, and we do not need w ≥ 0. The function δ is a function on Sym2 V. Only its values on edges matter: it must be nonnegative there. (theorem_2_1)

  2. "ε = O(1)" (precise reading of an asymptotic claim). Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.3 hold "for every ε = O(1)". We fix an arbitrary bound E and assume 0 < ε ≤ E. The constants depend on E: 3(34+2E)/ε rounds for d(v) ≤ 16 and 3(2+3E)·log d/(ε log log d) rounds for d(v) > 16. The exact count n ≤ 2K/ε + log d/log K (item 4) has no K term. E is needed for the other two parts of the bound. The log_K d term (at most 2K for K = log d/log log d, and < 1 for K = d+1) and the +1 (every vertex takes part in at least one iteration) become O(K/ε) only through 1 ≤ E/ε. For large ε the number of iterations is Θ(K + log_K d), not O(K/ε). The headline bound in terms of Δ follows from the per-vertex bound in d(v). The function log x/log log x is increasing for x > e^e ≈ 15.2, so for 16 < d(v) ≤ Δ we get log d(v)/log log d(v) ≤ log Δ/log log Δ. This step is not stated in Lean.

  3. Logarithms (no issue). We use natural logarithms in K_v = log d/log log d and in the final bound. The paper does not fix the base. With base 2 the bound changes only by a constant factor, and all the paper's side conditions also hold for ln when d > 16: ln ln d > 1 and ln K_v ≥ 1.

  4. Lemma 3.3, counting step (proof correct, made exact). The paper uses the dichotomy "d_{i+1} ≤ d_i/K or w_{i+1} ≤ w_i − ε'w_0/K". We prove the exact inequality w_i − w_{i+1} ≥ vault·d_{i+1}/d_i (drop). Then we count for every real K > 1: if v is still running at the start of iteration n, then n ≤ K(1−θ)/a + log d/log K (count_bound, active_bound). With a = θ = ε' this is n ≤ 2K/ε + log d/log K. So v takes part in at most n+1 < K/ε' + log_K d iterations, which is the paper's count. We checked that the off-by-one and the real (non-integer) K are harmless.

  5. Round count (made explicit). One iteration is three synchronous rounds: answer requests, collect answers and decide, notify. Round t belongs to iteration ⌊t/3⌋ (cfg_st). Requests are not sent in a separate round: vault(u)/d_i(u) is part of u's state, which its neighbours read. The paper only says "a constant number of rounds".

Model and algorithm

  1. Model (modelling choice). We use the synchronous LOCAL model as CSLib's SET-LOCAL/locally-iterative model, where a node sees the set of its neighbours' states. Nodes carry identifiers so that an answer can be addressed to one neighbour, and they know their initial weight and degree. The algorithm depends only on ε and the processing order. It needs no n and no Δ, as the paper says.

  2. "Arbitrary order" in line 17 (generalized). Line 20 is formalized for every processing order: a duplicate-free enumeration ord v S of the requesters, which may depend on the responder (greedyRule, greedy_spec). All correctness and round results are in fact proved for an abstract response rule R. Its specification Rule.Spec σ says: each answer is in [0, request], the answers sum to at most the bank, and if some request is not answered in full, the answers sum to at least bank − σ·w_0. The paper's rule has σ = 0.

  3. Neighbourhood bookkeeping (equivalent reformulation). Lines 9, 15–16 and 29–31 maintain N_i(v) explicitly. We define N_i(v) as the set of running neighbours and prove the two agree (actN_step). A neighbour is removed at line 16 only if its bank is exhausted, and then it returns InCover in the same iteration. The paper's proof uses this symmetry (u ∈ N_i(v) ⇔ v ∈ N_i(u)) implicitly.

  4. The weight function (no issue). w : V → ℝ with w ≥ 0, so zero weights are allowed. A vertex of weight 0 returns InCover after its first iteration.

  5. Not formalized: anonymity and asynchronous start. The paper says the algorithm also works without identifiers (port numbering) and with vertices starting at different times. Neither claim is formalized. Our model uses identifiers only to address answers, which port numbers could do as well.

Section 4 (CONGEST)

  1. Under-specified rule; our reading (under-specified definition, claims hold). Section 4 halves the vault to ε'w_0/2. A partial answer becomes t·ε'w_0(v)/2 for the largest integer t with t·ε'w_0(v)/2 ≤ budget_i(v,u). The paper leaves two things open. (a) Which amount does v deduct, the rounded one or the original budget_i(v,u)? If v deducted the original amount while u deducted the rounded one, the two endpoints would disagree on δ(e), and Theorem 2.1 would not apply directly. (b) What happens to the requests processed after a partial answer? We read it as: v grants and deducts exactly the rounded amount and goes on with the remaining bank (congestRule). With this reading the rule meets Rule.Spec (ε'/2). After a partial answer less than ε'w_0/2 is left in the bank, which is the paper's own remark. Then a + σ = ε'. Correctness holds (congest_correct), and the round bounds hold with constant 4 instead of 2: 3(68+2E)/ε and 3(4+3E)·log d/(ε log log d) (congest_small, congest_large).

  2. Message sizes not formalized. We do not model the O(log n)-bit messages, the assumption that weights and the max/min weight ratio fit in O(log n) bits, or the initial round in which w_0 is broadcast. CSLib's LOCAL model has no bandwidth accounting.

Section 5 (discussion of [KMW10])

  1. Arithmetic checked; the Ω-constant is implicit in the paper (imprecise, conclusion unaffected). Section 5 drops the constant hidden in Ω. With a constant c > 0 and n = 2^L, the smallest δ allowed by n ≤ 2^{2k³+4k}δ^{4k²} is at least c·n^{(1/4−ε)/k²} iff 2k³+4k ≤ 4εL − 4k²log₂ c. Hence k ≤ 4log₂(1/c) or k³ ≤ 4εL, i.e. k = O((log n)^{1/3}). For the second constraint, δ = Δ^{1/(k+1)}/2^{k/2} ≥ c·Δ^{(1−ε)/(k+1)} iff k(k+1) ≤ 2εD − 2(k+1)log₂ c, so k = O(√log Δ) (KMW.first_constraint_iff, KMW.second_constraint_iff, KMW.first_constraint_k_le, KMW.second_constraint_k_le). With c = 1 these are exactly the paper's conditions. That these constraints are the only ones available in [KMW10] is a claim about another paper and is not formalized. The abstract's "contradicts the lower bound of [KMW10]" is therefore not formalized either.
