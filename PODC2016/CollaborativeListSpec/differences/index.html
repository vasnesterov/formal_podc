Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Specification and Space Complexity of Collaborative Text Editing — PODC 2016.

Paper: Attiya, Burckhardt, Gotsman, Morrison, Yang, Zawirski, Specification and space complexity of collaborative text editing, TCS 855 (2021), which is the full version of PODC 2016. We use the TCS numbering: TCS Theorems 8 and 9 are PODC Theorems 4 and 5, and TCS Theorem 1 is PODC Theorem 1. The PODC version's Theorem 1 claims only the strong specification; the TCS version adds eventual visibility. The verdict judges PODC 2016 (with its extended version). Its RGA pseudocode (Sections 4.1 and 4.2), Definitions 1–13 and the lower-bound construction (Fig. 4) are the same as in TCS. Other renumberings: PODC Lemma 3 = TCS Lemma 6; PODC appendix Lemmas 13–16 = TCS Lemmas 3, 4, 5, 7; PODC Claims 6–9 = TCS Claims 10–13. PODC Theorem 5 claims the bound for "some replica"; TCS Theorem 9 claims it for every replica R₁. We prove the stronger TCS form.

Statement-level issues

  1. RGA's insert counts tombstones (Section 4.2 + "Insert" of Section 4.1) (🔧). As written, RGA violates condition (1c), so Theorem 1 is false for the protocol as stated.

    • Counterexample: ins(x,0); ins(y,1); del(x); ins(b,1) at one replica. The last response is [b, y], while (1c) requires b at index 1.

    • Lean: RGA.literal_not_weak (for any number of replicas) and RGA.literal_not_satisfies. There is no abstract execution in A_weak, so none in A_strong.

    • Fix: take the parent in the visible list s(N) \ T (RGA.parentFor), as the original RGA does. With it, Theorem 1 is proved (RGA.theorem1_strong).

    • The intent is recoverable from the paper itself. Section 3 says that ins(a,k) inserts at position k of the list returned to the user, and (1c) states this on the response. Insert's prose takes "the element to the left of the insertion position". Section 4.1 states Insert for trees without tombstones, where s(N) is that list. The fix is local: the same state, messages and tree rule (the new node becomes the child of its left neighbour), with the index computed in s(N) \ T instead of s(N). It is not a design change. The proof ("(1c) follows directly from the properties of the data structure") never reasons with the tombstone-counting index.

    • The pseudocode explicitly writes s(N), so Theorem 1 is false for the paper's own algorithm R_rga as printed. The abstract presents that claim as a contribution ("a strong list specification, which we prove is implemented by an existing protocol"). However, that abstract sentence remains true for the original RGA [24], which indexes the visible list.

  2. (TCS only: Theorem 1 of PODC 2016 does not claim eventual visibility.) Definition 10 (eventual visibility) has no connectivity assumption (✏️). As literally stated, the liveness part of TCS Theorem 1 fails: in RGA.def10_needs_connectivity, replica 0 inserts and never sends, and replica 1 reads forever. The intended hypothesis is recoverable from the paper:

    • the remark after Definition 10 ("a protocol would rely on the network being sufficiently connected");

    • the proof of Theorem 1 ("If α is infinite, this already follows from our definition of a sufficiently connected network");

    • the analogous Definition 18, which includes it.

    We prove Theorem 1's liveness for infinite executions under Definition 1 (RGA.theorem1_eventualVisibility). Taken literally, Definition 10 is violated by every protocol that can buffer an update: no protocol can make an unsent update visible. So no reader would take it literally.

The lower bound (TCS Theorems 8 and 9 = PODC Theorems 4 and 5) has no statement-level issue.

Proof gaps (no effect on the statements)

  1. Claim 12 (adjacency) (🔧). The paper concludes v = …[ᵢ₋₁ ]ᵢ₋₁… "from the definition of the weak list specification". The specification only fixes the set of elements of v. Adjacency, which lets R₂ insert x between the brackets, needs another argument. lo orders both v and R₁'s earlier response at ins ]ᵢ₋₁, which has the same elements, and lo is transitive on v. Hence v equals R₁'s list (eq_of_pairwise). That list is computed from the user's positions by induction (rval_insert_phase).

  2. Which state is σ_w (🔧). The paper takes σ_w at the end of α_w, after the final read. In the model of Section 2 a do(read) may change the state and the buffer. So σ_w ≠ σ_{w'} would not give distinct internal states q, which is what the metadata overhead measures. We take the state right after send(m_{d+3}). Its buffer is empty, and its content (the unique w with do(read, w) enabled) is [∗], which the appended read shows.

  3. User assumptions on the constructed executions. The paper never checks that α_w, β_w^i and γ_w^i respect the assumption that users delete only elements in the previous response. The protocol is only required to be correct on such executions. We prove it, together with well-formedness and the responses, by induction along α_w (alpha_ok).

  4. Lemma 7 / the list order. The paper defines lo from all replica states and argues totality "w.l.o.g. all messages are delivered" (extending finite executions). We define lo as the order of s(N_all) instead, where N_all is the TI tree of all nodes created, and use Lemma 6 for every replica tree N ⊆ N_all. No extension is needed. Infinite executions use the union over prefixes (RGALive.lean).

  5. Lemma 6's proof. The paper adds an arbitrary leaf whose parent is present. We prove the special case for the node with the largest timestamp (s_cons_max, which is also the remark after the Insert procedure) and induct by removing that node. The statement is the same.

Modelling choices

  1. Replicas. There is one internal-state type Q for all replicas (a per-replica family embeds into a sigma type). Transition functions are total, because every do and every receive is enabled. The protocol class is that of PODC 2016, Section 2, in full:

    • The response of do(op, _) is resp r σ op, a function of the whole state σ = (q, m) (PODC 2016 only requires determinism; the TCS version adds that it depends on q only).

    • doStep may change the whole state, including the buffer.

    • send from (q, m) moves to (sendStep r (q, m), ⊥) for an arbitrary function sendStep (PODC 2016: Δ((q, m), send(m)) = (q′, ⊥) for some q′; the TCS version requires q′ = q). The transition is also defined on (q, ⊥), but such a send is not enabled and never occurs in a well-formed execution.

    Since the TCS class is a subclass, theorem8/theorem9 cover both versions. RGA instantiates it with sendStep (q, m) = q and responses that read only q.

  2. Messages. A receive names the position of its send event, which gives the "unique messages" of the paper. The receive transition sees only the payload. Broadcast goes to all replicas, including the sender.

  3. Executions. Executions are finite lists. Infinite executions (ℕ → Act, all prefixes well formed) are used only for eventual visibility. Definition 1 is formalized for infinite executions. Its eventual delivery covers replicas R′ ≠ R, as in the paper.

  4. Abstract executions. Events are the positions of do events in the execution, which share event ids with the abstract execution (footnote 4), so compliance is built in. We drop the linearization H and require vis to be transitive, irreflexive and to contain replica order. For finite executions this is equivalent (topological sort). For RGA we also show vis ⊆ <, so the execution order is an H. For the lower bound, dropping H weakens the hypothesis.

  5. Causal atomic broadcast, Def. 3(3). Read as: if R receives m before m′ and R′ receives m′, then R′ received m before m′. This is the strongest sensible reading, and it appears only in the conclusion of Theorem 9.

  6. Push-based (Def. 13) is parametrized by the class of executions Adm. theorem9 assumes it only for executions with causal atomic broadcast that satisfy the user assumptions. This is weaker than the paper's hypothesis, and the paper's form theorem9_causal follows. The third condition directly states S (hist α) (visHB α).

  7. User assumptions (UserOK). Inserted elements are unique, and del(a) requires a in the response of the immediately preceding do event of the same replica.

  8. Elements. The universe U is infinite. The construction needs 2d+5 distinct elements, obtained from Infinite.natEmbedding.

  9. Positions in α_w. The paper gives positions relatively ("just after ]ᵢ₋₁"). We compute the integer positions from the intended lists A_j [ⱼ ]ⱼ B_j and prove that the responses are these lists. γ_w^i is the paper's β_w^i followed by R₃'s part, in the paper's order.

  10. Metadata overhead. |q| is the length of enc q for an arbitrary injective enc : Q → List Bool. Theorem 9 concludes ⌊(D−2)/2⌋ ≤ |enc q| with content [u], where u is fixed before D. Theorem 8 is stated with metadataOverhead σ = |enc q| / sz(content σ) for a state σ = (q, m), an arbitrary size measure sz, positive on non-empty lists, and constant c = 1/(5·sz [u]).

  11. Decoding. We do not construct the decoder of Fig. 4(b). We prove that w ↦ σ_w is injective: take the first differing bit i and run the Recover experiment as part of γ_w^i. By the congruence of replay (Protocol.foldl_step_congr), equal states and equal m₁ … mᵢ give equal answers. This is exactly what the decoder relies on.

  12. RGA details.

    • TI trees and the state sets N, T are lists, N duplicate-free; a receive uses List.union. Timestamps are ℕ ×ₗ ℕ, with replica ids 0 … n−1. "1 + the largest timestamp in N" is read as (1 + max x, i).

    • The DFS only descends to children with a larger timestamp. This guard ensures termination on arbitrary lists and is vacuous for TI trees.

    • The buffer (A, K) = (∅, ∅) is represented by none.

  13. Not formalized. Theorem 2 (O(D lg k) overhead of RGA). Section 6.1 (Proposition 14, Corollary 15). Section 7 (Theorem 16, Corollary 17, Lemmas 20–21, Propositions 18–19).
