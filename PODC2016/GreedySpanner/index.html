The Greedy Spanner is Existentially Optimal — PODC 2016

Arnold Filtser, Shay Solomon. The Greedy Spanner is Existentially Optimal. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933114. Formalized version: arXiv 1605.06852 v2 (full version, January 2020); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. Lemma 3, Observation 2, Theorem 5 (greedy is existentially optimal for size and lightness in any family closed under edge removal), Lemmas 5 and 6, Observation 7 and Theorem 6 (near-optimality in doubling metrics) are all proved, with only a radius typo, an off-by-one count, a maximum that should be a supremum and loose connectivity conventions to correct. Corollaries 1–3 are proved relative to the cited external bounds, which are taken as hypotheses. The approximate-greedy construction of Section 5 was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • In the proof of Observation 7, the covering balls in M should have radius r/4, not 4r (the next step's "t · 4r ≤ 2r" is false; with r/4 the proof goes through, observation7).

  • OPT_t(𝒢) is defined as a maximum over 𝒢. For lightness over an infinite family of weighted graphs the maximum need not be attained; we use the supremum.

  • Section 2 assumes all graphs connected, but a family closed under edge removal contains disconnected graphs. We define the MST weight as the weight of a minimum spanning forest. For connected graphs it is the minimum weight of a spanning tree (mstWeight_eq_iInf_isTree).

  • In the proof of Observation 4, "contains at least n + 1 edges" should be n: replacing one edge of a spanning tree by a path of at least two edges gives at least n edges, which already force the cycle the proof needs.

Model.

  • The setting is sequential and centralized: Section 3 is pure graph theory, with no distributed model.

  • A weighted graph is a CSLib SimpleGraph V on a finite vertex type (n = |V|) with weights w : Sym2 V → ℝ≥0. Distances are CSLib's wdist, in ℝ≥0∞, equal to ⊤ when disconnected. The stretch t is a real number t ≥ 1.

  • IsWSpanner G H w t: H ≤ G and δ_H(u,v) ≤ t·δ_G(u,v) for all u, v.

  • Algorithm 1 is modelled literally (greedySpanner w t L, see greedySpanner_eq_foldl). It scans an edge list L and adds e = {u,v} iff δ_H(u,v) > t·w(e) for the current H. "The greedy t-spanner" (IsGreedySpanner) is the output of any execution: L lists every edge exactly once, in non-decreasing order of weight, with ties broken arbitrarily. Every result holds for every execution.

  • Lightness is Ψ(H) = w(H)/w(MST(G)) in ℝ≥0∞. w(MST(G)) (mstWeight) is the minimum weight of a subgraph with the same connected components as G, i.e. a minimum spanning forest.

  • A graph family is a set 𝒢 of weighted graphs on V. "Closed under edge removal" means (G,w) ∈ 𝒢, H ≤ G ⇒ (H,w) ∈ 𝒢.

  • A metric is a weight function on the complete graph ⊤: zero on the diagonal, positive off it, triangle inequality. M_H is the complete graph with weights δ_H. IsDoubling w D means every closed ball of radius r is covered by ≤ 2^D balls of radius r/2. M(n, D) is metricFamily V D.

Lemmas 5 and 6 are proved for an arbitrary connected weighted graph G, not only for metrics. Lemma 6 additionally needs positive edge weights and 1 ≤ t < 2. Lemma 3 and Observation 2 hold for every stretch t (the paper assumes t ≥ 1) and for non-negative weights (the paper assumes positive weights).

Proof remark.

  • Lemma 6 (|H| ≤ |H'|), an unstated step. The injection f : H → H' sets f(e) = e for e ∈ H ∩ H' because "an edge e' ∈ H ∩ H' covers itself". So the fixed shortest path P_{e'} in H between the endpoints of e' must be the edge e' itself. This fact is used in the injectivity step ("it must hold that e₁ and e₂ are in H \ H'"), but the paper never proves it. It is true for the greedy spanner: a different path of weight ≤ w(e') would make H − e' a t-spanner of H, contradicting Lemma 3. Our proof avoids the special case. For every e ∈ H, f(e) is an edge of a shortest H'-path Q_e whose P_{f(e)} contains e (lemma6).

Not formalized. The cited Theorems 1–4 and Lemma 1 are used only as hypotheses, in Corollaries 1–3. For Corollaries 1 and 2 the hypothesis is stated for all weighted graphs on V, including disconnected ones; this follows from the cited bounds by applying them per component. Section 5 is not formalized: the approximate-greedy algorithm, Lemmas 8 and 10, Observation 9 and Theorem 7. Its claims are about running time in the algebraic computation-tree model and rest on cited geometric lemmas. The illustrative Petersen-graph example of Figure 1 is also not formalized.

Observation 4 (MST of M_G) as stated is not formalized either. Its proof replaces an MST edge e ∈ M_G \ G by a shortest path with at least two edges, and treats only MST edges outside G. An edge of G heavier than the distance between its endpoints needs the same treatment, and the argument works for it verbatim. Theorem 6 does not need Observation 4: all edges of a greedy spanner of a metric are shortest paths, so we prove the equality w(MST(M_H)) = w(MST(M)) directly (mstWeight_inducedW).

Trust. No sorry. All listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: every weighted graph has a greedy t-spanner (exists_isGreedySpanner). The hypotheses of Theorem 6 are met by the uniform metric, which has doubling dimension ≤ log₂ n (isDoubling_logb_card, and the example at the end of Doubling.lean). Distances and weighted walks come from CSLib (WeightedSpanner.lean, ShortestWalks.lean). The Moore bound and the cycle-splitting lemma also come from CSLib (Girth.lean, Cycle.lean). Weighted spanners with real stretch, the local criterion, two-weight distance comparison, attainment of weighted distances by paths and the greedy spanner with its scan properties, girth and MST bypass property are the shared library Cslib/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/{WeightedSpanner,GreedySpanner}.lean (also used by Dinitz–Krauthgamer for the [ADD+93] spanner).

CSLib candidates (marked -- CSLib candidate): Lemma 3, mstWeight with the exchange lemma mstWeight_eq_of_forall_walk and mstWeight_eq_iInf_isTree, finite metrics and doubling dimension.
