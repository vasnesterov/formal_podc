Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Greedy Spanner is Existentially Optimal — PODC 2016.

Paper: Filtser, Solomon, The Greedy Spanner is Existentially Optimal, PODC 2016; full version arXiv 1605.06852v2. Classification tags: [gap] = proof gap with a true statement, [def] = under-specified definition, [model] = modelling choice, [stronger] = we prove more than stated, [cond] = cited result taken as a hypothesis, [omitted] = not formalized.

Model and definitions

  1. [model] Weighted graphs are SimpleGraph V with w : Sym2 V → ℝ≥0 on a finite vertex type V with |V| = n. An "n-vertex graph family" is a set of weighted graphs on this fixed V, which is the same up to relabelling. Weights may be 0 wherever the paper's positivity is not needed (see items 9 and 13).

  2. [model] The greedy spanner (Algorithm 1) is modelled as a literal scan greedySpanner w t L over an edge list L. IsGreedySpanner G w t H says that H is the output for some list L containing each edge of G exactly once in non-decreasing weight order. This is the paper's "H designates an arbitrary such spanner" when weights tie. All results are proved for every such execution.

  3. [def] OPT_t^sparse(𝒢)/OPT_t^light(𝒢) are defined in §1.3 as a maximum over 𝒢. For infinite families of weighted graphs the maximum of the lightness need not be attained, so we use the supremum (familyOptSparse, familyOptLight, metricOptSparse, metricOptLight, valued in ℕ∞ and ℝ≥0∞). The per-graph optima optSparse/optLight are infima over all t-spanners. They are attained because the graphs are finite, but we never need that.

  4. [def] Section 2 assumes all graphs are connected, but a family closed under edge removal must contain disconnected graphs. mstWeight G w is the minimum weight of a subgraph with the same connected components as G, i.e. a minimum spanning forest. mstWeight_eq_iInf_isTree shows that for connected G this is the minimum weight of a spanning tree, i.e. w(MST(G)).

  5. [model] Lightness is wt w H / mstWeight G w in ℝ≥0∞. With n ≤ 1, or with no edges, this is 0/0 = 0. Such degenerate cases are irrelevant to the paper.

  6. [model] Stretch t : ℝ≥0. The paper's convention t ≥ 1 is assumed where it is used: the greedy output being a t-spanner, Theorems 5 and 6, and Lemmas 5 and 6.

  7. [model] A metric space on V is a weight function on the complete graph ⊤ (IsMetricW): 0 on the diagonal, positive off it, triangle inequality. The paper also views metrics as complete weighted graphs. The induced metric M_H has weights inducedW H w ({x,y}) = δ_H(x,y). It is a metric when H is a spanner of a metric (isMetricW_inducedW).

  8. [def] The doubling dimension is not defined explicitly in the paper. We use the standard definition (IsDoubling w D): every closed ball B(x,r) is covered by at most 2^D closed balls of radius r/2, with centres in V and D ∈ ℝ. Then M(n, D) = metricFamily V D.

Statements

  1. [stronger] Lemma 3 (lemma3) is proved without the hypothesis t ≥ 1 and for non-negative weights.

  2. [stronger] Observation 2 (IsGreedySpanner.exists_mst_le, IsGreedySpanner.mstWeight_eq) holds for every t and for non-negative weights. It is derived from a general exchange lemma (mstWeight_eq_of_forall_walk): if every edge uv of G missing from H ≤ G is bypassed in H by a walk of edges of weight ≤ w(uv), then H contains a minimum spanning forest of G. The paper calls Observation 2 "immediate" and cites [ENS14, CW18].

  3. [stronger] Theorem 5 (theorem5) is proved for any family closed under edge removal; the family may contain disconnected graphs. We also prove the two equalities that §1.3 uses as the definition of existential optimality (existentially_optimal_sparse/_light). The intermediate facts optSparse H w t = |H| and optLight H w t = Ψ_G(H) are optSparse_greedy and optLight_greedy.

  4. [cond]/[stronger] Corollary 1. The size part is proved unconditionally and with an explicit bound (corollary1_size). For every real t ≥ 2k − 1, which includes (2k−1)(1+ε), the greedy spanner has no cycle of length ≤ 2k (IsGreedySpanner.noShortCycles). Hence it has ≤ n^{1+1/k} + n edges by CSLib's Moore bound. The paper instead derives the size bound from Theorem 5 and Theorem 1 [CW18]. The lightness part O(n^{1/k}(1/ε)^{3+2/k}) rests on the cited Theorem 1 [CW18]. We prove the transfer statement greedy_le_of_forall_exists: any upper bound m on the size and ℓ on the lightness of some t-spanner of every weighted graph on V holds for the greedy t-spanner. The O(·) statements become explicit bounds taken as hypotheses. The hypothesis ranges over all weighted graphs on V, including disconnected ones and ones with zero weights, whereas the cited results are stated (under the paper's §2 convention) for connected graphs with positive weights. The cited bounds extend to these graphs by applying them to each connected component, so the hypothesis is still a true statement, but it is slightly stronger than the literal cited one.

  5. [cond] Corollary 2 is the same transfer statement (greedy_le_of_forall_exists) with the cited bound of [BFN19] as the hypothesis.

  6. [stronger] Lemmas 5 and 6 (lemma5, lemma6) are stated in the paper for metric spaces. We prove them for an arbitrary connected weighted graph G; a metric is the case G = ⊤. The triangle inequality of G is never used. Lemma 6 needs positive weights on the edges of G and 1 ≤ t < 2.

  7. [gap] Lemma 6, proof. "An edge e' ∈ H ∩ H' covers itself" assumes that the chosen shortest H-path P_{e'} between the endpoints of e' ∈ H is the edge e' itself. This is used in the injectivity argument ("It must hold that e₁ and e₂ are in H \ H'"). It is true for greedy spanners: otherwise H − e' would be a t-spanner of H, contradicting Lemma 3. The paper does not argue it. Our proof treats all e ∈ H uniformly. Q_e is a shortest H'-path with w_{M_H}(Q_e) ≤ t·w(e). Some edge f(e) ∈ Q_e has e ∈ P_{f(e)}, since otherwise H − e is a t-spanner of H (Lemma 3 again). Two edges with the same image would give w_{M_H}(f(e)) ≥ 2·min(w(e₁), w(e₂)) > t·min(…).

  8. [model] Observation 7 (observation7) is proved as stated for t ≤ 2, for any t-spanner H ≤ ⊤ of a metric in M(n, D). Typo in the paper's proof: the ball is covered by 2^{2ddim} balls of radius "4r" in M; this should be radius r/4 (two applications of the doubling property), which then lie in balls of radius t·r/4 ≤ r/2 in M_H. The Lean proof uses r/4.

  9. [gap] Observation 4 is not formalized. Its proof considers only MST edges of M_G that are not edges of G. An edge of G that is not a shortest path needs the same (verbatim) argument. In Theorem 6, Observations 2 and 4 are used only to show that M and M_H have the same MST weight. We prove this directly (mstWeight_inducedW): w ≤ w_{M_H} everywhere, with equality on the edges of H, and H contains an MST of M (Observation 2). Typo in the proof of Observation 4: "at least n + 1 edges" should be n, which already forces the cycle.

  10. [model] Theorem 6 (theorem6) assumes 1 ≤ t < 2; the paper writes only t < 2. For t < 1 no t-spanner of a metric with n ≥ 2 exists, so that case is degenerate.

  11. [cond] Corollary 3 (corollary3_of_forall_exists) takes the cited Theorem 4 [Got15], and for the size Theorem 3 [Smi09], as the hypothesis "every metric in M(n, 2D) has a t-spanner with ≤ m edges and lightness ≤ ℓ". The (ddim/ε)^{O(ddim)} bounds become this hypothesis.

Not formalized

  1. [omitted] Theorems 1–4 and Lemma 1 (all cited) appear only as hypotheses.

  2. [omitted] Section 5 is not formalized: Algorithm Approximate-Greedy, Lemma 8 (cited from [GLN02]), Observation 9 (cited from [Smi07]), Lemma 10 and Theorem 7. These are running-time claims (algebraic computation-tree model) on top of cited geometric facts.

  3. [omitted] The Petersen-graph example of Figure 1 / §1.3, which shows that the greedy spanner is not instance-optimal, is not formalized. It is an illustration, not a claim used later.
