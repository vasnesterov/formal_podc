Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Unbeatable Set Consensus — PODC 2016.

Paper: Castañeda, Gonczarowski, Moses, Unbeatable Set Consensus via Topological and Combinatorial Reasoning, PODC 2016, arXiv 1605.07354v1.

Errors in the paper

  1. Lemma 1 is false as stated. Counterexample with n = 6, k = 2, t = 1, m = 1, inputs in any D ⊇ {0,1,2} (lemma1_paper_false, Counterexample.lean). The protocol cexProtocol solves nonuniform 2-set consensus with t = 1, and low processes decide at time 0. Hypotheses 1–4 of Lemma 1 hold for process 0 at time 1 with v = 0, but it decides 2. The gap is in the "changes" of the combinatorial proof (and in the topological proof). A process j_b must receive no message carrying an already-used low value in round m. Messages carrying v from processes that do not crash in round m cannot be suppressed without exceeding t crashes. The failure is not tied to t < k (where k-set consensus is easy). Pen-and-paper variant for any t ≥ 1, k ≥ 2, D = {0, …, k} (e.g. t = k = 2, n = 7), not in Lean: take Optmin[k] and add the rule "at time 1, a process that was high at 0, whose only low value is 0 and that saw 0 from ≥ t + 1 processes decides k". Such a node proves that a correct process holds 0. Then every process alive at time ≥ 1 is low with Min = 0, and the correct processes that decided at time 0 also have input 0. So the correct decisions are in {0, k}. In runs without such a node the protocol behaves like Optmin[k]. With three carriers x, y, w of 0 and the same i, z, j₁, j₂ as above, i decides k. Fix: hypothesis 5 (Lemma1Hyp.lowCrash): every process alive and low at m − 1 crashes in round m. It holds when some process is high at time m (crash_eq_of_low_of_high). That covers both uses: Lemma 3, and the induction hypothesis inside Lemma 1 (where i is high at m − 1).

  2. Lemma 1, proof: the induction hypothesis is applied to i_w "to decide by m − 1" after checking only hypothesis 3. We apply it at the first time t₀ ≤ m − 1 at which i_w is low. We check hypotheses 1, 2 and 4 (the j_b, made correct, are hidden from ⟨i_w, t₀⟩ and high before) and 5.

  3. Lemma 5, m = 0: "all initial values are 0" contradicts r'_i(0) = r_i(0) when i's input is a low value other than 0. We let all inputs equal i's input.

  4. Theorem 3, proof: "K_j ∃correct(w)" (= "j knows that w will persist") is used as "some correct process knows w at m'_j". We prove instead the persistence claim stated after Definition 3 (persist), which suffices. We check that the d known failures are disjoint from the t − d carriers and from j.

  5. Typo in the proof of Theorem 3: v = Min⟨i, m'_i⟩ < k − 1 should be ≤ k − 1 (< k).

Modelling choices

  1. Runs / adversaries are CSLib SyncCrash.Run P ℕ: input vector, crash round c ≥ 1, and the set reached in round c. This is exactly the paper's crash failure pattern (correct before, partial in round c, silent after). Adm t D r: at most t crashes, inputs in D.

  2. Inputs are natural numbers in a set D with {0, …, k} ⊆ D (hypothesis hD). This includes the paper's {0, …, k} and footnote 4 ({0, …, d}, d ≥ k), and also D = ℕ. "High" means ≥ k. The paper's n ≥ 2 and t ≤ n − 1 are not needed.

  3. Protocols are decision rules of full-information protocols: dec r i m : Option ℕ, invariant under equality of views (SameView = CSLib FIIndist on the communication graph comm r). The local state ⟨β, G_α(i, m)⟩ is represented by the view alone: β is a function of the views at earlier times, which the view at m determines. A process decides at the first time its rule fires while it is alive (DecidesAt). LockStep.lean shows that every deterministic message-passing protocol of CSLib's SyncCrash model is such a rule with the same first-decision times (hist_eq_of_sameView, decidesAt_ofLockStep_iff). Hence theorem1_lockStep. This is our proof of the paper's appeal to Coan.

  4. Views of crashed processes are defined (the view a process would have). The paper does not define local states of crashed processes. Decisions require being alive. Hidden nodes may be nodes of processes that already crashed, as in the paper.

  5. Seen = message chain (DynNet.Journey on comm r, with stays). Guaranteed crashed (GuarCrashed): ⟨i, m⟩ has seen a node ⟨h, s + 1⟩ with s + 1 ≤ ℓ, h ≠ j, that did not receive j's round-s + 1 message. This makes the paper's "i heard from someone who did not hear from j in some round ≤ ℓ" precise (including h = i).

  6. Vals⟨i, m⟩ is defined as the set of seen inputs. The paper defines it by knowledge (K_i ∃v). The paper itself identifies the two; the direction used (decisions are seen values) is decided_mem_vals, our instance of Theorem 4 (KoP) for Validity. It needs two distinct values in D.

  7. Hidden capacity hc r i m = minimum over ℓ ≤ m of the number of nodes at time ℓ hidden from ⟨i, m⟩ (Definition 2).

  8. Unbeatability (Def. 1): Unbeatable k t D P = P solves the problem, and every solution Q with Q ⪰ P satisfies P ⪰ Q. This is equivalent to "no solution strictly dominates P". Domination is per process and per adversary, as in §2.2.

  9. Last-decider domination (Def. 6): "the last decision" is read as the last decision of a correct process (as in [4]): LDDominates Q P iff, in every run, whenever all correct processes have decided by m in P, they have decided by m in Q. This is equivalent to comparing the times of the last correct decisions.

  10. Uniform k-set consensus (SolvesUniformSetConsensus): Decision for correct processes, Validity, and at most k values decided by any processes.

  11. Definition 3: "i knows of d failures at m" is read as d = number of processes p with ⟨p, m⟩ guaranteed crashed at ⟨i, m⟩ (known). The nodes ⟨j, m − 1⟩ "seen by ⟨i, m⟩ that have seen v" are carriers; for m = 0 there are none. The condition "i is active at m" in the first bullet is not part of the rule: decisions are only taken by live processes.

  12. Protocol u-Pmin[k]: the first condition is parsed (low ∨ HC < k) ∧ persists (checked on the rendered PDF).

Statements

  1. Proposition 1: "all processes decide by ⌊f/k⌋ + 1" is stated for processes alive at that time (optmin_decidesBy); f is the number of crashes of the run.

  2. Lemma 1: stated for a process x alive at m (implicit in the paper). Hypotheses 1 and 4 are vacuous at m = 0 ("⟨j, −1⟩ is high"). Hypothesis 5 is added (item 1). The conclusion is exactly DecidesAt Q r x m v (first decision at m, value v).

  3. Lemma 2 is not one theorem but the construction chainRun with the lemmas ChainHyp.sameView (r'_i(m) = r_i(m)), .mem_vals (a), .vals_subset (b), .hidden_col and .hidden_of_not_w (c). It is parametrized by the last time L ≤ m of the witness columns. Lemma 3 uses L = m; Lemma 1 uses L = m − 1 and re-chooses the round-m messages (lastRun). The last column crashes in round L + 1 sending nothing; making i and other processes correct ("w.l.o.g. never fail") is the separate operation modify. We assume i alive at m (the paper: active at m − 1).

  4. Lemma 3 is stated for live ⟨x, m⟩ and concludes Q.dec r x m = none: the rule does not fire, which is stronger than "does not decide at m".

  5. Lemma 1's sequence of changes is processed in the order b = 0, 1, …, k − 1 instead of k, …, 1; this is immaterial.

  6. Theorem 3: the time bound is stated for processes alive at min(⌊t/k⌋ + 1, ⌊f/k⌋ + 2).
