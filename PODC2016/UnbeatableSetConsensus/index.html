Unbeatable Set Consensus — PODC 2016

Armando Castañeda, Yannai A. Gonczarowski, Yoram Moses. Unbeatable Set Consensus via Topological and Combinatorial Reasoning. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933120. Formalized version: arXiv 1605.07354 v1 (full version with appendices); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main results hold: Optmin[k] solves nonuniform k-set consensus with decisions by ⌊f/k⌋ + 1 (Prop. 1), it is unbeatable (Thm. 1) and last-decider unbeatable (Thm. 2), and u-Pmin[k] solves uniform k-set consensus by min(⌊t/k⌋+1, ⌊f/k⌋+2) (Thm. 3). But Lemma 1, the core of the combinatorial unbeatability proof, is false as stated; we prove it under an extra hypothesis that holds wherever the paper uses it, so the proof of Theorem 1 goes through. The topological proof of Lemma 1 was not checked beyond noting that it shares the gap.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 1 (low processes force the decision v at m)

Lemma 1 is false: with n = 6, k = 2, t = 1, m = 1 there is a 2-set consensus protocol in which low processes decide at once and a process i satisfying all four hypotheses with v = 0 decides 2 (lemma1_paper_false).

Impact. Lemma 1 is the core of the combinatorial unbeatability proof, but it holds with an extra hypothesis that is true wherever the paper uses it, so Lemma 3 and Theorems 1–2 are unaffected (lemma1 with Lemma1Hyp.lowCrash).

Details

Lemma 1 says: in any solution in which low processes decide at once, a process i that becomes low at m with a single low value v, hidden capacity ≥ k − 1, and k hidden processes j_b that are high at m − 1 must decide v at m. In the counterexample i has input 2, two processes x, y have input 0, a process z crashes in round 1 sending nothing, and j₁, j₂ have input 2. All four hypotheses hold for i at time 1 with v = 0 (⟨z, 0⟩ and ⟨j₁, 1⟩ witness HC ≥ 1). The protocol: a low process decides its input at time 0; at time 1, a high process that missed a round-1 message and whose only low value is 0 decides its smallest high value, and every other process decides its minimum. It solves 2-set consensus for t = 1: whoever misses a message knows the unique faulty process, so 0 is held by correct processes and only one more value can be decided. Here i decides 2.

The proof fails in the "changes": when j_{b'} (b' > b) has decided v, process j_b must get no message carrying v in round m. That needs every process that told i about v to crash in round m, which can exceed t (here x and y would both have to crash). The failure does not depend on t < k: with t + 1 carriers of 0, a variant of Optmin[k] breaks the lemma for every t ≥ 1, k ≥ 2 (sketch in differencies.md, item 1).

Repair

We prove Lemma 1 with an extra hypothesis 5: every process alive and low at m − 1 crashes in round m. In the proof of Lemma 3, hypothesis 5 holds because i is still high at m. Inside Lemma 1's own induction it holds because i is high at m − 1. (lemma1 with Lemma1Hyp.lowCrash)

✏️ Slips

  • Prop. 1 and Thm. 3 say "all processes decide by …"; this holds for processes alive at that time.

  • Proof of Theorem 3: "v = Min⟨i, m′_i⟩ < k − 1" should be ≤ k − 1.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 1 · Lemma 5

🔧 Lemma 1 (induction step: unchecked hypotheses)

The induction hypothesis is applied to the processes i_w "to decide w by time m − 1" after checking only hypothesis 3 (HC ≥ k − 1).

Impact. The (repaired) statement is true; the step is fixed (lemma1_step).

Repair

The lemma must be applied at the first time i_w is low, and hypothesis 4 must be checked too. It holds: j_1, …, j_k, made correct, are hidden from ⟨i_w, m''⟩ and high before. (lemma1_step)

🔧 Lemma 5 (Appendix D, case m = 0)

The run r' "with all initial values 0" contradicts r'_i(0) = r_i(0) when i's low input is not 0.

Impact. Statement true; the proof is fixed (lemma5).

Repair

All inputs equal to i's input works.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous message passing, n processes (any Fintype), crash failures with at most t crashes (no constraint t < n is needed). An adversary is CSLib's SyncCrash.Run: input vector, crash round, and the set reached in the crashing round. Inputs are naturals in a set D ⊇ {0, …, k}; the paper's {0, …, k} and its footnote-4 generalization are special cases.

  • Full-information protocols. The communication graph comm r depends only on the adversary. A view is CSLib's full-information indistinguishability FIIndist on that graph (SameView). A protocol is a decision rule dec r i m : Option ℕ invariant under SameView. A process decides when the rule first fires while it is alive. LockStep.lean shows that every deterministic CSLib protocol, with arbitrary messages, gives such a rule with the same decision times (Coan's reduction), so Theorem 1 holds against all of them (theorem1_lockStep).

  • Seen = message chain (DynNet.Journey). Guaranteed crashed = ⟨i, m⟩ has seen a node ⟨h, s + 1⟩, s + 1 ≤ ℓ, that missed j's round-s + 1 message. Hidden, HC, Vals, Min and low follow Definitions 2 and 5.

  • Unbeatability (Def. 1) quantifies over all solutions Q: Q ⪰ Optmin ⇒ Optmin ⪰ Q, comparing per-process decision times on the same adversary. Last-decider domination (Def. 6) compares the times by which all correct processes have decided.

Reading of Theorem 3. The proof writes K_j ∃correct(w) ("j knows that w will persist") and then uses it as "some correct process knows w", a consequence that needs its own argument. We prove the persistence claim stated after Definition 3 directly (persist): everyone alive at m + 1 has seen w. The count t − d there also needs d to count processes disjoint from the t − d carriers; we show this.
