Taubenfeld, *Computing in the presence of weak crash failures*, PODC 2016; full version *Weak Failures: Definitions, Algorithms and Impossibility Results*, §5: Theorem 7, Corollaries 1 and 2. Model: the asynchronous read/write SWMR model `Cslib.SWMR` (deterministic processes, atomic single-writer registers, executions given by schedules). An algorithm *tolerates* a failure pattern `adm : (ℕ → P) → Prop` (`WSolves A adm T`) if every finite execution satisfies the task (safety) and, in every infinite execution whose schedule satisfies `adm`, every correct process decides (termination). * `kSetConsensus k`: validity (a decided value is the input of some process) and k-agreement (at most `k` distinct decided values). * `WeakInitAdmissible m ℓ k`: "k crash m-failures and ℓ initial failures". * `WeakAdmissible m f` (from `Failures.lean`): "f crash m-failures". * `KSetImpossible K Vr V k`: the classical impossibility of k-set consensus tolerating `k` crash failures with more than `k` processes (Borowsky–Gafni, Herlihy–Shavit, Saks–Zaharoglou). It is **not** formalized here and appears as a hypothesis of Theorem 7 and Corollary 1. For `k = 1` it is FLP (Loui–Abu-Amara), which *is* formalized (`Alg.not_solves_binConsensus`), so Corollary 2 is unconditional (`kSetImpossible_one`). ## Main statements * `theorem7`: Theorem 7 (with the hypothesis `KSetImpossible`). * `corollary1`: Corollary 1, under the weaker bound `n > ℓ + k` (the paper assumes `n > 2ℓ + k`). * `corollary2`, `corollary2_paper`, `no_consensus_two_failures`: Corollary 2 (unconditional), for `n > ℓ + 1`, and in the paper's form (`2ℓ < n`) under the extra assumption `n ≥ 2`. * `corollary2_false_n1`: for `n = 1, ℓ = 0` Corollary 2 as stated is false: a single process deciding its own input solves consensus tolerating one crash 1-failure. * `weakAdmissible_iff_of_card_two`: for `n = 2`, two crash 1-failures are the same failure pattern as one crash
