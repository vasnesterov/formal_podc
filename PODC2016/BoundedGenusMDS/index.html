Local MDS Approximation on Bounded Genus Graphs — PODC 2016

Saeed Akhoondian Amiri, Stefan Schmid, Sebastian Siebertz. A Local Constant Factor MDS Approximation for Bounded Genus Graphs. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933084. Formalized version: arXiv 1602.02991 v2 (the latest arXiv version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The local constant factor on bounded genus graphs stands: on graphs whose depth-1 minors have edge density ≤ c and which exclude K_{t,3} as a depth-1 minor, the modified Lenzen–Pignolet–Wattenhofer algorithm is a (6c²t + (2t+5)c + 4)-approximation (Lemma 12), which we prove with the paper's constant, for every choice of dominators, and it runs in 6 LOCAL rounds. The counting lemmas behind it are false as stated (Lemma 7, Corollary 8 and Lemma 11 with Lean counterexamples, Lemma 9 with an informal one), but local changes repair them. With the genus facts as hypotheses, the O(g) factor of Theorem 13 is only proved as O(g²), and Section 4's 24g + O(1) factor was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Theorem 13 (O(g) approximation factor)

The O(g) factor is not established: the proof treats the density bound c as a constant, but Lemmas 5 and 10 need it for small depth-1 minors, where genus-g graphs have density Θ(√g), so the argument only gives O(g²) (density_unbounded).

Impact. The O(g) factor is unestablished, not refuted. A local constant factor for every fixed genus stands: we prove an O(g²) approximation (≤ 1100(g+1)², theorem_13, factor_le).

Details

Lemma 12's factor 6c²t + … is O(g) only if c = O(1). The paper takes c ≈ 3 ("c ≤ 3.01 in sufficiently large graphs", §4). However, Lemmas 5 and 10 apply the density bound to small depth-1 minors as well, and small graphs of genus g have density up to Θ(√g); for example, K_k has genus ⌈(k-3)(k-4)/12⌉. So c = Θ(√g) is necessary, and with t = 4g + 3 the argument gives O(g²). We show that the hypotheses alone force c to grow with g (density_unbounded). The claimed O(g) rounds are an overstatement in the other direction: the algorithm needs 6 rounds (output_isLocal).

Repair

We prove the theorem with the genus facts as hypotheses: c(g) = 3 + ⌊√(3g)⌋ + 1 suffices, and the factor is ≤ 1100(g+1)² (theorem_13, factor_le).

🔧 Lemma 7, items 2 and 3, and Corollary 8 (neighbours between stars)

Lemma 7 fails on the planar graph m_i – c' – u_k – m_j (k = 1..K), where all K leaves of m_j see the star of m_i, beyond the bound 2ct = 12 (item 2, K = 13) or 4ct = 24 (item 3, K = 25), and in the same graph Corollary 8's |Y| ≤ 6c²t|M| fails for K = 145, since Y contains all u_k, so |Y| ≥ K, while |M| = 2 (lemma_7_2_false, lemma_7_3_false, corollary_8_false).

Impact. The counting behind Lemma 12 breaks: its final count adds the faulty |Y| bound of Corollary 8 (and relies on the false Lemma 11). Corrected versions (bounds 2c(t-1) and 4c(t-1), a smaller Y) suffice to prove Lemma 12 with the paper's constant (lemma_12).

Details

The K_{t,3} argument picks c ∈ C_{m_j} and contracts the star of m_i minus c. If c is itself a leaf of m_i (c ∈ N_i), the vertices u ∈ N_j whose only neighbour in N_i is c are not bounded at all. In the counterexample, the graph is K_{2,K} on {c', m_j} × {u_k} plus the pendant edge m_i c'. Assumption 6 holds with c = 2, t = 3. M = {m_i, m_j} is a minimum dominating set and D = ∅. The stars are N_i = {c'} and N_j = {u_1, …, u_K}. Item 2 uses the order m_i, m_j; item 3 the order m_j, m_i. Item 1 has the same gap but is not refuted formally.

Repair

Count only leaves outside C = ⋃ C_m that have a neighbour outside C. Then the bounds are 2c(t-1) and 4c(t-1) (Setup.lemma_7_12, Setup.lemma_7_3). Our Y excludes C. With the corrected Lemma 7 we get |Y| + |Y_D| ≤ 6c²(t-1)|M|, where Y_D is the part seeing stars of M ∩ D (Setup.corollary_8). Lemma 12 then counts dominator images instead:

  • M ∪ C, Z (Lemma 10), and the corrected Y;

  • images of sources in M ∪ C or in Y_D;

  • images inside stars (corrected Lemma 11).

The total is (c+1) + 3(2c+1) + 6c²(t-1) + (2(t-1)+4)c ≤ 6c²t + (2t+5)c + 4 (per |M|).

🔧 Lemma 11 (dominators inside a star)

Lemma 11 fails on the book K_{1,1,K} (spine m x, pages u_k) with the legal dominator choice dom ≡ x: all K + 1 leaves of m have their dominator inside the star outside Y ∪ Z, beyond the bound (2(t-1)+4)c|M| = 16 for K = 17 (lemma_11_false).

Impact. Repaired by also excluding C from the dominator's range; then the paper's bound holds and Lemma 12 goes through.

Details

The proof applies Lemma 9 to dom(u), which may lie in C_m. In the counterexample, Assumption 6 holds with c = 2, t = 3; M = {m}, D = ∅, C_m = {x} (every admissible C_m contains x), and Y = Z = ∅. Since m and x tie for the maximal residual degree, dom ≡ x is a legal choice ("choose any").

Repair

Exclude C from the dominator's range as well. Then the paper's bound (2(t-1)+4)c|M| holds (Setup.lemma_11).

🔧 Lemma 9 (for some admissible C_m)

Lemma 9 fails when m and v are both adjacent to 18 vertices forming 6 paths a_i – c_i – b_i and C_m = {c_1, …, c_6}: v has 18 neighbours in the star, more than 2c(t-1) = 12 (informal counterexample, not formalized).

Impact. Repaired by counting only neighbours outside C, which is all Lemma 11 needs.

Details

The K_{t,3} argument covers only u ∈ N(c) for c ∈ C_m, not u ∈ C_m itself. In the example the graph is planar, so c = 3, t = 3, and D = ∅; C_m covers N(m) and v ∉ C_m.

Repair

Count only the neighbours outside C; then the bound 2c(t-1) holds (Setup.lemma_9).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 10's strict |Z| < |M ∪ C| fails for the empty graph, where both sides are 0. We prove ≤, which is all the paper uses (Setup.lemma_10).

  • Lemma 12 assumes "edge density at most c", but it needs Assumption 6 (density of all depth-1 minors).

  • The final line of the proof of Lemma 12 drops the factor |M|.

Typos

  • Algorithm 1 has a stray end if.

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 5 · Lemma 11 · Lemma 7

🔧 Lemma 5 (the contracted minor H̃)

H̃ contracts only the stars of m ∈ M \ D, so vertices of N(D \ M) \ (D ∪ M) whose unique M-neighbour lies in M ∩ D stay uncontracted and |V(H̃)| = |D \ M| + |M| is wrong.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (lemma_5), and it holds for every dominating set M.

Repair

We contract a star around every m ∈ M and exclude D-vertices from the leaves.

🔧 Lemma 11 (the E'-neighbours of m)

Lemma 11's proof says that m has at most 4c + 2c(t-1) E'-neighbours, but this fails when leaves of m are dominated by D: only the undominated ones are bounded, by deg(dom(u)).

Impact. The corrected Lemma 11 goes through (Setup.lemma_11).

Repair

Use the residual degree: d_{V-D}(m) ≤ d_{V-D}(dom(u)).

🔧 Lemma 7 (m_i ∉ C_{m_2})

In the proof of Lemma 7, "m_i ∉ C_{m_2}" should be C_{m_j}, and the corrected claim is still unjustified, since C_{m_j} is arbitrary and may contain m_i.

Impact. Harmless, because m_i covers no vertex of N_j (Setup.lemma_7_12).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Finite simple graphs G : SimpleGraph V.

  • LOCAL model: synchronous and deterministic, with no failures and unique identifiers.

  • The algorithm is the paper's modified Algorithm 1 with integer parameter c (line 5: |A| ≤ 2c).

  • Approximation: the Phase-2 dominator choice ("choose any dom(v) ∈ N[v] of maximal residual degree") is a hypothesis IsDominator, so the bound holds for every tie-breaking. "f-approximation" is stronger than the paper's: the output dominates and |S| ≤ f·|M| for every dominating set M.

  • Locality: identifiers are the elements of a linearly ordered V, and ties go to the smallest identifier (domMin). output_isLocal shows that membership of v in the output depends only on the radius-6 view of v (CSLib's Cslib.Local.IsLocal).

  • Depth-1 minors are defined as in §2 (pairwise disjoint stars with centers); "ε(H) ≤ c for all depth-1 minors" is Depth1DensityLE G c (|E(H)| ≤ c|V(H)|).

  • Mathlib has no surfaces, so the genus facts (Lemmas 1–4) are hypotheses for Theorem 13. Every depth-1 minor satisfies Euler's bound |E(H)| ≤ 3|V(H)| + 6g (Lemmas 1, 4), and G excludes K_{4g+3,3} (Lemma 2).
