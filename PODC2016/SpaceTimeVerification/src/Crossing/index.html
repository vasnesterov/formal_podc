Theorem 5 concludes that the label size of every `t`-PLS is `Ω(log r / q)` when some component of every crossing violates `P`. The proof says that every node of `N_t(e_i) ∪ N_t(e_j)` has "the same" `t`-neighbourhood in `G` and in the crossing. For `t ≥ 2` this is false when `e_i` lies on a short cycle: a node of a triangle sees, in two rounds, that its two neighbours are adjacent; after crossing two triangles they lie on a 6-cycle. Counterexample (`t = 2`, `q = 3`, every `r ≥ 2`): `G_r` = `r` disjoint triangles `{(i,0), (i,1), (i,2)}`, `e_i = ((i,0), (i,1))`; `P` = "every node lies on a triangle", `F` = all graphs. All hypotheses of Proposition 4 and Theorem 5 hold (`triangles_hyp`), and a crossing of two triangles is a 6-cycle on which `P` fails. But a 2-round verifier with *empty* labels decides `P` (`triScheme`: a node accepts iff two of its neighbours are adjacent; this is a function of its radius-2 ball, so it is allowed in the model with identifiers whose output does not depend on the identifiers, and in the ball model `BallIso`).
