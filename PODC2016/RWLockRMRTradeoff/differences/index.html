Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of On the Complexity of Reader-Writer Locks — PODC 2016.

Hendler, On the Complexity of Reader-Writer Locks, PODC 2016 (author copy "ReadersWritersLock-CR", Extended Abstract, 11 pp.). Numbering follows the paper.

Model

  1. Algorithms. A reader-writer lock for n readers and m writers is a CSLib implementation RWAlg n m ι V v₀ L := Impl mutexObj (Regs ι V v₀) (Proc n m) L of the lock object (lock = entry section, unlock = exit section) from registers rwcas V v₀, processes Proc n m = Fin n ⊕ Fin m. Processes are deterministic. Registers support read, write and CAS; CAS returns the old value, as in the paper (CSLib's casObject returns only a flag, which would cover fewer algorithms). Initial values are arbitrary (memInit).

  2. Steps. CSLib labels inv p o, tau p, ret p r. An invocation and a response are labels without a memory access (in the paper the first step of the entry section is already a memory access). They incur no RMR and never change knowledge, so counts are unaffected.

  3. Sections. remainder / entry / CS / exit = CSLib regions rem / trying / cs / exiting, computed from the history (regionOf). Properties are required for client well-formed executions (ClientWF), as in CSLib's mutex definitions.

  4. Extra hypothesis ∀ p o, I.allowed p o: every process may invoke lock/unlock (CSLib's Impl has an allowed field; the paper has no such notion).

  5. Mutual Exclusion (RWMutualExclusion): as in the paper.

  6. Bounded Exit (BoundedExit) and Concurrent Entering (ConcurrentEntering): the bound may depend on the execution prefix after which the exit (entry) section is running. This is weaker than the paper's constant bound, so our theorem is stronger. The paper's Concurrent Entering says "any reader that executes at most b steps … enters the CS"; we read it as "a reader that has taken b steps of its entry section (writers in the remainder section and not stepping) is in the CS", the evident intent (✏️).

  7. Deadlock Freedom: CSLib's Impl.DeadlockFree (in every fair client well-formed infinite execution, if at time t some operation is pending and nobody is in the CS, some operation completes later). The paper's version (infinitely many passages complete in every infinite execution without fail-stops outside the remainder section) implies it, so this hypothesis is weaker than the paper's. Only its consequence solo_returns_of_deadlockFree is used.

  8. CC model. The paper quotes the write-back and write-through protocols of Golab et al. We prove the lower bound for every CacheModel (three axioms: an access without a valid cached copy is an RMR; only the stepping process can gain a valid copy, of the variable it accesses; a step that changes the value of v invalidates the other processes' copies of v). writeBack and writeThrough are instances. Modelling choices inside the instances: in write-back every CAS needs an exclusive copy; in write-through every write/CAS is an RMR and leaves the writer with a valid copy.

  9. RMR complexity. WriterEntryRMR I cm Wb: in every execution, a writer incurs at most Wb RMRs between leaving the remainder section and starting its exit section. ReaderExitRMR I cm Rb: a reader incurs at most Rb RMRs from the CS until it starts a new entry section. Worst-case bounds over all executions, as in the paper.

  10. Knowledge. Definitions 1–2 are formalized forward along a fragment (kstep): a reading step (read or CAS) of p on v adds F(v) to AW(p); a non-trivial write sets F(v) := AW(p); a non-trivial CAS sets F(v) := AW(p) ∪ F(v); trivial steps leave F(v) unchanged. This is the paper's backward recursive definition read forward (equal by an immediate induction on E; not formalized separately). Knowledge is reset at the beginning of a fragment (Ext.fresh). Definition 3 (Expanding) is verbatim. Fact 1 = expanding_iff, Observation 1 = kstep_aw / xrun_aw_mono, Observation 2 is used through KInv.fam_sub (|F(v)| ≤ max_p |AW(p)|).

Statements

  1. Lemma 1 is false (🛠️): the fix changes the content of the claim and the paper's proof reasons with the wrong case. In the write-back model, q writes 1 to v, then p writes 1 to v (a trivial write, so F(v) stays {q}, but p gets an exclusive copy), then p reads v: expanding, no RMR (lemma1_false; same in write-through when a write leaves the writer with a valid copy: lemma1_false_writeThrough). The proof assumes that some q ≠ p writes v while p holds a valid copy, which fails when the last non-trivial write precedes p's caching step. Replaced by lemma1_count: in every fragment (arbitrary cache state at its start), every process has at least as many RMRs as expanding steps. This is what Theorem 5 needs.

  2. Lemma 2 (lemma2): stated for an arbitrary set T of processes poised on memory accesses (the paper: all processes in their entry or exit section); conclusion |AW(p)| ≤ 3B for all p whenever |AW(p)| ≤ B for all p before. True.

  3. Lemma 3 (lemma3): "steps executed by other processes when they are aware of p" is read as "steps after which the stepping process is aware of p" (awareness after the step). Read literally (awareness before the step) the statement is false: if p writes 1 to v and q then reads v, the read of q is kept but returns 0 instead of 1 in E'. The paper's proof ("this implies that s_q should have been removed") uses our reading (✏️, evident intent).

  4. Lemma 4 (lemma4): stated for any fragment C₁ ↪ E that starts with reader i in the CS and ends with writer w in the CS (the paper: the specific E₂E₃). The paper writes ⊂ for ⊆.

  5. Observation 3: exists_E1 (Concurrent Entering) and solo_returns_of_deadlockFree.

  6. Theorem 5. Exact form theorem5: n ≤ Wb · 3^Rb. Logarithmic form theorem5_logb: log₃ (n / C) ≤ Rb for every C ≥ Wb; with C = c · f(n) this is the paper's Ω(log (n / f(n))) with explicit constants: Rb ≥ log₃(n / f(n)) − log₃ c. When n / f(n) is bounded (e.g. f(n) = n/2) the asymptotic claim Rb = Ω(log (n / f(n))) would require Rb ≥ 1 for large n, which the paper's proof does not give (the reader of the last round may not exist when r = 0); this corner is outside the interesting range and is not formalized. theorem5_general: the same for any number m ≥ 1 of writers (one of them is used).

  7. Corollary 6 (corollary6): ⌊log₃ n⌋ ≤ Wb + Rb ≤ 2 · max(Wb, Rb).

  8. Corollary 7 is not formalized: it combines theorem5_general (which holds for any m) with the Ω(log m) mutual-exclusion lower bound of Attiya–Hendler–Woelfel, which is not formalized.

Proofs

  1. Lemma 2, case 1 (proof gap, 🔧): "if v was written to in σ₂, then its value after Eσ₁σ₂ differs from the expected parameter of all CAS steps of σ₃" is false (a write may restore the expected value), so one CAS of σ₃ may succeed. The bound still holds: all CAS steps of σ₃ on v expect the value of v after E, so at most one of them succeeds, and then F(v) ≤ 2M, AW ≤ 3M. We prove the lemma with a greedy schedule instead (lemma2).

  2. Theorem 5, use of Lemma 1 (🛠️): both uses ("W₁ performs O(f(n)) expanding steps", "R_i executes exactly r expanding events … The theorem now follows from Lemma 1") go through with lemma1_count, applied to the fragments C₁ ↪ E₂E₃ (writer) and C₁ ↪ E₂ (reader).

  3. Theorem 5, expanding steps after reordering (🔧): invariant 2 says that readers are about to perform expanding steps, but Lemma 2 reorders these steps, and whether a step is expanding depends on what was executed before it. Not argued in the paper; true because all poised steps are reading steps (read/CAS), during which familiarity sets only grow (round_expanding).

  4. Theorem 5, the steps σ'' (🔧): "let all the readers of R_j take steps until they either return to the remainder section or are about to perform another expanding step" — a non-expanding write of one reader can change whether another reader's poised step is expanding. e2_construct repeats single non-expanding steps until all readers are simultaneously poised on expanding steps or done (terminating by Bounded Exit).

  5. Theorem 5, Equation (9) ⇒ |AW(W₁)| < n (routine): during E₃ the writer's own writes change familiarity sets; each expanding step of the writer adds at most 3^r processes (aw_solo_bound). The cap of log₃(n/f(n)) iterations is not needed: the construction runs until all readers are back in the remainder section, and the count gives n ≤ Wb · 3^r with r ≤ Rb.

  6. Lemma 4 proof: as in the paper, via Lemma 3; ClientWF of the erased execution needs that the kept steps of every process form a prefix of its steps (eraseFrom_filter_prefix).

Algorithm A_f (Sections 4–5)

  1. Model (Alg/Model.lean): a concrete interleaving transition system following the pseudo-code line by line (one shared-memory access per step; line 51 is two steps). Counters C[i], W[i] are atomic objects with add/read, as the paper assumes (the f-array implementation and its O(log K) cost are cited, not modelled). One writer: the writers' lock WL is omitted and WSEQ is writer-local. Readers are partitioned by an arbitrary function grp : Fin n → Fin G. Line 30 writes ⌊p/f(n)⌋, intended ⌊p/K⌋ (✏️; covered by arbitrary grp).

  2. Line 51 order (🔧): the literal left-to-right evaluation of C[i].read() = W[i].read() violates Mutual Exclusion (mutex_violation_literal_order, not_mutualExclusion_literal_order; 3 readers, one group). The paper never fixes the order, and the proof of Lemma 8 reasons as if both counters were read atomically. With W[i] read first Mutual Exclusion holds (mutualExclusion_repaired, all n, all groupings, unbounded sequence numbers). Lemma 8, Lemma 9 and the Mutual Exclusion part of Theorem 18 are false as written.

  3. Line 47 (✏️): else if op = WAIT should be op' = WAIT. As written, Deadlock Freedom fails (deadlock_literal_line47, deadlock_literal_line47_noCS; one reader) and Mutual Exclusion fails (mutex_violation_literal_line47; one reader, two writer passages), for either order in line 51. Intent recoverable: the branch passes seq' (line 41) and the other branch tests op'.

  4. Tightness of the tradeoff. The abstract presents "a family of reader-writer lock algorithms that establishes that the tradeoff is asymptotically tight", and Section 4 ("we show that the complexity tradeoff … is tight. We do so by presenting a family A_f") uses the family as the witness of an existential claim. The family as written is wrong. A two-line repair, a swap of two reads plus the op' typo, makes Mutual Exclusion provable and leaves Lemma 17's RMR count unchanged, but its liveness and RMR bounds are not established here.

  5. Lemma 10 (Bounded Exit): proved for every variant (boundedExit_reader: from the CS a reader reaches the remainder section within 5 own steps; boundedExit_writer: within 2). The paper's proof refers to the "Bounded Waiting" of WL; irrelevant with one writer.

  6. Lemma 11 (repaired algorithm): lemma11_repaired.

  7. Not formalized: Lemmas 12–16 (Concurrent Entering, Deadlock Freedom, no reader starvation), Lemma 17 (RMR bounds), Observation 4 / multiple writers. Bounded explicit-state model checking (af.py, n ≤ 3, one group, ≤ 2 passages each) of the repaired algorithm found no state in which all non-remainder processes are stuck.

  8. Slips in Sections 4 and 5 (✏️): Lemma 8 writes t' < t where the order is the other way round; the description of line 14 in Section 4 says "read 0 from W[i]", meaning C[i].

  9. RMR model remark (Lemma 17, not formalized): the claim that the writer incurs one RMR per await in lines 14/21 assumes that failed CAS steps of readers on WSIG[i] do not invalidate the writer's cached copy; in protocols where every CAS takes the line exclusively, stale HelpWCS/line-45 CAS steps may cost the writer extra RMRs (not analysed).

Non-vacuity

  1. toyLock_spec (NonVacuity.lean): for every n, a simple single-writer lock (reader flags and a writer flag; readers back off while the writer flag is set) satisfies all non-RMR hypotheses of Theorem 5 (allowed, RWMutualExclusion, BoundedExit, ConcurrentEntering, Impl.DeadlockFree). Its RMR bounds are not analysed.
