This is false for the paper's own definitions in the write-back CC model (`Counterexample.lean`): after a *trivial* write (which does not change the familiarity set, Definition 1) the writer holds an exclusive copy and its next read of the variable can be expanding without an RMR. What the lower bound needs, and what we prove here (`lemma1_count`), is the counting version: in every execution fragment `C ↪ E` (knowledge measured from `C`, arbitrary cache state at `C`) and for every process `p`, the number of expanding steps of `p` is at most the number of RMRs of `p`.
