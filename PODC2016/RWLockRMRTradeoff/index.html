On the Complexity of Reader-Writer Locks — PODC 2016

Danny Hendler. On the Complexity of Reader-Writer Locks. PODC 2016. DOI 10.1145/2933057.2933099. Formalized version: the author's copy "ReadersWritersLock-CR" (Extended Abstract, 11 pp., with the proofs of Sections 3 and 5; no arXiv version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The lower bound holds: Theorem 5 (n ≤ Wb · 3^Rb, i.e. writer entry O(f(n)) forces reader exit Ω(log(n/f(n)))) and Corollary 6 are proved, but Lemma 1 is false (an expanding step need not be an RMR in the write-back model) and had to be replaced by a new counting lemma. The matching algorithm family A_f is incorrect as written, because of the order of the two counter reads in line 51 and of op for op' in line 47, and with both lines repaired we prove Mutual Exclusion. The liveness and the RMR upper bounds of the repaired A_f, hence the tightness of the tradeoff, were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🛠️ Lemma 1 (every expanding step is an RMR)

False in the write-back CC model with the paper's Definition 1: q writes 1 to v, then p writes 1 to v (a trivial write, so F(v) stays {q}, but p now holds v exclusively), then p reads v — an expanding step without RMR (lemma1_false).

Impact. Lemma 1 is used twice in the proof of Theorem 5, which survives: the counting version "every process performs at least as many RMRs as expanding steps in any fragment" is true (lemma1_count) and suffices.

Details

Definition 1 lets the familiarity set of v be determined by the last non-trivial step on v, so the trivial write of p does not make F(v) part of p's knowledge, yet it gives p a valid cached copy. The proof of Lemma 1 argues that, since p's copy stayed valid since its last caching step, "there must be a process q ≠ p that writes to v" in between; here the last non-trivial write precedes p's caching step. The same counterexample works in the write-through protocol if a write leaves the writer with a valid copy (lemma1_false_writeThrough).

Repair

lemma1_count: charge each expanding step of p without RMR to the earlier RMR of p that created its valid copy. Potential: the number of variables of which p holds a valid copy without knowing their familiarity set; it starts at 0 in every fragment and is increased only by RMRs of p.

🔧 Lemmas 8–9, Theorem 18 (Mutual Exclusion of A_f, line 51)

With HelpWCS reading C[i] before W[i] (line 51, if C[i].read() = W[i].read() read left to right), three readers of one group and the writer reach a configuration where the writer and a reader are both in the CS (mutex_violation_literal_order).

Impact. The algorithms as written do not satisfy Mutual Exclusion. Reading W[i] first repairs it: Mutual Exclusion then holds for every n, every grouping and unbounded sequence numbers (mutualExclusion_repaired).

Details

Writer at line 21 waits for <0,CS> because reader r₂ entered the CS on PREENTRY (so C = 1, W = 0). Reader r starts waiting (C = 2, W = 1) and in HelpWCS reads C = 2; reader r₃ then starts waiting (C = 3, W = 2); r reads W = 2, equal to the C it read, and CASes WSIG[0] to <0,CS>; the writer enters the CS while r₂ is still there. The proof of Lemma 8 treats the two reads as one atomic snapshot ("there is a time t' in which all readers that incremented C[i] … are waiting"). The paper never fixes the order, and nothing in it points to reading W[i] first (the prose, too, says HelpWCS "reads counters C[i] and W[i]").

Repair

Read W[i] first: while the writer is before its CS, W[i] can only grow and every reader counted in W[i] is counted in C[i], so c = w forces every group-i reader in a passage to be waiting when C[i] is read. The invariant (Inv in Alg/Safety.lean) also gives Lemma 11 (lemma11_repaired).

✏️ Slips

  • Line 47 of Algorithm 1 has op instead of op'. With the literal test op = WAIT (the opcode read on entry) a single reader breaks Deadlock Freedom (the writer waits in line 21 forever while the reader is in the remainder section, deadlock_literal_line47) and Mutual Exclusion (mutex_violation_literal_line47). The intent is evident (the branch uses seq' from line 41 and the PREENTRY branch tests op'), and with op' both executions disappear. Deadlock: the reader entered on PREENTRY, the writer then publishes WAIT and waits for <seq,CS>; the exiting reader reads <seq,WAIT> in line 41 but skips HelpWCS because its entry opcode was PREENTRY. Mutual exclusion: a reader that waited on entry reads <1,NOP> on exit and still calls HelpWCS(1); it signals <1,CS> to writer passage 1 before that passage publishes WAIT, then re-enters on PREENTRY, and the writer enters its CS too.

  • Concurrent Entering (§2.1): "any reader that executes at most b steps … enters the CS" should read "after b steps of its entry section".

  • Lemma 3: "steps executed by other processes when they are aware of p" must mean awareness after the step (read literally, a read that makes q aware of p is kept and returns a different value); the proof uses the intended reading.

  • Line 30 of Algorithm 1 writes the group index as ⌊p/f(n)⌋; it should be ⌊p/K⌋, as in the prose.

  • The proof of Lemma 8 writes t' < t where the order is the other way round.

  • Section 4, description of line 14: "one of the readers that read 0 from W[i]" should say C[i].

Typos

  • Lemma 4 writes ⊂ for ⊆.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 5 · Lemma 2

🔧 Theorem 5 (the tradeoff Ω(log (n / f(n))))

The proof relies on the false Lemma 1 and does not show that the readers' poised steps stay expanding after Lemma 2 reorders them, nor that its drain phase leaves all readers poised simultaneously.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (theorem5, exact form n ≤ Wb · 3^Rb).

Details

(1) Lemma 1 is replaced by lemma1_count, applied to the fragments C₁ ↪ E₂E₃ (writer) and C₁ ↪ E₂ (reader). (2) Whether a step is expanding depends on the steps scheduled before it; it is preserved here because all poised steps are reads or CAS steps, during which familiarity sets only grow (round_expanding). (3) In the phase σ'', a non-expanding write of one reader can change whether another reader's poised step is expanding; the construction (e2_construct) repeats single non-expanding steps until all remaining readers are simultaneously poised on expanding steps. (4) During E₃ each expanding step of the writer adds at most 3^r processes to its awareness set, although the writer itself changes familiarity sets (aw_solo_bound).

🔧 Lemma 2 (one round at most triples awareness)

Case 1 of the proof claims that after the writes of σ₂ the value of v differs from the expected value of all CAS steps of σ₃; a write may restore that value.

Impact. Statement true: at most one CAS of σ₃ on v succeeds, which keeps the factor 3. We prove it with a greedy schedule (lemma2).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, deterministic crash-free processes: n readers and m writers (Proc n m = Fin n ⊕ Fin m; Theorem 5 is stated for m = 1, theorem5_general for any m). Registers support read, write and CAS; CAS returns the old value, as in the paper.

  • A reader-writer lock is a CSLib implementation (Impl) of lock/unlock (entry/exit section); remainder/entry/CS/exit are CSLib's regions. Executions are arbitrary interleavings (adversarial scheduler); no randomness.

  • Properties (§2.1): Mutual Exclusion; Bounded Exit and Concurrent Entering with bounds that may depend on the configuration (weaker than the paper's constants); Deadlock Freedom in CSLib's form, which the paper's form implies. So our hypotheses are weaker than the paper's.

  • RMRs in the cache-coherent model: any CacheModel (three axioms), with the paper's write-back and write-through protocols as instances (writeBack, writeThrough). WriterEntryRMR/ReaderExitRMR are worst-case bounds over all executions.

  • Awareness and familiarity sets (Definitions 1–2) are computed forward along an execution fragment, starting from AW(p) = {p}, F(v) = ∅ (kstep, Ext.fresh); expanding steps are Definition 3 verbatim.

Alg/Safety.lean

Not formalized. Corollary 7 (needs the Ω(log m) mutual-exclusion lower bound of Attiya–Hendler–Woelfel; theorem5_general holds for every m). For A_f: Concurrent Entering, Deadlock Freedom and no reader starvation (Lemmas 12–16), the RMR bounds (Lemma 17; they also depend on the cited f-array counters), and m > 1 writers (the writers' lock WL is omitted). A_f is modelled with atomic counters C[i], W[i] (as the paper assumes), one writer, an arbitrary static partition of the readers into groups, and the two disputed lines as parameters.

So the tightness of the tradeoff, the second half of the abstract, is neither established nor refuted here: the repair of line 51 leaves the RMR counting of Lemma 17 unchanged, and bounded model checking of the repaired algorithm (n ≤ 3) found no stuck state, but this is not a proof. Lemma 17's one RMR per await assumes that failed reader CAS steps on WSIG[i] do not invalidate the writer's copy; we did not analyse this. Theorem 5 when n/f(n) is bounded is also open: the proof does not give Rb ≥ 1 (no last-round reader when r = 0).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: toyLock_spec (NonVacuity.lean) gives, for every n, a reader-writer lock (reader flags plus a writer flag) satisfying all non-RMR hypotheses of Theorem 5 (every process may invoke, Mutual Exclusion, Bounded Exit, Concurrent Entering, Deadlock Freedom). The model is built on CSLib's Impl, regionOf, Impl.DeadlockFree and InfExec (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/). CSLib candidates (marked -- CSLib candidate): CacheModel with writeBack/writeThrough (Model.lean), regFold lemmas (Regions.lean), solo_returns_of_deadlockFree (Solo.lean).
