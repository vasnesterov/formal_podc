Tree edges are identified with their lower endpoints. For a set `U` of *unusable* tree edges, the computed shortcut `H'_i` assigns to part `i` every usable tree edge that can see `P i` (`RootedTree.cutShortcut`: all ancestor edges of `P i` in the forest `T \ U`). * `CoreSlow` (Algorithm 1) processes the tree bottom-up: `L_v` is the set of parts that `v`'s parent edge can see in `T \ U` (where `U` is the set of edges marked so far, all below `v`), and the edge is marked unusable iff `|L_v| > 2c`. Its output `coreSlowCut T P c` is the unique set `U` with `v ∈ U ↔ v ≠ root ∧ 2c < |L_v(U)|` (`coreSlowCut_spec`, uniqueness: `RootedTree.cut_unique`; existence is the bottom-up construction `RootedTree.exists_cut`). * `many_good_of_cut` (the heart of the proofs of Lemmas 4 and 5): for **any** set `U` of tree edges each of which sees at least `2c` parts, if some `T`-restricted shortcut `H` has congestion `≤ c` and block parameter `≤ b`, then at least half of the parts get at most `3b` block components in `H'`. The blame argument is formalized via tops of blocks: `blockParam_cutShortcut_le` (`#blocks(H'_i) ≤ #blocks(H_i) + #missed_i`) and `card_blamed_le` (part `i` is blamed for at most `#blocks(H_i)` unusable edges). * `lemma_5`: CoreSlow's shortcut has congestion `≤ 2c` and at least half of the parts have at most `3b` block components; every list sent up the tree has at most `2c` part IDs (`card_coreSlow_list_le`), so the subroutine takes `depth(T)` phases of `≤ 2c` rounds. * `lemma_4_combinatorial`: the deterministic part of Lemma 4 (CoreFast): if, as the paper's Chernoff bounds give w.h.p., every unusable edge sees `≥ 2c` parts and every usable edge sees `≤ 8c` parts, then the congestion is `≤ 8c` and half of the parts have `≤ 3b` blocks. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars
