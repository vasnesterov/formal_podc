Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of How to Meet Asynchronously at Polynomial Cost — PODC 2013.

Paper: Dieudonné, Pelc, Villain, How to meet asynchronously at polynomial cost, arXiv:1301.7119v2.

Model and modelling choices

  1. Embedding. The paper embeds the graph in ℝ³ with pairwise disjoint edge segments. We use the embedding v ↦ e_v into V → ℝ (emb, point, posR in Model.lean). Any embedding with pairwise disjoint segments gives the same meeting relation, so the meeting notion is identical.

  2. Walks. Walk is the paper's walk written with a progress function. There are times τ 0 = 0 < τ 1 < … (the paper's t_i). p is continuous on each [τ i, τ (i+1)], takes values in [i, i+1] there, and p(τ i) = i. The paper's f_i(t) is the point at fraction p(t) - i of the i-th edge. We do not require τ i → ∞: accumulating times are allowed, as in the paper, and a meeting must happen at a time when both positions are defined (Meet).

  3. Exploration procedure (UXS "axiom"). Reingold's procedure R(k, ·) is replaced by a hypothesis. For the rendezvous analysis it is Explorer G P: trajectories R(k, v) from v, walks of exactly P(k) edges, covering all edges if |G| ≤ k. They may depend on the whole graph, so this is more general than a port-local procedure. Explorer.ofProc / theorem_3_1_ports instantiate it with deterministic port-local procedures (Proc), assuming they traverse all edges of graphs of size ≤ k (TraversesUpTo). For Lemma 2.1 the hypothesis is the paper's: the procedure visits all nodes of all graphs of size ≤ 2m (ExploresUpTo). The "visit all neighbours" transformation of §2 is not formalized; it is subsumed by the hypothesis.

  4. Assumptions on P. We assume P(k) ≥ 1 (field Explorer.P_pos) and P non-decreasing. The paper states monotonicity only at the very end of the proof of Theorem 3.1, but uses it in Lemmas 3.2–3.6 and in Theorem 3.1, which compare |A(·)|, |B(·)|, |K(·)| at different parameters. Both assumptions are harmless (pad by back-and-forth moves). They hold throughout the paper's use (✏️).

  5. Route as a fixed sequence. Before the meeting the agent learns nothing that depends on the other agent, so its route is a fixed node sequence determined by the graph, its start node and its label (Explorer.route). We do not formalize the agent as a Turing machine.

  6. Pieces, segments, atoms, borders, fences are index intervals of the route (iS, segS, fS, Algorithm.lean). Segments are 0-indexed (Lean segment i = paper's S_{i+1}; Lean border i = paper's K_{i+1,i+2}). Pieces and fences are 1-indexed as in the paper.

  7. "Not met before". Lemmas 3.2–3.6 assume NoMeet E x y Ex Ey: no meeting at a time t with t ≤ τ_x(Ex) and t ≤ τ_y(Ey). With the indices used this is the paper's "if the agents have not met before / by time t" (in Lemma 3.6, t = the first time an agent completes its (2(n+l)+1)-th piece). For Lemmas 3.3–3.5 the minimal admissible indices are the end of the (2N0)-th fence of x and y (Lemma 3.5: end of piece 2N0 + 1 of y), so the "not met before" window of Lemma 3.5 differs slightly from its literal reading (until b ends the first atom of S_1); it is the window of Lemma 3.6, in which the paper uses these lemmas.

  8. Parameter n + l. Lemmas 3.2–3.6 are proved for any N0 ≥ n in place of n + l (stronger). The theorem uses N0 = n + l, l = 2 min(|L₁|, |L₂|) + 2.

  9. Cost statement. "Meeting by the time one of them performs Π edge traversals" is formalized as a meeting time t ≤ τ_a(Π) and t ≤ τ_b(Π). Theorem 3.1's "polynomial Π(x, y), non-decreasing" is Π(n, m) = C (n + m + 1)^d (theorem_3_1), obtained from the explicit bound piBound P (2(n+l)+1) (theorem_3_1_explicit) and piBound_poly (which needs only P polynomially bounded).

  10. Lemma 3.1 is formalized for an explicit interval I = [s, e]: b is inside a block X(m, v)^c of its route during I, and a follows an occurrence of X(m, v) within I. The core lemma meet_of_rep is stronger: a only needs to follow R(m, v), the first half of X(m, v).

  11. Distinct start nodes are not needed (if they coincide, the agents meet at time 0); theorem_3_1_ports keeps the paper's assumption va ≠ vb only to have n ≥ 2.

  12. Modified labels use the binary representation via Nat.digits 2 (binDigits). The paper's "M(x) is never a prefix of M(y)" is used in the form exists_bit_ne: the two modified labels differ at a position 1 ≤ d < min(|M(L₁)|, |M(L₂)|) (0-indexed; the paper's 1 < λ ≤ l).

Proofs: gaps and repairs

  1. Lemma 3.3 (gap, statement true; 🔧). The proof claims that if a starts no fence during b's fence, a stays inside a piece. It overlooks that a may be inside a fence started earlier. We give a different argument (lemma_3_3). Take j = the last k ≤ 2N0 such that a completed piece k by the end of b's fence. Then:

    • a cannot follow a whole atom of an iteration ≥ N0 during the fence (catching against X(2N0, v_b));

    • a cannot stay inside a piece T_a(k), k ≤ 2N0 (counting);

    • j + 1 ≤ 2N0 in the remaining case, because b completed first.

  2. Length estimates in the proof of Theorem 3.1 (wrong claims inside a proof; 🔧). |Y(k, v)| ≤ 2P(k)·Q*_k and |A(k, v)| ≤ 2P(k)·Z*_k are false in general (paper_Ystar_wrong, paper_Astar_wrong: 10 > 6, 42 > 12 for P ≡ 1; at k = 1 the Y bound fails for every P, since |Y(1)| = 4P(1)² + 6P(1) > 4P(1)² + 2P(1) = Y*_1). With P ≡ 1, n = 2, labels 1 and 3, the paper's Π(2, 1) ≈ 2.77·10¹¹ is smaller than the position (≈ 2.92·10¹¹) where the longer-label agent ends its (2(n+l)+1)-th piece. So the paper's specific Π is not justified by its proof (computed by a script, not in Lean). The theorem only claims existence of a polynomial; we use exact lengths (lenX … lenOm, piBound).

  3. Lemma 3.6, Case 2, µ = 1 (wrong reference inside a proof; ✏️). The contradiction comes from Lemma 3.3 (a has not completed the first atom N of piece j+1 by the end of b's fence), not from Lemma 3.4. Our proof uses Lemma 3.3's second component.

  4. Lemma 3.6's induction. The paper argues with a minimal failing index µ and four cases. We prove Properties 1–4 by induction on i. Properties 1, 2 at i follow from Properties 3, 4 at i - 1 (or Lemmas 3.5 / 3.3 for i = 0). Properties 3, 4 at i follow from Properties 1, 2 at i. This is the same argument. Range (weaker statement): lemma_3_6 states the four properties only for i with i + 1 < nSeg for both agents. The paper also asserts Property 1 (resp. 2) when only the segment S_i of a (resp. b) and the border K_{i,i+1} of the other agent exist, i.e. for the last segment of the agent with the shorter modified label. This case is not formalized; its proof is the same counting argument, and Theorem 3.1 only uses Properties 3 and 4 below the first differing bit λ ≤ l. Lemma 3.6 also takes the conclusions of Lemmas 3.3 (second part) and 3.5 as hypotheses (for any j) instead of "b is first to complete its (2(n+l))-th fence, j = index of a"; core composes them.

  5. End of the proof of Theorem 3.1. The paper's two cases per bit come from comparing when the agents start and finish their segments S_λ. We make the case analysis explicit (meet_of_diff_bits): from Properties 3 and 4 at λ - 1, either an atom of a lies within b's segment or an atom of b lies within a's. This is the reason for doubling the atoms. The conditions used are n ≤ k_x ≤ 2k_y and n ≤ k_y ≤ 2k_x.

  6. "Integral trajectory forces progress" (§3, informal; not a mistake). "If one agent follows an integral trajectory during some time interval, then it must either meet the other agent or this other agent must perform at least one complete edge traversal during this time interval" is true if "complete" means "finishes a traversal" (reaches some τ_j in the interval). It is false if it means "performs an entire traversal inside the interval". Counterexample: a star with centre w, b moving from the middle of uw to the middle of wz, and a following z, w, y, w, u with the right timing. The counting form that the proofs use ("more integral trajectories than edge traversals") is correct and is meet_of_count, with ≥ instead of >.

  7. Lemma 2.1 (imprecisions). H must reuse G's port numbers on the trajectory edges. The paper does not say why the procedure behaves the same in H (same degrees and entry ports, and the exit ports used are occupied: obsRun_H). That a free port exists also needs connectivity. The port order at the new nodes y_q is arbitrary in our construction (the paper's "smallest unused port" is one choice).

  8. Small slips (✏️). Definition 3.8's pseudocode says "Follow trajectory Ω(k, v)" (should be X(k, v)). Lemma 3.3's proof counts 2(2(n+l)−1)|K| copies of X(2(n+l)) in Ω(2(n+l)) instead of (4(n+l)−1)|K| (harmless). §2's |x| = ⌈log x⌉ should be ⌊log x⌋ + 1. Lemma 3.2's "without loss of generality b is first" is unnecessary (the argument works for both orders; lemma_3_2 is for arbitrary x, y).

Not formalized

  • Theorem 2.1 (procedure ESST: termination, polynomial cost, all edges traversed).

  • Section 4 (Algorithm SGL and its applications: team size, leader election, perfect renaming, gossiping).

  • Reingold's theorem (hypothesis, see item 3).

Statement-level summary

All checked statements (Lemmas 2.1, 3.1–3.6, Theorem 3.1) are true as written under the paper's model with the implicit assumptions of item 4. Proof repairs: items 13, 14.
