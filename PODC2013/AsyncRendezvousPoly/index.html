How to Meet Asynchronously at Polynomial Cost — PODC 2013

Yoann Dieudonné, Andrzej Pelc, Vincent Villain. How to meet asynchronously at polynomial cost. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484245. Formalized version: arXiv 1301.7119 v2 (full version, 2015); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Lemma 2.1, Lemma 3.1, the synchronization Lemmas 3.2–3.6 and the main Theorem 3.1 are all true as stated, for the paper's fully adversarial continuous asynchronous model and any exploration procedure with a non-decreasing polynomial step count, with Reingold's theorem (UXS) as a hypothesis. Two proof steps need local repair: the case analysis of Lemma 3.3 misses a case, and the length estimates that define the polynomial Π are wrong for slowly growing P. Neither changes a statement, and the applications of Section 4 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Definition 3.8: the pseudocode loop body should be "Follow trajectory X(k, v)", not Ω(k, v).

  • §2: |x| = ⌈log x⌉ should be ⌊log x⌋ + 1, the length of the binary representation, which is what is used.

  • Lemma 3.6, Case 2, µ = 1: the contradiction comes from Lemma 3.3 (a has not completed the first atom N of piece j+1 when b ends its fence), not from Lemma 3.4.

  • Proof of Lemma 3.3: Ω_b(2(n+l)) contains (4(n+l)−1)|K(2(n+l))| copies of X(2(n+l)), not 2(2(n+l)−1)|K(2(n+l))|, and the printed "at most" bound for (2k−1)|K(k)| fails at k = 2(n+l). This is harmless, since the printed count is smaller.

  • The proof of Theorem 3.1 says "since the polynomial P(k) is non-decreasing". Monotonicity of P (and P ≥ 1) is used already in the correctness proof (Lemmas 3.2–3.6 compare |A(·)|, |B(·)| at different parameters), but it is stated only there. It is harmless: the step count can always be padded.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 3.3 · Theorem 3.1

🔧 Lemma 3.3 (missing case)

The proof says "if during this time interval agent a has not started any fence, it would have to follow a trajectory included in a piece T_a(k)", overlooking that a may spend the whole fence Ω_b(2(n+l)) of b inside one of its own fences, started before b's.

Impact. Statement true; proved with a different argument (lemma_3_3).

Details

That case satisfies the lemma's conclusion anyway, but the proof then derives that a must start a fence during the interval, which is not shown.

Repair

Let j be the last piece of a completed by the end of the interval. Then the last atom of piece j cannot have been followed entirely during the interval (catching, Lemma 3.1). Neither can the first atom of piece j + 1. Nor can a stay inside the single piece j + 1 (counting); here j + 1 ≤ 2(n+l) because b finished first. (lemma_3_3)

🔧 Theorem 3.1 (length estimates defining Π)

The proof's bounds |Y(k, v)| ≤ Y*_k = 2P(k)·Q*_k and |A(k, v)| ≤ A*_k = 2P(k)·Z*_k are false, e.g. for P ≡ 1, |Y(1)| = 10 > 6 = Y*_1 and |A(1)| = 42 > 12 = A*_1 (paper_Ystar_wrong, paper_Astar_wrong), so the paper's Π(n, m) is not justified.

Impact. Theorem 3.1 only claims that some polynomial Π exists, and that is true: we define Π from the exact lengths (piBound) and prove that it is polynomially bounded (theorem_3_1).

Details

Y'(k, v₁) inserts Q(k, ·) at each of the P(k) + 1 nodes of R(k, v₁) and adds P(k) edges, so |Y(k)| = 2(P(k)+1)|Q(k)| + 2P(k). Similarly for A. The same values arise for P(k) = k. At k = 1 the bound fails for every P: |Y(1)| = 4P(1)² + 6P(1) > 4P(1)² + 2P(1) = Y*_1. A numerical check (P ≡ 1, n = 2, labels 1 and 3) shows that the paper's Π is even smaller than the length of the first 2(n+l)+1 pieces of the agent with the longer label (2.92·10¹¹ vs 2.77·10¹¹). (paper_Ystar_wrong, paper_Astar_wrong, theorem_3_1)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Graph: finite, simple, connected, nodes anonymous. For Lemma 2.1 and theorem_3_1_ports, a CSLib port-labelled graph (PNGraph, IsSimple). For the rendezvous analysis, any SimpleGraph with an exploration family.

  • Exploration procedure (UXS "axiom"): Explorer G P is a family of trajectories R(k, v). Each is a walk from v of exactly P(k) ≥ 1 edges. It traverses every edge whenever G has at most k nodes (the paper's "integral"). P is non-decreasing, and polynomially bounded for the polynomial claim. The family may depend on G arbitrarily, so this is more general than Reingold's port-local procedure. Explorer.ofProc instantiates it with deterministic port-local procedures (Proc: history of (degree, entry port) ↦ exit port).

  • Agents: deterministic, distinct positive labels, modified labels M(x). Before the meeting, the route of an agent is a fixed node sequence E.route (modLabel L) v (the infinite concatenation of the iterations of the repeat loop).

  • Asynchrony and adversary: exactly the paper's walks. A walk is an increasing time sequence τ 0 = 0 < τ 1 < … (accumulation allowed) and a progress function p. On each [τ i, τ (i+1)], p is continuous with values in [i, i+1] and p (τ i) = i. The adversary may stop agents and move them back and forth inside edges. Positions live in the embedding v ↦ e_v ∈ ℝ^V (edges = segments, pairwise disjoint except at endpoints). A meeting is equality of positions, at a node or inside an edge. No randomness.

  • Cost: "meeting by the time one of them performs Π traversals" is t ≤ τ_a(Π) ∧ t ≤ τ_b(Π).

Lemmas 3.2–3.6 are stated for any N0 ≥ n in place of n + l, and with the hypothesis "the agents have not met before" as NoMeet (no meeting at a time when neither agent has passed a given index). Segments are 0-indexed. lemma_3_6 gives Properties 1–4 for the segment indices where both agents still have a border after segment i (i + 1 < min(k, s) for both).

Lemma 2.1: H must keep G's port numbers on the copied trajectory edges. The proof never says why the procedure follows the same trajectory in H (it sees the same degrees and entry ports). That a free port exists also uses connectivity. These details are routine and proved in lemma_2_1.

Not formalized. Theorem 2.1 (procedure ESST) and Section 4 (team size, leader election, renaming, gossiping via SGL). Properties 1 and 2 of Lemma 3.6 for the last segment of the agent with the shorter modified label are not stated (Theorem 3.1 does not use them). Reingold's theorem, and the transformation of his procedure into one traversing all edges, are hypotheses.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: k2Explorer (an exploration family on K₂) and idWalk satisfy all hypotheses, and k2_meet applies Theorem 3.1 to them. The model uses Mathlib (IVT, arccos ∘ cos as a continuous triangle wave) and CSLib's PNGraph.

CSLib candidates (Model.lean, Meet.lean, Traj.lean): the continuous asynchronous mobile-agent model (Walk, Meet) with the two meeting lemmas meet_of_rep and meet_of_count, and the trajectory algebra (Traj, OccIn, RepOcc).
