Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Speculatively Stabilizing Mutual Exclusion — PODC 2013.

Paper: Dubois, Guerraoui, Introducing speculation in self-stabilization: an application to mutual exclusion, PODC 2013 (arXiv 1302.2217v1).

Model and definitions

  1. Protocols. The paper defines a protocol as an arbitrary set of global actions (γ, γ') and then uses guarded rules. We use guarded rules: one deterministic local rule next γ v, computed from γ v and the registers of the neighbours of v. The guards NA, CA, RA are pairwise exclusive. For an isolated process, NA and CA can both be enabled, but they have the same action. Lemma 5 is false for general global protocols (the next state may depend on far-away processes), so this restriction is needed and matches the paper's intent.

  2. Daemons (Definitions 1–2). These are classes of executions ω t = (configuration, activated set). Unfair distributed daemon (unfairExecs): CSLib's IsUnfairRun. Only enabled processes are activated, and some process is activated unless the configuration is terminal. Terminal configurations repeat forever, standing in for the paper's finite maximal executions; SSME has no terminal configuration (ResetUnison.not_terminal). Synchronous daemon (syncExecs): all enabled processes are activated. The partial order on daemons is only used as syncExecs ⊆ unfairExecs (syncExecs_subset). The central daemon is not formalized.

  3. Configurations. Registers are integers. A configuration is valid (SValid) if every register lies in cherry(n, K) = {-n, …, K-1}. "Arbitrary initial configuration" means an arbitrary valid configuration.

  4. Clock arithmetic. φ, ≤_l and correct are written without mod, through succK c = c + 1, wrapping K - 1 ↦ 0. Equivalence with the paper's mod-based definitions on stab is proved (succK_eq_emod, locLe_iff, correct_iff with the paper's d_K).

  5. Guards. resetInit is ¬allCorrect ∧ r_v > 0; for clock values r_v ∉ init_X ⟺ r_v > 0. convergeStep keeps the paper's conditions r_u ∈ init_X = {-n, …, 0} and r_v ≤ r_u.

  6. Graph and identities. Connected simple graph with n ≥ 2 processes; n = 1 is trivial and not treated. Identities: any injective id : V → {0, …, n-1} (necessarily a bijection), as the paper assumes.

  7. Specification 1. MutualExclusion priv ω: at most one privileged process in each configuration, and every process is privileged and activated infinitely often. A privileged process "executes its critical section" when activated, as in the paper. The predicate priv v γ may depend on the whole configuration, so that the paper's "privileged over the variables of v and its neighbours" can be expressed.

  8. Self-stabilization (Definition 3) is CSLib's IsLegitimate / SelfStabilizing. Some set L of configurations is reached by every execution, and every execution starting in L satisfies the specification. For SSME, L = Γ₁. Convergence time: conv_time(π, d) ≤ T is PseudoStabilizingWithin Exec spec T, i.e. the suffix from time T of every execution satisfies the specification. This matches "the number of actions required to reach a configuration from which any execution satisfies spec" for the deterministic synchronous daemon.

  9. Definition 4 (speculative stabilization) and the Section 3 examples (Dijkstra's ring, min+1 BFS, maximal matching) are not formalized. The ratio condition conv_time(π,d)/conv_time(π,d') ∈ Ω(f) is asymptotic over graph families, and the claim that SSME is "sd-speculatively stabilizing" needs convergence-time bounds under the unfair daemon that the paper only quotes from [7].

Theorem 1

  1. The paper deduces convergence to Γ₁ from the main result of Boulinier–Petit–Villain [2], with α ≥ hole(g) - 2 and K > cyclo(g). We prove convergence for α ≥ n and K > n, which covers α = n and K = (2n-1)(D+1)+2, without hole/cyclo (ResetUnison.exists_legit). The proof uses chains of resets: each reset is caused by an earlier reset of a neighbour, or the process was positive and not allCorrect from the start. With α ≥ n, a chain has fewer than n links, because a process cannot leave init* while its successor in the chain is still positive. So there are finitely many resets and finitely many CA steps. After that, a process next to a frozen process moves at most twice, so a run that never reached Γ₁ would end in a terminal configuration, which does not exist.

  2. Liveness of unison in Γ₁ is also proved (live_of_legit, visits_of_legit); the paper takes it from [2]. It needs K > n (no deadlock in Γ₁, exists_normalStep_of_legit) and connectivity.

  3. Safety in Γ₁ follows the paper's argument: registers differ by at most the distance, modulo K, and different privileged values are more than D apart (legit_safe).

Theorem 2 and Lemmas 1–4

  1. Lemma 1 is proved for i ≤ D; the paper states i < D. We use i = D. Our version also gives the register bounds c - (i - j) ≤ r_u^j ≤ c and allCorrect at all j < i (lemma_1). The paper's form is lemma_1'.

  2. Definition 5 (islands) is ill-defined.

    • It requires neither connectivity nor r ∈ stab: an independent set of init processes is an island.

    • It excludes I = V, so a configuration with all registers in stab and only an incorrect chord has a process in no island. The proof of Theorem 2 nevertheless assumes u lies in an island.

    • depth(I) is a maximum over the island, but Case 1 uses it as the depth of u.

    Lemmas 2 and 3 are not formalized as stated. The proof of Lemma 2 uses "same island and dist ≤ D ⇒ d_K ≤ D", which is false: the shortest path may leave the island (only d_K ≤ n-1 follows). They are replaced by the light cone lemma cone, which needs no islands, and by no_two_priv_early for Case 1 (⌈D/2⌉ ≤ i ≤ D).

  3. Case 1 arithmetic. The paper splits on id_v - id_u ≥ 2 and = 1. We use one integer argument. The registers at time 0 differ by at most dist(u,v) ≤ D without wrap-around, and privileged values differ by 2D·(id_v - id_u). The equality case i = D (in the paper it falls into Case 2, whose Lemma 4 is false at i = D) needs the observation that a process moving at step 0 is a local minimum.

  4. Lemma 4 is false (lemma_4_false, C₅, registers (10,10,10,12,11), register 12 at time D = 2). The proof's claim "there exists ∅ ≠ V' ⊆ V enabled by RA in γ₀" also fails when all registers are negative. Corrected (lemma_4_corrected): from time D+1 on, every register is ≤ t or ≥ K-D-1. Case 2 then covers D < i < 2n: only the process with identity n-1 (privileged value K-D-1) can be privileged.

  5. Case 3 uses [3] (synchronous unison stabilizes within α + lcp(g) + D steps). We prove instead that a synchronous execution is in Γ₁ from time n + α = 2n on (legit_sync): no reset after time n (synchronous reset chains have consecutive times and fewer than n links), then α steps of CA. The paper needs Γ₁ only from time 2n + D on.

  6. Liveness in Theorem 2 comes from legit_sync and the unison liveness; the paper only argues safety.

Theorem 3

  1. Not formalized. It is a direct citation of Devismes–Petit [7] (2·diam·n³ + (α+1)n² + (α-2·diam)n steps).

Section 5 (lower bound)

  1. Lemma 5 is stated for protocols given by local rules (see 1). It is generalized to radius r + k → r, and the hypothesis "π is self-stabilizing" is dropped (it is not used). The paper's bound 1 ≤ k ≤ diam is not needed.

  2. Theorem 4 is false as stated when privileged_v reads the neighbours of v, as Specification 1 allows: theorem_4_false (complete graphs K_n, n ≥ 2, D = 1; a token-passing protocol with convergence time 0, under the synchronous and the unfair daemon, rr_unfair) and theorem_4_false_star (stars, n ≥ 3, D = 2, synchronous daemon). In general (theorem_4_false_centre), if some vertex z has eccentricity ≤ R, a clock at z broadcast along a BFS tree (c_v := c_parent(v) + 1, privileged_v ≡ c_parent(v) = id_v) gives mutual exclusion from time R - 1 on in all synchronous executions; for graphs of radius ⌈D/2⌉ (trees, complete graphs, stars, …) this is ⌈D/2⌉ - 1 (theorem_4_false_of_radius). Proved instead: theorem_4_radius, i.e. ⌈D/2⌉ ≤ T + r when privileged_v reads the r-ball. theorem_4 is the case r = 0, which is the paper's statement for state-local privileged. The domain of configurations must be a product closed under the protocol, so that two configurations can be spliced.

  3. In the lower bound we only use the liveness consequence "each process is privileged at some time ≥ T" and safety at time T, as the paper does. The hypothesis is the synchronous convergence time only, matching the abstract's "even if asynchronous stabilization is not required".

  4. Optimality of SSME (conv_time_ssme): conv_time(SSME, sd) = ⌈D/2⌉ exactly.
