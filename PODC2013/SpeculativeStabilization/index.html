Speculatively Stabilizing Mutual Exclusion — PODC 2013

Swan Dubois, Rachid Guerraoui. Introducing speculation in self-stabilization: an application to mutual exclusion. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484246. Formalized version: arXiv 1302.2217 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The protocol results stand: SSME is self-stabilizing for mutual exclusion under the unfair distributed daemon (Theorem 1), and under the synchronous daemon it stabilizes within ⌈diam/2⌉ steps (Theorem 2, by a new proof), which is optimal for SSME. But the optimality bound of Theorem 4 (no protocol stabilizes in fewer than ⌈diam/2⌉ synchronous steps), which the abstract stresses as a result of independent interest, is false under the paper's own Specification 1 and holds only for protocols whose privileged reads the process's own state, as in SSME. The claim that SSME is speculatively stabilizing was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Theorem 4 (lower bound ⌈diam/2⌉ for every protocol)

Theorem 4 is false when privileged_v reads the neighbours of v, as Specification 1 allows: on the complete graph K_n (n ≥ 2, diam = 1) a token-passing protocol has mutual exclusion from time 0, while the theorem claims at least ⌈1/2⌉ = 1 step (theorem_4_false).

Impact. The abstract's claim that ⌈diam/2⌉ is optimal "even if asynchronous stabilization is not required" is false in the paper's own model: on every graph whose radius is ⌈diam/2⌉ (every tree, complete graph, star, odd square grid, …) some protocol needs only ⌈diam/2⌉ - 1 steps. The bound holds when privileged_v reads only v's own state, as in SSME, so SSME is still optimal among such protocols (theorem_4, conv_time_ssme).

Details

Specification 1 lets privileged_v be "a predicate over variables of v and possibly of its neighbours". The proof of Theorem 4 only makes the t-balls around u and v agree with earlier configurations. A predicate that reads the neighbours needs the (t+1)-balls.

  • Complete graph K_n: take registers in ℤ_n. Process v is privileged iff the sum of all registers is v, and a privileged process increments its register. Exactly one process is privileged in every configuration, and the privilege goes round-robin. So mutual exclusion holds from time 0 under the synchronous daemon, and even under the unfair one (theorem_4_false).

  • Star K_{1,n-1} (n ≥ 3, diam = 2): a pointer held by the centre does the same under the synchronous daemon (theorem_4_false_star).

  • General construction: pick a centre z of eccentricity R and a BFS tree rooted at z. Process z increments a clock modulo n, every other process sets c_v := c_parent(v) + 1, and privileged_v ≡ (c_parent(v) = id_v). After R - 1 synchronous steps every clock within distance R - 1 of z equals c_z, so mutual exclusion holds from time R - 1 on (theorem_4_false_centre). Whenever the radius is ⌈diam/2⌉, this beats the claimed bound by one (theorem_4_false_of_radius).

Repair

If privileged_v reads the ball of radius r around v, the bound is ⌈diam/2⌉ - r (theorem_4_radius). For r = 0 this is the paper's bound (theorem_4). For r = 1 it is tight on all graphs of radius ⌈diam/2⌉. Optimality of SSME among protocols whose privileged reads only the own state is not affected, since SSME's privileged reads only r_v (conv_time_ssme).

🔧 Lemma 4 (registers at time D after a non-legitimate start)

Lemma 4 is off by one step: on C₅ (n = 5, D = 2, K = 29) with registers (10, 10, 10, 12, 11), process 0 holds 12 at time D, outside the claimed set init ∪ {K-D, …, K-1} ∪ {0, …, 2D-1} (lemma_4_false).

Impact. Only the lemma is false; its corrected version still gives Case 2 of Theorem 2, which holds (lemma_4_corrected, theorem_2).

Details

The lemma claims that if γ₀ ∉ Γ₁, every register at time D = diam lies in the set above. In the counterexample, the incorrect edge 2–3 resets at step 0 and its neighbours reset at step 1. Process 0 is at distance exactly D from the incorrect edge, keeps incrementing, and holds 12 at time D = 2. The proof assumes that the reset wave reaches a process at distance i ≤ D from the RA-enabled set by time i; it only acts there one step later.

Repair

From time D + 1 on, every register is ≤ t or ≥ K - D - 1 (lemma_4_corrected). This still gives Case 2 of Theorem 2, because the only privileged value ≥ K - D - 1 is that of the process with identity n - 1.

✏️ Slips

  • Proof of Lemma 4, first sentence: it claims that some process is RA-enabled whenever γ₀ ∉ Γ₁, which fails when all registers are negative. The corrected lemma covers this case with no new idea (lemma_4_corrected).

  • Lemma 5 is false under the paper's formal definition of a protocol (an arbitrary set of global actions, so the next state may depend on far-away processes); it holds for protocols given by local deterministic rules (lemma_5). Implied assumption: the paper uses guarded local rules everywhere else, so we state the lemma for local rules.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Definition 5 and Lemmas 2–3

🛠️ Definition 5 and Lemmas 2–3 (islands)

Islands (Definition 5) are ill-defined, and the proof of Lemma 2 uses the false step "same island and dist(u, v) ≤ D ⇒ d_K(r_u, r_v) ≤ D", so Lemmas 2–3 are not established as stated.

Impact. The statements we need hold: Case 1 of Theorem 2 is proved with a new argument that needs no islands (cone, no_two_priv_early), and Theorem 2 stands (theorem_2).

Details

An island is "a maximal set I ⊊ V all of whose internal edges are correct". This requires neither connectivity nor registers in stab, and it excludes I = V, which leaves a process uncovered when all registers are in stab but a chord is incorrect. With the intended reading (components of the correct-edge graph), the proof of Lemma 2 still uses the step above. It is false, because the path realising dist(u, v) may leave the island. Case 1 of Theorem 2 also mixes up island depth (a maximum over the island) with the depth of u.

Repair

We prove Case 1 with a light cone (cone): if u is privileged at time i ≤ D, every process within distance i-1-j of u is allCorrect at time j, and the registers in the cone differ from r_u by at most the distance, as integers. The equality case i = D needs one extra observation that the paper does not make: a process that moves at step 0 is a local minimum (no_two_priv_early).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Atomic-state model with CSLib's daemons (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SelfStabilization/Daemon.lean). The protocol is the local rule next : (V → ℤ) → V → ℤ (rules NA, CA, RA with exclusive guards). Unfair distributed daemon: IsUnfairRun (any nonempty set of enabled processes moves; no fairness). Synchronous daemon: all enabled processes move.

  • Executions are sequences ω t = (configuration, activated set). MutualExclusion = Specification 1: at most one privileged process in every configuration, and every process is privileged and activated (executes its critical section) infinitely often.

  • Graph: any connected simple graph with n ≥ 2 processes. Identities: any injective id : V → {0, …, n-1}. Registers are integers restricted to the clock cherry(n, K) by SValid. Deterministic, no failures besides the arbitrary initial configuration.

  • Self-stabilization (Definition 3) is CSLib's IsLegitimate/SelfStabilizing, with legitimate set Γ₁. conv_time(π, sd) ≤ T is PseudoStabilizingWithin (syncExecs …) … T.

  • For the lower bound: any protocol given by local deterministic rules, over any product domain of configurations, with privileged_v reading the r-ball around v.

Model.lean

Black boxes replaced by proofs. The paper uses the convergence of the Boulinier–Petit–Villain unison (PODC 2004, with conditions on hole(g) and cyclo(g)) and its synchronous bound α + lcp(g) + D (Algorithmica 2008). For α = n and K > n we prove both from scratch. Convergence under the unfair daemon uses chains of resets: with α ≥ n, a chain has fewer than n links. The synchronous bound becomes Γ₁ from time n + α = 2n on (legit_sync). Lemma 1 also holds for i = D (we use this).

Not formalized. Theorem 3 (O(diam·n³) steps under the unfair daemon; it only quotes Devismes–Petit [7]); Definition 4 and the asymptotic "speculation" claims of Section 3, including the claim that SSME is "sd-speculatively stabilizing" (it depends on the asymptotic ratio of two convergence times) and the examples (Dijkstra's ring, min+1 BFS, matching).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: Theorems 1 and 2 are instantiated on C₅ (example in Lemma4.lean). The counterexamples are concrete and checked by decide (C₅) or proved for whole families (K_n, stars). Infrastructure: the Boulinier–Petit–Villain unison (Podc/Computability/Distributed/SelfStabilization/ResetUnison/, CSLib candidate) over CSLib's SelfStabilization/{Defs,Daemon}.
