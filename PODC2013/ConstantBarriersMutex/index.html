O(1)-Barriers Optimal-RMR Mutual Exclusion — PODC 2013

Hagit Attiya, Danny Hendler, Smadar Levy. An O(1)-Barriers Optimal RMRs Mutual Exclusion Algorithm. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484255. Formalized version: the authors' extended abstract (11 pages, from Hendler's web page; no arXiv or full version available). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Theorem 1, the CAS version, holds in full under TSO: Algorithm 1 satisfies mutual exclusion, starvation freedom and (log n + 3)-bounded waiting (even log n + 2), and every passage costs at most 19 log n + 23 RMRs and two barriers. But the paper's safety proof treats every write as performed when it is issued, so Lemma 2 is false as stated and mutual exclusion needs a new inductive invariant over the store buffers. The read/write variant (Theorem 12), on which the abstract's "first read/write" claim rests, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🛠️ Lemma 2 under TSO (parts (b), (c); part (d) and Corollaries 3–4 rest on it)

The proof of Lemma 2 treats every write as performed when it is issued and never mentions store buffers, so parts (b) and (c) fail under TSO (lemma2b_false, lemma2c_false) and mutual exclusion under TSO is asserted rather than proved.

Impact. Mutual exclusion and the repaired parts hold (mutual_exclusion, lemma2d, corollary4, lemma2b, lemma2c). Part (b) holds under sequential consistency, but the paper's model is TSO. The whole proof of Lemma 2 reasons at the sequential-consistency level ("step s is the execution of statement 47 … p is the lock owner"), and no part of Section 5 explains why the store buffers do not matter for safety.

Details

  • Part (b). "If pc(C,p) = 48, then the lock owner is the process p dequeued" fails, because lock ← next (statement 47) may still be in p's store buffer, so C.lock = p (lemma2b_false, 31 events, 2 processes). Symmetrically, after lock ← next is performed but before signal[next] ← true is, p is still in its exit section by Definition 1 while pc ≠ 48 and p no longer owns the lock.

  • Part (c). A process that was signalled in an earlier passage issues signal[r] ← false (statement 17), but the write sits in its store buffer while C.signal[r] (memory) is still true. Two processes then satisfy the condition at once (lemma2c_false, 80 events, 2 processes).

  • Safety. The real argument needs facts the paper never states. Writes of one process are performed in FIFO order, so the next lock owner sees all writes of the previous one. A process that is enqueued has performed its signal[r] ← false before its apply[r] ← true. The holder reads promQ and inPromQ partly from its own buffer, and its view of promQ, inPromQ and exits is the logical value left by all earlier holders.

Repair

We use an inductive invariant over all TSO configurations (Inv, InvDefs.lean; preservation in InvFlush.lean, InvStep.lean), stated in terms of performed writes and store-buffer contents. It fixes the exact store-buffer contents at each program counter, the owner of lock, the "logical" queue (the lock owner's view), and the relation between exits and the number of CS entries. It was validated by an exhaustive model checker (n = 2, 3 passages each) before the Lean proof. lemma2b: during the exit section, p owns the lock in memory unless its write of statement 47 has been performed. In that case the lock owner is the dequeued process, which is in its entry section.

🔧 Lemma 2(c), range of pc (who holds signal)

The invariant "if signal[r] = true and 17 < pc(r) < 49, then r owns the lock" fails even under sequential consistency: a process r that entered the CS by being signalled and has executed statement 47 (lock ← next) is at pc = 48 with signal[r] still true while next owns the lock (lemma2c_false_sc, 2 processes, every write performed immediately).

Impact. Mutual exclusion (Corollary 3, part (d) of the lemma) is unaffected and is proved from a corrected invariant (lemma2c, lemma2d).

Details

Case 4 of the proof (statement 47) says that 17 < pc(C,p), pc(C_s,p) < 49, so invariant (c) "cannot be violated". This overlooks the exiting process itself when its own signal is set. Case 5 (statement 48) then applies (c) to that same process at pc = 48.

Repair

The corrected statement covers only processes past their barrier, statements 23–27 (lemma2c): if signal[r] is set in memory, then r owns the lock and nobody else is in the critical or exit section. It is a consequence of the TSO invariant Inv (clause sig).

🔧 Footnote 4 (reads of the process's own writes)

Footnote 4 says that "a read accesses a variable that was previously written to by the same thread only after a barrier", but the exit section reads promQ (statements 37, 42, 45) and inPromQ (statement 36, when the same process id is read at two consecutive levels) after writing them, with the writes still in its buffer.

Impact. Nothing in the paper depends on the footnote.

Repair

Drop the claim: the Lean model serves reads from the process's own buffer, and theorem1 holds.

Typos

  • Statement 45 says Q.dequeue() for promQ.dequeue().

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 7, base case of Claim 2 · Lemma 9 · Lemma 10

🔧 Lemma 7, base case of Claim 2 (role of statement 25)

The base case neither uses the exits − e ≥ 2 disjunct of statement 25 nor handles a process that waits at statement 25 forever.

Impact. Repaired (Run.waiting_entry, Run.starvation_freedom).

Details

This disjunct is exactly what guarantees that a lock owner enters after p's barrier. The owner r at p's failed CAS may already have read the root and buffered its increment of exits.

Repair

Suppose p completes its barrier at ta and never enters. Then some process enters the CS after ta (Run.waiting_entry). The argument tracks ent ≤ exits + 1 and shows that lock can only leave −1 by a CS entry. If nothing enters, the lock eventually stays free and p reads it. The inductive step of Claim 2 is formalized as stated: it takes the last write to the parent node before the stabilization time, uses TSO order of the doorway writes (perf, Doorway.lean), and the minimal level among starved processes.

🔧 Lemma 9 (missing case)

The case split on the process r read from n^m_p misses r = q′ (the reader itself wrote the node), and case 1 silently needs r to have stayed in its entry section since its write, so that apply[r] has been performed.

Impact. Both cases are easy. Lemma 8 is proved with the sharper bound log n + 2 (lemma8), and the paper's log n + 3 follows (lemma8_log_n_add_three).

🔧 Lemma 10 (RMR counts at statements 25 and 27)

"At most 4 RMRs at statement 25 (twice exits, twice lock)" is wrong: the true bound is 6, attained by a run with 4 processes in which p reads lock remotely 4 times and exits twice (stmt25_tight), and statement 27 can cost 2 RMRs, not 1 (stmt27_two).

Impact. Only constants change: lemma10_rmr gives ≤ 19 log n + 23 RMRs per passage, lemma10_barriers gives exactly 2 barriers per complete passage.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory under TSO, operational, after Section 3 of the paper.

    • Every process has a FIFO store buffer. A write is issued (appended) by the process and performed (removed from the buffer and written to memory) by an adversarial flush event at any time.

    • Reads return the newest buffered write to the variable, else memory.

    • A barrier step performs the oldest buffered write, or proceeds when the buffer is empty.

    • A CAS first performs the whole buffer.

  • n = 2^h processes, log n = h, complete binary tree in heap numbering. No crashes. Every step performs one shared access.

  • foreach loops follow a fixed order. Statement 34 reads n, n.left, n.right in this order. Candidates are deduplicated in order of first appearance.

  • exits ← exits + 1, enqueue and dequeue read and issue in one step. Only the lock owner accesses these variables, which corollary4 shows.

  • The CS is one local step.

  • Adversary/fairness: for safety, bounded waiting and RMR bounds, every schedule of steps and flushes. For starvation freedom, weak fairness (Run.Fair): a process outside the remainder section eventually takes a step. Flushes need no fairness, since barriers drain buffers.

  • RMRs (CC model of Section 3):

    • Every issued write and every CAS costs 1.

    • A read from the own buffer is free.

    • A read from memory costs 1 iff the reader's cached copy was invalidated, by a write of another process performed since its last read, or if it is the first read.

    • A failed CAS writes nothing and invalidates nothing. This is the paper's rule; with a convention where every CAS invalidates, the bound at statement 25 would not be constant. This is a remark only, not formalized.

  • Deterministic algorithm, no randomness.
