Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of O(1)-Barriers Optimal-RMR Mutual Exclusion — PODC 2013.

Paper: Attiya, Hendler, Levy, An O(1)-Barriers Optimal RMRs Mutual Exclusion Algorithm, PODC 2013 (extended abstract, 11 pp., author copy). Section numbers and statement numbers refer to it.

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 2(c) is false, also under sequential consistency (🔧, lemma2c_false_sc). With 2 processes, a process signalled in its current passage reaches pc = 48 after lock ← next; signal[r] is true but r does not own the lock. The fix < 48 is an index change, but the paper's proof reasons with the wrong range: case 4 claims statement 47 cannot violate (c), and case 5 applies (c) to the exiting process at pc = 48. The intended content (the signalled process waiting in the entry section owns the lock) is recoverable and is what the later proofs use (Lemma 6).

  2. Lemma 2(c) under TSO (🛠️, lemma2c_false): a stale signal[r] = true remains in memory while r's reset (statement 17) is buffered. Our corrected version restricts to statements 23–27 (after the barrier).

  3. Lemma 2(b) under TSO (🛠️, lemma2b_false): at pc = 48 the write lock ← next may still be buffered. The paper's model is TSO (Section 3), and C.lock is the memory value. Under SC (b) holds.

  4. Theorem 1 (the algorithm's properties) is true: none of these failures affects it.

Proof gaps (statements true)

  1. Safety under TSO (🛠️). The proof of Lemma 2 treats issued writes as performed. Replaced by the inductive invariant Inv (InvDefs.lean). It records buffer contents per program counter; lock ownership (Holds, Hands); the logical memory lv (the lock owner's view) for promQ, inPromQ, exits; a TSO version of (c) (sig); and exits against a ghost counter of CS entries (ex1–ex4).

  2. Lemma 7 / Claim 2, base case (🔧). The paper does not use the exits − e ≥ 2 disjunct of statement 25 explicitly and does not treat a process stuck at statement 25. Repaired by Run.waiting_entry.

  3. Claim 2, inductive step. "Let t′ be the point when n^k_p is last written in EE1" and "by total store ordering, t ≤ t1 ≤ t2" are made precise via the number perf of performed doorway writes (Doorway.lean). We use the minimal level among starved processes instead of induction on the level (equivalent). The stabilization time of Claim 1 is the maximum over the finite set of starved processes.

  4. Lemma 9 (🔧). The case r = q′ is missing; case 1 implicitly needs r to have remained in its entry section since its node write. Both are handled in BoundedWaitingCore.lean. Our Lemma 9 statement is the TSO version: "p has been promoted in [t₁, e] (it is in the promotion order BW.Q: the handed process followed by the logical queue), or some exit section reads anc p l at statement 34 before q's (l+2)-th entry, after a time at which anc p l holds p from p's current doorway". The paper's s(P) (enqueue time) is replaced by membership in BW.Q.

  5. Lemma 8 holds with log n + 2 (lemma8). The paper's log n + 3 is a corollary.

  6. Lemma 10 (🔧). The proof's "at most 4 RMRs at statement 25" is wrong: 6, attained by stmt25_tight. Its "a single RMR at statement 27" is wrong: 2, attained by stmt27_two, because the first read counts. Our total bound is 19·log n + 23 per passage (explicit constant).

  7. Footnote 4 (🔧; reads never hit the own store buffer) is false: statements 36, 37, 42, 45 read inPromQ/promQ entries written earlier in the same exit section with no barrier in between. (Statement 32 is not an instance: exits is written only there, and the previous write is followed by the barrier of statement 50.) Not used anywhere.

Modelling choices

  1. n = 2^h processes Fin (2^h); log n = h. The tree is in heap numbering (root 1, children 2v, 2v+1, leaf of p is 2^h + p). The paper's tree has n leaves; other n can be padded with idle processes.

  2. Node values Option (Fin (2^h)) (none = −1); lock : Option; exits : ℕ (unbounded, as in Algorithm 1; Observation 1 not formalized).

  3. TSO semantics (Section 3):

    • flush p events perform the oldest buffered write at any time (adversary).

    • A barrier step with a nonempty buffer performs one write; otherwise it proceeds.

    • A CAS performs the whole buffer first. The paper says "the adversary performs all these writes (and those preceding them)" only for writes to the CAS variable. At statements 23 and 26 the buffer is empty anyway (LI), so this makes no difference.

    • Reads take the newest own buffered write, else memory.

  4. Granularity:

    • One shared access per step.

    • Statement 25 is two steps (read exits, then read lock).

    • exits ← exits + 1, promQ.enqueue, promQ.dequeue read (own view) and issue the write in one step. They are executed only by the lock owner (corollary4), and their writes are buffered as usual.

    • promQ is a single shared variable holding a list (the paper does not fix its implementation).

  5. Statement 35's set {q1,q2,q3} \ {−1,p} is processed in order of first appearance among q1, q2, q3, without duplicates (the paper leaves the order open). No proof depends on the order.

  6. The critical section is one local step. A step in the remainder section starts a passage (issues statement 17). Processes may stay in the remainder section forever.

  7. Fairness (Run.Fair): a process outside the remainder section eventually takes a step. No fairness for flushes is needed (barriers drain buffers). Safety, bounded waiting and RMR bounds hold for every schedule.

  8. Definition 1. The exit section ends when the write of statement 43/48 is performed: Cfg.InCSX counts a process at statement 50 with a nonempty buffer. "Waiting section" = statements 24–27 (PC.Waiting), i.e. after the CAS of statement 23 was performed. The doorway ends there, as in the paper.

  9. Bounded waiting is counted as: while p is in its waiting section at all times in [t₁, t₂], the number of steps t ∈ [t₁, t₂) at which q ≠ p enters the CS.

  10. RMR model (RMR.lean):

    • Every issued write and every CAS costs 1 RMR.

    • A read served by the own buffer costs 0.

    • A read from memory costs 1 iff it is the first read or another process performed a write to the variable since the reader's last read (any write, as in the paper).

    • A failed CAS is not a write.

    • "Passage" = interval in which the process is not in the remainder section (Run.WithinPassage, Run.Passage).

  11. Starvation freedom is stated as: in a fair run, a process in its entry section at T is in the CS at some t ≥ T.

  12. Lemma 6 is generalised: any process to which the lock was handed (lock = p in memory, p in entry) enters the CS. Lemma 5(b) is stated for p ∈ lv.promQ.

Not formalized

  1. Section 6: Lemma 11 (writes performed by the comparison-object simulation of Golab et al.) and Theorem 12 (read/write version). They depend on a large external construction whose full proof is "in the full paper".

  2. Observation 1 (bounded exits modulo (n−1)(log n+2)+1).

  3. The appendix (experiments) and the remark that with PSO deadlock-freedom fails (no claim proved in the paper).
