import Papers.PODC2013.ConstantBarriersMutex.InvDefs /-! # The cache-coherent RMR cost model and barrier counting (Section 3 of the paper) The paper (Section 3): "Each write to a shared variable incurs an RMR. A read by process `p` of a shared variable `v` incurs an RMR if (1) it is the first read of `v` by `p`, or (2) it is preceded by a write of another process to `v` that was performed after `p`'s previous read of `v`." We model this with a ghost *cache* computed along a run (`Run.cache`); the configurations of `Model.lean` are unchanged. * `c p v = true` means that `p` holds a valid copy of `v`. Initially no copy is valid. * A read by `p` of `v` that is served from `p`'s own store buffer (TSO store forwarding) costs nothing and does not touch the cache. A read served from memory costs one RMR iff `p` has no valid copy, and afterwards `p` has a valid copy. * Every *performed* write to `v` by a process `q` (the adversary flushing `q`'s buffer, a barrier step of `q` performing a buffered write, the writes a CAS of `q` performs first, and a successful CAS of `q` itself) invalidates the copies of `v` of all processes other than `q` (rule (2): only writes of *another* process count, so `q`'s own writes neither invalidate nor validate its copy). A failed CAS writes nothing and invalidates nothing. * Every *issued* write costs one RMR (charged when it is issued, i.e. to the passage that issues it), and every CAS costs one RMR. Performing a buffered write costs nothing more. `Cfg.perf s e` is the list of writes performed by the event `e` (`mem_next`: the memory after `e` is the memory before updated by `perf`). Statement 35's `foreach`, the `enqueue`/`dequeue` of `promQ` and `exits ← exits + 1` are modelled as in `Model.lean`: each step performs at most one read and issues at most one write. -/ -- The case
