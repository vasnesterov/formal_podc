Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Pareto-optimal (unbeatable) consensus — PODC 2013.

Paper: Castañeda, Gonczarowski, Moses, Pareto optimal solutions to consensus and set consensus (BA, PODC 2013). Full version: arXiv 1311.6902v1, Good, Better, Best! — Unbeatable Protocols for Consensus and Set Consensus.

Errors in the paper

  1. Lemma 10 is false as stated (lemma10_false, SetConsensus.lean). It is the same statement as Lemma 1 of the PODC 2016 version (the five conditions are hypotheses 1–4 of Lemma1HypPaper in Papers/PODC2016/UnbeatableSetConsensus). Counterexample there: n = 6, k = 2, t = 1, V = {0, 1, 2}. The 2016 folder also proves Lemma 10 with the extra hypothesis "every process alive and low at m − 1 crashes in round m", which holds at every use, so Lemma 11 and Theorem 4 hold (lemma11, theorem4). The instance is not degenerate, and the fix adds a hypothesis that is not in the paper (🔧).

  2. Lemma 6 is false when d = t (lemma6_literal_false). Condition (b) holds vacuously for t − d = 0. With n = 3, t = 1, all inputs 1 and process 2 crashing silently in round 1, process 0 at time 1 satisfies (b) for v = 0, but K_0 ∃correct(0) fails. Proved version (lemma6): K_i ∃correct(v) iff K_i ∃v and ((a) or (b)), for i alive at m and t < n. The proof sketch ("the number of distinct processes knowing ∃0, including i itself") presupposes K_i ∃v, and the fix is local (🔧). The paper's statement "at time t + 1, K_i ∃v holds exactly if (a) or (b)" has the same issue.

  3. Corollary 1 (§3) needs t ≥ 2. With P0opt read from the paper's description (item 12), Opt0 strictly dominates P0opt when t ≥ 2 and n ≥ 4, and when 2 ≤ n ≤ t + 1 (corollary1). For n ≥ 3, t = 1, and for n ≥ 2, t = 0, P0opt and Opt0 make the same decisions (p0opt_sameDecisions), so P0opt is unbeatable (p0opt_unbeatable). Read as a universal statement over (n, t), it is false for t = 1, which is not degenerate. Read as the refutation of HMW's general claim, it is true, and the paper's own example needs two failures, so one condition on t fixes it (🔧).

  4. Lemma 15, proof. (a) The inner induction measure |C_i \ Z_i| need not decrease: in Case II the paper notes C_j ⊇ C_i, and j, which does not see ⟨k, m⟩, may know of fewer crashes, so C_j ⊋ C_i is possible. (b) The induction hypothesis is applied to j ∈ Z_i without checking its hypotheses: that K_j ∃0 first holds at m (a j heard by ⟨i, m + 1⟩ may have known 0 at m − 1), and that ¬K_j ∃correct(0) at m. (c) In the base case, z ∈ C_i needs an argument: a witness that z crashed would be a seen node that missed z's message, so it would not be in Z_i. We prove Lemma 15 as stated, with an induction on n − |Z_i| (ic). Case C (ic_caseC): if ⟨z, m⟩ is hidden from ⟨i, m + 1⟩, z survives round m in an indistinguishable run and decides 0 by the outer induction. Case II (ic_caseII): otherwise some k heard by ⟨i, m + 1⟩ missed z. We use runs x ~_i x' ~_j x''. In x', k crashes in round m + 1 reaching only i. x'' is x' where k also receives z's message. In x'', Z_i has grown by k and the hypothesis is applied to i. The paper's counting argument for Part 1 is not needed. Part 2 adds the case where z reached ⟨i, m + 1⟩ and the paper's "Z_i = {i}" argument (g15_succ). The lemma is true; its proof needs this new induction (🛠️).

  5. Theorem 2, derivation. "A precondition for deciding 1 is that no correct process ever decides 0" holds only for a correct decider. We make i correct in the run built from the hidden path (exists_run_of_hiddenPath) (🔧).

  6. Lemma 2, proof. "Since Q dominates P0, i must decide by time m" needs P0 to decide at m. This needs m ≤ t + 1, which holds because a value is first learnt by time f + 1 (first_le). A routine detail.

  7. Typos. Two different results are numbered "Corollary 1". In Lemma 15's last case, "j fails at (r', m) before sending any messages" must mean "j's round-m + 1 message reaches only i": ⟨j, m⟩ is seen by ⟨i, m + 1⟩, as the construction of r'' shows. In the same case, "the faulty processes in r' are those known by ⟨i, m⟩ to fail" should read ⟨i, m + 1⟩ (the count F⟨i, m+1⟩ + 2 that follows uses it). We use the corrected versions.

Modelling choices

  1. Model (from Papers/PODC2016/UnbeatableSetConsensus): runs are SyncCrash.Run P ℕ; views are SameView (CSLib FIIndist on the communication graph); protocols are view-invariant decision rules; DecidesAt is the first firing while alive. Coan's reduction to full-information protocols is the companion's theorem1_lockStep (stated for Optmin[k]; the argument is generic).

  2. Inputs: consensus and uniform consensus use V = {0, 1} (bin). The k-set part uses V = {0, 1, 2} for the counterexample and any D ⊇ {0, …, k} for Theorem 4.

  3. Knowledge (Knows): K_i φ at (r, m) holds iff φ holds in every admissible run r' in which i is alive at m with the same view. The paper's local state is ⟨d(i, m), G_α(i, m)⟩ and ⊥ once i has crashed; the decision history is a function of the view. Restricting to live i is needed: our views of crashed processes keep growing. All knowledge statements are made for live processes, as in the paper.

  4. ∃correct(v) (ExCorrect v r m): some correct process knows ∃v at time m. In Lemma 6 (b), "K_i(K_j ∃v held at m − 1)" is read as in the PODC 2016 version (Def. 3 there): ⟨j, m − 1⟩ is seen by ⟨i, m⟩ and has seen v (carriers). The number d of failures i knows of is known r i m, the processes p with ⟨p, m⟩ guaranteed crashed at ⟨i, m⟩. For live i and m > 0, this is exactly Definition 3's F⟨i, m⟩, the processes i did not hear from in round m (guarCrashed_now_iff). At m = 0 it is 0, as in the paper.

  5. P0opt of [HMW] is not defined in the paper. We use its one-line description: decide 0 when K_i ∃0; otherwise decide 1 at m ≥ 1 if every process known at m to have crashed was already known to have crashed at m − 1 (NoNewFailure). If HMW's actual protocol differs (e.g. only failures detected by missing messages count), the domination argument is unchanged (the condition still excludes hidden nodes at m − 1), but item 3's t ≤ 1 analysis would need to be redone.

  6. Hidden path (HiddenPath): ∃ b, ∀ ℓ ≤ m, ⟨b ℓ, ℓ⟩ hidden from ⟨i, m⟩. This is equivalent to hidden capacity ≥ 1 (hiddenPath_iff), as the paper notes.

  7. Maj: "Maj = 0" iff more than n/2 inputs are 0; "Maj = 1" iff at least n/2 are 1 (Thr). K_i(Maj = v) is proved equivalent to the seen-count condition (knows_maj_iff). Maj⟨i, m⟩ is 0 iff more than half of the seen inputs are 0.

  8. Uniform consensus: SolvesUniformSetConsensus 1 t bin from the companion (Decision for correct processes, Validity, at most one value decided by all deciders). UUnbeatable quantifies over uniform solutions.

  9. Generalizations: Theorem 2, Lemma 2 and Lemma 3 are proved for both preferred values a ∈ {0, 1} (Opt_a), which gives Opt1 for Theorem 3 with n = 2. Theorem 3 is proved for every n ≥ 1 (the paper: n > 2 by Lemma 8, plus n ≤ 2 informally). Lemma 7 is derived from an abstract forcing lemma at time 1 (time1_forcing), which also gives Lemma 14, Part 1.

Statements

  1. Lemma 2 is stated for live i at the first time it knows ∃a; it concludes DecidesAt Q r i m a.

  2. Lemma 3 is stated for live i, never-known(∃a) = "no correct process ever has a in its view".

  3. Lemmas 7, 8: i alive at time 1 (Lemma 7). Lemma 8 concludes ¬ DecidesAt Q r i m v for every v.

  4. Lemma 14, Part 2: "F⟨i,1⟩ < t" is known r i 1 < t. Lemma 15: "K_i ∃correct(0) holds at m + 1 for the first time" is ¬K_i ∃correct(0) at m (it holds at m + 1 by Lemma 6 (a)). Part 1 is stated with m = M + 1 to avoid subtraction. Part 2 is also proved at m = 0 (g15_zero, from Lemma 14).

  5. Theorems 5, 8: "all decisions are made by time f + 1 (resp. f + 2)" is stated for processes alive at that time. Theorem 8 is proved directly; the paper derives it from Theorem 6, which is not formalized.

  6. Lemma 16 / 17 conclude ¬ DecidesAt Q r i m v for every v. Lemma 16 is stated with 0 < t (and t < n), which the paper does not assume; for t = 0 its hypotheses are contradictory (K_i ∃0 implies K_i ∃correct(0) by Lemma 6), so nothing is lost. Theorem 5 covers t = 0 through uOpt0_eq_opt0 and Theorem 2, as the paper indicates.

  7. Corollary 1 (§3) is stated as ¬ Unbeatable 1 t bin p0opt under (t ≥ 2 ∧ n ≥ 4) ∨ (2 ≤ n ≤ t + 1). Since ¬ Unbeatable would also hold for a protocol that does not solve consensus, p0opt_solves proves that P0opt solves consensus for every t (a round without a new failure occurs by time t + 1).

Not formalized
