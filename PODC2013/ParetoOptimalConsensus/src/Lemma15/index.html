Let `i` know `∃0` for the first time at `m`, not know `∃correct(0)` at `m` (so `K_i ∃correct(0)` holds for the first time at `m + 1`), and be alive at `m + 1`. Then `i` decides `0` at `m + 1` if 1. (Part 1) `F⟨i, m + 1⟩ < t`, some `z` with `K_z ∃0` at `m - 1` and `⟨z, m - 1⟩` seen by `⟨i, m⟩` has `⟨z, m⟩` not seen by `⟨i, m + 1⟩`, and some `j ≠ i` with `⟨j, m⟩` seen by `⟨i, m + 1⟩` has seen `⟨z, m - 1⟩`; or 2. (Part 2) `F⟨i, m + 1⟩ < t - 1`. ## Proof structure (repairing the paper's proof) The paper proves both parts together, by induction on `m` and an inner induction on `|C_i \ Z_i|` (Cases I and II), with a counting argument for Part 1. We follow its constructions but organise the inner induction differently (`ic`): by induction on `n - |Z_i|`, where `Z_i = {k | ⟨k, m⟩ seen by ⟨i, m + 1⟩ and ⟨z, m - 1⟩ seen by ⟨k, m⟩}`. * (`ic_caseC`) If `⟨z, m⟩` is hidden from `⟨i, m + 1⟩`, a run indistinguishable to `i` lets `z` live through round `m`, hearing from everybody `i` heard from; then `z` satisfies Part 2 one level lower, so it decides `0` (outer induction), and so does `i`. * (`ic_caseII`) Otherwise some `k` seen by `⟨i, m + 1⟩` missed `z`'s round-`m` message. In `x''` the set `Z_i` has grown by `k`, so `i` decides `0` (inner induction); hence `j` decides `0` in `x''`, hence in `x'`, hence `i` decides `0` in `x`.
