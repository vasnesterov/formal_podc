Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Multi-Agent Rotor-Router on the Ring — PODC 2013.

Paper: Klasing, Kosowski, Pająk, Sauerwald, The Multi-Agent Rotor-Router on the Ring, PODC 2013, full version hal-00735113v2 (2014).

Model (Section 1.3, Section 2.1)

  1. Graphs. The paper uses the directed symmetric version of an undirected connected graph. RotorGraph allows any finite directed multigraph with out-degree ≥ 1 and a fixed cyclic order of the out-arcs of each node. Lemmas 2.1–2.3 are proved in this generality, which includes the paper's setting. Connectivity is not needed.

  2. Pointers. The paper fixes port labels so that the initial pointer is port 0. We keep the cyclic order port v 0, … and an explicit initial pointer ptr v (read mod deg v). This is equivalent.

  3. Delays. The paper's D(v, t) is the number of agents stopped at v in round t, implicitly at most the number present. We allow any D : ℕ → V → ℕ, and min (pos v) (D t v) agents stay. With this truncation the hypothesis D₁ ≥ D₂ of Lemma 2.1 is meaningful even when the two runs have different numbers of agents on a node. It covers the paper's reading.

  4. Visits. visits c₀ D t v = initial agents on v + arrivals at v in rounds 1..t, as in equation (4) / n_v(0) of the paper. A stopped agent does not create a new visit; this matches equation (2) (e_v(t+1) = n_v(t) − D(v, t+1)). Proved as exits_succ_eq (with truncation, as a max).

  5. Cover time. Covered c₀ D t (all nodes have positive visit count after round t) and CoverTime c₀ D T (covered at T, not before). The cover time of the paper is the T with CoverTime … T.

  6. Ring. ZMod n, port 0 to v + 1, port 1 to v − 1. On the ring only the initial pointers matter, as the paper notes.

  7. Labelled agents (Domains.lean) are used only for Lemma 2.4, since domains need identities. Agents on a node are released in index order (the paper: order irrelevant, agents indistinguishable). The labelled model is not connected formally to the counting model; for the counterexample the two agents are always on different nodes, so each simply follows its node's pointer.

Statements

  1. Lemma 2.1 (lemma_2_1): as stated (same initial configuration, D₁ ≥ D₂ pointwise). We also prove the joint monotonicity in the initial placement (visits_mono). That gives the remark R[k−1] ≤ R[k] (visits_remove_agent): remove one agent from a node, same pointers.

  2. Lemma 2.2 (lemma_2_2): as stated. τ = activeRounds D T counts the rounds t ∈ [1, T] with D(v, t) = 0 for all v.

  3. Lemma 2.3 (lemma_2_3): stated as "if CoverTime D T then there is C with CoverTime R C and τ ≤ C ≤ T", where τ is the number of fully active rounds among the first T. ("In at least τ of these rounds" follows.)

  4. Lemma 2.4, first property (lemma_2_4_false): false as stated; agents may swap along an edge without meeting at a node (✏️). The intended hypothesis ("never meet or pass each other on an edge") is recoverable from the paper itself (proof of Theorem 4.1 uses exactly this wording); the fix is a local strengthening of the hypothesis. The paper's later lemmas (2.5–2.9) do not reason about swaps at all. The counterexample is a delayed deployment (two undelayed rounds, then both agents stopped), which Lemma 2.4 allows ("a deployment"). The second and third properties are not refuted (in the counterexample the third holds for the agent 0: its domains after rounds 1 and 2 differ in 2 nodes). The counterexample depends on giving the (indistinguishable, Section 1.3) agents identities that are kept through an edge crossing; with the equally admissible convention that crossing agents swap labels (two bounces) it disappears.

  5. Repaired Lemma 2.4 (pattern_invariant): a label-free invariant of the undelayed system, valid for every initialization. For two adjacent empty nodes, the patterns (L,R), (L,U), (U,R) never occur (L/R = visited with pointer counter-clockwise/clockwise, U = unvisited). Equivalently, between consecutive occupied nodes the empty nodes read R…R U…U L…L: domains are arcs, and visited pointers point away from the agent of their domain. This is our formulation, not the paper's.

  6. Lemma 3.1 (lemma_3_1_false): false as stated; (1)–(4) are contradictory for every k ≥ 2. (🔧) The intended identity (4') a_i / a₁ = 2/a_i − … is recoverable from the lemma's proof (a₁ = 1/c²). But the proof of Theorem 3.2 reasons with the misprinted form: it computes g_i ≈ 2 a_i a_k k⁴ a₁, S_{j+1} = S_j + k⁴ a₁ a_k + 12k, B₁ ∈ Θ(n²/a₁), and then substitutes a₁ = Θ(1/H_k) to claim Θ(n²/log k). That is a second error; consistently it would give Θ(n² log k). (Checked on the rendered pages 14 and 17–18 of the full version: (4) is printed a_i · a₁, and the Phase B deployment itself is defined with the step ⌈k⁴ a₁ a_k⌉, so the corrected identity also changes the deployment, to steps of about 2k⁴ a_k / a₁.) The corrected Lemma 3.1, with (4'), is proved with all six properties for every k ≥ 2 (paper: k > 3) (lemma_3_1_corrected, H_k = harm k). The proof follows the paper's construction (b_i(c), root of d_{k+1}(c) = 0 by a sup/continuity argument). The upper half of (5), c² ≤ 4(H_k+1), gets a simpler proof than the paper's e^{c²/4} estimates: if 4H_k ≤ c² then b_j ≥ jc/2 for all j, which contradicts d_{k+1} = 0. The paper's step "by the intermediate value theorem" is made precise (the b_i are continuous only where the earlier b_j are nonzero; the first d_j to vanish is d_{k+1}, since d decreases). (1) a₀ = +∞ is encoded as 1/a₀ = 0.

  7. Theorem 3.7 (theorem_3_7):

    • hypothesis n ≥ 8k and k ≥ 1 instead of n ≥ 440k² (stronger theorem);

    • the hidden constant is explicit: every covering time T satisfies n² < 8192 k² T;

    • the pointer arrangement is explicit: all pointers point away from a node v chosen by counting (towards the farther neighbour; ties at the antipode arbitrary but fixed);

    • "any set of initial locations" is read as any multiset of k start nodes (pos₀ with Σ pos₀ = k), as in the paper's "not necessarily distinct starting vertices";

    • the paper's proof is replaced entirely. Its gaps (🛠️, proof of a true statement): reliance on Lemmas 2.5/2.6, whose proofs are sketchy, at a scale they are not stated for; "pointers point away from the closest agent" where the negative initialization (towards the closest agent) is meant; "by Lemma 2.1" where the lower-bound half of Lemma 2.2/2.3 is needed; the final Ω(n²/400k²) step is not derived. Our proof uses the potential identity potential_run (a Holroyd–Propp-type potential, not in the paper), hit_lemma, scale_step, multiscale and exists_good_center (a counting substitute for the good vertices of Lemma 3.6).

Not formalized

  1. Theorem 3.5 (well-spaced initialization, O((n/k)²) for every pointer arrangement; ❌). Proof gap: in the deployment, after each step agent i "returns to node q_i". But the pointer of q_i was flipped to the left when the agent left it to the right, so in the next step the agent would be sent left, not "moves (to the right)". Moreover, if agent i−1 does not move in the initial phase and agent i runs left down to p_{i−1}, then q_i = q_{i−1}, and the two agents on one node leave in opposite directions. Numerically the statement holds: adversarial hill-climbing over pointer initializations, agents equally spaced with gap L ∈ {3,5,8}, k ≤ 32 (k ≤ 16 for L = 8), maximal cover time found ≈ 1.25 (L+1)².

  2. Theorems 3.2, 3.4, Lemma 3.3. The proof of Theorem 3.2 is broken (❌) by the errors of item 13, and no repair is proved. It also asserts "the cover time is also monotonous with respect to the size of the ring" without proof. Simulation of its initialization (all agents on v, pointers along the shortest path to v) finds no violation for 4 ≤ n ≤ 59, k ∈ {1,2,3,4,5,8,16}. Monotonicity in k follows from Lemma 2.1 (visits_remove_agent). Also Θ(n²/log k) is meaningless for k = 1 (✏️).

  3. Lemmas 2.5–2.9, Theorem 4.1. Not formalized. Noticed: Lemma 2.8 says "2n² consecutive time steps t, …, t + n² − 1" (✏️); Lemma 2.5 is used in Theorem 3.7 with initial interiors n/(20k) but stated for 22k, and its proof only bounds the loss by 11k.

  4. Lemma 3.6 (≥ 0.8n − o(n) good vertices). Not formalized; the scanning algorithm mixes window lengths r(n/k) and r(n/(10k)) (✏️). The statement is plausible by a rising-sun/maximal-inequality argument.

  5. Section 3.3 (random walks) and the random-walk columns of Table 1: out of scope.

Typos

  1. Equation (4) in the proof of Lemma 2.1 and the recurrence in the proof of Lemma 2.2 use e_v(t) where e_v(t+1) is meant, relative to equation (2) and the visit convention (✏️). Our visits_eq_visitsOf / exits_succ_eq give the correct indexing.

  2. In the proof of Lemma 2.1 the notation switches between port_v(u) and port_u(v).
