The Multi-Agent Rotor-Router on the Ring — PODC 2013

Ralf Klasing, Adrian Kosowski, Dominik Pająk, Thomas Sauerwald. The Multi-Agent Rotor-Router on the Ring: A Deterministic Alternative to Parallel Random Walks. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484260. Formalized version: the full version HAL hal-00735113v2 (September 2014, 28 pp.); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The proofs of Theorem 3.2 (worst case Θ(n²/log k)) and Theorem 3.5 (best-case upper bound O((n/k)²)) are broken and nothing we proved repairs them; neither statement is refuted, but neither is established here. Of the range the paper claims, only the best-case lower bound stands: Theorem 3.7 (cover time Ω((n/k)²) for every start set) is true, and we prove it with a new argument and explicit constant, for n ≥ 8k instead of n ≥ 440k². The delayed-deployment technique (Lemmas 2.1–2.3, the slow-down lemma) is correct on every graph, while Theorem 3.4 and the return-time result (Theorem 4.1) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 3.2, proof (worst case Θ(n²/log k), Phase B and final bound)

The deployment and its analysis use the misprinted identity (4) of Lemma 3.1 (g_i ≈ 2 a_i a_k k⁴ a₁, S_{j+1} = S_j + ⌈k⁴ a₁ a_k⌉ + 12k), and the last step goes from B₁ ∈ Θ(n²/a₁) with a₁ = Θ(1/H_k) to Θ(n²/log k), although that would really be Θ(n² log k).

Impact. The worst-case bound of the abstract is not established. The statement is not refuted and looks true with the corrected lemma, but Theorem 3.2 is not formalized here.

Details

The two errors cancel: with the corrected identity the computation gives Θ(n² a₁) = Θ(n²/log k) as claimed. But the Phase B deployment itself is defined with the step ⌈k⁴ a₁ a_k⌉, so a correct proof needs a redefined deployment, with steps of about 2k⁴ a_k / a₁. That deployment is not in the paper, and we did not check that the proof works with it.

Repair

None proved. It needs the corrected lemma (lemma_3_1_corrected) and a reworked deployment.

❌ Theorem 3.5, proof (best-case upper bound O((n/k)²))

The deployment is inconsistent: an agent "returns to q_i" after each step, but q_i's pointer then points left, so the next step cannot start "to the right"; also, two of the q_i may coincide, and two agents on one node then leave in opposite directions.

Impact. The best-case upper bound of the abstract is not established; Theorem 3.5 is not formalized here.

Details

Simulation with hill-climbing over pointer initializations finds cover times ≤ 1.25 (L+1)² for gaps L ∈ {3, 5, 8} and up to 32 agents (16 for L = 8), so the statement looks true.

Repair

None proved. A rigorous proof needs the domain-size dynamics.

🔧 Lemma 3.1 (the sequence a_i, identity (4))

No sequence satisfies (1)–(4) for any k ≥ 2: summing (4) a_i · a₁ = 2/a_i − 1/a_{i−1} − 1/a_{i+1} over i = 1..k telescopes to a₁ Σ a_i = 1/a₁, so (3) Σ a_i = 1 forces a₁ = 1, which is impossible (lemma_3_1_false).

Impact. The intended identity is a_i / a₁ = …, which is what the lemma's own construction gives (a_i = 1/(c b_i), a₁ = 1/c²). The fix is a one-symbol change visible in the lemma's proof, but a later proof, that of Theorem 3.2, reasons with the wrong version.

Details

The telescoping uses 1/a₀ = 0 (property (1), a₀ = +∞) and a_{k+1} = a_k (property (2)). The right-hand sides sum to 1/a₁ + 1/a_k − 1/a_{k+1} = 1/a₁. Only (1)–(4) and positivity are used; (5) and (6) are irrelevant.

Repair

With the intended identity, all six properties hold for every k ≥ 2 (lemma_3_1_corrected).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 2.4, first property: in a deployment where no two agents ever share a node, a domain need not be a sub-path. On the 6-ring, agents on 0 and 3 with pointers 0→1, 1→2, 2→1, 3→2 swap along the edge {1, 2} in round 2, giving the agent that started on 0 the domain {0, 2} after round 2 (lemma_2_4_false; both agents are stopped afterwards). The intended hypothesis also excludes agents passing each other on an edge, as the proof of Theorem 4.1 says explicitly ("no two agents meet or pass each other on an edge"). Alternatively a swap can be re-read as two bounces, since agents are indistinguishable. A label-free form of the domain picture holds in the undelayed system for every initialization (pattern_invariant). The lemma is stated without proof and is used by Lemmas 2.5–2.9, which are not formalized.

  • Abstract and Theorem 3.2: Θ(n²/log k) is meaningless for k = 1, which the abstract includes ("1 ≤ k < n^{1/11}").

  • Proof of Lemma 2.1, equation (4) (and the recurrence in the proof of Lemma 2.2): the arrivals up to round t + 1 must use the exits e_v(t + 1), not e_v(t) (cf. equation (2)). The lemma is unaffected.

  • Lemma 2.8: "holds for 2n² consecutive time steps t, …, t + n² − 1".

  • Proof of Lemma 3.6: step (a) uses windows of length r(n/k) and step (c) jumps by r(n/k), while step (b) and the definition of good vertices use r(n/(10k)).

Typos

  • Proof of Lemma 2.1: the notation switches between port_v(u) and port_u(v).

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 3.7

🛠️ Theorem 3.7 (best-case lower bound Ω((n/k)²))

The proof rests on Lemmas 2.5/2.6, whose proofs are sketches, and it misstates its own tools.

Impact. Statement true; proved with a new argument and explicit constants (theorem_3_7): for n ≥ 8k, every start multiset has a node v such that, with all pointers pointing away from v, the ring is not covered before time n²/(8192 k²).

Details

The proof

  • initializes "in each node the pointer points away from the closest agent". It needs the opposite, the negative initialization of Lemma 2.5, where an agent entering a new node is sent back;

  • concludes "by Lemma 2.1", which only gives upper bounds on the undelayed cover time. A lower bound needs the left inequality of Lemma 2.2/2.3;

  • needs Lemmas 2.5/2.6 at scale n/(20k) instead of 22k: Lemma 2.5 is stated with the constants 22k and 11k, Theorem 3.7 applies it with initial interiors n/(20k) and needs the scaled conclusion Ω(n/k), and the proof of Lemma 2.5 only supports a loss of 11k (the "minimum configuration" 22k, 22k − 11, …), i.e. M − 11k ≥ M/2;

  • argues that the unexplored stretch "will be explored by agents having domains of sizes at least Ω(n/20k), so it takes Ω(n²/400k²)". This is not derived from anything proved.

Repair

We use a rotor-router potential instead of domains. For h : ZMod n → ℤ, Φ_h = 2 Σ_u pos(u) h(u) + Σ_v h(pointer target of v). Every release of an agent from v changes Φ_h by exactly h(v+1) + h(v−1) − 2h(v) (potential_run). With pointers pointing away from v, the pointer part can only grow for radially non-increasing h (ptr_term_ge). This gives two inequalities:

  • hit lemma (hit_lemma, parabola (R − d)²): reaching v from distance > R costs R² agent-rounds within distance R of v;

  • scale step (scale_step, bump of radius 2R): R² N_R(s) ≤ 2R² N_{2R}(0) + Σ_{s'<s} N_{2R}(s'), where N_R counts the agents within distance R of v.

Iterating over the radii 2^m R₀ (multiscale) and choosing v by counting (exists_good_center, which replaces the good vertices of Lemma 3.6) gives n² < 8192 k² T at any covering time T. Lemma 2.5 is not needed.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Graph and rotors (RotorGraph, Model.lean): finitely many nodes; node v has deg v ≥ 1 outgoing arcs in a fixed cyclic order (multi-arcs allowed). A configuration (Config) is the number of indistinguishable agents per node and a port pointer per node, as in Section 1.3.

  • Rounds: synchronous. In a round with delays d, node v keeps min(pos v, d v) agents. It releases the others one by one along consecutive arcs starting at the pointer, and the pointer advances by the number released (step). A delayed deployment D gives the delays of every round (more stopped agents than present means all stop); undelayed is R[k].

  • Visits: visits c₀ D t v is the paper's n_v^D(t), i.e. initial agents plus arrivals in rounds 1..t; Covered/CoverTime are defined from it. visits_eq_visitsOf is the paper's description by cumulative exit counts, the basis of Lemmas 2.1–2.3.

  • Ring (ring n, Ring.lean): ZMod n, port 0 to v + 1, port 1 to v − 1. The initial pointers are arbitrary or adversarial as each statement says; no randomness (Section 3.3 on random walks is not covered).

  • Labelled model (lstep, Domains.lean): used only for Lemma 2.4, since domains need agent identities. Agents on the same node are released in index order.
