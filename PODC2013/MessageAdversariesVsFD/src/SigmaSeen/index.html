We give a self-contained algorithm and a complete proof. ## Algorithm (every process runs it; processes are `0, 1, 2`) Every process broadcasts its whole local state at each of its steps. A local state records * `v`: the inputs it knows (initially its own; merged with the `v` of every received message), * `rd`: the processes from which it received a message sent in their *read phase*, * `acks`: the senders of received messages that knew its own input (write acknowledgements), * `resps`: the senders of received messages whose `rd` contained it (read responses), * `phase`: `0` (writing), `1` (reading), `2` (done). At a step, after processing the received message (if any), a process in phase `0` moves to phase `1` if its current `Σ` output `d` satisfies `d ⊆ acks` (its write is complete), and a process in phase `1` moves to phase `2` if `d ⊆ resps` (its read is complete); it then outputs `pair x₀ x₁` if it knows both inputs, `alone` otherwise. ## Proof idea *Safety* (not both `0` and `1` output `alone`): let `w_p` be the time at which `p` completes its write. If `p` outputs `alone`, take `k` in the intersection of `p`'s read quorum and `q`'s write quorum; `k`'s response to `p` was sent after `w_p` (it follows the reception of a read-phase message of `p`) and did not contain `x_q`, while `k`'s acknowledgement to `q` contained `x_q` and was sent before `w_q`; as knowledge is monotone, `w_p < w_q`. Both outputting `alone` would give `w₀ < w₁ < w₀`. *Liveness*: reliable delivery, infinitely many time-outs of correct processes, and the liveness of `Σ` (eventually the quorums of a correct process
