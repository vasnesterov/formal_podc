Message Adversaries vs Failure Detectors — PODC 2013

Michel Raynal, Julien Stainer. Synchrony Weakened by Message Adversaries vs Asynchrony Restricted by Failure Detectors. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484249. Formalized version: the full version, IRISA research report PI 2002 (Round-based Synchrony Weakened by Message Adversaries vs Asynchrony Enriched with Failure Detectors, hal-00787978v2, Feb. 2013); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. With the paper's own definition of solving a task in the synchronous model (every process outputs), the QUORUM characterization of Σ is false: a three-process task is solvable in AMP[fd: Σ] but not in SMP[adv: QUORUM] (Theorem 4, Lemma 6, Theorem 5), and the SOURCE + TOUR characterization of Ω (Theorem 2, Lemma 2, Corollary 1) fails in the same way, as does the hierarchy of Figure 1. The simulations of the asynchronous models in the synchronous ones leave weakly correct processes without output, and the converse simulations need exactly those outputs, so no reading of "solve" saves all lemmas and Theorem 3 stays unestablished. Theorem 1, Theorem 6 and the facts about strongly correct processes hold.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Lemmas 2, 4, 6 against Section 2.4 (Theorems 2, 3, 4, 5, Corollary 1, Figure 1, Conclusion)

The asynchronous-to-synchronous simulations treat weakly correct simulators as crashed and give them no output, although SMP processes are reliable and Section 2.4 lets only crashed processes lack an output.

Impact. Three things fail, and no corrected characterization is proved under any reading of "solve".

  • The QUORUM characterization of Σ is false, and so is Theorem 5 (SOURCE, QUORUM ≃ Σ, Ω, theorem5_false). Lemma 5 (the other inclusion, not formalized) is not affected.

  • The SOURCE + TOUR characterization of ARW[Ω] is false, and so is Corollary 1 (which adds the TP and PAIRS variants via Theorem 6; corollary1_false). The other inclusion (Lemma 1, not formalized) is not affected.

  • Theorem 3 (SOURCE characterizes Ω in AMP) is unestablished. We found no counterexample (the counterexamples below do not apply, since Tseen and Tcons are not solvable in AMP[Ω]); the statement is neither proved nor refuted.

Details

Theorem 4 and Lemma 6 (QUORUM characterizes Σ). The task Tseen ("of p₁, p₂ each outputs alone or both inputs, and at least one outputs both") is solvable in AMP_{3,2}[fd: Σ] but not in SMP_3[adv: QUORUM] (lemma6_false, theorem4_false). In SMP_3, let only p₃'s messages be delivered, every round, to everybody. Every two in-neighbourhoods contain p₃ and p₃ is strongly correct, so QUORUM holds (SOURCE too). The processes are reliable, so p₁ and p₂ must output (Section 2.4: "if no value is computed by p_j, it is because p_j has crashed"; Section 2.3: SMP_n is "made up of reliable processes"); neither ever learns the other's input, so both must answer alone (tseen_not_solvable). In AMP_{3,2}[fd: Σ], p₁ and p₂ write their input to a Σ-quorum and then read from a Σ-quorum (ABD); quorum intersection makes one of them see the other (tseen_solvable_amp_sigma, a complete proof in the CHT model). The proof of Lemma 6 fails at its last step: the simulation of Figure 7 stops the weakly correct simulators, which are reliable synchronous processes that must output. Here p₁ and p₂ are weakly correct and the simulation never gives them an output.

Theorem 2 and Lemma 2 (SOURCE + TOUR characterizes Ω in ARW). Consensus between p₁ and p₂, with p₃ as a helper whose output is free (Tcons), is solvable in ARW_{3,2}[fd: Ω] but not in SMP_3[adv: SOURCE, TOUR] (lemma2_false, theorem2_false). Take the patterns in which p₃ sends to everybody and hears nobody, while between p₁ and p₂ the adversary delivers one or both messages each round. They satisfy SOURCE (p₃), TOUR and even QUORUM. p₃ never learns anything, so p₁ and p₂ are in a two-process TOUR system. A Santoro–Widmayer bivalence argument then shows they cannot agree (tcons_not_solvable). In ARW[Ω] all three processes run an Ω-based consensus with commit-adopt rounds; p₃ proposes an input it has read (tcons_solvable_arw_omega). As for Lemma 6, the proof of Lemma 2 says weakly correct simulators "simulate a faulty process", which is not allowed for reliable synchronous processes.

Figure 1 and Conclusion (hierarchy of adversaries). The arrows SMP[adv: QUORUM] → SMP[adv: TOUR] and SMP[adv: SOURCE, QUORUM] → SMP[adv: SOURCE, TOUR] ("the first model is stronger"), the first of which the Conclusion repeats, are false. Tseen is solvable in SMP_3[adv: TOUR] in one round but not in SMP_3[adv: SOURCE, QUORUM] (not_quorum_stronger_than_tour, not_source_quorum_stronger_than_source_tour). This is a purely synchronous fact, independent of any failure-detector model.

Theorem 3 and the two readings of "solve". Lemma 4's simulation (Figure 5) never gives an output to weakly correct processes, and under the other reading of "solve" (only strongly correct processes must output) Lemma 3's simulation (Figure 4) breaks instead, since it deletes every message except the leader's, making every other process weakly correct. The same inconsistency affects all six lemmas. Lemmas 2, 4 and 6 (asynchronous → synchronous) treat weakly correct processes as crashed. That is valid only if they need not output. Lemmas 1, 3 and 5 (synchronous → asynchronous) need correct asynchronous processes to output, and their simulated processes may be weakly correct (Figures 2, 4, 6: for instance with Σ constantly {ℓ} in Figure 6). The paper never defines task solvability in SMP other than in Section 2.4, where only crashed processes may lack an output. For SMP[adv: ∞] both readings agree (solvable_inf_iff_local, solvableSC_inf_iff_local).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 3 is stated for SMP[adv: SOURCE, FAIR] and Lemma 6 for SMP[adv: FAIR, QUORUM], but FAIR is defined nowhere. It is a leftover from an earlier version: the titles, Figure 1, the introduction, Lemmas 3 and 4, and Theorem 4 all use SOURCE and QUORUM alone. We read the statements without FAIR.

  • Figures 4 and 6, line 5: r ← r_i + 1 should be r_i ← r_i + 1.

  • Figure 7, line 9: ∈ should be ⊆.

Typos

  • Figure 3, line 14 and Lemma 2, Part 1: seq_nq_i for seq_nb_i.

  • Lemma 2, Part 4: (r, s), (r, j) for the pairs (s, r), (j, r) of missed_i.

  • The proof of Lemma 4 stops mid-sentence ("It follows that").

  • Appendix A.3, Part 2: the heading "The source of SMP[SOURCE_pairs, PAIRS]…" should read SOURCE_tp, TP.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • SMP_n[adv: AD] (Model.lean): CSLib's heard-of model. n reliable processes run synchronous rounds. An adversary is a set of patterns p : ℕ → Graph P, so eventual properties are allowed, and properties combine by intersection. TOUR, SOURCE, QUORUM, TP, SOURCE_tp, PAIRS and SOURCE_pairs are taken literally. Algorithms are deterministic and full-information, with write-once outputs; this is no loss for solvability.

  • Strongly correct processes SC are literally Section 2.2's definition (the unique source component of ⇝∞, else ∅).

  • Tasks follow Section 2.4. Solvable: every process outputs, which is the paper's definition. SolvableSC: only strongly correct processes must output; this is the reading the proofs of Lemmas 2, 4 and 6 use.

  • AMP_{n,n−1}[fd: D] (Async.lean) is the staged CHT model (crash failures, at least one correct process, reliable non-FIFO links, broadcast steps) with inputs in the initial state. Σ is Cslib.FailureDetectors.SigmaFD (Podc/…/FailureDetectors/Sigma.lean), and (Σ, Ω) is a pair of histories.

  • ARW_{n,n−1}[fd: Ω] (ArwOmega.lean) has SWMR atomic registers, an infinite schedule (correct = steps infinitely often) and an Ω history that is arbitrary until it stabilizes on a correct leader.

  • Deterministic algorithms, no randomness; the synchronous adversary is non-oblivious.

Proof remarks.

  • Theorem 6, Part 2 (2n−3 rounds of TP simulate one round of TOUR): the counting chain ends with "|K_i ∩ K_j| ≥ n−1, hence {i, j} ∩ K_i ∩ K_j ≠ ∅", which skips a step. If neither process knows the other's message, K_i ∩ K_j ⊆ Π ∖ {i, j}, which has n − 2 elements: a contradiction (card_fwd_add_card_fwd, tp_journey). The statement is true (theorem6).

  • QUORUM's liveness part SC ≠ ∅ follows from its intersection part (SC_nonempty_of_inter), as the proof of Lemma 5 shows.

Not formalized. Lemmas 1, 3 and 5 (the simulations of Figures 2, 4 and 6; their proofs look right under the paper's reading of "solve"), Theorem 3, Appendix B (Ω_k, which presumably inherits the mistake above), and the Remark after Theorem 2.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: tcons_solvable_none (Tcons is solvable without an adversary), const_mem_SigmaFD, tseen_amp_sigma_nonvacuous (the Σ algorithm for Tseen has admissible runs with a valid Σ history, via the generic AlgAMP.exists_admissible_const), and the counterexample patterns are shown to satisfy the adversary properties (pat_N3_subset, pSrc_mem).
