import Papers.PODC2013.ByzantineVectorConsensus.Iterative /-! # Restricted synchronous round structure (Section 4, Theorem 6, synchronous case) Section 4 of the paper considers algorithms with a *restricted round structure*. In the synchronous case, in round `t + 1` every process 1. transmits its current vector state `v_i[t]` to all processes and receives a vector from every process (a missing message is replaced by a default value), and 2. computes `v_i[t+1]` as a function of `v_i[t]` and the received vectors. `RestrictedAlg P d` is exactly this class (the update may depend on the round number and on the process), `SyncExec` the adversary's choices (faulty set, inputs, and the vector each faulty process sends to each process in each round), and `RestrictedAlg.Solves f T ε K` says that after `T` rounds the states of the correct processes are valid and pairwise `ε`-close, in every execution with at most `f` faults and correct inputs in `K`. * `theorem_6_sync_restricted`: the Section 4 algorithm (Step 2 of Section 3.2 with `B_i = P`) is a restricted algorithm solving approximate BVC when `n ≥ (d + 2) f + 1` (sufficiency in Theorem 6). * `theorem_6_sync_necessity_false`: **the necessity claim of Theorem 6 (synchronous case) is false as stated.** Nothing restricts the update function, and a single vector of reals can encode any amount of real data (`(Fin 4 → ℝ²) ↪ ℝ²`). So the state can carry a full-information history: for `n = 4`, `d = 2`, `f = 1` (where `(d + 2) f + 1 = 5 > n`) the restricted algorithm "round 1: store the received vector of inputs; round 2: for each `k`, take the majority of the values for `k` relayed by the three other processes (OM(1) of Lamport–Shostak–Pease), then output the chosen point of `Γ`" solves even *exact* BVC in 2 rounds. `theorem_6_sync_necessity_false'` is a variant whose update ignores the round number (inputs in `[0, 1]²`, encodings outside it, so the own state tells the rounds apart). Letting `upd` depend on the round number is harmless anyway: synchronous processes know it. The claim presumably needs an extra restriction (e.g. the new state must lie in the convex hull of the received states, as in iterative algorithms à la [Vaidya, Tseng, Liang 2012]), which the paper
