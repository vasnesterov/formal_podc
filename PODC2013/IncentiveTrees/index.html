Fair and Resilient Incentive Tree Mechanisms — PODC 2013

Yuezhou Lv, Thomas Moscibroda. Fair and Resilient Incentive Tree Mechanisms. PODC 2013. DOI 10.1145/2484239.2484252. Formalized version: the journal version (Distributed Computing 29(1), 2016, DOI 10.1007/s00446-015-0250-y, author copy, full proofs in the appendix); all theorem, lemma, section and algorithm numbers below are the journal's. The PODC 2013 version (10 pp, proof sketches only) numbers the same results differently: Theorems 1–4 = PODC Theorems 4.1–4.4, appendix Lemmas 1–5 = PODC Lemmas 4.5–4.9, Theorem 5 = PODC Theorem 4.10; journal Sections 4.4, 5, 6 = PODC Sections 4.2, 4.3, 4.4. The two versions state the definitions of Section 3 identically (in particular PO with R(u) ≥ C(u)).

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. All four checked theorems hold once slips are fixed: the impossibility result (Theorem 3), the geometric mechanism (Theorem 1), TDRM (Theorem 4: everything except UGSA, in particular Sybil-proofness USA) and the CDRM family (Theorem 5: everything except URO and PO, in particular UGSA). The slips are a ≥ that should be > in the formal definition of PO (with it, Theorem 3 is false for budget Φ = 1), Theorem 5 also losing PO, and zero contributions / zero parameters. But the long appendix proof of TDRM's USA (Lemmas 1–5), the heart of the analysis of the paper's main mechanism, does not work, and we replace it by a new short induction.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Section 3.1, definition of PO (makes Theorem 3 false as stated). With PO formally defined as "R(u) ≥ C(u)" (zero profit suffices) and the allowed budget Φ = 1, the mechanism R(u) = C(u) satisfies SL, PO, UGSA and the budget constraint, contradicting Theorem 3 (theorem_3_false_for_weak_PO). The prose of the same paragraph says PO means positive profit, and the proof of Theorem 3 uses P(v*) > 0. With R(u) > C(u) Theorem 3 holds and the paper's proof is correct (theorem_3). R(u) = C(u) is subtree-local and has total reward C(T) = Φ·C(T). Every node makes profit 0 whatever the shape of its subtree, so PO (weak) holds, and any Sybil split has total profit 0 = the single node's profit, so UGSA (hence USA) holds. It also satisfies CCI and φ-RPC for every φ ≤ 1, but not CSI or URO. Whether the weak-PO version of Theorem 3 holds for Φ < 1 is open: the paper's argument needs a strictly profitable node, and we neither proved nor refuted that case.

  • Theorem 5 (CDRM mechanisms: "all properties, except URO"). A successfully contribution-deterministic mechanism also violates PO, since R(x, y) < Φx ≤ x (theorem_5). Section 4.4 announces that the Section 6 mechanism "achieves all the properties except PO/URO" and notes URO ⇒ PO. Everything else in Theorem 5 is proved, including UGSA.

  • Zero contributions and zero parameters (Theorems 1, 4, 5). Several claims fail only when a contribution or parameter is 0: CSI fails when the newcomer contributes 0 (all mechanisms) or C(u) = 0 (TDRM, CDRM), TDRM's chain for C(u) = 0 has N_u = ⌈0/μ⌉ = 0 nodes, Theorem 5 never constrains R(0, y), and Theorems 1 and 4 allow b = 0 (if φ = 0) and a, λ ≤ 0, where CCI/CSI/URO fail. We add the evident positivity assumptions (0 < b in Theorem 1; 0 < a, 0 < b, 0 < λ, 0 ≤ φ in Theorem 4; right-continuity of R(·, y) at 0 in Theorem 5, which forces R(0, y) = 0), use a one-node chain for C(u) = 0, and require positive contributions in CSI.

  • Appendix, Lemmas 1–5 (structure of an optimal Sybil partition for TDRM). The uniqueness parts ("must be an ε-chain", "at most one node below μ", "forms a tree") fail in degenerate cases: Lemma 5 fails whenever u has no solicitees (then R^e ≡ 0 and every partition maximizes it), and Lemmas 1–3 fail when a Sybil identity contributes 0. The proof of Theorem 4 only needs that some optimal partition is an ε-chain (which is what its proof idea says), and Theorem 4's USA is proved without these lemmas (tdrm_USA).

  • Algorithm 4. It creates the RCT edge (m^u_{N_u}, m^v_1) for an edge (u, v), but in Section 2 (u, v) means "u is a child of v"; Figure 3 shows the intended direction (tail of the parent → head of the child).

  • The UGSA counterexample after Theorem 4. P(u) should be ½((ak + ½)λμb + φμ − μ), not (ak + 1); the conclusion P'(u) > P(u) is unaffected (theorem_4, last conjunct).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 4 · Theorem 5

🛠️ Theorem 4 (TDRM satisfies USA; appendix Lemmas 1–5)

The proof argues about a reward-maximizing Sybil partition without showing that one exists, and its exchange steps only weakly increase the reward (Lemma 2 case (b) even leaves a 0-contribution node below μ), so "contradiction with maximality" does not follow.

Impact. Statement true; we give a new proof (tdrm_USA).

Details

The number of Sybil identities is unbounded and contributions are real, so the existence of an optimum is itself what has to be shown; with weak improvements the contradiction scheme of Lemmas 1–5 would have to be replaced by "some optimum has the structure", which presupposes the optimum. Also, Lemma 2's auxiliary fact S1 > S2 only covers transfers away from the balanced split; when C(x) < C(y) the transfer towards x first passes through it, and what is needed is convexity of the quadratic in the transfer.

Repair

We compute the closed form R(u) = (λb/μ)(Q(C(u)) + a·G(C(u))·X) + φC(u) (tdrm_node). Here Q, G are explicit functions of the ε-chain and X is the depth-weighted contribution of the children's RCTs. We then prove by induction on the Sybil forest (sybil_bound) that a Sybil tree of mass m earns at most Q(m) + a·E·G(m), the single-node value. Every optimisation fact of Lemmas 1–5 reduces to five inequalities about Q, G, H (Qc_add, Qc_superadd, Hc_add_le, Gc_add_le, Gc_subadd): concatenating chains never loses, and branching loses at least what the extra ancestor weight gains.

🔧 Theorem 5 (CDRM satisfies USA)

The minimal-counterexample merging argument is incomplete: merging can lower the reward of other Sybils, and "at least the same reward" does not contradict minimality of k.

Impact. Statement true; a direct induction on the Sybil forest with the same key inequality proves it: a Sybil tree of mass m with real contribution E earns at most R(m, E) (theorem_5; uses (iv) and monotonicity in y).

Details

In case (a) the merged ancestor–descendant pair need not be adjacent, which lowers the reward of the Sybils in between; case (b) ignores Sybil ancestors of p₂, which lose x₂ + y₂ from their subtrees.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Not a distributed-computing model: a mechanism maps a weighted referral tree (contributions C(u) ≥ 0) to rewards. No time, adversary or randomness. Sybil attacks are compared on final configurations, which under SL is the same as "for every i" along join sequences.

  • Only subtree-local mechanisms are modelled: a mechanism is f : Tree → ℝ, the reward of the root of the given subtree. All mechanisms of Theorems 1, 4, 5 are of this form, and Theorem 3 assumes SL.

  • Trees are rose trees with ordered children lists. Theorem 3 is thereby proved for a larger class (order-dependent mechanisms included); all our mechanisms are order-invariant.

  • A Sybil attack of u is a forest of Sybil trees (Sybil.node c sybilChildren realChildren). Every real child subtree of u is attached to exactly one Sybil (up to permutation). With SL, where the Sybil trees are placed in the rest of the referral tree is irrelevant. Single-identity "attacks" (k = 1) are included, as in the paper's UGSA counterexample for TDRM.

  • The budget is required over all nodes of every tree, including a root of contribution 0 (at least as strong as the paper's R(T) ≤ ΦC(T)). PO is "positive profit" (PO); the printed weak version is POWeak.
