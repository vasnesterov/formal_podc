TetraBFT — PODC 2024

Qianyu Yu, Giuliano Losa, Xuechao Wang. TetraBFT: Reducing Latency of Unauthenticated, Responsive BFT Consensus. PODC 2024. DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662783. Formalized version: arXiv 2405.02615 v2 (April 2026); its numbering is the same as v1's. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Termination (liveness after GST) in Theorem 1 is false for the protocol as specified: after GST the view change can deadlock forever, both with reliable channels (n = 7, f = 2) and in the paper's lossy pre-GST model (n = 4, f = 1). It survives only in a weaker form: we prove that the protocol terminates once its echo rule is repaired, but only on reliable channels, which the paper's constant-storage model does not allow; agreement and validity hold in every run. The storage, communication and latency claims and the multi-shot protocol were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Section 2 model and §3.2 (no retransmission); Theorem 1, termination

In the paper's own model, where messages before GST may be lost, n = 4, f = 1 already deadlocks: timers fire once per view and nothing is resent, so view-change messages lost before GST are never replaced (Lossy.termination_fails_lossy).

Impact. Termination fails in the model the paper insists on ("with constant storage, we must allow the loss of messages during asynchrony"), and no result here saves it there: our termination theorem below assumes reliable channels.

Details

This deadlock comes from the lack of retransmission, not from the echo guard: the repaired echo rule deadlocks there too.

Repair

None proved. The likely fix adds retransmission to the repaired echo rule.

🛠️ Theorem 1, termination proof (view synchronization)

The proof only cites Lemmas 2, 4, 5, which assume all correct nodes are in a good view after GST; the paper never proves view synchronization, and the 9Δ timeout justification assumes that every node echoes.

Impact. The statements of Lemmas 2, 4 and 5, the core of the liveness argument, are correct (Lemma 4 with its implicit hypotheses made explicit), and so is the 9Δ budget of a view. What is missing is the step that brings the correct nodes into such a view.

Repair

For the repaired echo rule on reliable channels we prove that views synchronize, with timeout T ≥ 8δ + 9, the discrete form of 9Δ, and round-robin leaders (sync_window, fixed_termination).

🛠️ Sections 1 and 5, Appendix B (mechanized check)

The TLA+ specification ShowsSafeAt has only item 2(b)iiiA of Rule 3 and omits item 2(b)iiiB, so the Apalache check covers a stricter vote-1 rule than the protocol of Section 3.

Impact. The paper's mechanized safety check does not cover the protocol it presents, and it covers only n = 4.

Repair

Our Lean proof of agreement covers the full Rule 3 for all n > 3f (tetra_agreement).

🔧 Theorem 1, termination (view-change echo rule of §3.2)

After GST the view change can deadlock forever: a node that has already echoed a higher view never echoes the view it is itself entering, so with n = 7, f = 2 (reliable channels) correct nodes end split between views 1 and 2 and no one ever decides (Deadlock.termination_fails).

Impact. The liveness half of Theorem 1 is false for the protocol as specified, even on reliable channels; agreement and validity hold in every run, and on reliable channels the protocol terminates with a repaired echo rule (fixed_solvesConsensus).

Details

The rule is: on timeout, send ⟨view-change, v+1⟩; on f + 1 view-change messages for v', send ⟨view-change, v'⟩ "if it has not sent a view-change message for view v' or any higher view"; on n − f of them, move to v'. Timers fire once per view and nothing is resent. The guard lets a node that has already echoed a higher view refuse to help the others reach the view it is itself entering.

Counterexample (n = 7, f = 2, Byzantine nodes 0 and 1 lead views 0 and 1, Δ = 1, timeout 9Δ):

  1. Nodes 2–5 move to view 1 together; node 6's copies of their messages are delayed until GST + Δ.

  2. Nodes 2–5 time out in view 1. Node 5 alone reaches view 2, with one Byzantine view-2 message.

  3. Node 5 times out in view 2. Its view-3 message and two Byzantine ones make node 6 echo view 3.

  4. Only then does node 6 receive the view-2 messages, and it enters view 2. Because it has already sent view 3, it never sends view 2.

  5. Nodes 2, 3, 4 now see 4 < n − f view-2 messages and 2 < f + 1 view-3 messages forever.

From time 41 nothing is in transit and no timer is pending, so no correct node ever decides (Deadlock.termination_fails, with timeout 9Δ; the same schedule rescaled deadlocks with timeout 17 = 8δ + 9, the bound of the repair theorem below: Deadlock17.termination_fails; both runs use reliable channels and satisfy CSLib's Run.Valid). The same pattern works for every f ≥ 2 (informal).

Repair

Echo every view ≥ the current one that was not echoed yet. For reliable channels we prove that this repair works: with timeout T ≥ 8δ + 9 and round-robin leaders, the repaired protocol solves consensus (fixed_solvesConsensus).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 4 leaves its hypotheses implicit: a correct leader, and proof messages from all correct nodes. The statement is true with these hypotheses made explicit (Execution.lemma4, follower_determines_safe).

  • Lemma 8 (d)ii: "either m or m' satisfies Rule 4 Item 3" should be "neither".

  • Section 6's proof says "vote-4 for block s − 4" but Fig. 2 gives s − 3.

Typos

  • Lemma 8 (d)iii writes the view as v̄ and then v̂.

  • Theorem 1's agreement proof says "i decides val_j" where val is meant.

  • A few spelling slips ("quadrati", "whooping", "struture").

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 7 · Lemma 2 · Theorem 1, validity

🔧 Lemma 7 (wrong step in the proof)

The proof asserts that once a correct node decides val in view v, every correct vote-1 in view v is for val, but with a Byzantine leader a synchronous run with n = 4 has correct node 1 decide true in view 0 while correct node 3 votes vote-1 for false in view 0 (Lemma7.lemma7_proof_claim_false).

Impact. Statement true; Lemma 7 follows from Lemma 6 instead (Execution.lemma7).

🔧 Lemma 2 (choice of quorum)

The proof picks the "last view with a vote-3" among a quorum that may contain Byzantine nodes with fake reports.

Impact. Statement true; the argument works when the quorum is the set of correct nodes (Execution.lemma2).

🔧 Theorem 1, validity

The validity proof is circular as written.

Impact. Statement true; we prove it by induction over time (tetra_validity).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib's partially synchronous Byzantine model (Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync). Nodes are Fin n, at most f are Byzantine, and 3f < n. Channels are authenticated, there are no signatures, and Byzantine nodes may equivocate. Time is discrete, and each node takes one deterministic step per tick.

  • Run.Valid f δ: reliable channels, with delivery by max(send, GST) + δ. LossyValid is the paper's model, where pre-GST messages may be lost.

  • tetra prm (Protocol.lean) is §3.2 written as a state machine. It covers timer, echo, view change, proof/suggest reports, the proposal (input if Rule 1 shows it safe), vote-1 (Rule 3), vote-2/3/4, and the decision. Rules 1–4 are verbatim (Defs.lean). Reports come from the full vote history; constant storage is not modelled. The flag prm.fixEcho switches to the repaired echo rule, which is not in the paper.

  • Safety is first proved for abstract executions. These are the votes cast by correct nodes, subject to four guarantees: one vote per phase and view, quorum behind each vote, Rule 3 on honest reports, quorum behind a decision. A refinement then shows that every run of tetra prm is such an execution, with no timing assumption, so safety holds in asynchronous runs too.

  • The counterexamples are concrete runs evaluated by decide +kernel through a memoised simulator (simHist, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/Sim.lean). A quiescence lemma extends them to infinite time.
