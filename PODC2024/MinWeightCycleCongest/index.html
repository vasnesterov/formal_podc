Minimum Weight Cycle in CONGEST — PODC 2024

Vignesh Manoharan, Vijaya Ramachandran. Computing Minimum Weight Cycle in the CONGEST Model. PODC 2024. DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662801. Formalized version: the PODC paper's Theorems 1.2.A and 1.4.A. Their proofs are deferred to the full version arXiv:2308.08670v3, which in turn defers them to the companion paper arXiv:2205.14797v2 (Near Optimal Bounds for Replacement Paths and Related Problems in the CONGEST Model, §3.1.1–3.1.2, Lemmas 13–14, Figures 4–5). We formalize those proofs.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Both checked lower bounds hold as stated: computing a (2 − ε)-approximation of the minimum weight cycle (MWC) needs Ω(n / log n) CONGEST rounds, both in directed graphs (weighted or unweighted) and in undirected weighted graphs, even on networks of constant diameter. The proofs need two local repairs: the cited constructions are disconnected, so the "constant diameter" part is not proved there, and Lemma 14's argument covers only the heavy weight w = 2, not the large w that the (2 − ε) claim needs. The sublinear-round algorithms, the paper's other main part, and the lower bounds for larger approximation factors were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

All in the companion paper's proofs; none affects a result.

  • The directed proof decides "by checking if the minimum cycle has length at least 4". Every cycle has length at least 4; the test should be "at least 8" (or "greater than 4").

  • The undirected proof likewise says "weight at least 6" where it should be "> 6" (or "≥ 8").

  • Both proofs say "the cut has 4k edges". The graphs as constructed have 2k cut edges (our graphs with a hub have 4k). This is harmless.

  • In Lemma 14 the case list of 4-cycles leaves out the all-heavy cycles r_i r′_j r_p r′_q on Bob's side. They weigh 4w, like the listed ℓ_i ℓ′_j ℓ_p ℓ′_q.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Thm 1.2.A, 1.4.A · Lemma 14

🔧 Thm 1.2.A, 1.4.A ("even on graphs with constant diameter")

The constant-diameter part is not proved: the graphs of Lemmas 13 and 14 in the cited proofs can be disconnected (for S_a = S_b = ∅ they are perfect matchings).

Impact. Statements true; repaired with a hub vertex, on diameter-2 networks and for every n (dComap_ediam, uComap_ediam, theorem_1_2_A, theorem_1_4_A).

Details

The companion paper adds a sink vertex with an arc from every vertex to get diameter 2, but only for its replacement-path bound, not for MWC. For directed MWC the same sink works, since a sink lies on no cycle. For undirected weighted MWC it must be done with care. A hub joined to all vertices by unit-weight edges creates triangles hub–ℓ_i–r_i of weight 3, below the gap 2w + 2, and the reduction fails.

Repair

We add a hub whose edges weigh 2w. Then every cycle through the hub weighs at least 4w, and the bound holds on diameter-2 networks. We also add pendant padding vertices so that every n is covered, not only n = 4k.

🔧 Lemma 14 (the heavy weight w needed for (2 − ε))

The proof bounds cycles with at least 6 edges by "at most as many light edges as heavy edges", which for heavy weight w gives only 3w + 3 for a 6-cycle, below the needed 4w once w > 3.

Impact. The lemma is true; the proof needed a parity step (uGraph.weight_ge).

Details

For (2 − ε)-approximation, the paper changes the heavy weight 2 to a large w and says one must then tell cycles of weight 2w + 2 from cycles of weight 4w. Its argument: the graph is bipartite and the light edges form a matching, so a cycle has at most as many light edges as heavy edges. With w = 2 this gives weight at least 9.

Repair

Light edges are the only edges between Alice's side L ∪ L′ and Bob's side R ∪ R′, so every cycle has an even number of light edges. A 6-cycle therefore has at most 2 light edges and weighs at least 4w + 2. (uGraph.weight_ge)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous CONGEST, deterministic (Cslib.Congest.Algorithm). Node IDs are the vertex names, and the vertex type V is arbitrary with |V| = n. Messages have exactly B bits per edge, direction and round. Nodes have unbounded local computation. There are no failures.

  • The network is the underlying undirected unweighted graph (§1.1). Node v's input is the weights of its incident arcs in both directions (directed case, WDigraph.view) or of its incident edges (WGraph.view). Weights lie in {0, …, W}.

  • MWC is the minimum weight of a simple cycle, ∞ if there is none (Def. 1.1). Simple cycles are periodic sequences of distinct vertices, of length ≥ 2 (directed) or ≥ 3 (undirected).

  • Output convention (ApproxOutput): every node outputs none or an estimate. At least one node outputs an estimate, and every estimate e satisfies MWC ≤ e ≤ α · MWC. The paper's own algorithms meet this, and it is the weakest convention its lower bounds claim ("even when only one node is required to know the weight").

  • The hypothesis is weaker than "solves MWC". The directed algorithm need only be correct on unweighted digraphs whose underlying network has diameter ≤ 2. The undirected one need only be correct on weighted graphs of diameter ≤ 2, with W ≥ W₀(α). So the theorems cover weighted and unweighted algorithms and the constant-diameter claim.

  • Bounds are explicit: n ≤ 32 · B · T + 12, i.e. T = Ω(n / B) = Ω(n / log n) for B = Θ(log n). Set disjointness on k² bits is embedded into graphs with n = 4k + 1 + p nodes and 4k cut edges. The deterministic bound CC(DISJ_K) ≥ K is proved in CSLib.

  • Randomized versions (the paper: "all our lower bounds hold for randomized algorithms") use private random strings per node. They are conditional on the randomized communication lower bound for set disjointness (Razborov / Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger), which enters as the hypothesis RandDisjLB δ κ (private-coin protocols with nonempty finite uniform coin spaces) and is not formalized.
