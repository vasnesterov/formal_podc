Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Game Dynamics in the Population Protocol Model — PODC 2024.

Paper: Alistarh, Chatterjee, Karrabi, Lazarsfeld, Game Dynamics and Equilibrium Computation in the Population Protocol Model, PODC 2024 (arXiv 2307.07297v3).

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 2.5, upper bound — false (❌). The theorem fixes any a, b > 0 with a + b ≤ 1 and claims t_mix = O(min{k/|a−b|, k²}·m log m) (a ≠ b) and O(k² m log m) (a = b), with the dependence on a, b shown explicitly. The text below the theorem stresses "the impact of more biased a and b". For k = m = 2 and b ≤ a, every t with d(t) ≤ 1/4 has t ≥ 1/(2a) (isMixedAt_two_two). So no constant C satisfies the bound for all a = b (theorem_2_5_upper_false), nor for all a = 2b (theorem_2_5_upper_false_ne).

    • Parameter range. a + b < 1 is explicitly allowed by Definition 2.3, and the paper's own application has a + b = γ < 1. Small a + b is a legitimate parameter range, not a degenerate corner.

    • Role in the paper. The paper presents Theorem 2.5 as a tool for Theorem 2.7 that "may be of independent interest". The contribution list (§1.2) names Theorem 2.7, not Theorem 2.5. The mixing bound of Theorem 2.7 looks fine after the fix (a + b = γ, m = γn), but we did not formalize it.

    • Location of the error. Proposition A.7. For a = b, G_t = Z_t² − t is not a martingale (E[Z_{t+1}² − Z_t²] = a + b), so E[τ_absorb] = k²/(2a). The bound "RHS of (26) ≤ k²" likewise tends to k²/(a+b). The correct statement multiplies the k² terms by 1/(a+b).

    • Reading of the O. theorem_2_5_upper_false* refute the bound read uniformly in a, b (the reading the paper intends: it displays the dependence on |a − b|, and Theorem 2.7 plugs a − b = γ(1 − 2β) into it). If the O were allowed to hide constants depending on a, b, the displayed Theorem 2.5 would be true, but then the false statement is located in the numbered, constant-free lemmas below, so a false statement remains under either reading.

    • Numbered lemmas behind it — false. Proposition A.7 (E[τ_absorb] ≤ k² for a = b; truth k²/(2a), false for every a < 1/2), Lemma A.5 (E[τ_coal] ≤ min{k/|a−b|, k²}, resp. ≤ k²; for k = 2, X_0 = 1, Y_0 = 2 the two walks coalesce at the first non-null step, so E[τ_coal] = 1/(a+b), false for a + b < 1/4) and Lemma A.8 (Pr[τ_couple > 2Φ log(4m)] ≤ 1/4). For Lemma A.8 we have a Lean refutation at fixed parameters (lemma_A_8_false). For k = m = 2, a = 1/100, b = 1/200 (so a > b, as Lemma A.8 requires) we have Φ = 8 and 2Φ log₂(4m) = 48, yet d(t) > 1/4 for all t ≤ 48. By the coupling inequality (22) (d(t) ≤ max_{x,y} Pr[τ_couple > t], a standard fact we did not formalize), some x, y violate Lemma A.8. Propositions A.7 and Lemma A.5 are checked by hand only (expected hitting times are not formalized).

  2. Theorem 2.7 / Section 2.2.1 — sampling convention (✏️). The formula p_j ∝ (1/β − 1)^{j−1} is exact iff the responder is drawn uniformly from all n agents, independently of the initiator (replKernel; theorem_2_7_stationary proves the stationary part in that model). Under the standard PP scheduler (distinct ordered pair, distinctKernel), the count process is the (k, γ(n−1−βn)/(n−1), γβn/(n−1), m)-Ehrenfest process (distinctKernel_counts). Its stationary law has ratio (1/β − 1) − 1/(βn). distinct_counterexample shows the claimed law is not stationary for one AD and two GTFT agents (k = 2).

    • Convention. The paper never names the scheduler, but its computation (5) uses the with-replacement probabilities (z_j/n)(1−β), so the intended model can be recovered from the paper. The formula is exactly right there.

    • Caveat. "Agents interact in pairs" normally means distinct pairs in the PP literature the paper cites, and a GTFT agent "meeting itself" is unusual.

  3. Proposition A.9 — "D ≥ km" (🔧). The diameter of the transition graph is (k−1)m. For k = m = 2 the graph is the path (2,0) – (1,1) – (0,2), so D = 2 < km = 4 (a hand check; not in Lean). The proposition's purpose, t_mix = Ω(km), holds. We prove t_mix ≥ (k−1)m/2 for all a, b and every distribution π (theorem_2_5_lower). The local fix k → k−1 is recoverable, and the claim is asymptotic.

    • The proof also reasons with km ("at least km total transitions are needed"). The proposition's stated purpose (t_mix = Ω(km)) is unaffected, and (k−1)m ≥ km/2 for k ≥ 2.

  4. Theorem 2.7, lower bound Ω(kn) (not formalized). Theorem 2.5 gives Ω(km) = Ω(kγn), which is Ω(kn) only for constant γ. The statement itself is plausible: the chain is lazy with rate ≤ γ, which compensates. We did not formalize it.

Proof gaps (statements true)

  • Theorem 2.4, uniqueness. A.3 checks detailed balance and concludes "π is the unique stationary distribution". Uniqueness needs irreducibility, which the paper argues only for k = 2, 3 ("easy to see"). We prove irreducibility for all k: every state reaches (m, 0, …, 0), and reversibility with a positive π makes steps symmetric (ehrenfest_isIrreducible). We also prove uniqueness for irreducible finite chains (IsStationary.unique).

  • Section 2.2.1, "{z^t} is exactly a (k,a,b,m)-Ehrenfest process". The paper only lists the transition probabilities of z. We prove exact lumpability of the agent-level chain along counts for any uniform scheduler (schedKernel_lump) and the resulting t-step identity (schedKernel_pow_counts).

  • Proposition 2.8. It uses E[π_j] = m p_j (the multinomial mean, stated as "known" in §2.1). We prove it (sum_mul_multinomialPMF) and verify the closed form via (λ−1)² ∑_{i<k} iλ^i = (k−1)λ^{k+1} − kλ^k + λ (generosity_closed_form). Both cases (β ≠ 1/2, β = 1/2) hold as stated.

Modelling choices and changed definitions

  • Indices. Urns / generosity levels are Fin k = {0, …, k−1}. The paper's g_j, x_j, p_j (j ∈ [k]) are our index j − 1, so p_j ∝ λ^{j−1} becomes ehrParam k λ j = λ^j / ∑_i λ^i, and g_j = ĝ (j−1)/(k−1) becomes ĝ · j/(k−1).

  • Definition 2.3. It is defined as an urn chain (urnKernel) with nearest-neighbour rates. The entrywise lemmas ehrenfest_apply_up/down/self/other show it is the matrix of Definition 2.3. The diagonal is given in "generator form", so row sums are 1 by construction.

  • Stationary distributions are probability vectors with π ᵥ* P = π. theorem_2_4 / ehrenfest_stationary_unique say: every stationary distribution equals the multinomial. ehrenfest_isStationary says: the multinomial is stationary.

  • Generality. ehrenfest_stationary_unique and the corollaries hold for all k ≥ 1, m ≥ 0 (the paper assumes k, m ≥ 2). theorem_2_4 keeps the paper's hypotheses k, m ≥ 2.

  • Theorem 2.7, hypotheses. We assume numAD > 0 (β > 0; otherwise 1/β is undefined) and at least one GTFT agent (m > 0). Together these give 0 < β < 1, 0 < γ, a + b = γ ≤ 1.

  • Theorem 2.7, "converges to π". Not formalized: we prove the count process is the Ehrenfest process and identify its unique stationary distribution. Convergence would need aperiodicity (true here: a + b = γ < 1 makes every state lazy) and the finite-chain convergence theorem, which is absent from Mathlib/CSLib.

  • Mixing time. t_mix ≤ T is "∃ t ≤ T with d(t) ≤ 1/4" (IsMixedAt), and d(t) = max_x ‖P^t(x,·) − π‖_TV. The negations in theorem_2_5_upper_false* refute "∃ C, ∀ admissible a, b, t_mix ≤ C·f(k,a,b,m)" for k = m = 2 (log is the natural logarithm; any base works).

  • Population. An (α, β, γ) population is a finite agent type A with a kind map τ : A → Kind. AC and AD agents never change (igt is one-way: only a GTFT initiator updates). The paper's Inc/Dec truncation is inc/dec. The configuration space Cfg τ contains the configurations consistent with τ.

  • Proposition 2.8. g̃ is stated as (1/m) ∑_j g_j · ∑_x π(x) x_j for any stationary distribution π of the count chain. By Theorem 2.7 this is the unique one.

Not formalized

Proposition 2.2 (payoff monotonicity), Theorem 2.9 (ε-approximate distributional equilibrium), Appendices B–D, the coupling upper bound of Theorem 2.5 (Lemmas A.5, A.8, Prop. A.6) in corrected form, and the mixing-time and convergence parts of Theorem 2.7.
