Definition 11 asks that for every sequential execution `α` containing some complete `prop_j`, the decisions lie in a set `S_α` of `k` processes, for every extension `α · α' · β_i` in which `p_i` completed `prop_i` (in `α · α'`) and `β_i` consists of `dec_i`. We formalize, over a *nondeterministic* sequential specification (`NSpec`), with sequential histories `List (Fin n × Op × Resp)` (process, operation, response): * `KOrdering` — Definition 11 as stated in the paper; * `KOrderingI` — the variant that the proof of Lemma 12 actually needs: in the proof, `f(F_i) = α · α' · γ` where `γ` is produced by `p_i`'s solo simulation of `dec_i`, but the strong linearization function `f` may also linearize, inside `γ`, the operations of other processes that are pending in `E_i*` (their next operation of `prop_j`). The paper writes `f(F_i) = α · α' · β_i` with `invs(β_i) = dec_i`, ignoring them. Also, validity only needs the chosen process `ℓ` to have *started* (its input is written before its first operation), and the paper's own stuttering-queue example only provides that. * `KOrderingW` — a common weakening of both (`KOrdering.toW`, `KOrderingI.toW`).
