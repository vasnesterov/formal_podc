No Broadcast Abstraction for k-Set Agreement — PODC 2024

Sylvain Gay, Achour Mostéfaoui, Matthieu Perrin. Brief Announcement: No Broadcast Abstraction Characterizes k-Set-Agreement in Message-Passing Systems. PODC 2024. DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662818. Formalized version: the extended version (HAL hal-04571653v1, May 2024), which contains the proofs the brief announcement omits; numbering follows it. The OPODIS 2024 version (hal-05521071, "No Symmetric Broadcast Abstraction …") has the same Lemma 9 (its Lemma 15) with the same proof.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Lemma 9 is false: a content-neutral, compositional broadcast abstraction on top of which k-set agreement is solvable can still admit N-solo executions for every N, and the paper's proof of the main Theorem 1 rests on it. Theorem 1 is true as far as we checked, and we prove it with a repaired adversary, which does not even need content-neutrality, but only for k-SA algorithms that communicate only through the broadcast abstraction. The paper also lets them use point-to-point channels, and its reduction to the channel-free case is unjustified.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Proof of Lemma 9 (reduction of A to a channel-free A')

"send and receive can be trivially emulated using B" overlooks that SR-Termination also holds for messages of crashed senders, while BC-Global-CS-Termination does not.

Impact. The case of a k-SA algorithm A that also uses the point-to-point channels of CAMP_n[B] is not covered. In our Theorem 1 the k-SA algorithm A uses only the broadcast abstraction, so for such A the theorem is not formalized (see differencies.md, item 8).

🛠️ Lemma 9 (a k-SA-solving abstraction has no N-solo execution), and the proof of Theorem 1

Lemma 9 fails for every k: "k-Bounded-Order broadcast restricted to chained messages" is content-neutral and compositional, an algorithm solves k-set agreement on top of it with k + 1 processes, and yet it admits an N-solo execution for every N (lemma9_false).

Impact. The paper's proof of Theorem 1 is Lemma 9 plus Lemma 10, so the main result is not established by the paper. Theorem 1 itself is true; we prove it with a different adversary (theorem1_paper, theorem1), with the restriction of the previous item. Our proof shows more: for 1 < k < n no compositional broadcast abstraction is equivalent to k-SA; content-neutrality is not needed.

Details

A message is chained if its sender had already delivered one of its own messages when it invoked broadcast for it. The abstraction (chainedBO) requires that among any k + 1 distinct chained messages, two are delivered in the same order by every process.

  • It contains every uniform total-order execution (totalOrder_mem), so it is implementable from consensus.

  • The algorithm (algQ) works as follows. Broadcast a dummy message. Once it has returned and been delivered locally, broadcast the proposal; this message is chained. Decide the value of the first value message delivered.

  • If k + 1 distinct values were decided, the first-delivered value messages of the deciders would be k + 1 chained messages from distinct senders. Two of them would be delivered in the same order by everybody, but each decider delivered its own decided message before the other one (algQ_solves, algQ_wellFormed; admissible executions exist: soloRun_admissible).

  • The N-solo execution: every process invokes its N broadcasts (each returns at once), then delivers its own N messages, then everybody else's. No message is chained, so the ordering property is void (soloExec_mem, soloExec_nSolo).

The proof breaks at "αᵢ is indistinguishable from δ, as both executions involve identical B-broadcast and B-delivery steps for pᵢ". Definition 5 constrains only the order of deliveries. Restricting and renaming an N-solo execution cannot change when pᵢ invokes its broadcasts relative to its returns and deliveries, so the local view of pᵢ in δ need not be its view in αᵢ. A further step of the proof is unjustified (it does not matter for the counterexample): δ is finite, while running A' "on δ" needs an admissible continuation in which A' keeps acting, possibly broadcasting after it decided.

The paper runs the dummy loop sync-broadcast(synch) on top of B, then renames the synch messages into A's messages. Lemma 9 shows that this does not reproduce A's local views. We keep the paper's scheduler (Algorithm 1), but let the upper layer of each process run a copy of A itself. On top of B, pᵢ's copy invokes A's own messages at the times A wants. Its view is its own invocations, returns and deliveries.

Repair

The execution αS (Theorem1.lean, names p_1 … p_{k+1} as in the paper):

  1. Each pᵢ, i < k, runs alone until its copy decides. The k-SA objects answer its own proposals. A run alone is admissible (solo_adm), so B produces an execution of S. Restricted to the copy's messages, it is an execution of A on top of S in which pᵢ is the only participant. k-SA-Termination and k-SA-Validity of A then say that the copy decides, and decides pᵢ's own proposal (early_copy, generic fin_copy).

  2. p_k runs alone. A proposal to an object already decided by p_1, …, p_{k-1} (a reset) discards the current copy and starts a new one. There are finitely many resets: p_1 decided finitely many objects, and proposals are one-shot. The copy started after the last reset decides p_k's proposal (kk_copy).

  3. p_{k+1} runs after p_k's last reset. It first receives the messages p_k sent it before that reset. On objects decided by all of p_1, …, p_k, it adopts p_k's value. Its copy decides its own proposal (z_copy, via the execution gZ).

  4. In αS (αS_adm), p_{k+1} receives the other pending messages once its copy has decided. B produces an execution of S. By compositionality, its restriction to the messages of the final copies is an execution of A on top of S (DS_adm) in which p_1, …, p_{k+1} decide k + 1 distinct values (false_of).

The paper's footnote 1 (SR-Termination is not proved for γ) is not a gap. Lemma 10 uses γ only as a prefix of an admissible execution (BC-Validity), and Lemma 7 proves SR-Termination in the one case where it needs it. Our counterparts of the γ executions (solo, gZ) are infinite admissible executions.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Broadcast abstractions (Spec.lean). An execution of a broadcast abstraction is a time-indexed sequence of invocations, returns and deliveries, together with its set of correct processes. A broadcast abstraction is a set S of such executions. Each satisfies BC-Validity, BC-No-Duplication, BC-Local-Termination, BC-Global-CS-Termination, unique messages and well-formed invocations. Messages are objects (id, content): algorithms choose contents, identities are fresh. Compositionality (Definition 2), content-neutrality (Definition 3, sender-wise injective renamings) and N-solo executions (Definition 5) follow the paper.

  • Algorithms on top of S (CAMP_n[S]). A is deterministic: its next action (broadcast a content, decide, or wait) is a function of its proposal and local history. An execution is admissible if its broadcast events form an execution in S, A's steps follow A, correct processes take infinitely many steps of A, and faulty processes finitely many steps. A uses only the broadcast abstraction (like the paper's A'; this makes our Theorem 1 weaker than the paper's, whose A may also use channels), never invokes while an invocation is pending, and has at least k + 1 possible proposals. k-SA: validity (a decided value was proposed by a participant), at most k decided values, termination of correct processes.

  • CAMP_n[k-SA] (Camp.lean). Asynchronous processes; any number of crashes (faulty processes take finitely many steps, correct ones infinitely many). Channels are reliable and non-FIFO (SR-Validity, SR-No-Duplication, SR-Termination). There are any number of k-SA objects (validity, agreement, termination, one answer per proposal). B is deterministic and proposes at most once per object; the upper layer is arbitrary but well-formed. B implements S if the broadcast events of every admissible execution form an execution in S. The scheduler is adversarial; there is no randomness.

Not formalized. The paper's Lemmas 1–8 and 10 for its own sync-broadcast(synch) adversary (we reprove their content for the copy-of-A adversary). Neither is the remark "CAMP_n[∅] with n − k − 1 initial crashes is CAMP_{k+1}[∅]": we work with n processes directly, the extra ones never taking a step. Theorem 1 for algorithms A that also use point-to-point channels is open here: it should still hold, since those messages can be held back until after the decisions and then simulated by the upper layer of p_{k+1}.

Trust. No sorry; lemma9_false, theorem1, theorem1_paper use only propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
