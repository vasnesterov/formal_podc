Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of No Broadcast Abstraction for k-Set Agreement — PODC 2024.

Paper: Gay, Mostéfaoui, Perrin, No Broadcast Abstraction Characterizes k-Set-Agreement in Message-Passing Systems, PODC 2024 brief announcement; extended version HAL hal-04571653v1 (numbering used here).

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 9 is false (lemma9_false, 🛠️). It is not the main result: the title and abstract claim Theorem 1, which is true.

    • The counterexample satisfies all of the paper's hypotheses. The abstraction chainedBO k is a set of executions satisfying the four BC properties (plus unique messages and well-formed invocations), defined "by a predicate on the relative order of emission and delivery events", which is exactly the class the paper says is compositional. It is compositional and content-neutral (chainedBO_compositional, chainedBO_contentNeutral).

    • The abstraction is not degenerate: it contains every uniform total-order execution (totalOrder_mem), so it is implementable from consensus, and the algorithm has admissible executions (soloRun_admissible).

    • The algorithm algQ is deterministic, well-formed (algQ_wellFormed), uses no point-to-point channel and proposes from ℕ. The counterexample works for every k, in particular k > 1, with k + 1 processes as in the lemma.

    • There is no degenerate case, and no implicit assumption of the paper rules it out. Definition 5 explicitly constrains only deliveries. The ordering predicate of chainedBO ("the sender had delivered one of its own messages before broadcasting") is of the same kind as causal broadcast's, which the paper lists among its examples; the abstraction never constrains invocations (it contains every total-order execution).

    • Lemma 9 might be rescued by strengthening Definition 5 to require that each pᵢ's selected messages are sync-broadcast one after another (the shape Lemma 10's β has; chainedBO admits no such execution for N ≥ 2) and by making A' use sync-broadcast. This is not a local fix: it changes a definition, needs a proof step the paper does not have, and the "δ is finite" gap (item 3) remains.

  2. Theorem 1 is true; its proof is repaired (🛠️). The paper derives it from the false Lemma 9. We prove it with a different adversary (README, item on Lemma 9), and in a stronger form: compositionality alone suffices (theorem1); theorem1_paper is the paper's form with content-neutrality as an (unused) hypothesis.

Proof-level differences

  1. Lemma 9's proof:

    • It fails at "αᵢ is indistinguishable from δ" (README).

    • δ is finite, and A' must be run "on δ" in an admissible execution in which A' may keep broadcasting after deciding; the paper does not address this.

    • The claim that A can be replaced by a broadcast-only A' ("send and receive can be trivially emulated using B") is unjustified: SR-Termination holds for crashed senders, BC-Global-CS-Termination does not. A claim inside a proof (⚠️); our model restricts A to broadcast-only algorithms anyway (item 8).

  2. Our adversary differs from Algorithm 1.

    • The upper layer runs copies of A, not sync-broadcast(synch). The phase of pᵢ ends when its current copy decides, not after N local deliveries.

    • A reset of p_k discards its current copy. A reset happens only while that copy is undecided, and the phase stops at its decision.

    • The messages p_k sent to p_{k+1} before its last reset are received by p_{k+1} at the beginning of its run, not at the reset times.

    • All other messages to p_{k+1} are received after p_{k+1}'s copy decided. The other processes crash at the end of their phase, so the execution is infinite and admissible; the paper's α is a finite prefix.

  3. Footnote 1 (SR-Termination for γ) is not a gap of the paper. Lemma 10 only needs γ as a prefix of an admissible execution (safety), and Lemma 7 proves SR-Termination in the case where it is needed. The extension of a finite γ to an admissible execution is left implicit by the paper; our counterparts (solo, gZ) are infinite admissible executions.

  4. Lemmas 1–8 and 10 are not formalized for the paper's own adversary. Their content is reproved for our adversary: admissibility of concatenations of runs (catRun_adm), safety facts (seg_deliver_own, MsS_inv_late), termination of the phases (early_copy, kk_copy, z_copy via fin_copy).

  5. Reduction from n to k + 1 processes ("CAMP_n[∅] with n − k − 1 initial crashes is functionally identical to CAMP_{k+1}[∅]"): not used. Our Theorem 1 is stated for Fin n directly; processes k + 1, …, n − 1 never take a step.

Modelling choices

  1. A uses only the broadcast abstraction (no point-to-point channels), as the paper's A' does. The paper's CAMP_n[B] also has channels. This weakens our Theorem 1 (theorem1, theorem1_paper) below the paper's claim: they exclude equivalences witnessed by a broadcast-only A, not by an A that also uses channels (the paper's reduction to broadcast-only A' is unjustified, item 3). Allowing them in A should not change the result: their messages can be held back until after the decisions and then simulated by the upper layer of p_{k+1}. This is not formalized.

  2. Messages are objects (id, content). Algorithms choose contents; identifiers are fresh. This is the paper's "each broadcast message is unique, regardless of having identical content". Content-neutrality renames message objects injectively, per sender (Definition 3's "injective functions r on the set of messages", applied to deliveries "from pᵢ").

  3. Algorithms are deterministic functions of local histories. For A, the history is its invocations, returns, deliveries and decisions. For B, it is its own actions, the invocations, the receptions and the k-SA answers (Definition 1 and "deterministic reduction algorithms"). The upper layer of CAMP_n[k-SA] may take internal steps (up), invisible to B.

  4. Hypotheses made explicit in Theorem 1:

    • A is well-formed (never invokes while an invocation is pending; Definition 1).

    • The proposal domain has at least k + 1 values. Otherwise k-SA is trivial and the theorem is false.

    • B proposes at most once to each k-SA object (the standard assumption of Section 4.1).

    • EquivalentToKSA packages "some A solves k-SA on top of S" and "some B implements S in CAMP_n[k-SA]".

  5. Executions are maps ℕ → Option step (stuttering allowed). An abstraction is a set of such executions (with the set of correct processes). Faulty processes take finitely many steps, in CAMP_n[k-SA] (Adm.crash) and in executions of A on top of S (Admissible.crash; added in review: without it SolvesKSA would also quantify over executions in which a faulty process keeps acting forever, a stronger hypothesis of Theorem 1). We do not impose "at least one correct process" (t = n − 1): this only adds executions, and our constructions always have one correct process.

  6. k-SA validity for A requires the decided value to be the proposal of a process that took a step. This is the paper's notion: a process proposes by invoking propose, which is a step. It makes algQ_solves stronger; in Theorem 1 it is part of the hypothesis "A solves k-SA" and does not weaken the theorem relative to the paper.

  7. N-solo (Definition 5) is formalized literally: N distinct messages broadcast by each process, each process delivering all its own selected messages before any selected message of another process.

  8. Non-vacuity: no implementation B of an abstraction is exhibited in our CAMP_n[k-SA] model. Theorem 1 is a non-existence result; its proof does not use vacuity.

  9. Brief announcement vs extended version: the BA states only the theorem and sketches the adversary ("it is easy to see that sufficiently large values of N inhibit A"); its slip "k = n … equivalent to Reliable Broadcast" (the extended version says Send-To-All) is irrelevant to the results.
