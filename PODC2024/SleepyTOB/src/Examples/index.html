import Papers.PODC2024.SleepyTOB.ExtendedGA /-! # Small examples: the literal integrity property, and why graded consistency alone does not lock D'Amato, Losa, Zanolini, PODC 2024. * `integrity_literal_false`: Definition 4 states integrity as "if a well-behaved process outputs a log with any grade, then there exists a well-behaved process that *inputs the log*". Taken literally this fails for every graded agreement with validity: a single honest process with input `[1]` outputs `[]` with grade 1. The intended property (as in [MMR] and in the proof of Lemma 1) is that some honest input *extends* the log; that is what `lemma_1` proves. * `graded_consistency_insufficient`: the proof of Lemma 3 derives the base case "all processes in `H_r` vote for an extension of the decided `Λ`" from graded consistency alone. Graded consistency only gives the other honest processes `Λ` with grade `≥ 0`, and bounded divergence allows them a second, conflicting and *longer* grade-0 output, which becomes their `L_{v-1}`. Below: 7 honest processes (5 voting `[1]`, 2 voting `[2,5]`) and 2 Byzantine processes that vote `[1]` towards `p` and `[2,5]` towards `q`; `p` outputs `[1]` with grade 1 while `q`'s longest output is `[2,5]`. The repair (`Exec.Follows.decision_lock`) uses integrity of `GA_{v-1,2}` and uniqueness of `GA_{v-1,1}`, as in [MMR]'s safety proof. -/ namespace Podc.SleepyTOB open Finset noncomputable section open
