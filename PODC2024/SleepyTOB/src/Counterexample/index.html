import Papers.PODC2024.SleepyTOB.Resilience /-! # Algorithm 1 as printed is unsafe; non-vacuity of Theorem 2 D'Amato, Losa, Zanolini, PODC 2024, Section 3.1, Algorithm 1, lines 6–7: "Start `GA_{v,1}` with a log in the propose message with the largest valid `VRF(v)` *not conflicting with* `L_{v-1}`". Safety needs the [MMR]-style lock rule (Algorithm 1 is said to recall [MMR]'s protocol): the proposal must *extend* `L_{v-1}`, and otherwise the process inputs `L_{v-1}` itself (the printed algorithm has no such fallback). With the printed rule (`FollowsLiteral`) a Byzantine proposer that wins the VRF lottery can propose a strict prefix of the decided log (e.g. the genesis log); all honest processes then input it, the lock is lost, and a conflicting log is decided two views later — in a completely synchronous execution with one Byzantine process out of four. This refutes Lemma 3, Theorem 1 (safety) and Theorem 2 for the printed algorithm (`algorithm_1_literal_unsafe`). The execution (`P = Fin 4`, process 3 Byzantine and silent, all processes always awake, all messages delivered at once, `η = 2`, `ra = 3`, `π = 1`): every honest process votes * `[1]` in rounds 1, 2: round 2 is the longest grade-1 output of round 1; * `[]` in rounds 3, 4: in round 3 (view 2) the processes decide `[1]`, `L_1 = [1]`, and the winning proposal is the Byzantine `[]`, which does not conflict with `[1]`; * `[2]` from round 5 on: in round 5 (view 3) `L_2 = []` and the winning proposal is an honest proposal `b || C_2 = [2]` (`C_2 = []` is the longest output of `GA_{2,1}`), which is then decided in round 7.
