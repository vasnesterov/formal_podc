import Papers.PODC2024.HistoryIndependent.UniversalFlat /-! # Theorem 32: wait-freedom of the flattened implementation The atomic potential `mu p` of `UniversalWF` measures the progress of process `p`'s operation at the level of Algorithm 5. In the flattened implementation a step of `p` may be a *stutter* (a `Read`, a failed `CAS`, or a `Load` of a right-hand side of `∥` that does not end it), which does not change `mu p`. Every failed `CAS` of `p` on a CAS object `X` is caused by a change of `X` by another process since `p`'s last `Read`; the paper's Lemmas 29–31, 41 and Claim 33 bound the number of such changes. We replace them by explicit potentials: * `G2`: the sum over processes of their atomic potentials (plus a bonus for a linearized operation that can still complete). It never increases at steps that are not *transitions* (successful `SC`s on `head`), and strictly decreases at every change of the memory that is not a transition (Lemmas 29/31: without mode transitions only finitely many context changes happen). * `Sj j`: the "stragglers" of `announce[j]` (processes about to `LL(announce[j])` after reading an old `head`) and its context; together with `G2` it bounds the changes of `announce[j]` that do not reset its context (Lemma 41). * `psi`: the local progress of `p` inside an Algorithm 6 operation.
