Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Swiper — PODC 2024.

Paper: Tonkikh, Freitas, Swiper: a new paradigm for efficient weighted distributed protocols, PODC 2024 / arXiv 2307.15561v2.

Errors in the paper

E1. Theorem 2.4 (Weight Separation) is false; its proof in Appendix A.2 is wrong; Swiper violates the bound

The theorem is false (ws_upper_bound_false, WeightSeparation.lean): α = 1/20, β = 1/10, parties 1..8 of weight 14 and 9..21 of weight 1 (W = 125, αW = 6.25, βW = 12.5). Light sets contain at most 6 weight-1 parties; all 13 weight-1 parties form a heavy set; each weight-14 party alone is heavy. If fewer than 6 weight-1 parties hold tickets, they (light) and all weight-1 parties (heavy) have equally many tickets — violation. Otherwise 6 ticketed weight-1 parties have ≥ 6 tickets, so each weight-14 party needs ≥ 7 and the weight-1 parties together ≥ 7: T ≥ 63 > 59.85 = (α+β)(1−α)/(β−α)·21. Caveat: this refutation relies on S₁ and S₂ being allowed to overlap (it compares a subset of the weight-1 parties with all of them). The paper's literal definition of Problem 3 allows overlap, so the theorem is false as stated; under a disjoint-sets reading this instance would not be a counterexample. The 3-party instance below (swiper_ws_family_exceeds_bound, with disjoint S₁ = ∅, S₂) refutes the paper's proof and Swiper's "Robustness" claim under either reading. (The optimum is exactly 63, found numerically.) The numerics subagent found more instances, e.g. (α,β) = (1/10,1/5), n = 22: OPT 60 > 59.4, and (14/15, 29/30) (α+β > 1), n = 27: OPT 104 > 102.6; for the family "h heavy parties of weight > βW plus g unit parties" OPT = h(a+1) + a + 1 + g − b (a = ⌈αW⌉−1, b = ⌊βW⌋+1), with OPT/n → α/β² asymptotically, which exceeds (α+β)(1−α)/(β−α) e.g. for small α, β = 2α (1/(4α) vs ≈ 3). No violation was found for (α,β) = (1/4,1/3) (best ratio OPT/bound 0.74).

Appendix A.2 uses t_i = ⌊w_i s + γ⌋, γ = (α+β)/2. Case 1 (t(S₁) ≥ γT) is correct and gives s < γ(1−γ)n/((γ−α)W). Case 2 (t(S₂) < γT) is claimed to follow "analogously, by substitution of 1−γ in place of γ and 1−β in place of α". This substitution would also require replacing the rounding offset γ by 1−γ, which is not done; it is valid only when γ = 1/2. Redoing case 2 gives s(β−γ)W < γ² n + (1−2γ)|S₂| ≤ max(γ², (1−γ)²) n, not γ(1−γ) n.

The statement about Swiper is false, not only the proof (WeightSeparation.lean):

  • swiper_ws_counterexample: w = (1, 1, 15), α = 1/20, β = 1/10, n = 3. The paper's s = 2γ(1−γ)n/((β−α)W) = 333/680 gives t = (0, 0, 7), and S₁ = ∅, S₂ = {1, 2} (parties 1 and 2 in the paper's 1-based numbering, weight 2 > 1.7) violates WS.

  • swiper_ws_family_exceeds_bound: every WS-valid t in the family with offset γ (w_i s + γ − 1 ≤ t_i ≤ w_i s + γ, i.e. all t(s, k) of §3.1) has T ≥ 14, while the claimed bound is (α+β)(1−α)/(β−α)·n = 8.55. Hence Swiper's WS output (a local minimum of that family) exceeds the bound, contradicting the "Robustness" claim of §3 for WS.

  • counterexample_optimum: t = (1, 0, 1) is a WS solution (T = 2), so on this small instance only Swiper's construction fails; the theorem itself is refuted by the 21-party instance above.

What we prove instead: ws_upper_bound, T ≤ ⌈(1 − d²)/(2d) · n⌉ with d = β − α, using the same family with offset (1−d)/2 and a direct analysis of the pair (S₁ \ S₂, S₂ \ S₁) (isWS_of_rounds: every member with T ≥ (1−d²)/(2d)·n is valid). This bound is symmetric under (α, β) ↦ (1−β, 1−α) (as it must be, isWS_iff_compl), beats the paper's bound when α+β is close to 1 (e.g. α=0.3, β=0.7: 1.05n vs 1.75n), and is worse when α+β is small (e.g. α=0.05, β=0.1: 9.98n vs 2.85n). Note also that the paper's bound is not symmetric under the complementation symmetry, so for α + β > 1 it is weaker than its own mirror image (2−α−β)β/(β−α)·n.

Minor: Theorem 2.4 is stated without ceiling (T ≤ (α+β)(1−α)/(β−α)·n), consistent with its fixed-s proof; our corrected statement has a ceiling.

E2. Gaps in Appendix A.1 (filled, result correct)

  • "Since we already proved that viable values of s exist" — no such proof appears in the paper. We prove it (exists_viable) from the key computation: T(t_s) ≥ sW − (1−αw)n → ∞, and every member of the family with T ≥ αw(1−αw)/(αn−αw)·n is viable (isWR_of_rounds).

  • "it is easy to see that such s* exists" — needs a discreteness argument. We take s* = inf {s > 0 | t_s viable}, show s* ≥ (1−αw)/W > 0 (for smaller s, t_s = 0) and that the infimum is attained because s ↦ t_s is right-continuous and integer-valued (exists_locallyMinimal). The global minimum is in particular locally minimal.

  • "there must be some j such that s* w_j + αw is an integer": proved (border_nonempty), via footnote 6 (ts_sub_eventually_eq), which needs 0 < αw < 1 (if αw were an integer, zero-weight parties would be on the border and t' would need negative tickets).

  • The removal process ("taking tickets from parties in J as long as the assignment stays viable") is modelled by an arbitrary K ⊆ J with t* − 1_K viable and t* − 1_{K∪{j}} not viable; removal_bound holds for every such stopping point, exists_removal shows one exists.

Differences in statements / modelling

  • Weights and thresholds are real numbers; tickets are natural numbers. The paper's ti ∈ Z≥0 is ℕ.

  • W ≠ 0 and w_i ≥ 0 are explicit hypotheses (as in the problem statements).

  • Theorem 2.1 is stated as the existence of a viable assignment (T ≠ 0 and the WR constraint), as in Appendix A.1. For W > 0 and αw > 0, T ≠ 0 is implied by the WR constraint (IsWR.sum_ne_zero, take S = ∅).

  • Our main proof of Thm 2.1 (wr_upper_bound) does not use the locally minimal s*: it takes the member of the family with exactly T = ⌈bound⌉ tickets (exists by Cslib.Apportionment.exists_rounds_sum_eq) — this is the "bootstrapping" remark of §3.1. The paper's route is formalized separately (wr_upper_bound_swiper).

  • The key lemma isWR_of_rounds does not need w_i ≥ 0 nor αn < 1, and uses the real bound T ≥ αw(1−αw)n/(αn−αw) (the paper's §3.1 phrases it with the ceiling).

  • Theorem 2.2 ("the problems are identical"): formalized as equality of the constraint sets for every t (isWQ_iff_isWR); no hypotheses on βw, βn or the weights are needed.

  • Theorem 4.2: only its combinatorial core is formalized (isBlunt_of_isWR). Definition 4.1's second conjunct ∃A ∈ A : A ∩ F = ∅ has F unbound; we read it as ∀ F ∈ F, ∃ A ∈ A, A ∩ F = ∅. Thm 4.2's proof correctly uses "(1−αn)T ≥ αn T". The §4.1 random-beacon example writes "(1−αn)T ≥ ⌈αn T⌉", which is false in general (αn = 1/2, T = 3). The slip is harmless there: honest parties hold an integer number of tickets > (1−αn)T ≥ αn T, hence ≥ ⌈αn T⌉. Qualified sets are those with t(P) ≥ ⌈αn T⌉ (ticketAccess).

Edge cases

  • αw = 0 (an edge-case note, not a paper error: the paper requires αw ∈ (0,1)): the WR constraint is vacuous, t = 0 solves Problem 1 with T = 0 = ⌈0⌉, but no viable assignment satisfies T ≤ ⌈0⌉. So the open interval is needed for the appendix formulation.

  • n = 1, zero weights: covered by the theorems (only W ≠ 0 is needed).

  • Ceilings in Thm 2.1/Cor 2.3 are exactly right for the method: the family attains every total, and the key lemma gives validity at T = ⌈bound⌉.

Not formalized

  • Sections 4–6 protocol transformations (cryptographic black boxes: secret sharing, threshold signatures/encryption, erasure codes, VBA, SSLE) and the overhead table (Table 1).

  • §3.1 algorithmic claims: Knapsack-based validity check in O(Tn) (dynamic programming by profits), quasi-linear upper/lower Knapsack bounds, --linear mode, binary search. (Validity of an assignment is trivially decidable by enumerating subsets; we did not formalize the DP.)

  • Section 7 and Appendices B (MIP) and C (experiments).

Numerical experiments

Done by a helper subagent with exact arithmetic (scripts were not kept in the repo; exhaustive search for n ≤ 8–22 on structured families, branch and bound over weight classes, cross-checked).

  • Thm 2.4: counterexamples as listed in E1. With offset (1−d)/2 Swiper-style chains reach the optimum on the counterexamples (60, 63); with the paper's offset γ the first valid member had T = 92 resp. 111.

  • Thm 2.1 is not tight in the worst case on small instances: max OPT/n found for n ≤ 10 was 1.0 for (1/4,1/3) (bound 2.25n), 0.9 for (1/3,1/2) (1.33n), 1.7 for (1/3,3/8) (5.33n); OPT = ⌈bound⌉ only when ⌈bound⌉ = 1. The paper's open question about lower bounds remains open.

  • The key lemma isWR_of_rounds is tight: there are invalid members of the αw-offset family with T = ⌈bound⌉ − 1, e.g. (αw,αn) = (1/3,1/2), w = (5,2), t = (1,1) (T = 2, bound 2.67), and (1/4,1/3), w = (19,3,3), t = (4,1,1) (T = 6, bound 6.75). So the analysis of Swiper's construction cannot be improved, although the optimum can be far smaller.

  • n = 1: WR optimum is 1 while the real bound can be below 1, so the ceiling in Thm 2.1 is needed.
