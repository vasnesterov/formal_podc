Miguel Alves Ferreira, Nirav Atre, Justine Sherry, João Luís Sobrinho. Impossibility Results for Data-Center Routing with Congestion Control and Unsplittable Flows. PODC 2024. DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662777. Formalized version: the PODC proceedings version (no arXiv version); numbering follows it.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Theorems 3.4, 4.2 and 4.3, the upper bound of Theorem 5.4 and all lemmas are correct, and the proofs that the paper omits as "routine" (Lemma 2.2, Lemma 3.2, Lemma 4.4, Lemma 4.6, Lemma 5.2) are also proved here. The tightness part of Theorem 5.4 is proved only for odd n: the paper reduces to odd n "without loss of generality", but for even n its construction gives only ε → 1/(n-2), which still shows that the bound becomes tight as n grows. The other gaps close locally, including the wrong rates that the proof of Theorem 5.4 computes for n = 3.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 5.4, second part (the "w.l.o.g. n odd" step)

The tightness proof stacks (n-1)/2 copies of the gadget of Example 3.3 plus one extra middle "doom" switch, which needs n odd, and nothing reduces even n to odd n; for even n the same idea gives only ε > 1/(n-2).

Impact. We prove the claim for odd n and the weaker bound ε > 1/(n-2) for even n ≥ 4 (theorem_5_4_tight_odd, theorem_5_4_tight_even). Whether the claim holds for even n is open. The paper's message ("the bound becomes tight as n grows") is unaffected.

Details

The claim is: for every n ≥ 3 and every ε > 1/(n-1), some collection of flows has T^{T-MmF} ≥ 2(1-ε) T^MmF. For even n only (n-2)/2 gadgets fit. Other gadget shapes do not seem to help. By an informal count (not formalized): take L ≤ n-1 type-1 flows on I_1/O_1 forming chains of length ≥ 2, consecutive flows of a chain joined by a bundle of k type-2 flows, B bundles in all, every bundle on the doom switch. Then the Doom-Switch throughput is L(1 - 1/B) + 1, while T^MmF → B ≥ L/2. For even n the best ratio is max(2 - 2/(n-2), 2 - 4/n + 4/n²), still below 2(1 - 1/(n-1)). (For n = 4 a single chain of three type-1 flows gives ratio → 5/4, i.e. ε → 3/8. This beats the formalized 1/(n-2) = 1/2 but not the paper's 1/3.)

✏️ Slips

  • In the routing of Lemma 4.6, the type-2.b flow is written (s_i^1, t_i^j). It should be (s_i^1, t_{n+1}^j).

Typos

  • Definitions 2.4/2.5 speak of "the" max-min fair allocation of every routing. Existence (water-filling) is assumed; we prove uniqueness, and our statements do not need existence.

  • The proof of Theorem 5.4 writes T^{T-MmF} ≤ T^{T-MT} = T^MT. Only ≤ is needed, so Lemma 5.2 is not used for the bound.

  • In (R1), "max-main" should be "max-min".

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Proof of Theorem 5.4, n = 3 · Lemma 4.6, Step 2

🔧 Proof of Theorem 5.4, n = 3 (Doom-Switch rates)

The proof says that under the Doom-Switch routing the type-1 flows get 1 - 2/(n-1) and the type-2 flows get 2/(k(n-1)), but for n = 3 (type-1 rate 0, type-2 rate 1/k) this allocation is not max-min fair (doom_paper_rates_not_isMaxMinFair).

Impact. The statement for n = 3 is still true, trivially, because then 2(1-ε) < 1. The rates are correct for odd n ≥ 5, where k(n-3) ≥ 2 (doom_clos_isMaxMinFair).

Details

The type-1 flows have no bottleneck, and giving every flow 1/(k+1) is feasible for the same routing and lexicographically larger.

🔧 Lemma 4.6, Step 2 (lexicographic principle)

Step 2 argues that every routing that raises some rate must lower "a flow of equal (or no greater) rate" and concludes a*↑ ⪰ a_r↑, but never states which lexicographic principle makes this inference valid, especially when several rates change at once.

Impact. Statement true; repaired, and Step 2 shows more than the paper claims (sortedRates_lt_of_forall, aStar_criterion, lemma_4_6).

Repair

We prove this principle in general (the leximin criterion): if a ≠ b and every flow that gains in b is matched by a flow of no larger a-rate that loses in b, then b↑ ≺ a↑. With it, Step 2 shows that a* lex-dominates every allocation that is feasible for any routing, whether max-min fair or not. Hence the lex-max-min fair allocation is unique and equals a*. (sortedRates_lt_of_forall, aStar_criterion, lemma_4_6)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Static rate allocation; there is no dynamics, so synchrony, failures and randomness do not arise. A collection of flows is a finite type F with source and destination servers (Server n = Fin (2n) × Fin n). A routing assigns a middle switch to every flow (unsplittable flows). An allocation is F → ℝ. It is feasible if all rates are non-negative and every link's load is at most its capacity.

  • C_n (clos fl r): n middle switches, 2n input and 2n output switches, n servers per switch, unit capacities. A flow's path is its two server links plus I M_{r f} and M_{r f} O. MS_n (macroSwitch fl): a flow traverses only its two server links. The infinite-capacity links between switches are omitted, since they never constrain an allocation.

  • Max-min fair (IsMaxMinFair): the sorted rate vector (Multiset.sort) is maximum in Mathlib's lexicographic order on List ℝ. Lex-max-min fair and throughput-max-min fair are defined over all routings exactly as in Definitions 2.4/2.5.

  • Statements quantify over every max-min fair, maximum-throughput, lex-max-min fair or throughput-max-min fair allocation, and separately exhibit one. So they do not rely on the (unformalized) general existence of max-min fair allocations. Indices are 0-based.

Omitted "routine" proofs. All statements are true. Lemma 2.2 is cited from [6,28]. Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 5.2 are "folklore", and Lemmas 4.4 and 4.6 (Step 1) are left to the reader. All of them are proved here: Lemma 3.2 via König's theorem in Hall-deficiency form, Lemma 5.2 via König's line-colouring theorem. (isMaxMinFair_iff_hasBottlenecks, lemma_3_2, lemma_5_2, lemma_4_4, aStar_isMaxMinFair)
