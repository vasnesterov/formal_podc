Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Data-Center Routing with Congestion Control — PODC 2024.

Paper: Ferreira, Atre, Sherry, Sobrinho, Impossibility Results for Data-Center Routing with Congestion Control and Unsplittable Flows, PODC 2024 (proceedings version).

Errors and gaps in the paper (see README)

  1. Theorem 5.4, tightness, even n. The proof assumes "w.l.o.g." that n is odd. Nothing in the paper reduces even n to odd n. Proved: odd n ≥ 3 with every ε > 1/(n-1) (theorem_5_4_tight_odd), and even n ≥ 4 with every ε > 1/(n-2) (theorem_5_4_tight_even). The paper's bound for even n remains open.

  2. Proof of Theorem 5.4 for n = 3. The claimed Doom-Switch max-min fair rates (1 - 2/(n-1) = 0 and 2/(k(n-1)) = 1/k) are not max-min fair (doom_paper_rates_not_isMaxMinFair). They are correct for n ≥ 5, i.e. g = (n-1)/2 ≥ 2 gadgets (doom_clos_isMaxMinFair). For n = 3 (and for even n = 4) the tightness statement is proved with a single flow, since then 2(1-ε) < 1.

  3. Lemma 4.6, Step 2. The informal "each increase is paid by a decrease of a flow of equal or smaller rate" is made rigorous by the leximin criterion sortedRates_lt_of_forall. The formal Step 2 (aStar_criterion) is stronger: it compares a* with every allocation feasible for any routing, not only max-min fair ones.

  4. Typo in Lemma 4.6 Step 1. The type-2.b flow is written (s_i^1, t_i^j) instead of (s_i^1, t_{n+1}^j).

  5. Unproved cited/"routine" results, all proved here. Lemma 2.2 (both directions), Lemma 3.2, Lemma 4.4, Lemma 4.6 Step 1 (feasibility of the routing and bottlenecks), Lemma 5.2 and the Section 2.3 remark that Clos-feasible allocations are macro-switch-feasible.

Modelling choices

  • Indices are 0-based. Switch I_i / O_i is i - 1 : Fin (2n); server s_i^j is (i - 1, j - 1) : Fin (2n) × Fin n; middle switch M_m is m - 1 : Fin n. In Section 4, n = k + 1 internally (tor i for i < n, torN k for I_{n+1}), and the final theorems are stated for n.

  • Collections of flows are finite index types F with maps src, dst (multisets of source–destination pairs). A routing is F → Fin n (the middle switch), equivalent to choosing one of the n paths.

  • Macro-switch. The infinite-capacity links between input and output switches are omitted, so a flow's path is its two server links. A link of infinite capacity never constrains feasibility and can never be a bottleneck, so this is equivalent. Both networks share the link type Link n. The macro-switch leaves the inner links unused (load 0).

  • Allocations are F → ℝ. Feasibility includes non-negativity of the rates (the paper says "non-negative rate" in the definition of an allocation).

  • Sorted vector and lexicographic order. sortedRates a = (univ.val.map a).sort and Mathlib's LinearOrder (List ℝ) (lexicographic). All compared vectors have the same length |F|, so this is the paper's order.

  • "The" max-min fair allocation. The paper presupposes existence (water-filling) and uniqueness. We prove uniqueness (IsMaxMinFair.eq) but not general existence. Every theorem either quantifies over all max-min fair allocations, or exhibits one, or both. The same holds for T^MT, T^MmF, a^{L-MmF}, a^{T-MmF}.

  • Definitions 2.4, 2.5, 3.1, 5.1 are IsLexMaxMinFair, IsThroughputMaxMinFair, Network.IsMaxThroughput and IsThroughputMaxThroughput, quantifying over all routings and all max-min fair (resp. maximum-throughput) allocations of these routings.

Statement-by-statement

  • Lemma 2.2 is proved for arbitrary networks with fixed routing and arbitrary real capacities (Network.isMaxMinFair_iff_hasBottlenecks). The "only if" direction needs no hypothesis: a flow with an empty path makes max-min fairness impossible.

  • Theorem 3.4, lower bound (theorem_3_4_lower): T^MmF ≥ t(b)/2 for every max-min fair a and every feasible b, i.e. with T^MT replaced by the throughput of any feasible allocation (stronger). The proof differs from the paper's. It uses the general bound throughput_le_mul_of_isMaxMinFair (every flow has a saturated link, and every macro-switch path has 2 links) instead of Lemma 3.2.

  • Theorem 3.4, tightness (theorem_3_4_tight): for n ≥ 1 and every ε > 0 there are flows (with existing max-min fair and maximum-throughput allocations) such that every max-min fair a and every maximum-throughput b satisfy t(a) ≤ (1+ε) t(b)/2. The explicit values T^MmF = 1 + 1/(k+1), T^MT = 2 are gadget_throughputs.

  • Lemma 3.2 (lemma_3_2): the 0/1 allocation of every maximum matching is maximum-throughput. Matchings of G^MS are sets of flows with pairwise distinct sources and destinations (IsMatching).

  • Example 4.1 / Theorem 4.2. example_4_1 generalizes Example 4.1 to every n ≥ 3: no routing makes the macro-switch max-min fair rates feasible. theorem_4_2 states a↑ ≺ a^MmF↑ for the max-min fair allocation a of every routing, which needs no existence of an L-MmF allocation. theorem_4_2' uses the flows of Theorem 4.3 and also exhibits an L-MmF allocation.

  • Lemma 4.4 (lemma_4_4) is proved for any number p ≥ 1 of copies of the type-1 flows: p = n+1 in the paper, p = 1 for Example 4.1.

  • Claim 4.5 (claim_4_5). Instead of "the collection consisting only of type 1 and 2 flows", the type-3 flow is present with an arbitrary non-negative rate (rate 0 gives the paper's setting). Part (1) is stated with the paper's counts x_i^m, y_i^m (cnt1, cnt2); part (2) counts the type-2.b flows per middle switch. The coprimality of p and n (used in the paper as lcm(n, n+1) = n(n+1)) is a hypothesis of the general version. The core lemma t3_le needs only lower bounds on the type-1/2 rates.

  • Lemma 4.6 (lemma_4_6). The routing of Step 1 sends the type-1 flows of server j+2 of I_{i+1} through M_{(i.succAbove j)+1}, i.e. through all middle switches except M_{i+1}, one per server. This is equivalent to the paper's M_{((i+j-2) mod n)+1}, which also avoids M_i exactly once. The type-3 flow uses M_n as in the paper. Additionally proved: every lex-max-min fair allocation equals a*.

  • Theorem 4.3 (theorem_4_3): existence of an L-MmF allocation, and a(f) = a^MmF(f)/n for the type-3 flow f and every L-MmF allocation a.

  • Lemma 5.2 (lemma_5_2): there are a maximum-throughput allocation of MS_n and a routing for which it is a throughput-maximum-throughput allocation of C_n. The link-disjoint routing uses CSLib's König line-colouring (Matrix.exists_split_parts) on the input/output-switch matrix of a maximum matching.

  • Theorem 5.4, upper bound (theorem_5_4_upper): t(a) ≤ 2 T^MmF for the max-min fair allocation a of every routing. This is stronger than for a^{T-MmF}, and Lemma 5.2 is not needed.

  • Theorem 5.4, tightness. The statement gives a routing r with max-min fair allocation a such that t(a) ≥ 2(1-ε) T^MmF, and the same bound for every throughput-max-min fair allocation. The construction stacks g gadgets on I_1/O_1 with q type-2 flows each. The type-2 flows go (s_1^{2p+1}, t_1^{2p}) (0-based), as in the paper's (s_1^j, t_1^{j-1}), j even. The routing (doomRouting) is the output of Algorithm 1 on this input: the type-1 flows form the maximum matching, each on its own middle switch; the type-2 flows all use middle switch 2g (0-based). The algorithm itself (maximum matching, colouring, choice of m') is not formalized in general.

  • Example 5.3 (example_5_3): n = 7, three gadgets, one type-2 flow each; 9/2 → 5.

Not formalized

  • Example 2.3 (an illustration of bottleneck transfer).

  • Algorithm 1 as a general algorithm.

  • General existence of max-min fair allocations.

  • The informal Theorems 1.1–1.3 (they are the formal Theorems 3.4, 4.3 and 5.4).

  • The discussion in Sections 6–7.
