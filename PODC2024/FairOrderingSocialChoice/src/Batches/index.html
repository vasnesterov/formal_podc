import Papers.PODC2024.FairOrderingSocialChoice.Defs /-! # Lemma 3.6: recovering batches from a minimal-batch-order-fair ordering Lemma 3.6 of [Ramseyer–Goel] claims that the strongly connected components of the ordering graph thresholded at `γ - δ` behave like "batches" of any `(γ, δ)`-minimal-batch-order-fair output: 1. if `γ n` replicas received `τ ≺ τ'`, then either `τ, τ'` are in the same component, or *all* transactions of the component of `τ` are output before *any* transaction of the component of `τ'`; 2. if moreover there is no chain `τ' = τ₁, …, τₖ = τ` of pairs each received in order by `(γ - 2δ) n` replicas, then all of the component of `τ` is output before all of the component of `τ'`. Both parts are false (`lemma_3_6_false`): Definition 3.5 only constrains the relative order of the endpoints of a `γ`-edge, not of the other members of their components. Counterexample with `n = 6` replicas, transactions `a b c d` (`Fin 4`), `γ = 2/3` (threshold: 4 votes), `δ = 0`: | replica | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | |---------|---|---|---|---|---|---| | order | abcd | adbc | bcad | dbca | cabd | dcab | Counts: `a≺b`, `b≺c`, `c≺a`, `a≺d`: 4 each; `b≺d`, `c≺d`: 3. The `γ`-graph is the triangle `a → b → c → a` plus `a → d`, so its components are `{a, b, c}` and `{d}`. The output `a, d, b, c` reverses only `c → a`, and outputs the chain `a → b → c` in order, so it is exactly `γ`-minimal; but `d` (component of `d`) is output before `b` (component of `a`) although `a → d` is a `γ`-edge between different components, and `d` has no out-edge at all. What *is* true (`lemma_3_6_pairwise`): the endpoints of a `γ`-edge `τ → τ'` lying in different components of the `(γ - 2δ)`-graph are output in order `τ` before `τ'`. -/ namespace
