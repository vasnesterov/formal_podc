Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Read-Write Wait-Free Coverings in the Fully-Anonymous Model — PODC 2024.

Paper: G. Losa, E. Gafni, Understanding Read-Write Wait-Free Coverings in the Fully-Anonymous Shared-Memory Model, PODC 2024 (brief announcement); formalized from the full version arXiv:2405.03573v2. Numbering follows the arXiv version.

Reading note. Lemmas 4.5–4.7 and the proof of Theorem 4.8 use the complement Ā (an overline), which plain-text extraction of the PDF drops. Read without the overlines, Lemma 4.5 and Lemma 4.6 look false and Lemma 4.7 looks vacuous. On the rendered page (p. 8–9) all three are as stated below, and true with N ≤ |R|.

Errors in the paper (statement level)

  1. Lemmas 4.6, 4.7 and Theorem 4.8 need at least N registers. 🔧

    • With 2 registers and 4 processors with inputs 0, 0, 1, 1, the two groups take turns covering both registers and scanning. Each processor reads only its own group, so the stable views {0}, {1} are incomparable (theorem_4_8_needs_registers').

    • With one register and two processors alternating write; scan, the same happens (theorem_4_8_needs_registers).

    • The same execution refutes Lemma 4.6 for A = {0}: at most 1 = |A| register is last written by Ā = {1}, Ā is live, and Ā never reads from A (lemma_4_6_needs_registers). Lemma 4.7 then fails as well, since A is closed.

    • Section 2 fixes an arbitrary number M > 0 of registers, and Section 4 never restricts it. Section 4 says its pathological executions arise "regardless of the number of registers". Its running example (§4.1, the 5-processor extension of Figure 2, reused in §5.1 to reason about stable views) has 5 processors and 3 registers.

    • On the other hand, the proof of Lemma 4.6 counts N registers. Section 2.1 shows that fewer than N registers are useless for wait-free synchronization, and Section 5 uses N registers.

    • The N = 4, M = 2 instance is not degenerate.

    • An exhaustive state-space search over Figure 1 finds no two-source execution for (N, M) = (2, 2) or (3, 2). In general, Exec.false_of_two_closed shows that the two groups with minimal views must together have more than M members.

    • Section 4 is labelled a warm-up.

    • We prove Lemmas 4.5–4.7 as printed (Exec.lemma_4_5; Exec.lemma_4_6, Exec.lemma_4_7 with N ≤ |R|) and Theorem 4.8 with N ≤ |R|.

  2. Lemma 5.2 is false as printed. 🛠️ The lemma itself is not formalized.

    • Lemma 5.2 says: if W is durably stored despite P at t, every processor that takes a step after t and then terminates has W ⊆ view.

    • Counterexample with N = 2: q runs solo to level 2 with view {1} and stops at label write. Then p (input 2) writes {1,2} into both registers, so W = {1,2} is durable (|R_W| = 2 > |P \ Q_W| = 1). q then takes its write/output steps and terminates with W ⊄ view[q].

    • The intended statement concerns scans that start after t. It is true, and our durable_scan proves it for a stronger durability notion.

    • The paper's derivation of safety from Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3 needs the printed version: a final scan of q may still be in progress when p terminates.

    • Our model merges the output step into the last read of the scan, so this counterexample is not expressible in it. 2b. Typos not affecting the formalization. Definition 3.2 reads "either o[i] ⊆ o[j] or o[i] ⊆ o[j]"; the second disjunct should be o[j] ⊆ o[i]. The first sentence of §5.1 says "Figure 3" where Figure 1 is meant.

Gaps in proofs (statements true)

  1. Lemma 4.5 is true as printed (Exec.lemma_4_5). We prove the stronger bound for registers not last written by A, which also counts registers never written (Exec.card_notWrittenBy_le).

  2. Lemma 4.6's proof ignores registers that still hold their initial value. In case |R_t^Ā| < |A|, the register p reads outside R_t^Ā ∪ (written by Ā) might be unwritten. The fix is to start the argument after every register has been written, which happens because Ā is live.

    • Our proof of Lemma 4.6 avoids the paper's case split. Suppose Ā never reads from A. Then Ā eventually reads only from itself, so by Lemma 4.5 at most |Ā| registers are not last written by Ā. With the hypothesis and |R| ≥ N, both counts are then exact.

    • The next write of a live member of Ā into a register not last written by Ā breaks the count (Exec.false_of_card_notWrittenBy_eq).

  3. Theorem 4.8's proof is wrong.

    • It claims, "by the contrapositive of Lemma 4.4", that live processors with a view in X (the views reachable from a source S) read only from P_X. Lemma 4.4 gives this for X̄ instead. A processor with view V₂ ∈ X̄ reading from a view V₁ ∈ X would have S ⊆ V₁ ⊆ V₂. A processor with a view in X, however, may read a smaller view in X̄.

    • Applied to the closed set P_X̄, Lemma 4.7 only says that some member of P_X reads from P_X̄, which is no contradiction.

    • Our proof takes two distinct minimal stable views S₁ ≠ S₂. After stabilization, the live processors with view Sᵢ read only from each other (readsOnlyFrom_minimal).

    • By Lemma 4.5, at most |Aᵢ| registers are not last written by Aᵢ. With M ≥ |A₁| + |A₂| registers this forces exactly |A₁| registers to belong to A₂ at all times. The first round-robin write of an A₁ member into one of them breaks the count (Exec.false_of_two_closed).

  4. GST (Definition 4.1) is too weak for Lemma 4.4. After the paper's GST, a register may still hold an old, pre-stabilization view of a live processor. The proof of Lemma 4.4 needs the value read to be the writer's stable view.

    • Our exists_gst also waits until every register has been rewritten after the views stabilized. Such a time exists because a live processor writes round-robin.

    • Lemma 4.4 itself has no time restriction in the paper. It is used only after GST, and we prove only that use (readsOnlyFrom_minimal).

  5. Wait-freedom (§5.3.3) invokes Theorem 4.8 for an execution of Figure 3; only the existence of a minimal stable view is needed, and the level-increase argument ("they keep incrementing their minimum level") needs the register contents to be fresh; formalized as snapshot_live_false.

  6. Safety (§5.3.2): the statement is true, the proof does not go through; we give a new proof. Not formalized as counterexamples, but noted:

    • Lemma 5.2 as printed is false (item 2).

    • The deduction of safety from Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3 does not cover two overlapping final scans: a scan of q that is in progress when p terminates may already have read (before they received W) the registers that keep W forever.

    • In the proof of Lemma 5.8, "W durably stored despite Q at the end of q's scan, thus despite Q ∪ {p} at the end of p's scan" assumes the count condition of Definition 5.1 is preserved over time; it is not (a write of a value ⊇ W can turn several "harmless" scanning processors into "threats"). Lemma 5.5's "the result follows" does not bound the threats that did not write during p's scan. Our proof (Safety.lean): call W durable at t if |R_W(t)| + |H_W(t)| > N (registers and processors containing W; Definition 5.1 with Q = P but without the exemption of scanning processors). (i) If W is durable at t₀, every scan starting at or after t₀ ends with a view ⊇ W (durable_scan, first-completing-counterexample counting). (ii) Below a terminated view W there is a chain of nested scans with view W and decreasing levels, each nested scan written by a new processor with view W; with L ≥ N − 1 the chain must reach a scan at whose start W is durable (chain). (iii) For incomparable terminated views W, W' the two durable starts would each have to precede the other. The proof needs |R| ≥ N and L ≥ N − 1, so it also proves footnote 4 ("reaching level N − 1 is sufficient"), which the paper states without proof.

Modelling choices

  • Anonymous memory. The paper fixes a wiring permutation per processor and lets the algorithms choose registers nondeterministically (with (r ∈ R \ written)). We let every write/read name a physical register chosen nondeterministically (subject to the round-robin written set and to reading each register once per scan). This over-approximates every wiring and every deterministic resolution, so all our positive results (Theorem 4.8, wait-freedom) hold for them; the counterexamples use concrete schedules that are realizable with suitable wirings.

  • Atomicity. As in the PlusCal code: a write together with the reset of the read set is one step; each read is one step; the end-of-scan computation is merged into the last read of the scan (the paper has a separate control step). For Figure 3 the output step is merged too: a processor whose level reaches L stops at the write label and afterwards only takes idle steps.

  • written reset. Figures 1 and 3 write written ∪ {r} = P; we read it as = R (typo).

  • Safety is stated for two processors that are terminated (level ≥ L at the write label) at the same time (snapshot_safety) or at different times (snapshot_safety_outputs; a terminated processor's state is frozen). Generalized to |R| ≥ |P| registers and L ≥ N − 1; the paper's setting is snapshot_safety_paper. Validity: own input ∈ view ⊆ inputs (snapshot_view_valid).

  • Executions are infinite sequences of steps; a stopped (terminated) processor can take idle steps so that infinite executions exist when everybody terminates. Live = infinitely many non-idle steps (the paper: infinitely many steps; same for Figure 1). Wait-freedom is stated as: no processor is live; equivalently every processor scheduled infinitely often terminates.

  • Inputs/views are Finset V; groups (Section 3) are inputs.

  • Stable view (Definition 4.2) is "the eventual view of a live processor"; Theorem 4.8 is stated as "some stable view is contained in every stable view", plus the unique-source form.

  • Section 3/6 are formalized at the level of outcomes (participants, groups, outputs), not of executions: renaming_of_groupSnapshot shows that the Bar-Noy–Dolev names computed from any group solution of snapshot group-solve adaptive renaming with n(n+1)/2 names. The paper's "saving grace" argument (incomparable snapshots reserve all sizes in between) is not needed: two different groups with equal-size snapshots appear together in some output sample, so their snapshots are equal and their ranks differ.

Not formalized

  • Section 2.1 (informal impossibility with N − 1 registers).

  • The paper's own induction (Definition 5.6, Lemmas 5.7–5.8) and the informal argument of §5.3.1.

  • Section 7 (long-lived snapshot, obstruction-free consensus): the paper only sketches it ("clearly", "safety follows like in [4]").

  • The 5-processor extension of the Figure 2 execution (Section 4.1).
