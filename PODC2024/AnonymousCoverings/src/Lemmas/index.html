import Papers.PODC2024.AnonymousCoverings.Stable /-! # Lemmas 4.5–4.7 of the paper, as printed In the rendered paper the three lemmas speak about a set `A` of processors and its complement `Ā` (an overline, which plain-text extraction of the PDF drops): * **Lemma 4.5.** If `A` is nonempty and live and, after `t`, every read of a member of `A` reads a register last written by a member of `A`, then at all times after `t` at most `|A|` registers are last written by `Ā`. * **Lemma 4.6.** If at all times after `t` at most `|A|` registers are last written by `Ā`, and `Ā` is live, then some member of `Ā` eventually reads from `A`. * **Lemma 4.7.** Under the hypotheses of Lemma 4.5, if `Ā` is live, then some member of `Ā` eventually reads from `A`. All three hold for every write–scan algorithm of `Model.lean`. Lemmas 4.6 and 4.7 need at least as many registers as processors (the paper's proof of Lemma 4.6 uses `N` registers); with fewer registers Lemma 4.6 fails (`lemma_4_6_needs_registers` in `Counterexamples.lean`).
