import Papers.PODC2024.AnonymousCoverings.WaitFree /-! # Safety of the snapshot algorithm (Section 5.3) **Theorem** (`snapshot_safety`, Section 5.3.2 of the paper): in every execution of the snapshot algorithm (Figure 3) with `N` processors, at least `N` registers and termination level `L ≥ N - 1`, the views of any two terminated processors are related by containment. The paper's setting (`N` registers, `L = N`) is `snapshot_safety_paper`; `L = N - 1` is the claim of the paper's footnote 4. The paper's induction (Definition 5.1, Lemmas 5.2–5.8) does not go through as printed (see `differencies.md`). We give a different, complete proof. Write `R_W(t)` (`regsW`) for the registers whose view contains `W` at time `t`, `H_W(t)` (`procsW`) for the processors whose view contains `W`, and call `W` *durable* at `t` (`Durable`) if `|R_W(t)| + |H_W(t)| > N`. * **Persistence** (`durable_scan`): if `W` is durable at `t₀`, every scan that *starts* at or after `t₀` ends with a view containing `W`. (First-completing-scan counting argument: each register of `R_W(t₀)` must be overwritten by a distinct processor outside `H_W(t₀)` before that scan reads it.) * **Chain** (`chain`): let `(y, s, c)` be a scan ending with view `W` and level `≥ k + 1`. If `W` is not durable at `s + 1`, some register is rewritten during the scan by a processor `x` with view `W`, and (if `k ≥ 1`) one of these processors performed a scan with view `W` and level `≥ k` entirely inside `(s, c)`; otherwise the last writers would all hold `W` at `s + 1` and be distinct, making `W` durable. Iterating collects distinct processors with view `W`; with `L ≥ N - 1` and one more processor that never has view `W`, the chain must reach a scan with view `W` at whose start `W` is durable. * **Safety**: for incomparable terminated views `W`, `W'` we get scans with views `W`, `W'` at whose starts `W`, resp. `W'`, are durable.
