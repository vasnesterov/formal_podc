Read-Write Wait-Free Coverings in the Fully-Anonymous Model — PODC 2024

Giuliano Losa, Eli Gafni. Understanding Read-Write Wait-Free Coverings in the Fully-Anonymous Shared-Memory Model. PODC 2024 (brief announcement). DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662786. Formalized version: arXiv 2405.03573 v2, the full version with proofs. Numbering follows it.

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The snapshot algorithm, the stable-view theorem and the renaming reduction hold with at least N registers, but Lemmas 4.6–4.7 and Theorem 4.8 are false with fewer, and Section 4 never bounds the number of registers. The proofs of Theorem 4.8 and of snapshot safety (§5.3.2) do not go through, and we prove both with new arguments. Obstruction-free consensus (Section 7) was not checked.

With at least N registers everything checked holds:

  • Lemmas 4.5–4.7 as printed.

  • Theorem 4.8: stable views form a DAG with a unique source.

  • The snapshot algorithm of Figure 3 is safe and wait-free. Level N − 1 (footnote 4) already suffices.

  • The renaming reduction of Section 6 is correct.

Mistakes in the paper

🛠️ Lemma 5.2 (durably stored values) and the derivation of safety

Lemma 5.2 is false as printed: with N = 2, q runs solo to level 2 with view {1}, then p writes {1,2} into both registers, so W = {1,2} is durable, and afterwards q executes its output step, "takes a step and terminates", with W ⊄ view[q].

Impact. Snapshot safety itself is true (see Proof gaps); the intended statement covers scans that start after t. This counterexample is not in Lean: our model merges the output step into the last read.

Details

Without the printed version, the derivation of safety from Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3 misses two final scans that overlap in time.

Repair

Restating the lemma for scans is not enough, so we prove safety by a new chain argument with a stronger durability notion (see Proof gaps). (snapshot_safety_paper, snapshot_safety_outputs, Exec.durable_scan, Exec.chain)

🔧 Lemmas 4.6–4.7 and Theorem 4.8 (fewer registers than processors)

Theorem 4.8 (stable views form a DAG with a unique source) and Lemmas 4.6–4.7 are false with fewer registers than processors: with two registers and four processors with inputs 0, 0, 1, 1, the stable views {0} and {1} are incomparable and the stable-view graph has two sources (theorem_4_8_needs_registers').

Impact. Section 4 is a warm-up for the snapshot algorithm, which uses N registers, so the snapshot and renaming results are unaffected; we prove Lemmas 4.5–4.7 and Theorem 4.8 with N ≤ |R|. The hypothesis M ≥ N is not merely implied, because the paper itself works with fewer registers than processors in Section 4.

Details

In the counterexample the two groups take turns: both members of a group write, one register each, and then both scan. Each processor only ever reads its own group's value.

  • With one register, two processors that alternate write; scan behave the same way (theorem_4_8_needs_registers). This execution also refutes Lemma 4.6 with A = {0}: Ā = {1} is live and covers at most one register, yet never reads from A (lemma_4_6_needs_registers).

  • Exhaustive search finds no such execution with 3 processors and 2 registers. In general, the two groups with minimal views must together have more members than there are registers.

  • Section 2 allows any number M > 0 of registers, and Theorem 4.8 states no bound.

  • Section 4 stresses that its pathology arises "regardless of the number of registers". Its running example (§4.1, reused in §5.1) has 5 processors and 3 registers.

  • The proof of Lemma 4.6 does count N registers ("|R_t^Ā| + |Ā| < N registers"). Section 5 uses N registers.

Repair

Lemmas 4.5–4.7 and Theorem 4.8 with N ≤ |R|. (Exec.lemma_4_5, Exec.lemma_4_6, Exec.lemma_4_7, theorem_4_8)

🔧 Definition 4.1 (GST), used by Lemma 4.4 and §5.3.3

The GST of Definition 4.1 does not require registers to have been rewritten after the views stabilized, so a register may still hold an old view of a live processor, and "reads from a processor that has view V₁" does not give a view containing V₁.

Impact. Lemma 4.4 needs registers to hold the writers' stable views, and the wait-freedom proof tacitly uses fresh register contents after stabilization.

Details

The wait-freedom proof only needs a minimal stable view, not Theorem 4.8.

Repair

One extra clause in Definition 4.1: wait until every register is rewritten after the views stabilized. (Exec.exists_gst, Exec.readsOnlyFrom_minimal, snapshot_live_false)

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 4.6's proof ignores registers that still hold their initial value: the case "|R_t^Ā| < |A|" needs a scan that starts after every register has been written (Exec.lemma_4_6).

  • Figure 1 has a typo: written ∪ {r} = P should be = R. Figure 3 has the same typo.

  • Definition 3.2 reads "either o[i] ⊆ o[j] or o[i] ⊆ o[j]"; the second disjunct should be o[j] ⊆ o[i].

Typos

  • §5.1, first sentence: "the write-scan loop in Figure 3" should refer to Figure 1.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 4.8 · Snapshot safety

🛠️ Theorem 4.8 (unique source)

The proof claims, by Lemma 4.4, that live processors with a stable view in X (the views reachable from a source S) read only from P_X, but Lemma 4.4 gives the opposite inclusion.

Impact. The statement holds with N ≤ |R|; new proof (theorem_4_8, theorem_4_8_unique_source, Exec.false_of_two_closed, Exec.exists_gst).

Details

  • Lemma 4.4 says that processors with a view in X̄ read only from P_X̄. A processor with a view in X may read from a smaller view in X̄.

  • Applying Lemma 4.7 to the closed set P_X̄ yields no contradiction.

  • The GST of Definition 4.1 does not ensure that registers hold the writers' stable views, which Lemma 4.4 needs.

Repair

  • Take two distinct minimal stable views. After a strengthened GST, the processors holding each of them read only from each other.

  • By Lemma 4.5, each group leaves at most its own size of registers to the others. With at least N registers, this pins the partition exactly.

  • The next round-robin write of a group member into the other group's registers then breaks the count.

(theorem_4_8, theorem_4_8_unique_source, Exec.false_of_two_closed, Exec.exists_gst)

🛠️ Snapshot safety (§5.3.2, Lemmas 5.5 and 5.8)

The derivation of safety from Lemmas 5.2 and 5.3 misses two final scans that overlap in time, and Lemma 5.8 assumes that "durably stored" is preserved over time, which it is not.

Impact. Statement true; new proof (snapshot_safety_paper, snapshot_safety).

Details

In Lemma 5.8, a write of a value containing W can turn several harmless scanners into threats. Details in differencies.md, item 8.

Repair

Call W durable when the registers containing W plus the processors containing W number more than N.

  • Persistence: every scan that starts after a durable time ends with a view containing W.

  • Chain: below a terminated view W there is a chain of nested scans with view W. Each scan in the chain is performed by a new processor with view W, so the chain must reach a scan at whose start W is durable.

  • For two incomparable outputs, the two durable starts would each have to precede the other.

This proof needs only level N − 1, so it also proves footnote 4, which the paper states without proof. (snapshot_safety_paper, snapshot_safety, Exec.durable_scan, Exec.chain)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous read/write shared memory: N processors (P) and |R| multi-writer atomic registers. There are no failures besides stopping, and the schedule is arbitrary (adversarial). The model is deterministic and has no randomness.

  • Processor anonymity: all processors run the same program and differ only in their input.

  • Memory anonymity: each write or read names a physical register, chosen nondeterministically. Writes follow the round-robin written set, and each scan reads every register once, in any order. This over-approximates every wiring permutation, so the positive results hold for all wirings. The concrete counterexample schedules are realizable with suitable wirings.

  • Registers carry a ghost "last writer" field, which the algorithm never reads.

  • Executions are infinite. A terminated processor only takes idle steps. Live means taking infinitely many non-idle steps.

  • Atomicity follows the PlusCal code. The end-of-scan computation and the output step are merged into the last read of the scan.

  • Sections 3 and 6 are formalized on outcomes: participants, groups (= inputs), and outputs.

Remark. The "saving grace" argument of Section 6 is correct, but a simpler argument suffices. Two groups with equal-size snapshots appear together in some output sample, so their snapshots are equal and their ranks differ.
