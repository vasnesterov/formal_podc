Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of 3-colouring grids in Online-LOCAL — PODC 2024.

Paper: Chang, Mishra, Nguyen, Yang, Yeh, A Tight Lower Bound for 3-Coloring Grids in the Online-LOCAL Model, PODC 2024.

Overall: the results formalized here (Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, Lemmas 3.3–3.6) are correct. No step fails. The differences below are imprecise statements, implicit assumptions, and changed constants or conventions.

Model

  1. Identifier space. The paper allows identifiers from {1, …, poly(n)}. We use an identifier type ι with exactly n elements, so the input is a bijection pos : ι ≃ V. The adversary is therefore weaker and the lower bounds are stronger. An algorithm for a larger identifier space restricts to one for ours.

  2. Re-embedding is not an adversary primitive. The paper says the adversary "has the liberty to adjust how the current discovered region fits into G". Here the adversary only chooses pos and σ. Re-embedding is derived as an indistinguishability lemma: if two inputs agree on all edges seen from the revealed nodes, the labels agree (label_eq_of_agreeOn, exists_move). This makes precise the requirement the paper leaves implicit: moved parts must stay at distance ≥ 2T+2 from the rest (or touch nothing) and must keep their radius-T neighbourhood away from the grid border. Translating a piece that touches the border would change its view.

  3. Locality T = 0 is allowed. The paper assumes T(n) ≥ 1 ("3-coloring cannot be solved with zero locality"). Our proofs need no such assumption.

  4. Colours. We use Fin 3 = {0,1,2}; colour 2 is the paper's colour 3, and 0, 1 stand for 1, 2. aval x y = x - y for x, y ≠ 2, which is the paper's a-value up to this renaming (1–2 edges have a = ±1, edges touching colour 3 have a = 0).

  5. Grids. We use Mathlib's pathGraph, cycleGraph and box product □, with rows first: grid a b = P_a □ P_b, cylinder a b = P_a □ C_b (each row is a cycle), and torus a b = C_a □ C_b. These match §2.1.

Lemmas 3.3–3.5

  1. Lemma 3.4 is strengthened and proved differently. The paper sums the cells "inside" a simple cycle, which implicitly uses the Jordan curve theorem for grid cycles. We build a potential φ with a(c p, c q) = φ q − φ p on every edge (grid_exists_potential). It is defined along an L-shaped path, and the horizontal edges are checked by induction using Lemma 3.3 on each cell. As a result, every closed walk has b = 0 (grid_bval_closed), not only simple cycles.

  2. Lemma 3.5 is stated for arbitrary walks; the paper states it for paths. The hypothesis that the colouring is proper (along the walk) is explicit. The paper's "Throughout this section, c is any proper 3-coloring" carries it implicitly.

Lemma 3.6

  1. Correctness of the algorithm is a hypothesis. The paper claims the adversary "constructs a directed path with b-value at least k". This is only meaningful if the algorithm colours the revealed segment properly: the parity argument (Lemma 3.5) fails otherwise. We state it as "if the revealed segment is properly coloured, then it contains a row path with |b| ≥ k" (Lemma36). If the colouring is not proper, the final colouring is already wrong.

  2. Orientation. The paper writes b(P_{u,v}), b(P_{s,t}) ≥ k−1 and concatenates P_{u,v}, P_{v,s}, P_{s,t}. This silently requires orienting the second piece, by reflecting it, so that both large b-values point the same way. We make the reflection part of the move: the choice of fl in choose_move. When k − 1 = 0 the orientation is arbitrary.

  3. Where the second piece is built. The paper does not say where P_{s,t} is constructed before it is moved. We build it in a dedicated stretch [a+s+2T+2, a+2s+2T+2) of the same row, then translate or reflect it next to the first piece.

  4. Constants. The segment produced for k has length < segLen T k, where segLen T k + 2T + 2 = 2^k (2T+3) (segLen_add). The paper gives a "discovered region" of length ≤ 5^{k+1} T; growth 2^k suffices. The paper is silent on the T-margins (our reading: its bound counts them inside the region), while ours requires them outside it, as hypotheses T ≤ a and a + segLen T k + T ≤ m. Rows also need margins: T ≤ r, r + T < m. The paper does not mention the vertical margins.

Theorem 1.1

  1. Explicit statement. theorem_1_1: if 2^(4T+5)(2T+3) ≤ m, then no algorithm with locality T 3-colours the m × m grid. theorem_1_1_log: every correct algorithm satisfies log₂ n < 12T + 14 with n = m². The paper states Ω(log n) without constants. Our choice of k = 4T+5 matches the paper's condition k > 4T+4.

  2. Second path. As in the paper, P_{s,t} is revealed directly in row r₂ = r₁ + 2T + 2, exactly above P_{u,v} (it is not built with Lemma 3.6). It is reflected about the midpoint of its column range if its b-value has the same sign as b(P_{u,v}). The paper says "we can simply reverse the direction of P_{s,t}". Reversing must keep s above v and t above u, which is what a reflection about the midpoint achieves.

  3. Rectangle identity. It comes from the potential (item 6), not from summing cells.

Theorem 1.3

  1. ✏️ The statement in the paper omits that the side length must be odd. Theorem 1.3 says "the locality of 3-coloring √n × √n toroidal and cylindrical grids is Ω(√n)", with no condition on n. The proof chooses n with √n odd. For even √n these graphs are bipartite. The O(log n) algorithm of Akbari et al. for bipartite graphs (Corollary 1.2) then applies, so Ω(√n) cannot hold for all n. The correct reading is "for all n with √n odd" (or "infinitely often"). Oddness appears only in the proof and in a remark at the end of §2.1; the abstract and the theorem statement omit it. We state it with b (columns) odd and b ≥ 3; rows are arbitrary with a ≥ 4T+3 (cylinder) or a ≥ 4T+4 (torus).

  2. Torus constant. The torus needs 4T + 4 ≤ a, one row more than the cylinder: the two revealed bands of rows [0, 2T] and [2T+2, 4T+2] must also be separated across the wrap-around. The paper uses 4T + 4 ≤ √n for both graphs, which is consistent.

  3. General shape. We allow a × b grids with a and b independent, as in the paper's closing remark ("Ω(a) whenever b is odd"). The √n corollaries are theorem_1_3_*_square.

Not formalized

  1. Corollary 1.2 (Θ(log n)) and the other-models part of Corollary 1.4 need the O(log n) algorithm of Akbari et al. and the simulations between the LOCAL, SLOCAL and Dynamic models. They are outside this paper. We formalize the Online-LOCAL upper bound T = O(√n) for cylinders and tori (torus_square_tight, cylinder_square_tight). For the grid we only show that T = 2m suffices (grid_bounds).

  2. Theorem 1.5 / Corollary 1.6 ((2k−2)-colouring k-partite graphs, Ω(n)). The paper gives only a proof sketch and defers to the full version. Not formalized.

  3. Theorems 1.8, 1.10 and Corollaries 1.9, 1.11 are proved only in the full version. Not formalized.
