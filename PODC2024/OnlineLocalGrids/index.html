Yi-Jun Chang, Gopinath Mishra, Hung Thuan Nguyen, Mingyang Yang, Yu-Cheng Yeh. A Tight Lower Bound for 3-Coloring Grids in the Online-LOCAL Model. PODC 2024. DOI 10.1145/3662158.3662794. Verdict: ✏️ Slip. Theorem 1.1 (Ω(log n) for grids) and Theorem 1.3 (Ω(√n) for cylinders and tori) are proved, with explicit constants, together with Lemmas 3.3–3.6. No proof step fails, and the only labeled mistake is that Theorem 1.3 as stated omits that √n must be odd, which its proof assumes; read as a bound for all large n, it is false for even √n. The results on k-partite graphs (Theorems 1.5, 1.8, 1.10) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 1.3 (and the abstract): the theorem claims locality Ω(√n) for 3-colouring √n × √n toroidal and cylindrical grids with no condition on n, but for even √n these graphs are bipartite and the O(log n) Online-LOCAL algorithm for bipartite graphs (the one behind Corollary 1.2) applies, so it is false if Ω is read "for all large n". The proof picks √n odd, and the correct reading is "for √n odd" (or "infinitely often"); we prove the general a × b form with b ≥ 3 odd (theorem_1_3_cylinder, theorem_1_3_torus). Outside the proof, only §2.1 (odd-column cylinders/tori are not bipartite) and the closing remark of §3.3 (the Ω(a) bound for a × b grids needs b odd) mention parity.

Model (CSLib, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Local/OnlineLocal.lean, following §2.2 of the paper).

  • Deterministic algorithms View ι → L; the algorithm may depend on n and the host graph arbitrarily. The input is an identifier placement pos : ι ≃ V on the host grid/cylinder/torus and a reveal order listing every node once. When vᵢ is revealed the algorithm sees the subgraph induced by B({v₁,…,vᵢ}, T) with identifiers and the reveal sequence, and irrevocably labels vᵢ. Correct = proper for every placement and every order.

  • The identifier space has exactly n elements (the paper allows poly(n)): this weakens the adversary, so the lower bounds are stronger. The adversary chooses only pos and the order; re-embedding is derived from an indistinguishability lemma (label_eq_of_agreeOn), not assumed.

  • T = 0 is allowed (the paper assumes T ≥ 1). Colours Fin 3, our 2 is the paper's 3. Grids are Mathlib's pathGraph/cycleGraph box products.

Proof remarks. No step of the paper fails, but its adversary arguments (Lemma 3.6, Theorem 1.1, Lemma 3.4) leave conditions implicit: the margins needed when the discovered region is re-embedded, the proper colouring in Lemma 3.6, and the reflections of the second path. We make them explicit and prove all results.

  • Re-embedding. The adversary "adjusts how the discovered region fits into G". This is sound only if moved pieces stay at distance ≥ 2T+2 from everything else and keep their radius-T balls away from the grid border (translating a piece that touches the border changes its view). The paper leaves these margins, including all vertical ones, unstated.

  • Lemma 3.6 only makes sense if the algorithm colours the revealed segment properly (the parity argument of Lemma 3.5 fails otherwise); we state it as "if properly coloured, then a row path with |b| ≥ k exists". Concatenating P_{u,v}, P_{v,s}, P_{s,t} silently requires reflecting the second piece so that both large b-values point the same way, and the paper never says where P_{s,t} is built before being moved.

  • Theorem 1.1: "simply reverse the direction of P_{s,t}" must be a reflection about the midpoint of its column range, so that s stays above v and t above u.

  • Lemma 3.4 sums cells "inside" a simple cycle, implicitly using a Jordan curve theorem for grids. We avoid it with a potential function and get the stronger statement that every closed walk has b-value 0.

Constants. Region length 5^{k+1}T in Lemma 3.6 can be 2^k(2T+3).

Not formalized. Corollary 1.2 and the other-model parts of Corollary 1.4 (they need the O(log n) algorithm of Akbari et al. and model simulations); Theorem 1.5 / Corollary 1.6 (only sketched); Theorems 1.8, 1.10 and Corollaries 1.9, 1.11 (proved only in the full version).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Proper colourings are CSLib's Cslib.LocallyIterative.IsProper (re-exported as IsProper). Non-vacuity: the "see everything" algorithm is correct once radius-T balls cover the graph (seeAll_colors in CSLib), so locality 2m suffices on the m × m grid (grid_bounds) and O(√n) on square cylinders and tori, making Theorem 1.3 tight up to constants.
