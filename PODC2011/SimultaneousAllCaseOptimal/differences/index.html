Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Simultaneous Tasks: From Worst-Case to All-Case Optimal — PODC 2011.

Paper: M. P. Herlihy, Y. Moses, M. R. Tuttle, Transforming worst-case optimal solutions for simultaneous tasks into all-case optimal solutions, PODC 2011 (ACM proceedings version, 8 pages; no arXiv version).

Errors in the paper (statement level)

  1. Lemma 5 is false for t = n − 1 (⚠️: the paper explicitly assumes t ≤ n − 1 in §2.1 and again at the start of §4, so the missing t ≤ n − 2 is not an implied convention). For every n ≥ 2, t = n − 1 and every complete input set: in the run r⋆ where all processes but p₀ crash in round 1 without sending, W(r⋆) = t − 1, so round 1 is premature, as it is in the failure-free run r₀ (W = 0). At time 1 only p₀ is alive in r⋆ and it received nothing, so (for a protocol in which everybody sends in round 1) every run indistinguishable from r⋆ at time 1 again has p₀ alone with an empty inbox; r₀ is not in the component. (lemma5_false, not_lemma5Hyp, Lemma5False.lean.) Lemma 7 fails in the same way (c = 1, k = 1 ≤ t). Lemma 5 is cited from [DM90, MR08], which presumably assume n ≥ t + 2; the paper restates it with its own range of t.

  2. Theorem 6 vs. Theorems 2/3 for t = n − 1 (⚠️, same mistake as item 1; see "Status of the claims" below). n = 3, t = 2, binary consensus: 2 is a worst-case lower bound (consensus_lb_two), FloodSet solves consensus in 2 rounds (floodSet_solvesWithin), the inputs are complete (theorem6_hypotheses). In r⋆ (two processes crash silently in round 1) W = 1, so Theorem 3 says Opt(A, 2) decides at min{2, 3 − 1} = 2; but loneSurvivor (the survivor sees an empty round-1 inbox, knows it is alone and decides its input at time 1; otherwise FloodSet) solves simultaneous consensus and decides at time 1 in r⋆ (loneSurvivor_solvesSim, loneSurvivor_decides). Hence no protocol has both the timing of Theorem 3 and the all-case optimality of Theorem 6 (thm3_thm6_incompatible). For every C with the ConCon properties listed in §2.3 (accuracy, consistency, (i), (ii)), Opt(floodSet 2, 2) is not all-case optimal (theorem6_fails), and such a C exists (loneCC_isConCon, theorem6_counterexample). Which claim fails for the real ConCon of [MM08] depends on how that protocol behaves for t = n − 1: §2.3 also claims ConCon's core is maximal among CC protocols (iii); a CC protocol may put the lone survivor's value in its core at time 1 in r⋆, so (ii) and (iii) cannot both hold for t = n − 1 (argued on paper, not formalized). If ConCon satisfies (ii), Theorem 6 fails; otherwise ConCon(A) decides before t + 1 − W, and Theorem 2's "decides at time t + 1 − W(F)" and Theorem 3's "execution time min{k̂, t + 1 − W}" fail.

  3. Corollary 9 is false for k = 1, n = 3, t = 2 (⚠️, application). It claims Opt(sa, ⌊t/k⌋ + 1) = Opt(sa, 3) is all-case optimal and halts at min{3, 3 − W}, i.e. at time 3 in failure-free runs; floodSet 2 decides at time 2 in every run, so no protocol has both properties (corollary9_false). This does not depend on ConCon. The cause: the paper quotes the [CHLT00] bound ⌊t/k⌋ + 1 without its hypothesis n ≥ t + k + 1; for n = 3, t = 2, k = 1 it is not a lower bound (not_lb_three). "Halts in time min{…}" is read as "decides at that time" (Thm3Timing), as in Theorem 3 ("decide at the earlier of k̂ and t + 1 − W"); under the weaker reading "by that time" the corollary still fails for every ConCon with property (ii), since then Opt(sa, 3) decides at time 3 in failure-free runs. (Also for t ≤ n − 2 with n < t + k + 1 the premise "⌊t/k⌋ + 1 is the worst-case bound" may fail; not explored.)

Proof gaps (statements true, proofs repaired)

  1. Simultaneous protocols and processes that crash later (🔧; used in Lemma 1, Theorem 3, Lemma 4). The proofs use, without argument, that if a simultaneous protocol has some process decide at time k, every process alive at time k (possibly crashing later) decides at time k (Lemma 4: "Since the nonfaulty processes decide at time k in r and p has not failed, p must decide at time k" — p only survives round k; Theorem 3: "Whichever simultaneous protocol caused p to decide at time k_p would also have caused q to decide at time k_p"; Lemma 1: a faulty process of A could a priori decide after k̂, breaking the simultaneity of Delay(A, k̂)). True: truncate the failure pattern after round k (the process becomes correct, its state up to time k is unchanged) and use completeness and simultaneity there (SolvesSim.decides_of_alive, SolvesWithin.decides_of_alive, Run.trunc).

  2. Theorem 2, correctness (🔧). "All processes … simulate the same run of A, and hence choose output values consistent with the input values" gives outputs consistent with the simulated input vector, which differs from the real one on processes outside the core (and the paper does not say which inputs those get; for non-complete input sets they must be chosen so that the vector is allowed). Repair: the simulated run is indistinguishable, for the core processes, from the run of A with the real inputs and the same silent crashes; A's correctness there gives an output vector for the real inputs (hist_simRun, decides_simRun, proof of theorem2).

  3. Theorem 2, the simulated run is admissible: the non-core processes are crashed in the actual run (the core contains all processes alive at t + 1 − W), so at most t crash; implicit in the paper (simRun_isRun).

Modelling choices

  • Model — CSLib's synchronous crash model Cslib.SyncCrash: runs = input vector + crash round c ≥ 1 + set reached in the crashing round; at most t crashes (Run.Admissible). Protocols are deterministic and history-based (Cslib.Byzantine.LockStep.Protocol): the local state of p at time m is its input and its received messages (perfect recall). The paper allows arbitrary local states; with a state that forgets, Lemma 4 would only give "decide by time k" (that suffices for Theorem 6). Protocols know the clock (history length), as in the paper (shared global clock). No self-messages (irrelevant: a process knows its own state).

  • Decisions — the output register is decide p x h; "p decides d at time m" = the first time the register is nonempty (Decides); the process must be alive at time m. A process that crashes in round c is alive at times < c.

  • Tasks — Task = set of input vectors + relation; correctness quantifies over an output vector o with (ı, o) ∈ P matching every decision, as in §2.2. The output sets Oᵢ are a single type O (no loss of generality). sim(P) = SolvesSim.

  • Worst-case execution time / lower bound — SolvesWithin A T t k: every nonfaulty process decides by time k. WorstCaseLB T t k̂: no protocol (with any message type in Type) solves T within k < k̂. "A solves P in time k̂" (Theorem 6) is SolvesWithin A T t k̂.

  • All-case optimality — AllCaseOptimal X: X solves sim(T), and for every protocol B solving sim(T) and every run (input vector, failure pattern) in which B's nonfaulty processes decide at time k, X's nonfaulty processes decide at some time ≤ k.

  • Waste — E(F, k) counts processes with a missing round-j ≤ k edge to a process surviving round j (Run.exposed); W = max_{k ≤ n} (E(F,k) − k) (equal to the paper's max over all k, since E ≤ n and E(F,0) = 0); premature: k + W < t + 1.

  • Similarity graph — vertices are the runs (≤ t crashes, allowed inputs); an edge needs a process alive at time k in both runs with the same local state (input and history).

  • ConCon is not formalized. ConCon C A T is built from any CC protocol C (CCProtocol, a core P → Option I per local state) with the properties listed in §2.3 (IsConCon: accuracy, consistency among processes alive at time m, (ii) empty core before t + 1 − W, (i) at t + 1 − W the core holds the inputs of all processes alive then). Maximality (iii) is not needed and not assumed. Non-vacuity: floodCC (t = 1, any n) and loneCC (n = 3, t = 2).

  • ConCon(A)'s simulated run — the processes outside the core crash in round 1 and send nothing; inputs: some allowed input vector extending the core (chosen by Classical.choose; the real one exists). Decisions in the simulated run are computed locally (unbounded local computation, as in the paper).

  • A else B — messages are pairs of optional component messages; decisions as in [MR08].

  • Lemmas 5 and 7 are hypotheses (Lemma5Hyp, Lemma7Hyp), quantified over all protocols (the proof of Theorem 6 applies Lemma 5 to the constructed protocol Opt(B, k₀)). They are cited from [DM90, MR08] and not proved here except for t = 1, n ≥ 3 (lemma5_t1); they are false for t = n − 1 (item 1). The completeness / c-coverability of the input set enters only through these hypotheses.

  • Theorem 8 — the hypothesis k̂ ≤ t + 2 − c is stated as k̂ + c ≤ t + 2 (natural numbers).

  • Not formalized: the ConCon protocol of [MM08] and its maximality; Lemma 5 / Lemma 7 for t ≤ n − 2; the applications' cited bounds (Corollaries 10, 11: d-tight conditions, strong renaming with log n); the topological discussion (§6).

Status of the claims

For t ≤ n − 2, all checked statements other than Corollary 9 hold given Lemma 5 / Lemma 7 (Corollary 9 also needs [CHLT00]'s n ≥ t + k + 1).

Lemma 5 (numbered, restated in the paper's setting "fix … t ≤ n − 1" of §4, and used in the proof of Theorem 6) and Corollary 9 are false regardless of ConCon; t = n − 1 with n = 3 is not degenerate, and the paper explicitly allows it twice.

For t = n − 1, either Theorem 6 or the timing claims of Theorems 2/3 are false. Theorem 6 is refuted for every ConCon satisfying the properties the paper lists in §2.3 (accuracy, consistency, (i), (ii); such protocols exist), but not for the actual [MM08] protocol, whose behaviour for t = n − 1 we do not formalize. A maximal ConCon (iii) would put the lone survivor's value into the core at time 1 in r⋆; then Opt(A, k̂) may well be all-case optimal for t = n − 1, and the false claim is the timing in Theorems 2/3 (not in the abstract). So for the actual protocol Theorem 6 at t = n − 1 is unestablished, not refuted.
