Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Unbounded-Concurrency Objects — PODC 2011.

Afek, Morrison, Wertheim, From Bounded to Unbounded Concurrency Objects and Back, PODC 2011 (author copy of the PODC version, 11 pages, proofs included).

No checked statement is false; the proof of Lemma 15 is broken as written (repaired), and Lemmas 1, 5 and Theorem 9 have smaller gaps. Typos (Lemma 14/15 proofs, §4 overview/§4.1 prose, Theorem 9 proof) have evident intent.

Common model (Sections 3, 5)

  • Model: Cslib.SharedMemory.Arrival (staged in Podc/): processes ℕ, deterministic, each one starting with its input (an input vector x : ℕ → X fixed up front; the paper's processes "arrive" — a process arrives at its first step, which is equivalent). One process = one invocation, as in the paper ("we think of each new invocation as coming from a new process"). Each step is one atomic operation on one base object; local computation is folded into the continuation.

  • Unbounded concurrency = every infinite schedule is admissible (infArrival); wait-freedom = every process that takes infinitely many steps eventually outputs. Bounded concurrency (Alg2.BoundedConcurrency): the number of processes that have taken a step and not yet output is bounded along the execution, by a bound depending on the execution and unknown to the algorithm — the paper's definition.

  • Consensus: agreement for all processes that took a step, validity (the impossibility results assume only weak validity — all inputs equal v ⇒ output v — which makes them stronger). Binary inputs for the impossibility results, arbitrary input type for Algorithm 2.

  • Base objects of an algorithm may be initialized arbitrarily and there may be infinitely many of them (the paper fixes initial states; allowing more only strengthens the impossibility results).

Theorem 10

  • Non-vacuity (NonVacuity.lean, consAlg_solvesCons): with one consensus object accessible to all processes, agreement, validity and wait-freedom under unbounded concurrency hold in our model; the impossibility therefore comes from the access bound AccessBounded, not from the encoding.

  • Statement generalized. no_consensus_of_rwLike_or_accessBounded: every base object is either read/write-like (ObjSpec.RWLike: each operation is read-only or a blind overwrite; registers of any value type) or accessed by at most c processes in every execution (AccessBounded), whatever its specification. theorem_10 is the paper's special case: registers and c-consensus objects (consObj, multivalued) with bounds c depending on the object.

  • "A c-consensus object can be accessed by at most c processes" is modelled as a requirement on the algorithm (AccessBounded A i c: in every execution at most c distinct processes apply an operation to i). The proof only uses the consequence that at most c processes can be simultaneously poised on it (AccessBounded.card_le). A different reading (the object answers arbitrarily after c accesses, as consₙ in PMB'20) is not covered.

  • Proof: as in the paper (growing chain of n-critical executions, w.r.t. the processes p₀, …, p_{n-1}, rather than arbitrary finite sets P); the "standard valency arguments" are exists_crit (CSLib-staged) and crit_same_object/rw_pair here. No gap.

Theorem 11, Lemmas 13–15

  • Model of the impossibility. The paper does not list the base objects of algorithm A in Section 5.3; its case analysis covers "atomic register read or write, or iterator stack read or write". We take any family of read/write-like objects (registers) and iterator stacks (ST B W, indexed by R ⊕ S).

  • Iterator stack (IteratorStack.lean). The paper's infinite iterator sequence I (0 = uninitialized) is stored as the list of initialized iterators (in every reachable state these are exactly the first m, m = number of writes); iterators are numbered from 0 (the paper: from 1). From the initial state the object behaves exactly as the paper's. Arbitrary initial states of our object (allowed by the model) include states with a position 0 inside the list; such iterators are incremented on reads (the paper never has them).

  • Theorem 11 (theorem_11) is stated as a conjunction: for every n, Algorithm 2 solves n-process consensus (registers + one iterator stack; "infinite consensus number"), and no algorithm from read/write-like objects and iterator stacks solves binary consensus wait-free under unbounded concurrency (no_consensus_iterator_stack).

  • Valency (Uni): w.r.t. all extensions by any processes, as in the paper. Uni α v means "no extension decides ¬v"; with wait-freedom this is v-valency.

  • Definition 2 / Lemma 13 (lemma_13): stated as the existence of α, P, Q, v with α P only v and α Q P only ¬v. Bivalence of α and P ≠ Q follow (lemma_14 proves P ≠ Q). The proof follows the paper (alternate solo runs of P with walks along an extension of opposite valency); the paper's remark "each step in L₁ but the last keeps the system in a bivalent state" is not true for an arbitrary L₁ but is not needed. The paper's proof starts from "a bivalent state s₀"; we construct it (input false for p₀, true for p₁, weak validity).

  • Lemma 14 (lemma_14): stated as in the paper ("both are writes to the same iterator stack"), including the preceding paragraph's case analysis (different objects, registers, two reads). The contradictions "s_P and s_Q would be indistinguishable" are made precise by running a fresh process (false_of_mem_eq): the local states of P, Q (and of R) do differ. The register case is Herlihy's argument for any read/write-like object. The write/read-of-next-iterator case is write_read_next_false; typo: "In e_P (where I[m+1] is initially 1)" should be 2.

  • Lemma 15 (lemma_15): statement as in the paper (typo: "o_P = write(v_Q)" should be o_Q). The proof is broken as written and needed a new construction (🛠️):

    1. the paper freezes every new process at its first read "using an iterator i ∈ T", even a read that cannot distinguish the executions (e.g. of an exhausted iterator). Then all new processes may be frozen forever without any write and without moving R — the claim "if only (1) occurs, then eventually all iterators in T become invalid … a contradiction" is false (nothing forces reads of different iterators);

    2. after two writes the paper lets R read "the value in V[k]", but the frozen readers may have advanced R's own iterator (any process can read any iterator), so that after the two writes it points to v_P/v_Q again. Repair (unblock, round, false_of_rel15): freeze only at distinguishing reads (iterator position in the two-entry window holding v_P, v_Q); keep writers pending and execute two writes at once (executing one write at a time would not suffice: alternating writes and reads of R's iterator would keep R blocked); re-check R after each frozen read. Termination: the potential Σ (w + 3 − pos) over iterators strictly decreases with each distinguishing read while no write is executed.

  • The final contradiction is with wait-freedom of R in the limit execution (infinitely many processes, infinitely many frozen and hence active forever — unbounded concurrency), as in the paper.

Theorem 12 (Algorithm 2), file Alg2.lean

  • Objects: register result (initially ⊥) and one iterator stack; the paper's local "atomic registers" last, itr, cur are local variables. Line 6 returns the local last instead of re-reading result (by agreement the same value). Unreachable branches (last = ⊥ at line 5, ill-typed responses) are given arbitrary continuations; the invariant proves they never occur.

  • Agreement and validity are proved for all finite executions (any concurrency).

  • Wait-freedom (bcWaitFree) under bounded concurrency. The paper's one-sentence argument ("before result is written, only a finite number of processes can participate … so only a finite number of values can be written") is correct but terse: a process that read result = ⊥ may write to the stack after result is written; the count works because all stack writers are active simultaneously just before the first write of result (pot_le_active, pot_bound). Loop termination also uses that each iterator is read only by its owner and that an iterator position is at most |V| + 1. Proof remarks, not gaps.

  • Extra (not in the paper): not_waitFree_infArrival, Algorithm 2 is not wait-free under unbounded concurrency.

Section 4 (Algorithm 1, swap), folder Swap/

  • Model. Each process performs one swap(x p); invocation = first step, response = output state. Base objects are atomic: max_depth F&A over ℕ initialized to 1 (as in the pseudocode), the unbounded snapshot accessed (snapObj: cell d = last node posted by the depth-d process, scan returns the whole array), and per tree node a register (values Option V) and a test-and-set (CSLib convention: returns the old bit, "win" = false). The implementations of F&A/snapshot from 2-consensus objects/registers ([2], [10]) are not formalized, so "swap from 2-consensus objects" is verified only modulo these (atomic) objects.

  • Tree. Nodes (depth d, index k < 2^d), in-order given by the dyadic key (2k+1)/2^(d+1) (Swap/Tree.lean).

  • NextUnaccessedNodeAtDepth with an empty candidate set: the process keeps re-scanning (stuck), so wait-freedom genuinely needs Lemma 1 (Inv.next_ne_none). Line 9's bound is the value maxdepth_i read at Line 7, over all nodes of depth ≤ maxdepth_i (the pseudocode); §4.1's prose says "depth ≤ depth_i" (slip).

  • Overview text: "process P₀ that obtained depth = 0" captures the root; with max_depth initialized to 1 depths start at 1 and the root is never used. Harmless (slip).

  • Lemma 1 (lemma1_paper, as stated: whenever a process holds cap = w with w in the right subtree of v, update(v) has been executed; state form lemma1). Proof gap: the case u = v (resp. cap_j = v) is missing; excluded because the capturer of v posts v before blocking (🔧: proof gap, statement true).

  • Wait-freedom (waitFree : WaitFree (alg V) infArrival): the paper's "Lemma 1 implies wait-freedom of the capturing phase" leaves termination implicit (captures at a fixed depth move strictly right among 2^d nodes); proved with a lexicographic progress measure.

  • Lemma 2 (Inv.lemma2): stated for reachable configurations (via the invariant inv_exec).

  • Lemma 3: second half Inv.tryRet_unique; the consequence used in the paper (each T&S accessed by ≤ 2 processes) is lemma3_tst. No circularity with Lemma 1 (Lemma 1 uses only the trivial first half).

  • Lemmas 4–6 assume a complete execution (as the paper says); Lemmas 4 and 5 also need at least one process (degenerate). Lemma 5: the paper proves only that P_big's input is not returned; uniqueness follows from the chain structure / counting (proof gap, 🔧). Lemma 8 is ¬ TransGen RetFrom p p.

  • Theorem 9. Linearizability is our definition SwapLin (one-shot swap with infinitely many processes: a duplicate-free list of processes of σ containing all completed ones, the j-th completed returns ⊥ for j = 0 and the input of the (j−1)-th otherwise, real-time order respected); tied to CSLib's swapObj sequential specification by swapResp_eq. The paper's "Lemmas 4–8 give a chain" is not argued (done by induction along the order of captured nodes); "ordered before P_j" should read "after" (slip). The paper proves only complete executions and asserts the general case; linearizable proves every finite execution (pending operations completed by solo runs).
