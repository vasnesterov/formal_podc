Easy Impossibility Proofs for k-Set Agreement — PODC 2011

Martin Biely, Peter Robinson, Ulrich Schmid. Easy Impossibility Proofs for k-Set Agreement in Message Passing Systems. PODC 2011 (brief announcement). DOI 10.1145/1993806.1993846. Formalized version: arXiv 1103.3671 v2 (full version, latest arXiv version, March 2011); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Theorem 1, the paper's main tool, is saved by local repairs and its asynchronous consequences hold: k-set agreement is impossible with f crashes when k ≤ (n−1)/(n−f) (Theorem 2 with asynchronous processes, Corollary 5, by the paper's reduction to FLP), and with up to f < n initially dead processes it is solvable iff kn > (k+1)f (Theorem 8). But the claim that Theorem 2 tightly captures the impossibility is lost: its bound is not matched, and the exact border is proved only for initial crashes, by a separate partitioning argument. The failure-detector part (Section VII) was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Section VI opening and contribution list ("Theorem 2 tightly captures the impossibility")

The bound (n−1)/(n−f) of Theorem 2 is not matched by the solvability bound f/(n−f) for initial crashes, because the two results are about different failure models.

Impact. A tight border is proved only in the initial-crash model, by a separate partitioning argument (theorem8). Tightness of Theorem 2's own bound is not saved.

Details

Theorem 2 assumes a different and harsher failure model (f − 1 initial crashes plus one crash during the run). Example: n = 5, f = 3, k = 2 is impossible under Theorem 2's failures but solvable with initial crashes only (theorem2_vs_theorem8).

🔧 Theorem 1 (unstated property of the model)

Theorem 1's proof tacitly assumes that the processes of D̄ cannot decide a value proposed only in D, and without this the theorem fails in a model where process 0's input is fixed (Abstract.Counterexample.theorem1_statement_false).

Impact. The theorem fails only in the literal "arbitrary system model" generality the paper claims; the statement is under-specified. In the paper's own models the missing hypothesis holds, and the applications are not affected. Repaired by one added condition (E): Abstract.theorem1_corrected.

Details

Fact 1 ("in every run of R(D̄) all of D̄ decide on a common value") is derived from (B) and k-agreement alone. Then A|D̄ is said to solve consensus in M'. Both steps need the unstated property. Otherwise D̄ may split between v₁ and a new value w, which together with v₁, …, v_{k−1} are still only k values, and A|D̄ need not be valid in M'.

Counterexample: n = 3, k = 2, D₁ = {0}, D̄ = {1, 2}, no communication, and process 0 always proposes 0 in M. "Everybody decides 0" solves consensus in M, yet (A)–(D) hold, with M' = two silent processes with inputs in {1,2,3}.

The counterexample needs a model in which D̄'s view is correlated with D's inputs (here: process 0's input is fixed). The paper's models ([DDS87]-style, all inputs x_q ∈ V possible, admissibility independent of input values) exclude this. There the missing hypothesis follows by changing the inputs of D.

🔧 Theorem 1, condition (B) (fixed values v_i)

Condition (B) cannot hold as written in the applications: the values v_i are fixed in advance, so a run of R(D̄) in which a process of D̄ proposes v₁ has no D̄-indistinguishable partner in R(D, D̄) (Abstract.condB_fails).

Impact. The statement is under-specified: the values must be allowed to depend on the run, and the proof works verbatim then (Abstract.theorem1_corrected).

Details

R(D, D̄) requires v_i to be proposed in D. With arbitrary distinct inputs (Theorems 2 and 10) such runs exist.

🔧 Theorem 1, proof ("Therefore, we have that, in fact, ρ ∈ R(D̄)")

"Clearly ρ ∈ R(D̄)" does not follow from indistinguishability until decision.

Impact. Requiring the runs given by (D) to lie in R(D̄) repairs it, and this holds by construction in the applications (Abstract.theorem1_corrected).

Details

A process of D̄ that decided early may hear from D in ρ before the other processes of D̄ decide.

🔧 Theorem 1, proof (termination of A|D̄)

Termination of A|D̄ in M' is never argued: a process correct in ρ' might be faulty in the compatible run ρ.

Impact. One clause added to (D), preserving correctness, closes the gap, and it holds in the applications (Abstract.theorem1_corrected). Condition (A) is never used.

🔧 Section VI ("complete and consistent knowledge of G after the second stage")

The claim that processes have "complete and consistent knowledge of G" after stage 2 is too strong: a process only learns its ancestors.

Impact. The algorithm is correct as intended (InitDeadAlg.kSafe).

Details

Ancestor knowledge suffices, but one must also argue that a chosen root's ancestor set is frozen once all its ancestors have chosen their in-lists.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 8 (in the form kn > (k+1)f) fails for f = n ≤ k, where "decide your own input" works (theorem8_false_of_f_eq_n, theorem2_false_of_f_eq_n). The paper implicitly assumes f < n (its sets D_i of size n − f must be nonempty). Theorem 2's bound (n−1)/(n−f) is undefined at f = n; in the multiplied-out form k(n−f) ≤ n−1 it would fail for f = n = k.

  • Lemma 6 needs a nonempty graph: the empty graph has no source component (SourceComp.exists_sourceComponent).

  • For k = 1, what Section VI chooses is a source component, not a "clique" (a fully connected subgraph).

Typos

  • In the proof of Theorem 10, "applying Theorem 2" should be Theorem 1.

  • The proof of Lemma 7 cites "the previous theorem", meaning Lemma 6.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 8, "only if"

🔧 Theorem 8, "only if" (proved only for kn = (k+1)f)

The paper proves impossibility only for the border case kn = (k+1)f and for kn < (k+1)f relies on "Theorem 2 tightly captures the impossibility", which does not transfer.

Impact. The paper's partitioning argument works unchanged for all kn ≤ (k+1)f, with k+1 groups of size n−f (initDead_impossible).

Details

Theorem 2's failure model is harsher than initial crashes, so its impossibility does not transfer, and its bound (n−1)/(n−f) is not f/(n−f).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous message passing on top of CSLib's FLP model (Cslib.FLP.Algorithm), with n processes, deterministic state machines and reliable, unordered channels. A step receives one message and atomically updates the state, sends messages and possibly decides.

  • A crash means the process stops receiving; crashes are clean (no partial last broadcast). This adversary is weaker than the paper's, so our impossibilities imply the paper's.

  • KAlgorithm lets a process have an input in an arbitrary V. Messages arriving before a process' first step are buffered (only restricts schedules).

  • k-set agreement: k-agreement and validity in every reachable state, for faulty processes too; termination of every non-faulty process in every admissible infinite run.

  • Failure models: InitDead f (up to f processes never take a step, all others correct), OneCrash f (Theorem 2's pattern: f − 1 initially dead plus one more crash) and Crash f (CSLib's runs with ≤ f faulty processes).

  • Theorem 2 is proved for asynchronous processes (Corollary 5's setting) with Theorem 2's failure pattern. The paper's version with synchronous processes and atomic broadcast relies on [DDS87, Table I] and is not formalized.

  • Theorem 1 is formalized twice. Abstract.lean has the generic version (a model is a set of runs with views, Definition 2 is equality of views); there the literal statement is refuted and a corrected one proved. theorem1_async is the concrete asynchronous instance: (C) is CSLib's FLP theorem, and the other conditions follow from solo runs, merging and lifting.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The model and the consensus impossibility are CSLib's Cslib/Computability/Distributed/FLP/. Non-vacuity: Theorem 8's algorithm satisfies the specification, and the trivial algorithm own solves k-set agreement for k ≥ n.
