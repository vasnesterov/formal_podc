Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Easy Impossibility Proofs for k-Set Agreement — PODC 2011.

Paper: Biely, Robinson, Schmid, Easy Impossibility Proofs for k-Set Agreement in Message Passing Systems, PODC 2011 (2-page brief announcement, pp. 227–228). We formalize the full version, arXiv 1103.3671v2 (March 2011, the latest arXiv version, which predates the conference). Notation as in the paper: D = D₁ ∪ … ∪ D_{k-1}, D̄ = Π \ D (the subsystem in which consensus is impossible). In Lean the groups are Dg i and D̄ is Dbar.

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Theorem 1 is false in its literal generality (under-specified statement: an implicit input-independence property of the model; the paper's applications are not affected). The proof derives Fact 1 ("all processes of D̄ decide on a common value in each run of R(D̄)") from (B) and k-agreement only, and then claims that A|D̄ solves consensus in M'. Both steps need that the processes of D̄ can only decide values proposed in D̄: otherwise D̄ may decide some v_i (two values v_1, w in D̄ plus v_1, …, v_{k-1} in D are only k values), and the decision of A|D̄ need not be valid in M'. Counterexample (Abstract.Counterexample.theorem1_statement_false): n = 3, k = 2, D₁ = {0}, D̄ = {1, 2}, V = {0,1,2,3}; in M nobody communicates and process 0 always proposes 0; A = "decide 0" solves consensus (hence 2-set agreement) in M; (A), (B), (D) hold trivially and (C) holds for M' = two non-communicating processes with distinct inputs in {1,2,3}. The counterexample restricts the inputs of M (process 0 always proposes 0). The paper's models do not: all x_q ∈ V are possible, and changing the inputs of D changes neither D̄'s views until decision nor admissibility. In such models (E) below follows by changing D's inputs. Combined with item 2, the literal statement is then vacuously true. Repaired version: Abstract.theorem1_corrected, with the extra hypothesis (E) "in the runs of R(D̄) the processes of D̄ decide only values proposed in D̄". In the concrete asynchronous instance (theorem1_async) (E) is obtained by giving the initially dead processes inputs of D̄.

  2. Theorem 1, condition (B) is unsatisfiable as stated in the paper's applications (under-specified statement). The values v_1, …, v_{k-1} are fixed before the runs, and R(D, D̄) requires v_i to be proposed in D. Any run of R(D̄) in which a process of D̄ proposes v_1 (such runs exist whenever inputs are arbitrary distinct values, as in Theorems 2 and 10) has no D̄-indistinguishable run in R(D, D̄) (Abstract.condB_fails). The proof works if the values may depend on the run; theorem1_corrected is stated that way.

  3. Theorem 1, two further proof gaps (proof gaps; repaired by strengthening (D) in theorem1_corrected). (a) "Clearly, the same is true for the indistinguishable run ρ, … hence ρ ∈ R(D̄)": indistinguishability is only until each process' own decision, while (dec-D̄) forbids receptions until all of D̄ has decided; a process of D̄ that decided early may receive a message from D in ρ before another process of D̄ decides. (b) Termination of A|D̄ in M' is not addressed: a process that is correct in ρ' and never decides could be faulty in the compatible run ρ; (D) has to preserve correctness. In the paper's applications both hold by construction. Condition (A) is never used (it is implied by (B)–(D) whenever M'_{A|D̄} is nonempty), so theorem1_corrected drops it.

  4. Theorem 8, "only if", is not proved for k n < (k+1) f (proof gap; the statement is true). Section VI only treats the border case k n = (k+1) f by partitioning and otherwise relies on "Theorem 2 tightly captures the impossibility". But Theorem 2 is about a different failure model (f − 1 initially dead processes plus one crash during the execution), which is strictly harder for the algorithm than "up to f initially dead", so its impossibility does not transfer; and its bound (n − 1)/(n − f) is not the bound f/(n − f) of Theorem 8 — e.g. for n = 5, f = 3, k = 2 Theorem 2 gives impossibility (with one running crash) while Theorem 8 gives solvability (initial crashes only). The paper's partitioning argument works verbatim for every k n ≤ (k+1) f (split into k+1 groups of size n − f); we prove it that way (initDead_impossible).

  5. Theorem 8 is false for f = n (false only in a degenerate case). With all processes possibly initially dead and k ≥ n, k n > (k+1) f fails, yet "decide your own input" solves k-set agreement (theorem8_false_of_f_eq_n). We assume f < n.

  6. Theorem 2 / Corollary 5 need f < n (degenerate case; the paper's bound (n−1)/(n−f) is undefined at f = n, so this concerns only the multiplied-out form; the sets D_i of size n − f must be nonempty). For f = n = k, k (n − f) = 0 ≤ n − 1, but "decide your own input" solves n-set agreement (theorem2_false_of_f_eq_n).

  7. Section VI, imprecisions (the algorithm and Theorem 8 are unaffected). Lemma 6 needs a nonempty graph (the empty graph has no source component); δ > 0 is not needed (SourceComp.exists_sourceComponent assumes Nonempty α). After stage 2 a process does not have "complete knowledge of G", only of its ancestors; this suffices, but the safety proof also needs that the ancestor set of a chosen root is frozen once all its ancestors have chosen their in-lists (InitDeadAlg.kSafe). For k = 1 the paper calls the chosen set an "initial clique (fully connected)"; it is a source component, only strongly connected.

  8. Typos / slips. Proof of Theorem 10: "the conditions required for applying Theorem 2" should be Theorem 1. Proof of Lemma 7: "the previous theorem" is Lemma 6. Section VI: "when 2δ ≥ n, then there can be only one source component" (true, already for 2δ ≥ n − 1).

Modelling choices

  • Model. CSLib's asynchronous FLP model (Cslib.FLP.Algorithm): a step is the reception of one in-flight message (multiset, unordered, no loss) with atomic local transition, sending and deciding; stuttering steps; a crash is "stops receiving from some point on while a message to it is in flight"; correct = receives every message sent to it. The paper's model (from [DDS87]) lets a step receive any subset of the buffer and lets a crashing process omit some sends in its last step. Our adversary is weaker (fewer schedules/failures), so our impossibility results imply the paper's; the possibility result (Theorem 8) only involves initial crashes.

  • Inputs. CSLib's processes have Boolean inputs delivered by a wake-up message. KAlgorithm has inputs in an arbitrary V, in the initial state; messages that arrive before the wake-up message are buffered and processed right after it (equivalently: the first step of a process receives nothing). This only restricts schedules; an unbuffered algorithm can buffer itself. The input x : P → V is arbitrary (not only distinct); the paper's problem quantifies over all inputs from V with |V| ≥ n. Our impossibilities only need k + 1 distinct values.

  • Specification. k-Agreement and Validity are required in every reachable state (so they bind correct and faulty processes, as in the paper); Termination in every admissible infinite run for every non-faulty process. Decisions are write-once by construction.

  • Failure assumptions are run predicates: InitDead f (≤ f processes never take a step, all others correct), OneCrash f (≤ f − 1 initially dead plus at most one other faulty process — Theorem 2's pattern), Crash f (CSLib's admissible runs with ≤ f faulty processes).

  • Theorem 2 is formalized for asynchronous processes (Corollary 5's model) with Theorem 2's failure pattern. The paper's Theorem 2 has synchronous processes and atomic broadcast; its condition (C) then rests on [DDS87, Table I], which we do not formalize. Corollary 5 (asynchronous, up to f crashes) follows (corollary5).

  • Theorem 1 is formalized twice: abstractly (Abstract.lean: models as sets of runs with views, inputs and decisions read off views, Definition 2 as equality of views; the literal statement is refuted and a corrected one proved), and concretely for the asynchronous model (theorem1_async), where (C) is CSLib's FLP theorem and (A), (B), (D), (E) are discharged by solo runs, merging and lifting.

  • In theorem1_async the processes outside all groups are not required to belong to D̄ (D̄ need not be Π \ D); the extra processes are simply never scheduled.

  • Theorem 8's algorithm (Algorithm*.lean): stage 1 broadcasts, a process waits for stage-1 messages from n − f − 1 others (its in-list); stage 2 broadcasts (input, in-list); a process decides once its knowledge is closed under in-lists, on the input of the minimum root (a vertex in a source component) among its ancestors. Messages carry the sender id explicitly.

Not formalized

  • Section VII (failure detectors Σ_k, Ω_k, partition failure detector, Lemmas 9, 11, 12, Theorem 10, Corollary 13): needs a failure-detector framework and cited results (weakest failure detector for consensus, (Σ, Ω₂) strictly weaker than (Σ, Ω) via [21] and a shared-memory simulation [9]).

  • Definition 6 / Observation 1 (T-independence) as a separate notion; Lemma 4 is used in the form "solo runs of the groups are admissible".

  • The partially synchronous variant of Theorem 2 (synchronous processes, atomic broadcast).
