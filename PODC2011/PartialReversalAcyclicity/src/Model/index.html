import Mathlib /-! # Partial Reversal Acyclicity — the model Radeva, Lynch. *Partial Reversal Acyclicity.* PODC 2011 (brief announcement); full version MIT-CSAIL-TR-2011-022. Section 2 of the paper: an undirected graph `G = (V, E)` with a destination `D`, and an initial acyclic orientation `G'init`. We take `G'init` as the primitive datum (`Network.init u v` means "initially the edge `{u,v}` is directed from `u` to `v`", i.e. `u` is to the left of `v`) and define `G` as its underlying undirected graph. `in-nbrsᵤ`, `out-nbrsᵤ` and `nbrsᵤ` are defined from it. The three automata of the paper: * `PR` (Algorithm 1): action `reverse(S)` for a nonempty set `S` of sinks with `D ∉ S`; state: the direction variables `dir[u,v]` and the lists `list[u]`; * `OneStepPR` (Algorithm 3): the same with `S = {u}`; * `NewPR` (Algorithm 2): action `reverse(u)`; state: `dir[u,v]` and the step counters `count[u]`. The effect of `reverse(S)` is given in closed form (`PR.effect`): every node of `S` reverses the edges in `PR.revSet` (its edges not in `list[u]`, or all edges if `list[u] = nbrsᵤ`), adds itself to the lists of the corresponding neighbours, and empties its own list. The paper writes it as a `for each u ∈ S` loop; the two agree whenever the nodes of `S` are pairwise non-adjacent, which holds in every reachable state (two neighbours are never both sinks, Invariant 3.1). The directed graph `s.G'` of a state is `arcs N s.dir`: there is an arc `u → v` iff `{u,v} ∈ E` and `dir[u,v] = out`. -/ noncomputable section open Classical namespace Podc.PartialReversalAcyclicity
