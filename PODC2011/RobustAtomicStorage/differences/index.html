Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Complexity of Robust Atomic Storage — PODC 2011.

Paper: D. Dobre, R. Guerraoui, M. Majuntke, N. Suri, M. Vukolić. The Complexity of Robust Atomic Storage. PODC 2011 (EPFL accepted version, 10 pages, proofs included).

Model (Model.lean)

  1. Round model. The paper's Definition 1 lets the client end a round after "a sufficient number of replies", subject to (a)/(b): a round may end without the reply of s_i only if s_i is faulty or some indistinguishable partial run has s_i faulty; together with wait-freedom this means the adversary can force a round to end exactly when the missing objects could all be faulty. We formalize the round model of [1] (Abraham et al.; the paper says "this is equivalent to the round model of [1]", right after the FIFO rule for pending invocations): a round may end as soon as S − t objects replied, and the scheduler decides when. This drops condition (a)/(b), so the Lean adversary may end rounds that the paper's adversary may not (e.g. skip correct objects while malicious responders forge), Proto.Atomic quantifies over more runs, and the Lean theorems are formally weaker than the paper's (they cover a smaller class of protocols: those that cannot wait for more than S − t replies after detecting a faulty reply). The paper's arguments that the terminations in its runs are legal ("rd_j cannot wait for additional replies", via Δpr) are therefore not checked in Lean. The reviewer checked them on the symbolic Python replay of the runs (outside the repository): every terminated round of every run of Proposition 1 (k ≤ 5) and Lemma 1 (k ≤ 6 with the paper's sizes; k ≤ 5, t_k ≤ t ≤ 3t_k + 3 with the repaired sizes of exists_fits) has a witness run with the same client view in which the skipped objects together with all objects that forged so far are at most t objects. So the constructions are legal in the paper's model, jointly (stronger than the per-object reading of (b)). Rule: modelling choice, not a statement issue.

  2. Fixed number of rounds. Writes take exactly K rounds and reads exactly N rounds in every run (the paper: "completes in N rounds", i.e. at most N, possibly adaptively). Adaptive round counts are not representable in Proto. For writes, the statements are proved for every fixed K up to the bound. Proposition 1 is proved only for N = 2 (not N = 1 or reads taking one or two rounds depending on the run), and Lemma 1/2 and Proposition 2 only for N = 3. A protocol whose operations take at most N rounds can be padded to exactly N rounds with no-op rounds (objects ignore no-op messages; delaying responses only removes precedence constraints, so atomicity is preserved); this padding is not formalized. Rule: modelling choice; the formal statements are weaker than the paper's "at most" only by this standard reduction.

  3. Deterministic protocols, objects described by their logs (every deterministic object automaton is of this form). The paper uses I/O automata and implicitly treats them as deterministic ("forge their state to σ").

  4. One-shot clients: each client invokes at most one operation, and the writer writes a value v ≠ ⊥. Clients receive no messages from other clients (the paper restricts to such runs "without loss of generality"). Restricting the runs only weakens the hypothesis Proto.Atomic and so strengthens the impossibility results.

  5. Malicious objects act by overwriting their state (forge) and otherwise behave correctly. This is exactly the Byzantine behaviour used in the proofs, and a special case of arbitrary behaviour (again a restriction of runs). A reply is received in the same step in which the object processes the message (no separate "in transit reply"); replies in transit at the end of a partial run of the paper are modelled by the client never using them (it crashes / is slow).

  6. Atomicity is the paper's definition (properties (1)–(4) of §2.2), specialised to at most one write (enough in a one-shot model with one writer).

  7. Wait-freedom is built into the round model (every round can terminate once S − t objects replied), so it is not a hypothesis.

Proposition 1 (ReadLB.lean, Main.proposition1)

  1. More general than stated in the proof: the paper's proof uses blocks B₁, B₂, B₃ of size exactly t ≥ 1 and 1 ≤ |B₄| ≤ t (so 3t + 1 ≤ S ≤ 4t) and, for S ≤ 3t, implicitly the resilience bound S ≥ 3t + 1 of [23]. We prove the statement for all S ≤ 4t (four blocks of at most t objects, possibly empty) and every number K ≥ 1 of write rounds, with no appeal to [23].

  2. Reconstruction of the runs. The general step of the paper (pr_{4i+(j mod 4)}, states σ^r_{4i+(j mod 4)−3} and σ_{((j mod 4)/j)(k−i−1)}, footnote 3) is index-heavy and partly garbled; we index read instances by n = 1, …, 4k − 1 (reader (n−1) mod 4) and give closed forms of all object states (ReadLB.L). The base runs pr₁, …, pr₃ are uniform instances of the general step once labels of non-existent instances are dropped. Where the text and Fig. 1 differ in notation we follow Fig. 1 (validated by a symbolic replay of all runs).

  3. Only the runs pr_n (and the final no-write run) are built: the paper's Δpr_n serve to compare views; in our proof they are prefixes of pr_{n+1}, and indistinguishability is the canonicity of every round's transcript (Sim.lean).

Lemma 1 (WriteLB.lean, Main.lemma1, Main.write_lb_general)

  1. Block form. We prove the construction for every block assignment satisfying only upper bounds (Blocks.Fits: every set malicious in some run or skipped by some terminated round has at most t objects); the paper's sizes (|B_l| = t_l − t_{l−2}, …, equations (1)–(3)) are an instance (Arith.exists_fits_paper, Arith.paper_sizes).

  2. k = 1. The paper proves k ≥ 2 and cites [1] for k = 1; the same construction covers k = 1 and we prove all k ≥ 1.

  3. Read rd_k. As in the paper, its third round skips M_{k−2} ∪ P_{k+1} (not M_{k−2} ∪ 𝒞_{k+1} as for the other reads).

  4. Typo: "We refer to the state of {B_l, C_l} at the end of Δpr_1 as σ^r_l" should be Δpr_l. The proof assumes "at most 3t_k + 1 objects" but fixes the block sizes summing to exactly 3t_k + 1; fewer objects are covered by our upper-bound formulation (11).

  5. Only the runs pr_l^C (l = 1..k) and Δpr_k are built (see 10).

Lemma 2 (Main.lemma2)

  1. Proof gap (🔧). "By Lemma 1 … Observe that this is valid even with R ≥ k readers and t ≥ t_k faults": Lemma 1 is about 3t_k + 1 objects and t_k faults, and going to S = 3t + 1 objects and t > t_k faults needs larger blocks; the paper's scaling argument (Proposition 2) only handles t_k ∣ t. Repaired by Arith.exists_fits (extra objects in the blocks B_k, C_k, B_{k+1}, which never lie in a common constrained set).

  2. Direction of "solving for k" (✏️). The proof needs k ≤ ⌊log ⌈(3t+1)/2⌉⌋ ⇒ t ≥ t_k but derives the converse from the closed form; both directions hold (Arith.tseq_le_iff).

  3. Degenerate cases. For R = 0 (no reader) or t = 0 the bound min {R, ⌊log ⌈(3t+1)/2⌉⌋} is 0 and the statement is about 0-round writes; our model has no 0-round operations, so we state Lemma 2 for 1 ≤ K ≤ min {…}. (With R = 0 the statement would be false — atomicity is vacuous without reads — but the paper's "multi-reader" excludes it.)

  4. We prove the bound for every write-round number K with 1 ≤ K ≤ min {R, ⌊log₂ ⌈(3t+1)/2⌉⌋} ("completes in" read as "at most").

Proposition 2 (Main.proposition2)

  1. Proof gap (🔧). "By carefully choosing c = t/t_k, we obtain a lower bound proof for S' = 3t + ⌊t/t_k⌋ and t faults": multiplying every block by c requires an integer c, i.e. t_k ∣ t; for other t it yields only 3c t_k + c < 3t + c objects. Statement true: we add d = t − c·t_k objects to each of B_k, C_k, B_{k+1} (Arith.exists_fits; total 3t + c), and use that the construction only needs upper bounds. Smaller S is handled by shrinking blocks.

  2. ⌊log ⌈(3t_k + 1)/2⌉⌋ = k (Arith.log_tseq), so the bound is min {R, k}; for K < k we use t / t_k ≤ t / t_K.

Not formalized

  1. The upper-bound discussion of §5 (2-round-write / 4-round-read implementations via regular storage of [15] and the regular-to-atomic transformations of [4, 20]) and the secret-value model of [8]: they rely on other papers' algorithms.

  2. The resilience bound S ≥ 3t + 1 of [23] is not needed (see 8, 20).
