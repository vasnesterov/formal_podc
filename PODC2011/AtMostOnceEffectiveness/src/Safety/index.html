Notation for a local state `s` of process `p`: * `Ann s`: `p` has announced `NEXT_p` in `next_p` in its current iteration (status `gatherTry`, `gatherDone`, `check`, `do` or `done`); * `ReadNext s r`: `p` has read `next_r` in its current iteration; * `ReadDone s r`: `p` has read line `r` of `done` in its current iteration; * `Busy s`: `p` passed `check` and has not yet recorded the job (`do` or `done`); * `line C p`: the jobs recorded so far in line `p` of `done`; * `Claimed C p i`: `i` is recorded in line `p`, or `p` is busy with `i`. The heart of the proof (the paper's Case 2) is captured by two invariants: * `K`: two processes that announced the same job and read each other's `next` registers, saw it (one of them has it in its `TRY` set); * `L`: a process `q` that announced job `i` and already read `next_p`, where `p` claims `i`, has `i` in its `TRY` or `DONE` set, or `i` is recorded in line `p` and `q` will still read that line.
