At-Most-Once with Nearly Optimal Effectiveness — PODC 2011

Sotirios Kentros, Aggelos Kiayias. Solving the At-Most-Once Problem with Nearly Optimal Effectiveness. PODC 2011. DOI 10.1145/1993806.1993847. Formalized version: arXiv 1107.2990 v2 (Dec 2013, the full TCS version with all proofs); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. KK_β never performs a job twice (Lemma 4.1), is wait-free for β ≥ m (Lemma 4.3), and with up to m − 1 crashes has effectiveness exactly n − (β + m − 2) (Theorem 4.4 for f = m − 1), which for β = m is the abstract's n − 2m + 2; Theorem 2.1 (E ≤ n − f for every algorithm) holds too. Theorem 4.4 claims the same value for every f ≤ m − 1, and with fewer crashes the effectiveness can be larger, so "=" becomes "≥" there, and the proof of Lemma 4.3 needs one definition changed. The work bounds, IterativeKK(ε) and Write-All were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 4.4 (equality for f < m − 1 crashes)

The theorem claims E_{KK_β}(n, m, f) = n − (β + m − 2) for every f ≤ m − 1, but with fewer crashes KK_β can be forced to perform more jobs: for m = 2, β = 4, n = 5 = β + m − 1 and f = 0 the formula gives 1, yet every fair execution performs at least 2 jobs (theorem_4_4_false_few_crashes).

Impact. The lower bound E ≥ n − (β + m − 2) holds for all f ≤ m − 1 (theorem_4_4_ge), and the value is exact for f = m − 1 (theorem_4_4), which is the case the abstract's n − 2m + 2 and its comparison with the bound n − m + 1 = n − f refer to. Only the "=" for f < m − 1 is false. The paper's matching adversary crashes m − 1 processes, so its proof covers only f = m − 1.

Details

With m = 2, β = 4, n = 5 and no crash, process 1 always picks the smallest available job, and process 2 always picks an element of rank ≥ 2. So process 2 never announces job 1 (u1), and process 1 performs job 1 in its first iteration (invA). Process 2's first target is job 3. While only job 1 is done, process 1 announces only jobs 1 or 2, so process 2's first check cannot fail and it cannot terminate before performing a job other than 1 (invB). By wait-freedom and fairness both processes terminate, so at least two jobs are performed (kk254_do_ge). Exhaustive model checking (kk_modelcheck.py) finds more counterexamples, also with crashes and well above n = β + m − 1: (n, m, β, f) = (9, 2, 6, 0) has E = 4 against 3, and (8, 3, 6, 1) has E = 2 against 1.

Repair

Theorem 4.4 with f = m − 1 (theorem_4_4), plus the lower bound for all f ≤ m − 1 (theorem_4_4_ge).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 4.4 (small n): for n ≤ β + m − 2 the formula n − (β + m − 2) is ≤ 0, the regime where the paper's guarantee is vacuous (fewer than about β + m jobs), but KK_β can be forced to perform jobs: for m = n = β = 2 and one crash the effectiveness is 1, not 0 (theorem_4_4_false_small). As long as no job has been performed, process 1 always targets job 1 and process 2 always targets job 2, so no check fails and no process can reach end before some job is done (noJob_inv); a fair run with at most one crash therefore performs a job (kk222_do_pos), and Theorem 2.1 gives E ≤ n − f = 1. Our exhaustive model checker shows the same for other small cases: n = m = β = 3 with f = 2 also has E = 1, against a formula value of −1. For n ≥ β + m − 1 and f = m − 1 the theorem holds as stated (theorem_4_4).

  • Lemma 4.2: stated for "any finite execution", which in the I/O-automaton model is trivially true (the input stop_p is always enabled), whereas the intended statement needs at most m − 1 crashes and an extension by a step of a live process; it holds in that reading (lemma_4_2_extend). We also prove the stronger form: while fewer than n − (β + m − 2) jobs are done, no process has terminated (lemma_4_2).

  • rank(FREE_p, TRY_p, p) is undefined when |FREE_p \ TRY_p| < p, which can only happen for β < m. The text nevertheless says correctness holds for β < m. We clamp the rank; safety holds for all β ≥ 1.

Typos

  • Fig. 1 declares TMP_p ∈ {0, …, n}, but compNext stores the rational (|FREE_p| − (m−1))/m in it.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 4.3

🔧 Lemma 4.3 (wait-freedom, the set POOL_α)

The proof claims that no process other than p₀ ever holds the job x₀ of rank k_{p₀} in POOL_α = J \ (J_α ∪ STUCK_α), but STUCK_α excludes only jobs of crashed processes, while a terminated process keeps its last, possibly abandoned and unperformed, job (maybe x₀) in next.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (lemma_4_3).

Repair

Define POOL as the unrecorded jobs minus the next values of all processes that stop taking steps, crashed or terminated (pool). With this change the paper's argument goes through. We prove a stronger statement: KK_β has no infinite execution at all, fair or not.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory with m deterministic processes (Fin m) and atomic read/write registers (Model.lean). Each process has exactly one enabled locally controlled action per state, as in the paper's I/O automaton.

  • KK_β is transcribed action by action from Figure 2 (kk, Algorithm.lean). FREE_p is not stored but defined as J \ DONE_p, which the paper's updates keep invariant.

  • Crashes are fairness exemptions: a run is Fair F if every process outside F steps infinitely often or terminates. Runs may stutter, so finite fair executions (all live processes terminated) and infinite ones are both runs. This matches the paper's fairexecs_f, where at most f processes receive stop.

  • The adversary is omniscient and online, i.e. it chooses any schedule. There is no randomness.

  • Do(α) counts the distinct jobs of {1, …, n} performed. Effectiveness is the infimum of Do over fair runs with at most f crashes (Definition 2.4).

Proof remarks.

  • Safety (Inv, Safety.lean) is one inductive invariant. Its core replaces the paper's case analysis on the order of events by two invariants. K: two processes that announced the same job and read each other saw it. L: a process that read next_p after p claimed the job has it in TRY or DONE, or will read it from p's line of done.

  • Lemma 4.3 is proved by contradiction (WaitFree.lean). First, after a time K0 the done matrix and the non-stepping processes are frozen. A measure shows that every live process keeps cycling through compNext … check. Then three stable predicates are established: Synced, TryFull and GoodNext. Finally the smallest live process picks x₀ and passes check.

  • Theorem 2.1 uses the standard round-robin adversary: it crashes a process exactly when the process is about to perform a new job. Such a job can never be performed by anyone else.

Not formalized. Section 5 (work O(nm log n log m) for β ≥ 3m², Lemmas 5.1–5.5, Theorem 5.6), Section 6 (IterativeKK(ε), Lemmas 6.1–6.2, Theorems 6.3–6.4) and Section 7 (Write-All, Theorem 7.1). For β = m and f < m − 1 the exact value is not proved: the formula agreed in every case we could model-check (m = 2, f = 0, n ≤ 7; m = 3, n = 5 with f ≤ 1 and n = 6 with f = 0).

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: the effectiveness bounds are attained by explicit fair runs (the adversary of Theorem 4.4 and the round-robin adversary of Theorem 2.1). The exhaustive model checker used for small parameters (outside Lean) is kk_modelcheck.py.
