Locally Checkable Proofs — PODC 2011

Mika Göös, Jukka Suomela. Locally Checkable Proofs. PODC 2011. DOI 10.1145/1993806.1993829. Formalized version: the journal version Locally Checkable Proofs in Distributed Computing, Theory of Computing 12(19), 2016 (33 pp., full proofs); numbering follows it.

PODC 2011 version vs. journal version. The PODC version (authors' copy: lcp-podc.pdf) has no numbered theorems; its results are stated in the text. Its §5 (the Θ(log n) results and the gluing proof), §6.1–6.2 (symmetric graphs, fixed-point-free symmetric trees) and §7.3 (coLCP(0) ⊆ LogLCP) make the same claims as the journal's Theorem 5.1, Theorems 6.1 and 6.3, and §7.3, with the same proofs. The only differences are the identifier layout of the cycles C(a,b) (identifiers up to about n² + 3n instead of n²; both are polynomial) and an even shorter 6.1 step ("if G₁, G₂ are not isomorphic, G must be asymmetric", with no argument). The journal's Theorem 7.1 (properties closed under lifts, port-numbering verifiers) is not in the PODC version. We formalized it anyway; its slip below exists only in the journal version.

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. The only mistakes are a typo in the gluing step of Theorem 5.1 and an imprecise honest proof in Theorem 7.1 of the journal version. Every formalized statement is true as written, and apart from these the paper's proofs go through. Theorem 6.4 (non-3-colourability), §4 and Theorem 7.2 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 5.1, gluing step: the text adds the edges {b_{i-1}[1], a_i[1]} "for each i > 1", which produces a path, not a kn-cycle; the definition b_0 = b_k and Figure 1c show that every i is meant (glued, glue_accept).

  • Theorem 7.1 (journal version only): the honest proof is described as P(v) = (H, v), but the encoded graph must have identifiers {1, …, n(G)}. One has to use a relabelled copy of H, which lies in P because isomorphisms are coverings (theorem_7_1).

Formalized.

  • the Ω(log n) gluing lower bound (Theorem 5.1) for odd n, non-bipartiteness, leader election, spanning trees and maximum matchings on cycles, together with the matching O(log n) spanning-tree schemes of §5.1;

  • the Θ(n²) bound for symmetric graphs (Theorem 6.1) and the Θ(n) bound for trees with a fixed-point-free symmetry (Theorem 6.3);

  • coLCP(0) ⊆ LogLCP (§7.3) and the colouring upper bounds of §2;

  • Theorem 7.1 (properties closed under lifts) with its converse (journal version only).

Model.

  • Deterministic, synchronous LOCAL verification with unique identifiers. Nodes are their identifiers: an instance Inst L is a Finset ℕ of nodes, a graph on ℕ with edges between nodes, marked edges S ≤ G (0/1 edge labels, for graph problems) and node labels lab : ℕ → L.

  • Proofs are bit strings ℕ → List Bool, and the proof size is the maximum length.

  • A verifier with horizon r is any function whose output at v depends only on the induced radius-r view: the ball V[v,r], the induced edges and marked edges, labels and proof labels (IsLocal). This is equivalent to the paper's A(G,P,v) = A(G[v,r],P[v,r],v). There are no computability or resource limits (Remark 2.1).

  • AdmitsLCP F P s is Definition 2.2. Graph problems also come in a weak version, AdmitsWeakLCP, where the scheme may choose the solution.

  • Lower bounds are stated for every family that contains the needed instances, and use the smallest identifier ranges the constructions need. Theorem 5.1 needs cycles with identifiers in {1,…,n²}; Theorems 6.1 and 6.3 need identifiers in {1,…,n}. Upper bounds use families with identifiers in {1,…,n^c}.

  • For unlabelled graph properties the family contains only unmarked instances (Plain), so the lower bounds are not weakened by extra instances.

  • Port-numbering verifiers (§7.1) are modelled conservatively: the output depends on the node's own label and the multiset of its neighbours' labels (IsPN). This is a sub-class of PN algorithms, so Theorem 7.1 with it is stronger.

  • Ω/o claims are stated as explicit inequalities s(n) ≥ c·log n − C, s(n) ≥ c·n², s(n) ≥ c·n, plus ¬ s =o(…). The constant c depends on the horizon r.

Proof remarks. A few proof steps are only sketched and a few details are imprecise; all are repaired or confirmed in Lean, and none affects a statement.

  • Theorem 5.1, graph problems with edge labels. The glued instance inherits only node data L(v) and P(v). For spanning trees and matchings the solution lives on edges, and the paper does not say what label the new edges {b_{i-1}[1], a_i[1]} get. We store the label of the edge to a node's successor at that node, so a new edge inherits the label of the removed edge {a_i[1], b_i[1]}, and we put these labels into the colour c(a,b). The argument then works as the paper says, and the lower bound holds for weak schemes too. (glue_accept, theorem_5_1_spanningTree, theorem_5_1_maxMatching)

  • Theorem 6.1. The paper says that "by the construction of G" every non-trivial automorphism of G₁ ⊛ G₂ swaps G₁ and G₂, and gives no argument. This is true in general, with a short argument (checked on paper, not in Lean). The connecting path lies in a run of degree-2 nodes with at least k nodes. Every other such run lies inside one copy and has fewer than k nodes. So ϕ fixes or reverses this run. If it reverses the run, the two sides have equal size, and ϕ maps the G₁ copy onto the G₂ copy. If it fixes the run, it restricts to automorphisms of G₁ and G₂, which are trivial. We sidestep the step with a smaller family of G₁: asymmetric graphs in which any two vertices have three common neighbours. In G₁ ⊛ G₂ the degree-2 nodes are then exactly the path nodes. We prove that this family still has at least 2^{C(k,2)}/2 labelled members, replacing the citation of Erdős–Rényi. (symmetric_lcp_lower_bound, exists_many_good)

  • Theorem 7.1. Left implicit: surjectivity of ϕ needs the encoded graph to be connected, which the check G ∈ P ⊆ F provides. (theorem_7_1)

  • §5.1 certifies non-bipartiteness by a counter "along the odd cycle". It certifies only an odd closed walk, which also rules out a 2-colouring. Our scheme uses two BFS-like trees instead (nonBipartite_logLCP).

Not formalized.

  • §4: reachability, connectivity, bipartite matching.

  • Theorem 6.4 (non-3-colourability Ω(n²/log n)). Its fooling-set step is elementary, but the 3-colouring gadgets are not formalized.

  • Theorem 7.2 (alternative characterizations of LogLCP); §7.4–7.5.

  • The LCP(0) membership claims; the O(log n) upper bound for maximum matching on cycles (Table 2).

  • The Hamiltonian-cycle entry of Table 2: it lists Θ(log n), but §5 gives no lower-bound argument for it. The cycle gluing does not apply: the glued all-marked 2n-cycle is a Hamiltonian cycle. We did not look further.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

  • Every lower-bound theorem is paired with a matching upper bound (*_theta), so its hypotheses are satisfiable.
