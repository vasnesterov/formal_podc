import Papers.PODC2011.LocallyCheckableProofs.Universal /-! # Theorem 7.1: properties closed under lifts and port-numbering verifiers (Section 7.1) * `IsCovering ϕ H G`: `ϕ` maps `V(H)` onto `V(G)` and, for every node `v` of `H`, maps the neighbours of `v` bijectively onto the neighbours of `ϕ v` (so it is a homomorphism). `H` is a *lift* of `G` (`IsLift H G`); `P` is *closed under lifts* in `F` (`ClosedUnderLifts F P`) if `G ∈ F ∩ P` implies `H ∈ P` for every lift `H ∈ F` of `G`. * **Port-numbering verifiers.** We model a PN verifier conservatively by `IsPN A`: the output of `v` is a function of its own proof label and of the *multiset* of the proof labels of its neighbours only (no identifiers, no port numbers; the degree is the size of the multiset). Every such verifier is implementable by a one-round PN algorithm, so positive results with this class are at least as strong as with PN algorithms. It has local horizon `1` (`IsPN.isLocal`), hence `AdmitsPNLCP F P s → AdmitsLCP F P s`. * `IsPN.fooled`: a PN verifier is fooled by lifts, `A(H, P ∘ ϕ, v) = A(G, P, ϕ v)`; hence every property admitting PN-checkable proofs (of any size) is closed under lifts (`closedUnderLifts_of_admitsPNLCP`, the remark before Theorem 7.1). * `theorem_7_1` (**Theorem 7.1**): on connected unlabelled graphs (any identifiers), a property closed under lifts admits PN-checkable proofs of size `24 n²`; `theorem_7_1_idBound`: the same on the family of connected unlabelled graphs with identifiers in `[1, n^c]`, `c ≥ 1`. `closedUnderLifts_iff_admitsPNLCP`: the resulting characterisation. The scheme is stated once for any graph description `dec : K → Inst Unit` with an injective bit encoding (`admitsPNLCP_of_codes`; reused for trees in `TreeUpper.lean`). The proof label of `v` is a pair `(description of a graph G, ϕ(v))`. The honest proof uses the canonical copy `canon H` of `H` with identifiers `1, …, n` (enumerating `V(H)` by `Finset.toList`) and `ϕ(v)` the new identifier of `v`. Node `v` checks: `G ∈ F ∩ P`; `ϕ(v) ∈ V(G)`; all neighbours hold the same code; the multiset of the neighbours' `ϕ`-values is exactly the set of neighbours of `ϕ(v)` in `G` (equivalent to the paper's
