import Cslib.Probability.Finite.Uniform import Mathlib /-! # Finite uniform probability for the sampling argument of Section 2 The conversion of Theorem 2.1 puts every vertex into the "fault set" `J` independently with probability `p = 1 - 1/m` (the paper uses `m = r`, and `m = 2` when `r = 1`). We realise this exactly on a *finite uniform* sample space: every vertex draws a uniform label in `Fin m` and survives (is not in `J`) iff its label is `0`. Repeating `α` times independently is the uniform distribution on `Fin α → V → Fin m`. Probabilities are then ratios of cardinalities (`uprob`, from the shared finite-probability library `Cslib/Probability/Finite/Uniform.lean`, with the union bound `uprob_exists_le` and independence `uprob_pi`), and no measure theory is needed.
