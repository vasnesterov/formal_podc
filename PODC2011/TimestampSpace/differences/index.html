Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Space Complexity of Timestamps — PODC 2011.

Paper: Helmi, Higham, Pacheco, Woelfel, The space complexity of long-lived and one-shot timestamp implementations, PODC 2011; formalized from the full version arXiv:1103.5794v2 (numbering as there).

Classification: [false] false statement, [degenerate] false only in a degenerate case, [gap] proof gap with true statement, [spec] under-specified definition, [typo], [model] modelling choice / generalization.

Model and definitions

  1. [model] Deterministic processes. The paper allows nondeterministic processes and proves its lower bounds for "non-deterministic solo-termination". We use CSLib's deterministic Impl, where this notion is obstruction-freedom (SoloTerm, soloTerm_false_iff). The paper itself fixes the nondeterministic choices in its proofs.

  2. [spec] Covering for nondeterministic processes. The paper says p covers r "if there is a non-deterministic decision such that the one-step execution is a write to r". With nondeterminism a process may then cover several registers at once. This breaks the signature counting of Section 3 ("∑ cᵢ = k" for k covering processes) and the counting of Section 4. With fixed choices (deterministic processes) every process covers at most one object (Impl.Covers.unique); both arguments need this.

  3. [spec] Timestamp specification.

    • We additionally require that every completed getTS returns a timestamp (TSCorrect.getTS_ts). The paper leaves this implicit. Without it a getTS returning a Boolean makes the specification vacuous.

    • "Any later compare" is read as "any compare invoked after the later getTS returned". This is the weakest reading, so it makes the lower bounds strongest.

    • compare may access shared memory (non-static timestamps, as in the paper).

    • The universe T is arbitrary, and may be finite; the lower bounds do not use unboundedness.

  4. [model] Base objects. The lower bounds hold for any finite family of read/write-like objects (RWLike: every primitive is read-only or a blind overwrite). Registers with arbitrary, possibly different, value types and arbitrary initial values are the main example. The historyless extension of Theorem 1.2 (Section 7) is not formalized.

  5. [model] One-shot. "Each process calls getTS at most once" (Adm true). The number of compare calls is unrestricted, and correctness and progress are only required on such executions.

  6. [model] Invocations are separate steps (CSLib labels inv/tau/ret), and idle processes are those that never invoked anything. A quiescent or idle process never covers an object.

Lemma 2.1 (Ellen, Fatourou, Ruppert)

  1. [model] Form of the lemma. Formalized (lemma_2_1) in the form used by the paper. The complete getTS of the Uᵢ-only execution βᵢ is by a process qᵢ ∈ Uᵢ that is idle in C and invokes only this getTS in βᵢ. The other processes of Uᵢ are arbitrary. The hypothesis "does not write outside R" is weakened to "the memory outside R is unchanged at the end of βᵢ", which makes the lemma stronger. The paper cites this lemma without proof; we prove it. The proof uses the compare operation: after the two orders πB₀β₀πB₁β₁πB₂ and πB₁β₁πB₀β₀πB₂, which reach the same configuration, q₀ runs compare(t₀,t₁) solo.

Section 3 (Theorem 1.1, long-lived)

  1. [model] Lemma 3.2 generalized to any set S of ≥ 2k processes instead of P_{2k} (lemma_3_2).

  2. [gap] Lemma 3.1, missing hypothesis. The conclusion is stated "for any quiescent configuration D", but the proof applies the hypothesis to later configurations and uses solo-termination, so it needs D to be reachable. We assume this (lemma_3_1).

  3. [gap] Commutation of the first two block writes. The proof of Lemma 3.2 uses πBᵢπB₁₋ᵢπB₂η(C₀) = C₁ without justification. It holds because πB₂ obliterates whichever of the two first block writes came last (blockWrite_swap).

  4. [gap] Three disjoint covering sets in Lemma 3.1. Their existence relies on every process covering at most one register. This is true for fixed nondeterministic choices (see 2) and implicit in the paper (exists_triple).

  5. [typo] In the proof of Lemma 3.2, "πBᵢλπB₁₋ᵢπBⱼη(C₀)" should be πB₂.

  6. Theorem 1.1 is proved in the form ⌊n/2⌋ ≤ 3m (theorem_1_1), hence m ≥ ⌊n/6⌋ (theorem_1_1_div) and m > n/6 - 1 (theorem_1_1_real), as the paper states.

Section 4 (Lemma 4.1, Theorem 1.2, one-shot)

  1. [model] Lemma 4.1 output. lemma_4_1 returns the schedule as moves (process, labels): πc₀ M₀ πc₁ M₁ with {c₀, c₁} = {B₀, B₁}, |M₀| + |M₁| = |U| - 1 and |M₀| ≥ ⌊|U|/2⌋. Each process of M₀/M₁ covers an object outside R right after its own segment, and hence also at the end (SD.moved_U). This is the paper's (a)–(f) plus the per-prefix information the proof of Theorem 1.2 needs. The proof is the paper's induction (two_choice, iter, assemble).

  2. [gap] Case 2 counting, and an off-by-one in the log term. The paper claims |participants(σσ')| ≥ ⌈|idle(C_k)|/2⌉ in Case 2. But γ_{k+1} may cut σ', so only the participants of σ (at least ⌊|idle|/2⌋) are guaranteed to leave the idle set. The idle set therefore shrinks to ≤ ⌈|idle|/2⌉, not ≤ ⌊|idle|/2⌋, and Case 2 occurs at most ⌈log₂ n⌉ times, not log n times. For example, idle sizes 5 → 3 → 2 → 1 allow three Case-2 steps, while log₂ 5 ≈ 2.3. The proof therefore gives #objects ≥ ⌊√(2n)⌋ - ⌈log₂ n⌉ - 2, not ⌊√(2n)⌋ - log n - 2. Theorem 1.2's √(2n) - log n - O(1) is unaffected (theorem_1_2; theorem_1_2_real gives > √(2n) - log₂ n - 4). Also [typo] "idle(Cᵢ) < idle(Cᵢ₊₁)" should be >.

  3. [gap] Bound on poised(C_{k+1}, Q) when γ_{k+1} is empty. The paper derives |poised(C_{k+1}, Q)| ≤ Σ(ℓ_k - c - 1) + 1 from the minimality of γ_{k+1}. After a Case-2 step ℓ decreases, so the next configuration may already contain a full set Q' w.r.t. the new ℓ. Then γ_{k+2} is empty and minimality says nothing. Our invariant keeps sparsity w.r.t. the previous threshold, Inv.sparse: no nonempty S ⊆ R̄ has all counts ≥ ℓ + 1 - j - |S|. This is preserved in both cases and suffices for all bounds.

  4. [gap] The final counting ("|idle(C_last)| ≤ 1 is impossible"). The paper uses "each Cᵢ is ℓᵢ-constrained", which is not part of the stated construction invariant (a)–(e) and is not proved for the columns in Rᵢ. It also uses an "association" of processes to the last register they cover, with g(r) = |poised(C_{f(r)}, {r})|. This equality is inexact: block-writers can cover other registers after their write, and a process may be associated with a register it does not cover at time f(r). We replace this with an inductive invariant, Inv.nbound: the number of non-idle processes covering no object outside R is at most Σ_{c=1}^{j}(m - c). Together with Inv.sparse it gives n - |idle| ≤ m(m-1)/2 ≤ n - 2, so at least two processes are always idle (idle_ge_two). This also gives the paper's invariant (c), which we do not need to carry.

  5. [gap] Counting equality in the step. The paper says |poised(C_k, R̄_k)| + |idle(C_k)| = |poised(γ_{k+1}(C_k), R̄_k)| + |idle(γ_{k+1}(C_k))|. After their write, block-writers may cover registers outside R_k, so only ≤ holds. This is the direction needed.

  6. [spec] "Every register in R_last is covered by at least one process". This may fail: the threshold ℓ - j can be ≤ 0 in the last step. It is not needed: only |R_last| ≤ #objects is used.

  7. [model] log is read as log₂; m = ⌊√(2n)⌋ is Nat.sqrt (2n). We assume n ≥ 3, as the paper does, and #objects ≤ m (otherwise the bound is trivial).

Section 5 (Lemma 5.1, simple one-shot algorithm)

  1. [typo] The comment says register R[i] is written by processes 2i and 2i+1, but the code (i = ⌈p/2⌉, 1-based) gives processes 2i-1 and 2i. We use 0-based ids with register p/2.

  2. [spec] sum := sum + R[i] is ambiguous: it may re-read R[i] or use the value just written. Both readings are formalized (reread flag) and both are correct.

  3. [model] R[i] := R[i] + 1 is a read followed by a separate write.

  4. The proof is correct. Its key step, "registers never decrease", really needs one-shot: with two getTS calls per process the algorithm is incorrect, as shown by a Lean counterexample for n = 2 (SimpleTS.cex_not_correct, SimpleTS.not_isTS_longLived).

Section 6 (Algorithm 4, Theorem 1.3)

  1. [model] Atomic scan. As in the paper ("we treat it as atomic"), scan is atomic. In CSLib an atomic multi-register scan needs a single base object, so the memory is one object holding an unbounded array ℕ → Option Val (1-based), with primitives read i, write i v and scan k (returns R[1..k]; the algorithm scans R[1..myrnd+1], the only entries it uses). "Uses m registers" means that every accessed index lies in [1, m]. The Afek et al. double collect is not verified (it would be correct here: every write to a given register writes a distinct value). Consequently the upper bound's base object is not RWLike.

  2. [model] Line 2 reuses the value read at line 1. Line 10 is a separate read, as in the proof of Claim 6.13. The getTS-id of an invocation is (p, number of completed operations of p), which is distinct per invocation.

  3. [gap] Lemma 6.5's final count. "Σ_{ϕ=1}^{Φ} ϕ ≤ 2M" counts the last, possibly incomplete, phase as having Φ invalidation writes. Claim 6.10 only covers completed phases; the honest count gives ⌈2√M⌉ + 1 registers. The statement is still true. Repair: the getTS that starts a phase never makes a "first invalidation" (I₃) write, since its line-11 writes are in phase myrnd and hence not invalidations by Claim 6.11. Its unused credit gives #invalidations + Φ ≤ 2M, hence K² + K + 2 ≤ 4M for every written index K. This recovers exactly ⌈2√M⌉ (sq_le_of_written, alg4_written_lt, alg4_space).

  4. Correctness (Lemma 6.4) holds for every execution, with no bound on the number of getTS calls (alg4_correct). Wait-freedom (Lemma 6.14) is proved for executions with at most M getTS calls (alg4_waitFreeUpTo), as stated. Obstruction-freedom holds in general (alg4_obstructionFree).

  5. Bounded model checking (atomic scan, all interleavings, M ≤ 5; random schedules up to M = 25) found no violation; the largest written index observed is about √(2M).

Model notes moved from the paper review

  1. [spec] Nondeterministic covering (see 2): with nondeterministic choices "p covers r" lets a process cover several registers. This is a definitional imprecision, not an error of the proofs, which fix the choices.

  2. Algorithm 2 needs one-shot (see 24): this matches the paper's claim, which is only for one-shot. The counterexample shows the restriction is essential, not that the paper is wrong.

  3. Non-vacuity of Theorem 1.1. The paper does not need this, but we add the folklore "max + 1" long-lived algorithm from n registers (MaxAlg.lean, MaxTS.maxTS_isTS).

Not formalized

  • The historyless-object version of Theorem 1.2 (Section 7 remark).

  • Nondeterministic and randomized implementations (see 1, 2). They reduce to deterministic ones with the same registers: before each step, read all registers and take the choice with the shortest solo-termination distance. This reduction is not formalized.
