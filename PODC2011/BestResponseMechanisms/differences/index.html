Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Best-Response Mechanisms — PODC 2011.

Paper: Nisan, Schapira, Valiant, Zohar, Best-Response Mechanisms (ICS 2011, full version; PODC 2011 brief announcement Incentive-compatible distributed greedy protocols). Numbering follows the full version.

Section 2 (framework, Theorem 2.7)

Mistakes

  • 🔧 Theorem 2.7 (2), number of rounds. The theorem claims incentive compatibility for every activation order with at least T = e_G rounds. False: Counterexamples.theorem_2_7_false (4 players, binary strategies, strict utilities, e_G = 2, clear outcome; after 2 rounds of round robin the deviator ends with utility 2 instead of 1). Under the alternative reading of Definition 2.4 with one player per elimination step, Counterexamples.theorem_2_7_false_single_steps (e_G ≤ 4, deviation pays after 4 rounds). The proof shows that the deviator i is confined after tᵢ rounds, tᵢ being the index of the clear-outcome witness for i, and tᵢ can exceed e_G. Repair (proved): theorem_2_7_incentive_compatible with ≥ max(e_G, tᵢ) rounds; informal_theorem: Σᵢ|Sᵢ| - n rounds (the bound of the informal theorem of Section 1.3, which is correct) via ClearWitness.exists_short.

  • 🔧 Extension (1) after Theorem 2.7 (outdated information). With the paper's definition of a round ("each player is active at least once"), convergence fails when best responses are computed from outdated information: Counterexamples.outdated_information_false (every player best-responds at every step, to information from time 0; the run never converges). Repair (proved): rounds measured by information freshness, AsyncRounds (from the end of round k+1 on, every current strategy is a best response to information produced since the start of round k+1); converges, deviator_utility_le.

  • ✏️ Remark after Definition 2.3 ("elimination of NBR strategies has a unique order-independent outcome, that is a pure Nash equilibrium"). False for infinite strategy spaces, which the paper explicitly allows: Counterexamples.nbr_outcome_not_unique (one player, S = ℕ, u(x) = x: every x is a one-step outcome and none is an equilibrium). True when tie-broken best responses exist (HasBR; automatic for finite games with strict total orders, hasBR_of_finite): Solves.unique, Solves.isNash. Every best-response mechanism presupposes HasBR (implied assumption).

Modelling choices and imprecisions

  • Ex post, one realization. Incomplete information enters only through "for every realization of (u₁,…,uₙ)". We fix one realization: a TieBreakGame (utilities and tie-breaking orders); all theorems are for an arbitrary game, hence for every realization. Uncoupledness of tie-breaking (it may depend only on uᵢ) plays no role in the proofs.

  • Utilities take values in an arbitrary linear order R (paper: reals); the lim sup version uses ℝ and assumes utilities bounded below (needed for lim sup in ℝ to be meaningful; true in finite games).

  • Tie-breaking (Def. 2.1, "a full order"): the definitions take an arbitrary relation prec; total strict order is assumed only where needed (hasBR_of_finite). Def. 2.2 is literal (u(sᵢ) < u(s'ᵢ) ∨ (= ∧ sᵢ ≺ s'ᵢ)).

  • Subgames are products of strategy sets; an elimination step removes, simultaneously for any set of players, strategies that are NBR in the current subgame (Def. 2.4's "removal of sets of NBR strategies"). NBR is evaluated in the current subgame (both the opponents' profiles and the beating strategy range over the subgame), as in the paper.

  • Clear outcome (Def. 2.6): ClearWitness s i E r k asks for an index k with E k i = Sᵢ and s globally optimal for i in E k; the paper takes k = first step that eliminates a strategy of i. Equivalent: optimality is inherited by smaller subgames (ClearWitness.of_le). The order must end at s* (the paper: "order of elimination", which in finite games always ends at s*).

  • Globally optimal (Def. 2.5) is relative to the current subgame, as the paper uses it.

  • Best-response mechanism. Mechanism = starting profile + adaptive activation rule (a set of active players, possibly several, as a function of the whole history). A repeated-game strategy of a player maps histories to announcements; the best-response strategy announces br i (current profile) for a fixed tie-broken best-response selection br (IsBRRule). Total utility: last-stage utility at a horizon N (IsIncentiveCompatible), or lim sup (theorem_2_7_limsup). IC = repeated best response is a pure Nash equilibrium of the repeated game, quantified over all repeated-game strategies of the deviator (any history dependence).

  • Rounds (HasRounds): consecutive windows [T k, T (k+1)) in each of which every player is active, with T m ≤ N. Since activation may be adaptive, the round count is a hypothesis on every run (∀ σ, HasRounds (M.acts σ) N m); for round robin it holds for all runs (roundRobin_hasRounds).

  • Extension (3) (stop when all players pass) is formalized statically: a profile where all pass is s* (eq_of_all_pass), and a profile where all players but i pass gives i at most uᵢ(s*) (utility_le_of_others_pass); no round bound is needed for it.

  • Extension (2) (adaptive orders) is built into Mechanism.active and the per-run AsyncRounds.

  • Proof of Theorem 2.7. Convergence: as in the paper, via the survival of tie-broken best responses (IsElimSeq.mem_of_isBR), stated for an arbitrary NBR sequence of length r (paper: r = e_G). IC: the paper's argument ("after tᵢ − 1 rounds the game is effectively G_{tᵢ}") is correct for tᵢ rounds (deviator_utility_le); only the final round count is wrong (see above).

  • The paper's examples: Figure 1 (figure_1: NBR-solvable, not IC with round robin), Figure 3 (figure_3: NBR-solvable with clear outcome, both players prefer (C, C); collusion-proofness is not defined formally beyond this example).

  • Section 4 (collusion-proofness of the applications) and Section 3.4 (TCP games, Theorem 3.9) are not formalized.

Section 3.2 (cost sharing, Theorems 3.3, 3.4)

  • ✏️ Tie-breaking rule "if |s − vᵢ| ≤ |t − vᵢ| then t ≺ᵢ s" is reflexive, so by Def. 2.2 every bid is NBR in every subgame (gameLit_isNBR) and every profile is a one-step outcome (gameLit_solves). Repaired to "strictly closer to vᵢ wins; at equal distance the lower bid wins" (Closer, a strict total order).

  • 🔧 (proof gap, statement true) e_G ≤ n: the paper's elimination has 3 steps, so it only gives e_G ≤ 3; for n = 1, 2 we give separate sequences (elimParam_le_card).

  • The serviced set is defined as the union of all feasible sets; serviced_unique_maxCard shows it is the unique maximum-cardinality feasible set, so the paper's lexicographic tie-breaking among such sets never applies.

  • The paper's vᵢ > 0 is not needed. Cost shares are ℝ≥0-valued, cross-monotonic (CostSharingRule).

  • "Reasonable cost shares as in the Moulin mechanism": Moulin's mechanism (iteratively drop users with vᵢ < cᵢ(S)) is formalized (moulinSet); moulinSet_eq shows its set is the serviced set at b = v; the outcome s* services exactly that set and charges cⱼ(R_v) ≤ vⱼ (theorem_3_3).

  • Clear outcome: the player-specific order is ours (others first drop bids above their values; then s* is optimal for i, witness index 1, clearWitness); the paper's description of this order is garbled but the idea is the same.

  • Theorem 3.4: the mechanism's bid rule is a tie-broken best response (isBR_mechBid); the round-robin mechanism with n² steps (n rounds) is IC and outputs s* (Applications.theorem_3_4), via the corrected Theorem 2.7 (e_G ≤ n, witness index 1 ≤ n).

Section 3.1 (stable roommates, Theorems 3.1, 3.2)

  • ✏️ (implied assumption) Theorem 3.1, intern–hospital case. The paper's game lets every student propose to every other student (Sᵢ = {j ≠ i}), and intern–hospital markets are "the special case where all hospitals have the same ranking of interns". Such markets need not be cycle-free (interns may rank interns cyclically), and the theorem fails: internHospital_counterexample (3 interns with cyclic preferences, 1 hospital ranked last by everybody): not cycle-free, not NBR-solvable for any irreflexive tie-breaking, no stable matching (engine: subset_elimSeq_of_isBR, not_nbrSolvable_of_isBR: a product subgame in which every strategy is a best response to something survives every elimination). Repair (proved): bipartite strategy spaces (interns propose to hospitals and vice versa), cycleFree_of_bipartite, theorem_3_1_internHospital; this is what the paper's cycle argument ("any cycle must include a hospital placed after a desired intern and before a less desired one") presupposes. Labeled ✏️ rather than 🔧 (review): intern–hospital matching is bipartite by definition (interns have no preferences over interns), the counterexample uses intern–intern preferences, and the paper's proof presupposes the bipartite structure.

  • ✏️ "G's unique PNE": for an odd number of students the last student has all strategies at utility 0, and there are several PNEs (exists_two_nash, a 3-student correlated market). The PNE is unique only under tie-breaking (theorem_3_1: the outcome of every NBR elimination), and all PNEs found have the same matching (not formalized in general).

  • 🔧 (proof gap) Clear outcome. Theorem 3.1 does not state, and the proof does not argue, that the outcome is a clear outcome, which Theorem 3.2 needs; it holds with the same elimination sequence (theorem_3_1, clearWitness_of_good).

  • Model: acceptability relation acc (symmetric, irreflexive; paper: acc = (≠)), ranks α i j > 0 injective on acceptable partners (paper: ranks 1, 2, …; only order and positivity matter). Utility exactly as in the paper (footnote 5's game). The matching of a profile = mutual proposals (matchingOf); stability: no acceptable pair preferring each other to their matches, unmatched = worst.

  • CycleFree follows the paper but quantifies only over cycles of distinct students (a weaker hypothesis, so a stronger theorem).

  • Correlated markets: weights w symmetric with values in any linear order, ranks order-compatible with w (cycleFree_of_correlated, theorem_3_1_correlated).

  • e_G ≤ n: our sequence fixes a pair (or a student without acceptable partners left) per step, ≤ n steps.

  • Theorem 3.2: the announcement rule ("most preferred student to whom I can make a better offer") is a tie-broken best response whenever a better offer exists (mechanism_isBR); when none exists the paper leaves the announcement unspecified, so Theorem 3.2 is stated for any tie-broken best-response rule (Applications.theorem_3_2: round robin, n² steps, IC, final matching stable). The mechanism's output (pairs announcing each other) is matchingOf.

Section 3.3 (BGP, Theorems 3.5, 3.6)

  • ✏️ Theorem 3.6 omits "No Dispute Wheel", which the proof uses ("which we assumed is not contained in the graph") and the surrounding text states. Without it the theorem is false: BAD GADGET has no pure Nash equilibrium, hence is not NBR-solvable (badGadget_noNash, badGadget_noNash', badGadget_not_NBRSolvable).

  • ✏️ Connectivity of the graph is implicit (a node without any route has all actions at utility 0: ties); assumed.

  • 🔧 (proof gap) "If no such node exists, a₀ can guarantee its most preferred route." Not as stated: downstream unfixed nodes on R₀ may deviate. It is the last unfixed node on R₀ that can guarantee its route. Our exists_guarantee takes a_{j+1} := last unfixed node on R(a_j) and derives a dispute wheel from a cycle.

  • Model: SimpleGraph V, destination d, players = {v // v ≠ d}, strategies = neighbours; the route of i under f follows next hops and exists iff d is reached without repetition; ranks positive and injective on simple routes (paper: ranks 1, 2, …). A dispute wheel requires pivots ≠ d and RᵢQᵢ₊₁ to be a simple route (rankings are only defined on simple routes); this makes NoDisputeWheel weaker and the theorem stronger.

  • All eliminations are strict, so Theorem 3.6 holds for every tie-breaking rule, and the PNE is unique outright (theorem_3_6); "stable routing tree" = every node has a route (IsRoutingTree) and the profile is a PNE.

  • Theorem 3.5 ([7]) is derived from Theorems 3.6 and 2.7 (corrected) for best-response mechanisms with ≥ n rounds (Applications.theorem_3_5); the asynchronous, outdated-information BGP model of [7] is covered by AsyncRounds (converges, deviator_utility_le) with rounds defined by information freshness.

  • Section 4 (collusion-proofness of the stable-roommates, BGP and TCP mechanisms); only Figure 3's example.
