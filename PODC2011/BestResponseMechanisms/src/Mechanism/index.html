import Podc.Foundations.GameTheory.NeverBestResponse /-! # Best-response mechanisms and Theorem 2.7 [NSVZ] N. Nisan, M. Schapira, G. Valiant, A. Zohar, *Best-Response Mechanisms*, ICS 2011 (brief announcement *Incentive-compatible distributed greedy protocols*, PODC 2011). We fix one realization of the private utilities (the theorem is ex-post, i.e. per realization), so the base game is a `TieBreakGame ι S R`. ## Two layers 1. **Asynchronous runs** (`FreshBR`, `AsyncRounds`). A run is any sequence of profiles `s : ℕ → ∀ i, S i`. We do not fix how players are scheduled; instead, the hypothesis `AsyncRounds G s P T m` says that the time points `T 0, T 1, …, T m` delimit `m` *rounds* for the players in `P`: from time `T (k + 1)` on, the current strategy of every player of `P` is a tie-broken best response to *some* profile whose entries were all current at times `≥ T k`. This covers sequential activations, simultaneous activations, outdated information and adaptive (history-dependent) schedules, extensions (1) and (2) after Theorem 2.7: for outdated information the round boundaries must be placed so that information read in round `k + 1` was produced in round `k` or later (see `Counterexamples.lean` for why a round cannot just be "every player was active once"). * `converges` (Theorem 2.7 (1)): after `r` rounds, `s t = s*` (where `E 0, …, E r` is any NBR-elimination sequence ending at `s*`). * `deviator_utility_le` (core of Theorem 2.7 (2)): if all players but `i` best-respond, then after `k` rounds, where `k` is the index of a clear-outcome witness for `i`, the stage utility of `i` never exceeds `u_i(s*)`, whatever `i` does. * `eq_of_all_pass`, `utility_le_of_others_pass`: extension (3) (termination when all players pass): a profile at which everybody passes is `s*`, and a profile at which everybody but `i` passes gives `i` at most `u_i(s*)`. 2. **The repeated game** (`Mechanism`, `run`, `IsIncentiveCompatible`). A repeated-response mechanism: an initial profile, an adaptive activation rule (who is active next, as a function of the history), and a horizon. A player's strategy in the repeated game maps histories to announcements; a best-response mechanism prescribes a tie-broken best response to the current profile. Total utility: last-stage utility (finite horizon) or `lim sup` of stage utilities. * `theorem_2_7_convergence`, `theorem_2_7_incentive_compatible`: the corrected Theorem 2.7: the mechanism converges to `s*` once every run has `≥ r` rounds, and is incentive compatible (repeated best response is a pure Nash equilibrium of the repeated game) once every run has at least `max r kᵢ` rounds, `kᵢ` being the indices of the clear-outcome witnesses. `Counterexamples.theorem_2_7_false` shows that `e_G` rounds (the paper's bound) do not suffice. * `theorem_2_7_limsup`: the same for infinite runs with `lim sup` utilities. * `informal_theorem`: in finite games `Σᵢ |Sᵢ| - n` rounds suffice (the bound of the informal theorem of Section 1.3). * `roundRobin`, `roundRobin_hasRounds`: round robin on `Fin (n + 1)`;
