Each source node `i` has a private strict ranking of all simple routes from `i` to `d` (`Rankings`: a positive number for each route, injective on the simple routes of `i`; larger is better). In the *BGP game* * the players are the source nodes (`Player d`), * the strategies of `i` are its outgoing edges, i.e. its neighbours (`Strategy G d i`), * given a forwarding profile `f`, the utility of `i` is the rank of the simple route from `i` to `d` obtained by following the next hops of `f` (`route f i`), or `0` if there is no such route. ## Main statements * `theorem_3_6` (**Theorem 3.6**): if `G` is connected and has *No Dispute Wheel* (`NoDisputeWheel`, an assumption used but not stated in the paper), then for every tie-breaking rule the BGP game is NBR-solvable in at most `n` steps (`n` = number of source nodes) with a clear outcome `s`; `s` is a stable routing tree, and it is the unique pure Nash equilibrium. All eliminations are *strict* (the eliminated strategy is strictly worse), so tie-breaking plays no role. * `exists_guarantee`: the dispute-wheel argument of the proof sketch. We take as `a₁` the *last* non-fixed node on `a₀`'s best existing route `R₀` (not the paper's "closest to `d` that prefers another route": when no such node exists, it is this node, not `a₀`, that can guarantee its route).
