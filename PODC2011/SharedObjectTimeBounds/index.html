Time Bounds for Shared Objects in Partially Synchronous Systems — PODC 2011

Jiaqi Wang, Jennifer L. Welch, Hyunyoung Lee. Brief Announcement: Time Bounds for Shared Objects in Partially Synchronous Systems. PODC 2011. DOI 10.1145/1993806.1993877. Formalized version: the full version, J. Wang's MS thesis of the same title (Texas A&M, December 2011, advisor J. L. Welch); numbering follows the thesis (Chapter IV for the lower bounds).

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The d + min {ε, u, d/3} lower bounds hold: for every strongly immediately non-self-commuting operation (read-modify-write, dequeue, pop; Theorem C.1) and for |OP| + |AOP| under the hypotheses of Theorem E.1. The (1 - 1/k) u bound of Theorem D.1, one of the three main results, is established only under a stronger hypothesis, which covers writes and every k = 2, and for skew ε ≥ (1 - 1/k) u; the claim that Theorem E.1 covers push/peek on a stack is false. The upper bounds of Chapter V were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ §IV.E and Table III (push and peek on a stack)

The thesis says that Theorem E.1 "can be applied to push and peek on a stack" and lists d + min {ε, u, d/3} as a lower bound for |push| + |peek|, but push/peek never satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem E.1 (stack_push_peek_not_E1).

Impact. The push+peek entry of Table III (and the same claim in Chapter VI) is unproved, and we have neither a proof nor a refutation of the bound itself; the theorem itself and its application to enqueue/peek on a queue are fine (queue_E1_hyps). For E.1's hypotheses, a peek after pushes behaves like a read after writes, a case the thesis itself excludes from Theorem E.1 (overwriting).

Details

Hypothesis A asks that exactly one of ρ ∘ op₁ ∘ aop₁ and ρ ∘ op₂ ∘ op₁ ∘ aop₁ be legal. With op₁, op₂ pushes and aop₁ a peek, both sequences leave the element of op₁ on top, so they are legal together or illegal together (as soon as hypothesis C makes op₁, op₂ legal instances). A peek of a queue sees the first element, which is why enqueue/peek works.

⚠️ Theorem D.1 of Chapter IV (Step 1.1 of the proof)

Step 1.1 asserts a single legal permutation π of the k concurrent operations that fixes the results of every later sequence of operations; correctness does not give this, and without it the runs of the proof yield no contradiction (tr_answers).

Impact. Theorem D.1, a main result of the thesis (abstract), is unestablished in its stated generality; we have neither a proof nor a counterexample. We prove it under a stronger hypothesis, LastRevealing (one fixed query sequence whose results determine the last of the k operations). This covers writes with k = n (write_lastRevealing) and every case k = 2 (theorem_D1_two).

Details

Correctness gives a linearization for each complete run; an algorithm may decide the order of concurrent operations lazily, depending on later operations. The proof's runs R₁ and its shifts R₂^z only imply: the answers of later queries are compatible, for every z, with some permutation that respects the real-time order of R₂^z. If |OP| is close to (1 - 1/k) u (above (1 - 2/k) u), that order only puts op_z before op_{z+1 mod k}, so op_z is not last. For the "test-and-reset" register (writes W a; T a answers whether the value is a and resets it), the writes satisfy the theorem's hypothesis (tr_nonEquiv), yet for k = 3 every query sequence has answers compatible, for every z, with a permutation whose last write is not z (tr_answers; only the last write matters, so one with op_z before op_{z+1} exists). So the hypothesis does not imply LastRevealing (tr_not_lastRevealing). We do not know whether Theorem D.1 itself fails for this object.

Repair

theorem_D1: with ε ≥ (1 - 1/k) u and LastRevealing, |OP| ≥ (1 - 1/k) u. The thesis's shifts and its six-case delay check are correct (after the typo in the shift, see Slips).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem D.1 (clock skew): the run R₂ of the proof has skew (1 - 1/k) u, but the theorem allows any ε; the bound is established only for ε ≥ (1 - 1/k) u (theorem_D1). The proof states that it assumes the optimal skew ε = (1 - 1/n) u ≥ (1 - 1/k) u, and Chapter V makes the same assumption. For smaller ε the bound appears to fail: Algorithm 1 of Chapter V answers pure mutators (writes) after ε (not formalized).

  • Definitions D.1, D.2, D.4: read literally, an accessor has a legal ρ with ρ ∘ op illegal, so ρ ∘ op is not equivalent to ρ and op is a "mutator". No operation type is then a pure accessor (not_pureAccessor) and Theorem E.1 is vacuous. The intended definition (Kosa) compares only legal sequences; with it (NonMutator) Theorem E.1 is proved (theorem_E1).

  • Proof of Theorem D.1, Step 2: the shift x_i = [-(k-1)/2 + ((z-i) mod k)/k] u should read -(k-1)/(2k) (as in Step 2.2 and in the skew computation).

  • Proof of Theorem C.1, Step 2: "messages from p_j to p_k cost δ = d - m" should be p_j to p_i.

  • Proof of Theorem E.1, Step 1: "since aop'₁ and aop'₂ are pure mutators" should be pure accessors.

  • §III.B.2: views require strictly increasing clock times, which rules out simultaneous receipts such as those at p_k in the run R₁ of Theorem C.1; non-decreasing times are meant.

  • Claim B.2: for timed automata whose timers accumulate at a finite time (Zeno), a finite admissible run has no complete admissible extension; non-Zeno algorithms are meant.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem E.1

🔧 Theorem E.1 (both orders of op₁, op₂ are legal)

The proof infers that ρ ∘ op₁ ∘ op₂ and ρ ∘ op₂ ∘ op₁ are both legal from immediate self-commutativity, which needs ρ ∘ op₁ and ρ ∘ op₂ legal; hypothesis C gives only one of them.

Impact. Statement true; repaired by running each operation alone after ρ: a correct implementation returns a legal value, and determinism does the rest (theorem_E1).

Details

We also invoke the accessors at fixed times (t + B₁ + η and m later) instead of the thesis's adaptive times; this makes the case analysis of Step 2.2a unnecessary.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Partially synchronous message passing, n processes (Fintype P), no failures, reliable channels, message delays in [d - u, d] chosen by the adversary as a function of sender, receiver and send time, drift-free clocks with offsets of pairwise difference at most ε (Cslib.TimedMP.Scenario.Admissible). Deterministic algorithms, no randomness. The adversary also chooses the clock offsets and the invocation times (one pending operation per process, WF).

  • An algorithm is given by causal local behaviours in local clock time (Cslib.TimedMP.Algorithm): every non-Zeno timed automaton of the thesis is one. Runs exist and are unique (exists_run, IsRun.unique); shifting a run is IsRun.shift; chopping and extending is running another scenario that agrees before a causally closed cut (agree_of_cut, agree_window).

  • Hypotheses on the algorithm, all from §III.B.4: correctness (Correct: unique responses, linearizability with the real-time order "response < invocation"), bounded operation time (TimeBound … Bop), finite runs (consequence of bounded quiescence, FiniteRuns), history-obliviousness (HistoryOblivious, same residual behaviour after two runs executing the same operations). Theorem D.1 needs only correctness and Bop.

  • |OP| ≥ X is stated as: if every OP operation responds within B in all admissible runs, then B ≥ X.

  • Proof remarks: Theorem C.1 holds for n ≥ 2 (the thesis assumes n ≥ 3); the ρ-prefix of every run is a fresh admissible run glued by history-obliviousness (restart), which is necessary when 2 min {ε, u, d/3} > u; where the thesis invokes an operation "immediately after" a response, we leave a small gap, since precedence is strict.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The hypotheses of the theorems on specifications are shown satisfiable by concrete objects (Examples.lean); the hypotheses on the algorithm (Correct, TimeBound, FiniteRuns, HistoryOblivious) are those of the thesis; their joint satisfiability is not machine-checked (for u < d a centralized implementation, which forwards every operation to one process that applies them in arrival order and keeps only the object state and message counters, satisfies them with |OP| ≤ 2d).
