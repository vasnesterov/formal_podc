We assume, as everywhere in the thesis, that it is *deterministic* (Definition A.1) and, as is standard, that legality is *prefix closed*. Definitions B.1–B.3 (immediately non-commuting, non-self-commuting, strongly non-self-commuting), C.1–C.2 (looks like, equivalent), D.1–D.4 (mutator, accessor, pure accessor). Under the literal Definitions D.1, D.2 and D.4 no operation type is a pure accessor (`not_pureAccessor`): an accessor `op` has a legal `ρ` with `ρ ∘ op` illegal, and then `ρ ∘ op` is not equivalent to `ρ`, so `op` is a mutator. The intended notion (Kosa) only compares legal sequences: `NonMutator` (for every legal `ρ ∘ op`, `ρ ∘ op` is equivalent to `ρ`).
