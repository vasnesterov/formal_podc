Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Byzantine Agreement with Homonyms — PODC 2011.

Paper: Delporte-Gallet, Fauconnier, Guerraoui, Kermarrec, Ruppert, Tran-The, Byzantine Agreement with Homonyms, PODC 2011 (HAL hal-00580133v1, with appendix). All theorems and the characterization of Table 1 are true; Prop. 26 is false only for α = 0 and Lemma 34 is unestablished (see Proposition 18); several proofs needed repairs.

Model (Section 2)

  • Messages. A correct process sends one Option M per identifier and round, to all processes with that identifier (the paper: "can direct the message to all processes that have a particular identifier"). A process receives its own messages. Byzantine processes deliver arbitrary multisets per recipient, tagged with Byzantine identifiers.

  • Numerate/innumerate. One algorithm class: innumerate = the transition depends only on the set of received pairs. Lower bounds are proved for all (numerate) algorithms, upper bounds for innumerate ones (except Prop. 18, numerate by design).

  • Restricted Byzantine processes: per recipient and round, at most #{b ∈ byz | id b = j} pairs tagged j. This is equivalent to "each Byzantine process sends at most one message to each recipient per round".

  • Timing. Synchronous = gst = 0. Partially synchronous = DLS basic model (losses only before gst; all processes start at round 0 and step every round). The paper's remark that other partial-synchrony models are equivalent is not formalized.

  • Decisions. Decides p v := ∃ r, decide (state r p) = some v. Agreement is also required for p = q, so decisions are stable. An algorithm meeting the paper's first-decision spec can latch its first decision, so the two notions of solvability coincide; our algorithms latch.

  • Values. Lower bounds hold for any value type with two distinct values. Prop. 2 assumes Nonempty V, used for the default value of the classical black box; Props. 5 and 18 need nothing.

  • Standing assumptions n > 3t, 0 < t < n, ℓ ≥ 1 are explicit hypotheses where used. Prop. 4 needs t > 0. For t = 0 the partition argument does not apply, and the paper excludes this case. Prop. 5 holds without n > 3t.

Proposition 1 (and the lower bound of Theorem 19)

  • "It suffices to prove … ℓ = 3t": the reduction (merging identifiers by a surjection Fin ℓ' → Fin ℓ) is Solvable.mono_ids. It needs n ≥ 3t, which the standing assumption n > 3t gives.

  • The cover of Figure 1 is built with two copies of Fin n. In copy 0 the processes k ≥ 3t form the stack of identifier 0; in copy 1 they form the stack of identifier t. Segment adjacency is the hexagon Z₁–X₀–Y₀–Z₀–X₁–Y₁–Z₁. Each scenario uses one copy as its n-process system, which avoids cardinality arithmetic.

  • Thm. 19 necessity is proved with the same cover (innumerate algorithm, restricted Byzantine processes sending one copy per identifier), not with the paper's simulation. The paper's proof has a gap (README, Proof gaps), repaired either by choosing the clone identifier among correct processes or by our cover argument. Typos in A.4: ℓ < 3t (should be ≤), "If ℓ ≥ t" (should be ≤), "less than 3t" (at most 3t). These are typos with evident intent (✏️).

Proposition 2

  • The black box A is an algorithm of CSLib's classical model (PartialSync.Algorithm on Fin ℓ, synchronous runs gst = 0, δ = 0). It may send different messages to different processes (the paper: broadcast WLOG). Its existence for ℓ > 3t is derived (classicalBA_exists), not assumed.

  • T(A) latches the simulated decision (lat). The paper instead assumes A's decision is stable, so a correct group keeps announcing it. A process decides at most once.

  • Line 5's "deterministic choice" is pick, a fixed choice on the set of candidate states. Line 8's count is the number of distinct identifiers announcing v.

Proposition 4 (and the lower bound of Theorem 20)

  • Executions α, β, γ as in Figure 4 (0-based identifiers). The isolated stack of γ gets input v₀ instead of the paper's "2" (irrelevant). Byzantine processes are silent from round T = gst on.

  • Thm. 20: the stack processes of α and β are clones (st_clone), so one copy per identifier suffices (restricted, innumerate), as the paper argues.

Proposition 5 (Section 4.2, Appendix A.1)

  • Each round's output is one record (broadcast init, all echoes, proper set, lock/ack/decide). Lock/ack/decide carry no phase tag; receivers interpret them by their own round counter. Phase = 8 rounds: propose at 8ph, lock at 8ph+2, vote at 8ph+4, ack at 8ph+6, decide at 8ph+7. The leader of phase ph has identifier ph mod ℓ (0-based).

  • Broadcast (Prop. 6): echoes omit the superround tag (every broadcast message carries its phase). Amplification applies in every round; accepts are cumulative. Timings match the paper (accepted within the superround after GST; relay by the next superround).

  • Proper sets: rule 1 counts distinct identifiers in the current round. Rule 2 is read existentially ("some 2t+1 received sets, no value in t+1 of them"). This is the literal reading of "received proper sets from 2t+1 different identifiers and no value appears in t+1 of them" and matches the paper's validity remark, so it is not counted as a mistake. Under the universal reading (all received sets) termination fails (README); the paper's termination proof needs the existential rule, which would be a local fix. "All values" = Set.univ.

  • Decisions are latched; the leader decides only its own lock request.

  • Proof repairs: Lemma 12 Case 2 (README); Lemma 10's hypothesis weakened to what the proof uses; Lemma 11's tight timing (gst ≤ 8·ph₃+6); termination uses leader phases Q = ℓ(gst+1) + id of single-process correct identifiers. There are at least 2ℓ−n−t ≥ 2t+1 of these, by the strict n < 2ℓ−3t. The paper writes n ≤ 2ℓ−3t and "superround 3" for the ack round (✏️).

Proposition 16 (Section 5.1, Appendix A.2)

  • System: processes k < ℓ are Byzantine with identifier k; all n − ℓ ≥ 2 correct processes have identifier 0. Any correct process can then be impersonated by Byzantine process 0.

  • Configurations = run prefixes. Dec inp adv k = decisions in runs extending the prefix (decisions made in the prefix included). Lemma 17 is proved for two runs whose correct processes other than p agree up to round k. This is exactly the situation in Lemmas 21 and 22, and it also handles decisions made before round k. Valencies are for arbitrary value types: univalent = at most one reachable decision.

  • The infinite multivalent run is the limit of advSeq, built with Classical.epsilon.

Proposition 18 (Appendix A.3)

  • One record message per round; the echo table a is sent as a function and init as a set, so every message is "valid" by construction. a := max(a, initCount, α₁) instead of overwriting at round 2k, and echo updates apply to all k; both are equivalent by Lemma 23. α₁, α₂ ≤ n.

  • Schedule: phase = rounds 8ph…8ph+7; lock removal uses the accepts of round 8ph+7 (per-superround witnesses, the paper's definition). Choices use pick on candidate sets.

  • Proof repairs: relay holds in every later superround (needed by Lemma 36); Lemma 30 keeps r ≤ r′ (needed by Lemma 35); Lemma 37 Case 2 splits on proper sets (as Lemma 12); proper rule 2 is existential; termination uses the deterministic choice on the common set of lock requests.

  • Lemma 34 ("locks contain at most one pair at the end of each phase") is unestablished, not formalized (❌). Its proof assumes that at lines 24–26 the process still has line 16's n−t witnesses. With per-superround witnesses (the paper's definition; Lemma 36 needs those of superround 4 at lines 24–26, our reading) this fails before T: the echoes of the last round can all be lost, so an older lock with another value survives. Reviewer's hand-built run (not in Lean): n = ℓ = 4, t = 1, Byzantine b; p locks v′ in phase ph′ (votes of p, q₁, b; the messages to q₁ in its lock round are lost), later q₁, q₂, b propose and vote v, p locks v, and all echoes to p in superround 4 are lost, so p ends the phase with two pairs. Under a cumulative reading of witnesses (all accepts so far, max multiplicity per identifier) the paper's proof of Lemma 34 is correct; Lemma 30 (unforgeability bounds every accept) and Lemma 36 (relay) should survive it, but that variant is not formalized. Lemma 34 is not used: Prop. 39 only needs that some lock (v₁, ∗) is held (line 6 then excludes all other values), decAt_agree. The lemma is neither proved nor refuted, and the pseudocode admits a reading under which it holds.

  • Prop. 26 is literally false for α = 0 (it demands an Accept when nobody broadcast; Figure 6 accepts only after n−t echoes). It is a degenerate case (✏️), never used with α = 0 (the paper's Lemma 24 itself separates α > 0). We prove the witness form cc_le_witnesses (summed over identifiers: correct broadcasters ≤ witnesses in round 2k+1 after GST); its proof gives the per-identifier bound α ≤ α′ for every identifier.

  • Lemma 33's proof cites "Lemma 22" (means 32). Prop. 25 (unicity) holds by construction.

Theorems 3, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20
