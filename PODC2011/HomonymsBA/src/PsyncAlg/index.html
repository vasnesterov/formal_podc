import Papers.PODC2011.HomonymsBA.Basic /-! # Proposition 5: the partially synchronous algorithm (Figure 5 + Proposition 6) The algorithm of Figure 5 of the paper, together with the authenticated broadcast of Proposition 6 (Srikanth–Toueg style echo broadcast), written as an `Algorithm ℓ V Msg St`. Correct processes start at round `0` and step in every round, so the local round counter `rnd` is the global round. Superround `s` consists of rounds `2s, 2s+1`; phase `ph` consists of the four superrounds `4ph, …, 4ph+3`, i.e. of rounds `8ph, …, 8ph+7`: * round `8ph` (superround 1, round 1): `Broadcast(⟨propose V, ph⟩)` (line 8): `init` field; * round `8ph+2` (superround 2, round 1): the leaders send `⟨lock v, ph⟩` (line 12); * round `8ph+4` (superround 3, round 1): `Broadcast(⟨vote v, ph⟩)` (line 16); * round `8ph+6` (superround 4, round 1): `⟨ack v, ph⟩` (line 20); * round `8ph+7` (superround 4, round 2): `⟨decide v⟩` (line 24). Everything a process sends in a round is bundled into one `Msg` (sent to all identifiers): the `init` of a broadcast, all `echo`es, the proper set, and the lock/ack/decide items. Direct (non-broadcast) items carry no phase tag: the receiver interprets them by its own round counter (all correct processes have the same one). Authenticated broadcast (Proposition 6): a process that receives `⟨init m⟩` from identifier `i` in the first round of a superround echoes `(m, i)` from the next round on, forever; a process that has (cumulatively) received the echo `(m, i)` from `ℓ - 2t` distinct identifiers echoes it too; `Accept(m, i)` holds once the echo `(m, i)` has been received from `ℓ - t` distinct identifiers. (The superround tag of the paper's `⟨echo m, r, i⟩` is dropped: every broadcast message already carries its phase, and no property used by the algorithm depends on the tag.) The transition only looks at the *set* of received pairs (`Algorithm.Innumerate`); all counting is by distinct identifiers (`Set.ncard` of sets of identifiers). -/ namespace Podc.HomonymsBA.Psync open
