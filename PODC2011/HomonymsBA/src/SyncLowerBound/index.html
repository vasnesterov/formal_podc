import Papers.PODC2011.HomonymsBA.Cover /-! # Proposition 1 and the lower bound of Theorem 19: `ℓ ≤ 3t` makes synchronous BA unsolvable The covering system of Figure 1 of the paper: two copies (`false`/`true`) of an `n`-process system with `ℓ = 3t` identifiers, split into three classes `X = [0, t)`, `Y = [t, 2t)`, `Z = [2t, 3t)`. In copy `false` the processes `k ≥ 3t` all get identifier `0` (a stack of `n - 3t + 1` processes with identifier `0 ∈ X`), in copy `true` they get identifier `t ∈ Y`. Copy `false` has input `v₀`, copy `true` has input `v₁`. A process of segment `(b, c)` (copy `b`, class `c`) hears the segments of the hexagon `Z₁ – X₀ – Y₀ – Z₀ – X₁ – Y₁ – Z₁` adjacent to it, and its own segment (`segAdj`). The three scenarios (all `n`-process synchronous runs with at most `t` Byzantine processes): * `S₀`: copy `false` with `Z` Byzantine; the correct processes (`X₀ ∪ Y₀`) all have input `v₀`. * `S₁`: copy `true` with `X` Byzantine; the correct processes (`Y₁ ∪ Z₁`) all have input `v₁`. * `S₂`: copy `false` with `Y` Byzantine, where the `Z` processes are mapped to `Z₁` (input `v₁`). By validity the process `(false, 0)` decides `v₀` in the cover and `(true, 2t)` decides `v₁`; both are correct in `S₂`, contradicting agreement. For numerate processes the Byzantine processes deliver the whole multiset sent by the cover nodes they replace (several copies from the stack: unrestricted). For innumerate processes it is enough to deliver one copy (the stack processes are clones, `cover_clone`), so the Byzantine processes are restricted: this proves the lower bound of **Theorem 19** directly (the paper derives it from the classical bound by a simulation, see `differencies.md`).
