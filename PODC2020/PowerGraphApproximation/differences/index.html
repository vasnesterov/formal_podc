Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed Approximation on Power Graphs — PODC 2020.

Paper: Bar-Yehuda, Censor-Hillel, Maus, Pai, Pemmaraju, Distributed Approximation on Power Graphs, PODC 2020, arXiv 2006.03746 v1.

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Corollary 17, proof gap (🛠️). The proof applies Theorem 12 to the remaining graph G²[U], which is not a square. Algorithm 2 is not a 5/3-approximation there. Cor17Gap.corollary_17_proof_gap gives a run of Phase I (ε = 1/2) that ends with G²[U] = K₄,₄, followed by a complete run of Algorithm 2 that outputs 8 vertices while 4 suffice. The corollary itself is true. We prove the approximation part (corollary_17) with a new accounting (Centralized.induced_bound): on G²[U₀], 18|S₂| ≤ 30|OPT ∩ U₀| + 2|V \ U₀|, because a blue edge's midpoint is in V1 or outside U₀. Combined with Lemma 5 (2|S| ≤ 3|OPT ∩ S|), this gives 3|S ∪ S₂| ≤ 5|OPT|. Only the CONGEST variant (Phase I of Theorem 1) is formalized, not the CONGESTED CLIQUE one (Theorem 11). The same accounting works there, because it only uses that S is charged at ratio ≤ 3/2.

  2. Lemma 6, proof (✏️). "This implies |I| < n/α" is false (lemma_6_strict_false: P₄, r = 2). The argument gives |I|·α ≤ n (card_mul_le_of_indep), which suffices for the statement.

    • Our proof of |I|·α ≤ n uses disjoint balls of radius ⌊r/2⌋ and |B(v, j)| ≥ min(n, j + 1) in a connected graph (card_ballF). The paper instead assigns "⌊r/2⌋ unique vertices on the path from u to v", which needs a second vertex of I. For |I| = 1 it needs r ≤ n, which the hypothesis r ≤ n provides.

    • Hypothesis 1 ≤ r is not needed for the first sentence (for r = 0 the bound is trivial).

  3. Lemma 6, second sentence (✏️). "(1 + 1/⌊r/2⌋)-approximation" is undefined for r = 1. We state it for 2 ≤ r ≤ n (lemma_6_approx). With the convention 1/0 = ∞ the case r = 1 is trivially true. It is only a degenerate parameter corner.

  4. Theorem 12, degree-3 case (typo in proof). The disjoint edges are {y₁,z₁}, {y₂,z₂}, {x,y₃}. The paper writes {x,y₂}, which meets {y₂,z₂}.

  5. Theorem 12, degree-2 case (routine detail). {z,y₁} and {x,y₂} are disjoint because z ≠ y₂. This follows from triangle-freeness, which the paper does not mention. In the degree-3 case the existence of z₁ ≠ z₂ outside {x,y₁,y₂,y₃} likewise uses triangle-freeness for zᵢ ≠ yⱼ. We prove these in exists_step and inv_deg2.

  6. Lemma 13 (3) (routine detail). The paper does not explicitly exclude that the midpoint v left V' as an isolated vertex in part 1. It cannot have, since it has two remaining neighbours. This is covered by Inv.triClosed.

  7. Theorem 1, case ε > 1. The claim "G²-MIS has size ≤ n/2 in a connected graph" fails for n = 1 (degenerate, inside a proof). This case is not formalized.

  8. Phase I, CONGEST implementation (prose slip, not formalized). The pseudocode (line 4), Lemma 2 and Lemma 5 use "more than 1/ε neighbours in R", but the description of the distributed symmetry breaking says "any vertex with degree at least 1/ε in R is a candidate". With "at least", Lemma 5's bound dᵢ ≥ l + 1 fails. We follow the pseudocode.

  9. Lemma 5 typo. Sᵢ = N(vᵢ) ∩ R should read N(cᵢ) ∩ R.

  10. Theorems 20/22 typo. V_B = V(G_{x,y}) \ V_B should read \ V_A.

  11. [CKP17] bit gadgets. The order of the 4-cycle on t_A, f_A, t_B, f_B is not given. We take t_A–f_A–t_B–f_B–t_A, so the 2-independent sets are {t_A,t_B} and {f_A,f_B}, as the reduction needs.

  12. Lemma 24. Lemma 24 follows without Lemma 23: restricting a cover to the core gives a cover of G, and the triangle P[1]P[2]P[3] forces 2 vertices per gadget. Lemma 23 is proved anyway.

Modelling choices

  • Algorithm 2.

    • The state stores V' only, with E' := G²[V']. This is justified by the paper's own remark that the remaining graph always equals G²[V_R].

    • Isolated vertices may be removed at any time in parts 1 and 2. The paper removes them eagerly, so our runs are a superset of the paper's.

    • The degree-3 guard is "no vertex of degree 1 or 2", where the paper says "< 3". With eager removal the two agree; ours allows more runs.

    • In the degree-1/2/3 cases the vertex x (left isolated) is removed in the same step, as the paper's eager removal does.

    • The choices of triangle, x, z, z₁, z₂ and the part-3 cover are nondeterministic. Part 3 is "any cover C ⊆ V' of G²[V'] with |C| ≤ 2|D| for every cover D ⊆ V'". A maximal matching provides one (exists_twoApprox).

    • Theorem 12 is stated with α = 5/3 (the abstract's value; the theorem says "a constant α < 2") as 3|S| ≤ 5|OPT| for every vertex cover OPT of G².

  • Polynomial time is formalized as run_length_le: at most n + 3 steps (for init U₀, at most |U₀| + 3). The cost of a step is not formalized.

  • Lemmas 13–15 are not separate theorems. Their content is in the invariant Centralized.Inv:

    • noTri is Lemma 13 (1) / Lemma 14 (1).

    • Red edges forming a matching and s₁ ≥ |E^blue| are used inside blue_bound, which proves Lemma 14 (4) as 3|V_{R'}| ≤ 2s₁ (generalized: + 2|V \ U₀|).

    • charge combines opt₁ ≥ (2/3)s₁ and opt₂ ≥ (3/5)s₂ into 10s₁ + 9s₂ ≤ 15|OPT ∩ (U₀ \ V')|.

    • opt₃ ≥ s₃/2 is the 2-approximation in part 3.

    • The paper's OPT_i (optimal covers of G²[W_i]) are replaced by restrictions of one fixed cover OPT. This is equivalent for the final bound.

  • Algorithm 1 / Theorem 1.

    • Only the sequential semantics is formalized, with ε = 1/l. The paper reduces general ε ≤ 1 to this case.

    • Phase II takes any R* that is optimal among covers of G²[U] contained in U.

    • The CONGEST implementation and round count are not formalized. Formalized are their combinatorial inputs: Lemma 2's "≤ l neighbours in U" (card_nbrs_U_le), Lemma 3, and "≤ n/(l+1) iterations" (phase1_length).

    • Lemma 3 is stated as a characterization of G²[U]-adjacency in terms of F.

  • Corollary 17.

    • Formalized only for Phase I of Theorem 1 followed by Algorithm 2 on G²[U], in sequential semantics.

    • "Nodes only use polynomial computations" and the O(n) round bound are not formalized.

    • Our proof does not use that Phase I has finished. Any reachable Phase-I state works.

  • Lower bounds (Theorems 20, 22).

    • CSLib's deterministic CONGEST model with exactly B bits per edge, direction and round. Node input is the adjacency row (and the weight for Theorem 20).

    • "Computes the minimum (weighted) vertex cover of G²" is formalized as "all nodes accept after T rounds iff H² has a cover of size (weight) ≤ W'" (the decision version used by Theorem 19). Turning an MVC output into the decision needs an aggregation the paper does not discuss either.

    • The bound is stated as k² ≤ T · 2 · |cut| · B + 2 with |cut| = 4 log k, and with n = |V(H)| as n² ≤ c log² n (8 T B log n + 2) for the family sizes n_L only. There is no padding to arbitrary n.

    • Theorem 20 uses weights in {0, 1}. Section 3.2's "WLOG positive weights" is not assumed there, as in the paper.

    • The randomized versions take CC^R(DISJ_K) ≥ κK as a hypothesis, which is not proved in CSLib. The constant κ is real and the coin spaces are nonempty; otherwise the hypothesis is only satisfiable with κ = 0 (empty coin spaces make ret true a zero-error, zero-cost protocol; a natural-number constant κ ≥ 1 would claim CC^R(DISJ_K) ≥ K, false for large K, since CC^R(DISJ_K) ≈ 0.49K).

    • The _connected versions require correctness only on connected members of the family (all members except x = y = 1).

  • Graph power. G.power r uses edist ≤ r, so unreachable pairs are never adjacent. The paper's G² ("at most two hops") agrees.
