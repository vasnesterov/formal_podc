Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Self-stabilizing Systems in Spite of High Dynamics — PODC 2020.

Paper: Altisen, Devismes, Durand, Johnen, Petit, Self-stabilizing Systems in Spite of High Dynamics. PODC 2020 brief announcement (DOI 10.1145/3382734.3404502, no proofs); formalized from the full version (Theoretical Computer Science 2023, author manuscript HAL hal-04109662, 48 pp.). Numbering follows the full version.

Mistakes and imprecisions in the paper (with labels)

  • ✏️ Corollary 1 is stated too strongly. It says that if a set L of legitimate configurations for V is closed in TC_B(Δ), then A "should not be size-ambiguous". The paper calls it the contrapositive of Theorem 1, which only gives "A is not (k, V)-ambiguous" for this V. The printed version is false. Algorithm 1 is size-ambiguous, yet for a single process its quasi-legitimate configurations form a closed set of legitimate configurations (corollary_1_as_stated_false, alg1_singleton_closed). We prove the contrapositive (corollary_1). Theorem 3 is unaffected: it applies Corollary 1 to every V.

  • ✏️ Theorem 6, Corollary 8, Lemma 13, Corollary 9: two implied assumptions. The proof swaps the leader p_ℓ for a fresh process v ∉ V and keeps the states of the other processes.

    1. It needs |IDSET| > n. With IDSET = Fin 2, the algorithm knowsN2 (input n; lid := 0 if n = 2, lid := id(p) if n = 1) is pseudo-stabilizing (knowsN2_pseudoStab), SizeOnly, and correct from round 0 for n = 2 (theorem_6_needs_large_IDSET). We assume [Infinite ι].

    2. It needs the possible local states of a process to depend on the system only through |V| (SizeOnly). Otherwise the states of V \ {p_ℓ} need not be states of the new system. If the processes know V (state = V, lid := min V), the algorithm is pseudo-stabilizing and every execution is correct from round 0 (knowsV, knowsV_pseudoStab, theorem_6_needs_sizeOnly). The paper's algorithms know at most n and Δ, so this is an implied assumption of the model.

  • Algorithm 3, line 7: insert(p, ⟨id(0), 0⟩) is a typo for insert(p, ⟨id(p), 0⟩).

  • Lemma 11, proof case 2: "edge (r, p)" should be "edge (r, q)".

  • Section 3.3, before Corollary 1: "the set of legitimate configurations of Algorithm 1 is not closed" holds only for systems of at least two processes (alg1_not_closed). For one process it fails (alg1_singleton_closed).

Proof gaps (statements true)

  • 🔧 Lemma 6 (Algorithm 2). The proof says that after id is pushed, evicting it requires inserting n distinct identifiers not in members(p). This is false: re-inserting identifiers ahead of id (lines i2–i3) moves them behind it (Alg2.lemma_6_counting_claim_false: with n = 3, one new identifier evicts 5 from [1, 2, 5]). The lemma is true. From round Δ + 1 on, the longest suffix of the queue whose identifiers are all real only grows, and none of its elements is evicted: if the head is in it, the queue is all of V (n distinct real identifiers), so no absent identifier is inserted (Alg2.lemma_6_suffix, Alg2.lemma_6). The proof also uses, without stating them, that n = |V|, that the identifiers in the queue are distinct and that the queue has at most n elements. All three hold in configurations of Algorithm 2 for V. Lemma 6 is stated as: if t ≥ Δ and q = p or some in-neighbour of p sends a pair ⟨q, _⟩ in round t + 1, then q ∈ members(p) at the end of every later round.

  • 🔧 Lemma 11 (Algorithm 3). "id(p) cannot be removed from members(q) … by Lemma 10" and "this pair is not rejected" need a counting step that the proof leaves out. The max-timestamp entry with timestamp ≤ i − 2 is evicted at line i6 only if every entry has timestamp ≤ i − 2. Then, by Lemma 10, all keys are real. The map is full, so its keys are exactly V, and no absent identifier can arrive. This must hold at every intermediate state of the line 4–5 loop, for every processing order and tie-break of max(p), and also at line 7, which the proof does not discuss. Repaired in Alg3/Round.lean (mem_keys_of_full_lt) and Alg3.lemma_11.

  • Theorem 2: the proof shows only that its set L is closed and correct. Convergence of L is not mentioned. It follows because every legitimate set of A is contained in L. Proved in theorem_2.

  • Lemma 9 (Algorithm 2) and the proof of Theorem 8 (Algorithm 3): "members has at most n entries, hence it is exactly V" uses that the identifiers are distinct and that n = |V|; both come from the configuration invariant.

  • Lemmas 3 and 4 (Algorithm 1): the steps "lid(p) stays id(ℓ)" and "no process can reset" also use that every received identifier is ≥ id(ℓ), i.e. quasi-legitimacy (Lemma 2). The proofs do not cite it. Trivial.

Modelling choices

  • Processes = identifiers. ι is IDSET, with a linear order. A system is a finite V : Finset ι, nonempty (SP_LE cannot hold for V = ∅; the paper implicitly has n ≥ 1).

  • TVGs. A TVG is a dynamic graph G : ℕ → Graph ι with IsDG V G (loopless, edges inside V). The static arc set E and the lifetime are implicit. Rounds are 0-indexed: G t is the snapshot of the paper's Round t + 1, and exec G γ t is γ_t, the configuration at the end of Round t. TC_B(Δ), TC_Q(Δ), TC_R are the all-pairs classes JssB, JssQ, Jss of the PODC 2021 formalization (Papers/PODC2021/StabilizingLEDynamic). They are defined by temporal distance (tdist, journeys with strictly increasing times). The paper's d̂_{p,t}(q) with departure > t is tdist G (t − o_T + 1) p q. In particular d̂_{p,o_T}(q) = tdist G 1 p q.

  • Algorithms (SAlg). The algorithm is defined for every set of processes. The code of process p (one synchronous round, as a function of its state and the states received) does not depend on V. Knowledge such as n is an input stored in the state, so it is captured by the sets S V p of possible local states, exactly as in the paper's discussion before Definition 3. This makes the code-independence used in the proofs of Theorems 1 and 6 explicit. Algorithms are full-information: a process receives the whole state of each current in-neighbour and knows who sent it. This is at least as strong as the paper's SEND of part of the state, so the impossibilities are not weakened. The algorithms of Sections 4–6 only use the parts they send.

  • Definitions 1, 2, 6 are stated for recurring classes in the form of Remarks 1 and 2 (executions from time 0 of every graph of the class). All classes used are recurring (J_shift).

  • Stabilization time "at most T" is stated as "every execution is in the legitimate set from round T on" (Corollaries 5/6, Theorems 7, 9). Unboundedness (Corollaries 8, 9) is stated as "there is no f such that, for every V of size n, some legitimate set is reached within f(n, Δ) rounds by every execution". This is the strongest form of the negation.

  • Theorem 2 assumes that executions stay in the configurations of A for V (Invariant). The paper builds this into the definition of an execution. Theorem 3 and Corollary 3 are proved without this assumption: they apply the core of Theorem 1 directly to the set L of the proof of Theorem 2.

  • Theorem 1 is proved with a simpler TVG than the paper's. It is the complete graph on a k-subset U not containing the leader in even rounds and the complete graph on V in odd rounds; the rotating window is not needed. The paper's configurations γ^i are exactly the execution in the static complete graph on U.

  • Theorem 6, Lemma 13 do not assume the algorithm pseudo-stabilizing (a bound would imply it). They take the extra assumptions [Infinite ι] and SizeOnly A (see above).

  • Statement forms. Lemma 3 (Algorithm 1) is proved by induction on the length of a journey from ℓ instead of the paper's double induction on (d, j). Lemma 4 also covers r = 0. Lemma 7 is stated with any j ≥ Δ such that tdist G j q p ≤ Δ, instead of the minimal t(q, p). Several lemmas need fewer hypotheses than in the paper (for example, Lemmas 1, 5 and 10 hold from any configuration). Δ ≥ 1 is assumed where the paper has Δ ∈ ℕ*.

  • Algorithm 1. The state is ⟨lid, ttl⟩ with ttl < 2Δ (S V p, independent of V). Line 7's lexicographic minimum is lexMin.

  • Algorithms 2 and 3. The state is ⟨n, members, lid⟩, with members a list of pairs. In Algorithm 2 it is the FIFO queue, head first. In Algorithm 3 it is a map, with the insertion order irrelevant. S V p requires n = |V|, at most n entries with distinct identifiers, and, for Algorithm 2, timestamps ≤ Δ. The received pairs are processed in the order of Finset.toList, which is arbitrary, and all round lemmas are proved for every list (any order, duplicates allowed). Algorithm 3's max(p) breaks ties by List.argmax; the proofs only use maximality. Algorithm 3's line 7 uses id(p) (typo fixed). For Algorithm 2's queue, distinct identifiers are our reading of the paper's notation members(p)[id] (the variable declaration only says "queue of at most n elements"); for Algorithm 3's map they are part of the notion of a map.

  • Processing order. The algorithms Alg2.alg and Alg3.alg fix one order of the received pairs (that of Finset.toList, with each distinct pair once). The execution-level theorems are stated for this algorithm, but their proofs only use the round lemmas, which hold for every list of received pairs.

  • Complexity claims (memory and message sizes in Corollary 6, Theorem 5) are not formalized.

  • Remark 4 / Remark 5 (other problems, counting n) are not formalized.
