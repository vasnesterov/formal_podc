Why Extension-Based Proofs Fail — PODC 2020

Dan Alistarh, James Aspnes, Faith Ellen, Rati Gelashvili, Leqi Zhu. Brief Announcement: Why Extension-Based Proofs Fail. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405743. Formalized version: the full version, arXiv 1811.01421 v3 (August 2020, NIS model, the model of the brief announcement); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The limitation result stands for the provers we checked: for every k ≥ 2, no extension-based prover whose infinite chain is eventually played alone can refute every wait-free k-set agreement protocol in the NIS model, for any number of processes and any finite input set. The adversary of Section 5 is saved only by a new output-query rule with a stronger invariant, because its answers none are not shown to stay correct after later subdivisions, and Lemma 4.13, whose proof ends with an unjustified inequality, needs a new carrier argument. Provers that interleave an infinite chain with finitely many others belong to the paper's class but were not checked (see Not formalized.); nothing about them is shown false.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • §5 defines X_t(a) as the set of descendants in all levels V_r, r ≤ t. The adversary instead adds sets of vertices of G_t to X_t(a). Invariant 6 only makes sense for the vertices of G_t: a non-terminated vertex of a lower level is not a vertex of G_t.

  • Theorem 5.7, Cases 1 and 2 of output queries: the Q-only schedule "from C that results in process p_i being in state v = (i, {u})" exists only if no process has updated S_{t+1} in C, which the paper never states (invariant 3 allows 2t + 1 steps). The schedule exists for the paper's own adversary, so this is a missing justification, not an error. The paper's strategy keeps a stronger, unstated invariant: after every response, active processes are at levels < t, and processes at level t are terminated (invariant 2 and the query rule). So nobody has written to S_{t+1} in a reached configuration. A schedule that brings p_i to u can be cut to the causal past of u, and then p_i p_i gives (i, {u}). If some process had written to S_{t+1}, as invariant 3 permits, p_i's scan would return (i, τ) with |τ| ≥ 2. That vertex can be adjacent to vertices that have not seen y. Our adversary is different and uses its own construction (oq_some_of_unprot): in Lean, undecided states are active, so p_i runs alone, round after round, until no other process has written to the next snapshot object. Each new state stays unprotected (unprot_round), and the next solo state is safe (safe_solo).

  • Case 1 and the start of phase 2: "each vertex in X_t(y) is adjacent to a vertex in T_t(b)" drops invariant 6's alternative v ∈ N_t(y). The dropped case is covered, since T_t(y) is far from N_t(y).

Typos

  • Lemma 5.5's proof writes χ(N_t(a), Δ) and χ(T_t(t), δ) for χ(N_t(a), δ) and χ(T_t(a), δ).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 5.7 · Lemma 4.13

🛠️ Theorem 5.7 (adversary, output queries answered none; Lemma 5.5, invariant 6)

The adversary answers none by looking only at the vertices of G_t, although its promise covers all future Q-only executions from C, and Lemma 5.5 asserts without proof ("by the definition of χ") that invariant 6 survives a subdivision.

Impact. The correctness of the adversary, hence the proof of Theorem 5.7, is not established as written. We prove the theorem with a modified rule for output queries and a stronger invariant (not_hasEBProof_setAgreement, oq_spec). This repair holds for every prover; only interleaved infinite chains remain unchecked (see Not formalized.).

Details

After t is incremented, X_{t+1}(a) contains the new descendants (i, τ) of the recorded vertices. A carrier τ may mix a vertex u of Case 3, which is adjacent to T_t(b) only through another clique, with vertices of other processes. Then (i, τ) has seen a, so it is not in N_{t+1}(a). It is adjacent to a terminated vertex only if some clique contains τ and a terminated vertex, which need not exist. The paper gives no argument that such vertices are protected. This matters twice. Later in phase 1, the adversary terminates a vertex with a whenever this keeps the invariants, and it uses invariant 6 to argue that this vertex is not in X_t(a). In phase 2, it outputs a at every vertex of F' that is not adjacent to a terminated vertex. Either step could make a vertex in X_t(a) output a, contradicting an earlier none.

Repair

For a query (C, Q, y) our adversary considers the Q-descendants of C at all levels, in the protocol where all undecided states stay active (oq).

  • If one has already output y, it returns a schedule to it.

  • Otherwise, if some undecided descendant with at least t rounds has seen y and is not adjacent to a vertex terminated with another value, it lets that process run alone until it is ahead of everybody. It then subdivides and terminates the next state with y. That state is safe (safe_solo); the paper's Cases 1 and 2 are special cases.

  • Otherwise it answers none. Then every undecided descendant is protected: it has not seen y, or it is adjacent to a vertex terminated with another value (RecOK).

Protection survives later terminations (adj_transfer, which keeps an edge alive by restricting an execution to the causal past of its two endpoints). A safe vertex is never protected, so no later termination contradicts a none (AInv.terminate, dstar_none).

🛠️ Lemma 4.13 (subdivision increases distances)

The last inequality, "distance(A, A'∩C) + 2 + distance(B'∩C, B) > distance(A, B)", does not follow from the preceding decomposition of shortest paths, which only gives the reverse inequality (the points of A'∩C and B'∩C closest to A and B need not be adjacent).

Impact. The lemma is one of the paper's main technical tools. Its statement is true; proved by a different argument (Far.subdiv_succ, far_layer_succ).

Repair

We use a carrier argument. Every vertex of G_{t+1} has a carrier in G_t, and the carriers of adjacent vertices lie in a common clique. Mapping a walk of G_{t+1} to its carriers gives a walk of G_t that is no longer. If the image walk uses an edge between two active vertices, their carriers are nested, so one step can be saved. The same argument gives Lemma 4.11 directly (Far.subdiv).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • NIS model (shared with the PODC'21 companion ExtensionBasedReductions). Asynchronous, wait-free, deterministic, full information. n processes form any finite type, and each process updates and then scans each snapshot object S_1, S_2, … at most once. A protocol is its decision function δ, consulted after each scan. There is no randomness, and crashes are processes that stop being scheduled.

  • Extension-based proofs. A prover is a deterministic strategy on the history. It uses queries (C, q), output queries (C, Q, y) and phase ends, as in §3. Output queries may name any set Q (the paper requires Q active), which only helps the prover. The protocol may answer correct output queries adversarially. The prover wins if all its moves are legal and it reaches a violating configuration, or plays infinitely many phases, or from some point on plays one infinite chain of consecutive queries.

  • Task. k-set agreement setAgreement X k: every input vector over a finite set X is allowed, and the outputs are inputs, with at most k distinct values. The paper's case is X = {0, …, k} and n > k.

  • Graphs G_r. Defined semantically: two states are adjacent if they belong to different processes and occur together in a reachable configuration (Adj). The paper's N_r(a) is replaced by the set Uset of vertices that have not seen a, which is its vertex set by Lemma 5.3. The adversary's partial protocol treats undecided states as active (AState.term).

Proof remarks.

  • Lemma 5.6 uses the input of the process with the smallest view of S_r as the common seen value, instead of "the first process to scan S_{t'+2}" (common_seen).

  • The completion at the start of phase 2 assigns values on all states with t + 1 rounds, not only on F'; states outside F' are never reached by the prover.

Not formalized. Section 3's example (the FLP-style valency proof is extension-based). Lemmas 4.4–4.9 and Corollary 4.9: connectivity is not needed, and the χ-construction is replaced by the semantic graph. Lemma 5.3 is avoided by working with Uset.

Provers that interleave several chains, one of them infinite (differencies.md, item 8). The shared game counts an infinite chain of queries as a prover win only if, from some point on, the prover plays nothing but that one chain. The paper (§3) also lets a prover interleave finitely many chains in a phase, one of which is infinite. So our provers are a strict subclass of the paper's, and theorem_5_7 does not formally imply the paper's Theorem 5.7. The paper's argument (every chain of phase 1 is finite, Lemma 5.6, and a phase has finitely many chains and output queries) covers interleaving. Our proof of Lemma 5.6 (IsChain.false) assumes that the adversary state changes only through the chain's own queries, so it would need to be generalised. This case is not formalized.

The paper's invariant 6. Open: whether it can actually fail after a subdivision. We neither proved nor refuted it, and we did not try to build a prover that triggers the scenario of the first proof gap.

Trust. No sorry. theorem_5_7 and not_hasEBProof_setAgreement use only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.
