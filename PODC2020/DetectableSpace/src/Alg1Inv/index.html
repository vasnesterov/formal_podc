Linearization: a `Read` is linearized at its read of `R`; a `Write` that writes `R` at that write; a `Write` `W` of `p` that has executed line 1 and has not yet written `R` is linearized *speculatively* immediately before the first write to `R` after its line 1 (`s p < wc`). Such a speculative entry is always immediately followed by another write in the linearization (`Good`), so it can be removed again if `W` later writes `R` itself (then it is re-linearized at that write) or if `W.Recover` returns `fail`. Views of a process (for a `Write` in progress): `Ph 1` (past line 1), `Ph 2`, `Ph 3`, `Dec` (decided to complete: lines 8–13 or `CP = 2`), `vw = (⟨qval, q, qtoggle⟩, mtoggle)` and the bit `A[p][q][1 - qtoggle]` (`bitOf`). The heart of the proof are the fields `f1` (all writes of `p` to `R` after its line 1 are by its current `Write`), `f2b`/`f3` (a process never reuses the toggle index of its own last visible write —
