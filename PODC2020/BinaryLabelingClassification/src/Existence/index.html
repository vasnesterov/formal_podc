import Papers.PODC2020.BinaryLabelingClassification.Families /-! # Existence of solutions and the `O(n)` upper bounds of §8 Upper-bound halves of Theorems 8.1 and 8.3 of *Classification of Distributed Binary Labeling Problems* (Balliu et al., arXiv:1911.13294v3). * `whiteSolvable_card`: every problem that has a solution on every instance on `V` is solvable in `n = |V|` rounds (gather the whole tree, pick a solution canonically; *localization trick*). * `va_exists`: problems of family V.a always have a solution (root the tree, label an edge by the parity of `⌊dist(r, u)/2⌋` of its white endpoint `u`). * `viHard_exists`: the hardest problem of family VI.b (`W = 010⁺`, `B = 0⁺10`) always has a solution (root the tree at a leaf, put an edge into `X` iff its black endpoint is the parent of its white endpoint, i.e. orient the edges *away from* the leaf). * `theorem_8_1_upper`, `theorem_8_3`: the `O(n)` upper bounds for V.a/V.b and VI.a/VI.b. ## Remark on the proof of Theorem 8.3 With the paper's convention (an edge in `X` is oriented from its black to its white endpoint), `W = {1}`, `B = {δ − 1}` asks that every relevant node has indegree exactly `1`. Orienting all edges *towards* a leaf root gives a non-root node of degree `k` indegree `k − 1`, which violates the constraints for `k ≥ 3`; the correct choice is to orient *away from* the leaf root (every non-root node then has indegree `1`, and the root is a leaf hence irrelevant). The same slip appears in the remark about Theorem 8.2 ("orient a tree towards a root").
