Consecutive Ballots in Paxos — PODC 2020

Eli Goldweber, Nuda Zhang, Manos Kapritsos. Brief Announcement: On the Significance of Consecutive Ballots in Paxos. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405700. Formalized version: arXiv 2006.01885 v1 (full version); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main claim holds: a value may be learned once a majority has accepted it in a consecutive span of ballots, and this keeps Paxos safe (Theorems 3.1, 3.2, Lemmas 4.1–4.3), also when combined with Consecutive Proposals (Consecutive Paxos). Definition 6 (CQ Chosen) is written so loosely that the auxiliary Lemmas 4.2′ and 4.3′ are false as stated, the proof of Lemma 4.3′ misses a case and the TLA+ invariant printed in Appendix B is vacuous, but local changes repair all three. The IronFleet/Dafny artifact of §6 was not inspected.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Definition 6 (CQ Chosen), with Lemmas 4.2′ and 4.3′

Both lemmas are false for the literal Definition 6, because the ballot-i accept it requires need not be one of the accepts forming the Consecutive Quorum (lemma_4_3'_literal_false, lemma_4_2'_literal_false).

Impact. The proof reads the ballot-i accept as part of the CQ ("the acceptor in Qᵢ that accepted v in ballot i"), and under that reading both lemmas hold (lemma_4_3', lemma_4_2'). Safety is not affected; Lemma 4.3 itself is true, but the paper derives it from the false Lemma 4.3′.

Details

Definition 6 says Chosen(i, v) ≡ ∃Qᵢ. CQ(Qᵢ, v) ∧ ∃α ∈ Qᵢ. accept(i, v)_α ∈ sent_α. Counterexample with five acceptors in Classic Paxos:

  • Ballot 3: A, B, C promise; x is proposed and accepted by A.

  • Ballot 5: B, C, D promise, all ⊥; y is proposed and accepted by D.

  • Ballot 9: A reports (3, x), B, C report ⊥; x is proposed and accepted by A, B, C.

Then {A, B, C} in ballot 9 is a CQ for x, and A also sent accept(3, x), so "x is chosen in ballot 3". But y was proposed in ballot 5 > 3 (against Lemma 4.3′) and accepted by D in ballot 5 > 3 (against Lemma 4.2′). Safety is not affected: y can never be chosen.

Repair

Require the ballot-i accept to be a member's CQ accept (x_α = i; our CQChosen').

Typos

  • In the proof of Lemma 4.1′, "an acceptor that has accepted v in ballot j" should say w.

  • Appendix A writes Chosen(v, i) for the earlier Chosen(i, v) and uses ℤ⁺ for ballots, where Section 3 uses ℕ.

  • Definitions 7–9 are properties of the protocol (Definition 8 is an invariant), not definitions.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 4.3′ · Appendix B

🔧 Lemma 4.3′ (missing case in the proof)

The base case treats i as the largest ballot of the CQ, but when i is a smaller ballot of the CQ the intersecting acceptor may have accepted v in a ballot ≥ j, after promising j, so its promise reports nothing useful.

Impact. Statement true (for the intended Definition 6); repaired (propose_eq_of_interval).

Details

The base case says the intersecting acceptor accepted v "in ballot i or in some ballot < i". The statement still holds because every ballot in the CQ's span carries v.

Repair

Strong induction on the ballot over the whole span [lo, hi] of the CQ: every proposal in a ballot ≥ lo has value v (propose_eq_of_interval).

🔧 Appendix B (ConsecQ, SafeValue), cited by §6.2

The TLA+ check is vacuous as printed: ConsecQ(Q, b, v) ≜ ∀ r ∈ Acceptor : ∧ NotIn(Q, r) ∧ … makes r ∉ Q a top-level conjunct for every acceptor r, so ConsecQ is false for every nonempty Q, AgreedConsecQ never holds, and the invariant SafeValue is true independently of the protocol (tla_agreedConsecQ_false).

Impact. This concerns the paper's supporting evidence, not its theorems: safety of Consecutive Paxos holds (theorem_3_2 with cp = true). We judge the typeset text only; the file actually model-checked may differ.

Details

NotIn(Q, r) ⟹ … was presumably meant, and the inner ∃ n ∈ msgs : … ⟹ … should presumably be ∀. Even with these changes, ConsecQ does not express Definition 5.

Repair

Replace the operator ConsecQ by Definition 5; the invariant then says what cq_propose_eq proves with cp = true.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Single-decree Paxos over asynchronous message passing, in the style of the paper's own TLA+ appendix. The global state holds, for each acceptor, maxBal and its highest accepted proposal. It also holds the set of all messages ever sent.

  • Actions: prepare (1a), promise (1b), propose (2a), accept (2b). Each may fire whenever its guard holds, in any interleaving. A crash is an agent that stops taking steps. Messages can be lost or delayed, because a proposer or learner may use any subset of the sent messages.

  • There is no Byzantine behaviour and no randomness. Ballots are ℕ, with one proposal per ballot.

  • Quorums form an arbitrary intersecting quorum system. The paper's majorities of 2f+1 acceptors are an instance (QuorumSystem.majority); the proofs only use intersection.

  • A flag cp switches the Consecutive Proposals of Definitions 11–12 on (Consecutive Paxos) or off (Classic Paxos). All safety results hold for both.

  • Proposers may use any sent message. That is more than they can actually have received, so the model allows more behaviours and the safety results are stronger.

  • Learning (Definition 3 and its CQ version) is a predicate on the set of messages a learner received. Any two learners, at any two times of an execution, are covered.
