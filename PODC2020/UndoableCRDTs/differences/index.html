Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Only Undoable CRDTs are Counters — PODC 2020.

Paper: S. Dolan, The Only Undoable CRDTs are Counters, PODC 2020 (brief announcement), full version arXiv:2006.10494v1 (with Appendix A).

Every claim below was formalized and holds under the paper's definitions, up to the degenerate case of items 6 and 7 (✏️). Item 2 is a clarification of how Axiom 1 is read, and items 4 and 17 list typos.

Definitions (Section 2)

  1. Partial operations and actions. A primitive operation is app p : S → Option S. An action is a List P applied left to right, so act s [p, q] = s · p · q = q(p(s)). Ok s a is "s · a ok". This matches the paper's conventions exactly.

  2. Clarification: Axiom 1 includes p = q. This is not an error in the paper. Axiom 1 as stated has no distinctness condition, and Fact 2 is derived "by applying Commutativity with a = b". Only the introductory prose ("any two primitive operations … commute") is ambiguous. We state Axiom 1 literally, as "for all p, q". Everything after Fact 2 depends on it, and the case is needed: We added OpSystem.CommutativeDistinct, Axiom 1 for p ≠ q only. The nonnegative counter nnCounter has states ℕ and a dec that is undefined at 0. It satisfies CommutativeDistinct, Axiom 2, finiteness and reachability. However, it is not equivalent to any undoable CRDT (nnCounter_not_equiv), and in particular to no tuple of counters. So under the "distinct operations" reading, which is natural if Axiom 1 means "two different concurrent operations", the main theorem is false. The paper's justification for p = q is that two replicas can independently perform the same operation. Shapiro et al.'s Definition 2.6 concerns concurrent operation instances, which may be equal as operations. The paper's reading is therefore legitimate but strong. Even the plain nonnegative counter, often considered an operation-based CRDT, is not a CRDT under it (nnCounter_not_commutative). The paper's Section 3 remark "an undoable CRDT cannot represent a nonnegative counter" is formalized by nnCounter_not_equiv: the nonnegative counter is equivalent to no undoable CRDT. That result does not use Axiom 1 for nnCounter itself.

  3. Standing assumptions are part of the definition. "P is finite" and "all states are reachable from s₀" are fields of CRDT (finite, reachable). The paper's other assumption, "distinct members of S are logically distinct states", has no formal content and is not modelled.

    • Finiteness of P is used only in Section 2.5, to show that G_s is finitely generated, and it is needed there. For example, ℚ with the operations ±1/n for all n is commutative and undoable, but its group of actions ℚ is not finitely generated. This is not formalized.

    • Reachability is used for "G_{s₀} ≅ states" (stateEquiv) and for Fact 6 at s₀.

  4. Equivalence (Section 2.2) is stated literally. "φ(s) · ψ(p) = φ(s · p) if s · p ok" means that the left side is well defined and equal to the right side. The paper's third bullet reads "φ⁻¹(s') · ψ'(p) = φ⁻¹(s' · p')". This is a typo for ψ'(p'), and we use ψ'(p'). The definition only constrains applicable operations. It does not require φ(s) · ψ(p) to be undefined when s · p is undefined. This is what makes Proposition 3 work with dead operations (item 6), which are sent to ε.

  5. Counters and tuples. The counter modulo n is OpSystem.modCounter n, with states ZMod n and the operations inc and dec. The unbounded counter is modCounter 0, because ZMod 0 = ℤ definitionally. A tuple is indexed by an arbitrary finite type ι with decidable equality, instead of 1..n. It has states ∀ i, Sᵢ and operations Σ i, Pᵢ (Section 2.3).

Statements

  1. Section 3, "All operations are always valid". Nothing in the definitions forbids a primitive operation that applies in no state. deadOp is an undoable CRDT with one state and one never-applicable operation (deadOp_not_valid). The correct statement, which we prove as all_operations_valid, is: an action that applies in some state applies in every state. This is harmless for the main theorem, because the weak equivalence of item 4 maps dead operations to anything.

  2. Section 3, "Negative states always exist" is stated for actions that apply somewhere (negative_states). For a dead operation it is false.

  3. The main theorem (theorem_main) is stated as: there are a finite type ι and moduli m : ι → ℕ such that C is equivalent to counters m, the tuple of counters modulo m i. We additionally record that each m i is 0 (unbounded counter) or a prime power p^k. Here k = 0, giving a trivial counter, is not excluded, since Mathlib's structure theorem does not exclude it.

  4. Proposition 3 is stated for two undoable CRDTs. This is implicit in the paper, since G_{s₀} is only defined, as a group, for undoable CRDTs.

  5. Section 2.6 ("the group of actions of a counter is ℤ or ℤₙ; of a tuple, the direct sum") is proved directly for tuples of counters: countersGroupEquiv : G_{s₀}(counters m) ≃+ ∀ i, ZMod (m i). The general statement "G of a tuple = direct sum of the G's" is not formalized, since the main theorem does not need it.

  6. Section 3, trade-off. "In any CRDT with 2ⁿ states … one of the following must be true" is formalized for every CRDT as tradeoff: either some operation is not undoable, or S is infinite, or there is k ≥ 1 such that s · pᵏ = s for every applicable p and every s. The 2ⁿ "membership" states are not modelled. The informal "extra states" reading is "infinitely many states". finite_states_periodic gives the stronger uniform statement for actions: k is the exponent of G_{s₀}.

Proofs

  1. Propositions 1 and 2, Facts 1–6 follow the paper's inductions. One difference is that Proposition 2 is proved by induction on the first operation of the action, not the last: (p b)⁻¹ = b⁻¹ q₁…qₙ. Fact 3 is stated with an explicit chosen inverse a' satisfying s · a a' = s. Fact 1 keeps the paper's hypothesis that s · a₁ ok, although it is not needed.

  2. The group G_s (Lean-only detail). The paper's argument is complete: inverses in a monoid are unique, so "inverses exist thanks to fact 5" suffices. In Lean, neg is defined from a chosen inverse a⁻¹ₛ (Proposition 2). We therefore first build the commutative monoid on the quotient, then prove well-definedness from uniqueness of inverses (Neg instance in Group.lean).

  3. Section 2.5. The paper writes "generated by P/≡ₛ". We use the finite set of classes [p] of the primitive operations that apply in s.

  4. Proposition 3. The paper defines φ(s) = s₀' · ψ(a) for some a with s₀ · a = s. We use φ = e₂ ∘ ψ ∘ e₁⁻¹, where eᵢ : G_{s₀} ≃ S is [a] ↦ s₀ · a (stateEquiv, a bijection by injectivity of ≡ and reachability). For a primitive operation p that applies at s₀, ψ(p) is a representative (Quotient.out) of ψ([p]). For a dead operation it is ε. The verification of the three conditions is as in the paper.

  5. The structure theorem (Kronecker/Poincaré/Noether) is Mathlib's AddCommGroup.equiv_free_prod_directSum_zmod. directSumEquivPi identifies ℤⁿ × ⨁ᵢ ℤ/pᵢ^{eᵢ} with ∀ j, ZMod (m j).

Typos in the paper

  1. Besides ψ'(p) for ψ'(p') (item 4):

    • The proof of Proposition 3 writes ϕ(s) · ψ(p) = s₀ · ψ(a) · ψ(p); it should be s₀'.

    • Section 2.5 writes "by fact 5 s · a⁻¹ₛ ok"; this is Fact 4.

Not formalized

  1. Section 1 (OR-Set, PN-Set, T-Set, Figure 1, Logoot-Undo) is informal motivation. The OR-Set does not fit the paper's own model with finite P: its removes carry sets of observed IDs, and its adds carry fresh IDs.
