The Only Undoable CRDTs are Counters — PODC 2020

Stephen Dolan. Brief Announcement: The Only Undoable CRDTs are Counters. PODC 2020, pp. 57–58. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405749. Formalized version: the full version arXiv:2006.10494v1, whose Appendix A contains the proofs. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. The main theorem (every undoable CRDT is equivalent to a tuple of counters and modular counters), Propositions 1–3 and Facts 1–6 hold under the paper's own definitions. Only two Section 3 consequences miss the degenerate case of operations that apply in no state. Beyond that there are only typos and one point of reading that matters (Axiom 1, below).

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Section 3: "All operations are always valid" fails for dead operations: a one-state CRDT with one primitive operation that applies in no state is undoable (deadOp_not_valid). The same caveat applies to "negative states always exist". The main theorem is unaffected, because the paper's equivalence only constrains applicable operations, so dead operations can be mapped to the empty action. An action applying in some state applies in every state (all_operations_valid); likewise for negative states (negative_states).

Typos

  • In the definition of equivalence (Section 2.2), φ⁻¹(s') · ψ'(p) should be ψ'(p').

  • In the proof of Proposition 3, s₀ · ψ(a) · ψ(p) should be s₀' · ψ(a) · ψ(p).

  • In Section 2.5, "by fact 5, s · a⁻¹ₛ ok": Fact 4 is the direct reference. This is not an error, since Fact 5 (a⁻¹ₛ a ≡ₛ ε) also gives s · a⁻¹ₛ ok, as ≡ₛ requires both sides to be defined.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. Purely algebraic, exactly as in the paper, with no replicas, network, failures or randomness.

  • An OpSystem has states S, an initial state s0, primitive operations P, and partial maps app p : S → Option S. Actions are lists of operations applied left to right.

  • A CRDT additionally has finitely many operations, every state reachable from s0, and Axiom 1 stated literally for all p, q. An UndoableCRDT also satisfies Axiom 2.

  • CRDTEquiv is the paper's equivalence of Section 2.2, verbatim (with the ψ'(p') typo fixed). It consists of a bijection of states and translations of operations into actions in both directions, constrained only on applicable operations.

  • Counters: OpSystem.modCounter n has states ZMod n (the unbounded counter is n = 0). counters m is the tuple of counters modulo m i, indexed by an arbitrary finite type.

Axiom 1 must include p = q (a clarification, not an error). Axiom 1 has no distinctness condition, and Fact 2 uses "Commutativity with a = b", so the paper is consistent. The introductory prose ("any two primitive operations … commute") could be read as "two different operations", and under that reading the theorem is false. The nonnegative counter (states ℕ, dec undefined at 0) commutes for distinct operations, is undoable, has finitely many operations and every state is reachable. Yet it is equivalent to no undoable CRDT, so it is not a tuple of counters. Under the paper's literal Axiom 1 it is simply not a CRDT: from state 0, inc then dec with p = q = dec breaks the axiom. (nnCounter_commutativeDistinct, nnCounter_not_equiv, nnCounter_not_commutative)
