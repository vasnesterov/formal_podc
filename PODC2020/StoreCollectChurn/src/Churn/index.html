We abstract a timed execution (Section 3) to what the counting lemmas need: every node `p` has an entry time `enter p ≥ 0` (the time of its first step; the initial members `S0` are the nodes with `enter p = 0`, a nonempty set), and possibly a leave time or a crash time (at most one of the two, both not before the entry). A node is *present* at `t` if it entered at or before `t` and did not leave at or before `t`; a crashed node stays present forever; a node is *active* at `t` if it is present and has not crashed at or before `t`. `N t` is the number of present nodes. The paper's assumptions: * **Churn Assumption**: for all `t > 0`, the number of ENTER and LEAVE events in `[t, t + D]` is at most `α · N(t)` (`ChurnExecution.ChurnAssumption`); * **Minimum System Size**: `N(t) ≥ Nmin` for `t ≥ 0` (`ChurnExecution.MinimumSystemSize`); * **Failure Fraction Assumption**: at most `Δ · N(t)` nodes are crashed at `t ≥ 0` (`ChurnExecution.FailureFraction`). The paper implicitly assumes `N(t)` is finite; we assume the slightly stronger (and, given the churn assumption, equivalent) *local finiteness*: only finitely many nodes have entered by any time `t`. Main results: * `lemma1_a`, `lemma1_b` (Lemma 1), and their extension to intervals of length `≤ i · D` (`enters_le`, `N_le`); * `lemma2` (Lemma 2, as stated: `0 < α < 0.206`, `i ≤ 3`), the sharper `lemma2_of_sq` (`2 (1 - α)^2 ≥ 1`, i.e. `α ≤ 1 - 1/√2 ≈ 0.293`, suffices for `i ≤ 3`) and `lemma2_four` (`α < 0.206` even gives `i ≤ 4`); the general `leaves_le`; * `lemma3` (Lemma 3); * `corollary1` (Corollary 1, from the cleared Constraint (A') and (B)), and `corollary1_of_Z_pos`, which only needs `Z > 0` and `Nmin ≥ 1`: Constraint (A) is not needed for Corollary 1.
