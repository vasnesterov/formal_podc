Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Store-Collect in the Presence of Continuous Churn — PODC 2020.

Paper: Attiya, Kumari, Somani, Welch. Store-Collect in the Presence of Continuous Churn with Application to Snapshots and Lattice Agreement (arXiv 2003.07787v3, full version of the PODC 2020 brief announcement).

Store-collect specification (Section 2) — Spec.lean

  • A schedule is a List (SCEv P V); an operation is identified by the position of its invocation, its response is the next event of the same client (Next). Views are partial functions P → Option V.

  • Regularity (1), second half, "no other store operation by p occurs between this invocation and the invocation of cop" is read as: no store of p whose invocation and response both lie in that interval (the usual regular-register reading). This is the weaker of the two possible readings; Theorem 8 is proved under it, so it also holds under the stronger one. The CCC counterexample violates part (2), which does not depend on this choice.

  • V₁ ⪯ V₂ follows the paper literally ("the invocation of STORE_p(v₁) does not occur after the response of STORE_p(v₂)"; vacuous if that store has no response).

  • Uniqueness of stored values (UniqueValues) is a separate predicate. Algorithm 7 never needs it as a hypothesis (its stored values are distinct automatically); the CCC counterexample satisfies it.

CCC algorithm (Algorithms 1–3) — CCC.lean, CCCCounterexample.lean

  • Static fragment. The counterexample uses executions without ENTER/LEAVE/CRASH, so Algorithm 1 never fires and Members = S₀ is constant; only Algorithms 2–3 are modelled operationally. Such executions are executions of the full algorithm.

  • Times are natural numbers (the paper uses reals); D = 20; delays in (0, D]; FIFO per sender; reliable broadcast to all nodes including the sender; the execution is complete (every broadcast received by every node, no event afterwards), hence a full admissible execution of Section 3 with all nodes active forever.

  • The server handler for store broadcasts two messages (store-ack, store-echo) in one step, as in the pseudocode (Section 3 allows one message per step; immaterial).

  • optype is replaced by an explicit phase (idle/collect/store).

  • Charitable repair 1 (✏️ typo): is_joined is initialized to true for the nodes of S₀ (the pseudocode initializes it to false for everyone, which would make every collect and every join block forever in a system consisting of S₀; the proofs assume S₀ nodes are joined).

  • Charitable repair 2 (✏️ missing case): client handlers act only on messages of the current phase of a pending operation. Literally, collect-replies arriving during the store-back increment the shared counter, and store-acks arriving after the return trigger "return" again. In the counterexample no stray reply/ack reaches p₁/p₂ before the violation (up to time 12), so the violation also occurs under the literal code; the repair keeps the tail of the run well formed.

  • Parameters of the counterexample: α = Δ = 1/100, γ = 3/4, β = 3/5, N_min = 2, 5 nodes; they satisfy Constraints A–D (printed and cleared forms, params_ok).

  • Theorem 6 / Corollary 7 are false (theorem6_false), Lemma 10 is false (lemma10_false), Observation 5 is false (observation5_false). Lemmas 7 and 8 (and Case I of Lemma 10) rely on Observation 5 with the same view(·); we did not refute them separately. ❌: the paper's algorithm (CCC) is wrong as written, and no repair is proved. The likely repair is local (Line 47 should return the view broadcast at Line 36, and view(φ) should be the view broadcast in φ), but it is not recoverable from the paper (the text says "returning LView", view(φ) is explicitly "LView at the end of the phase", and the proofs of Observation 5, Lemmas 7, 8, 10 reason with that view). The justifications of Observation 5 ("p receives phase s's store message directly") and of Lemma 10 Case II (a node of Qs ∩ Qc has LView ⪰ view(s)) are valid exactly for the view carried by the store message, so with view(φ) := that view and Line 47 returning it, the paper's own arguments for Observation 5 and Lemmas 7, 8, 10 and Theorem 6 go through essentially verbatim (informal check, not in Lean). The error is a missed interleaving (the client returns a variable that its own server thread mutates), i.e. a protocol bug, not a symbol slip; prose, pseudocode and definition consistently say "LView"; Theorem 6(2)'s proof uses view(s) = V₁, which needs the printed definition; and the journal version (Information and Computation 285, 2022, Line 51 "else return LView", same definition of view(φ)) keeps it.

  • Observation 5 and Lemmas 7, 8 refer to store phases that call "broadcast (Line 42)", the store operation's broadcast; the Lean refutation (observation5_false) uses a store-back phase (Line 36), which is how Lemma 10 / Theorem 6(2) apply them. The same failure occurs for a store operation (its LView at the end can contain a concurrently merged value; not in Lean). We did not verify the repaired algorithm (the churn part, Lemmas 4–9, is not formalized).

  • Typo: the proof of Lemma 10, Case I, says "enter-echo message" for "collect-reply".

Churn model and arithmetic (Lemmas 1–3, Corollary 1, Constraints) — Churn.lean, Constraints.lean

  • Nodes have an entry time, an optional leave time and an optional crash time (at most one of the two), S₀ = nodes entering at 0. Local finiteness (finitely many nodes have entered by any time) is an explicit assumption; the paper uses it implicitly (N(t) is a number).

  • The Churn Assumption is stated as in the paper, for t > 0 only; the case t = 0, used by Lemmas 1–2 at t = 0, is derived (churn_zero, needs local finiteness). Proof remark.

  • Lemma 1 and the extension to intervals of length ≤ i·D (N_le, used implicitly by Lemma 3 and Lemma 10 at times that are not multiples of D) — routine.

  • Lemma 2 is proved as stated (lemma2, α < 0.206, i ≤ 3). Remark: the proof needs only 2(1−α)² ≥ 1, i.e. α ≤ 0.293 (lemma2_of_sq); α < 0.206 ≈ 1 − 2^{-1/3} already gives i ≤ 4 (lemma2_four). Lemmas use the weaker hypotheses α ≥ 0, Δ ≥ 0.

  • Lemma 3 needs t₁ ≥ 0 (implicit in the paper).

  • Constraint (A) (✏️): printed as N_min ≥ 1 / (Z + γ − (1+α)³); its denominator can be ≤ 0 while (B)–(D) hold, and then (A) is vacuous (literal_constraints_not_imply_ZNmin: α = 0.04, Δ = 0.01, γ = 0.1, β = 0.8, N_min = 1 satisfy the printed A–D but Z·N_min < 1). We use the evident intent N_min·(Z + γ − (1+α)³) ≥ 1 (ConstraintA'), which is what Lemma 5 uses.

  • Constraint (D) is used in the cleared form; equivalent to the printed one under β > 0 and (C) (constraintD_iff).

  • Corollary 1 (corollary1) is proved from (A′), (B), γ > 0. Remark: (A) is not needed for it — Z > 0 and N_min ≥ 1 suffice (corollary1_of_Z_pos).

  • The standing constraints imply Lemma 2's hypothesis: (C) ∧ (D) force α < 1/20 (alpha_lt_of_CD, alpha_lt_0206_of_CD) and Δ < 0.22 (Delta_lt_of_CD); Section 7's "less than one-third" is true but loose (Delta_lt_third_of_CD).

  • Example parameters (p. 11): the set α = 0.04, Δ = 0.01, γ = 0.77, β = 0.80, N_min ≥ 2 satisfies A–D (example_alpha_004); the set α = 0, Δ = 0.21, γ = β = 0.79 satisfies A–D at α = 0 (example_alpha_zero) but α = 0 is excluded by the model (α > 0), and for every α > 0 these γ, β violate (B) and (C) (not_constraintB_021_079, not_constraintC_021_079); γ = β = 0.78 works at α = 1/1000 (example_alpha_small). Not a statement error (the text speaks of the extreme case α = 0).

  • The arithmetic core of Lemma 10 Case II (lemma10_core) and the inequalities used in Theorems 3–4 (from (B), (C)) are proved; the operational parts are not.

Algorithm 7 (atomic snapshot) — Snapshot*.lean

  • Abstract store-collect object. Algorithm 7 runs on top of an arbitrary object: the environment chooses the interleaving and the views returned by collects; the only hypothesis is that the resulting schedule is Regular (Section 2). This is exactly the paper's assumption ("Our algorithm … uses a store-collect object"); in particular the theorem holds for any correct store-collect implementation, independently of the churn model.

  • Nodes form an arbitrary type P (possibly infinite), values an arbitrary type V; ⊥ is none. Snapshot views, scounts are partial functions.

  • Each node runs Algorithm 7 as a deterministic automaton (lstep); the choice of q at Line 77 ("if ∃ q such that …") is an arbitrary choice rule Pick (any rule returning a witness when one exists); the theorem holds for every rule.

  • Line 71 STORE_p(⟨−, −, ssqno, −, −⟩) — "with all the other components unchanged": we read the − components as those of the value p stored last (the paper's wording). With the other conceivable reading (the current local variables), the embedded scan of an UPDATE would store the new scounts (assigned at Line 79) together with the old sview of the previous update, and a scan could borrow a view older than its own start: e.g. q completes UPDATE(a) whose embedded scan returned ∅; w completes UPDATE(b); p starts SCAN; q starts UPDATE(a′), its Line 79 sees p's ssqno, its Line 71 stores old sview = ∅ with the new scounts; an update of a fourth node between p's two collects makes p's double collect fail, and p borrows ∅, missing b although UPDATE_w(b) completed before p's scan started. Not formalized in Lean; recorded as an ambiguity of the pseudocode, not as an error (the text supports our reading).

  • Executions are finite lists of events (hist E = CSLib Labels, store-collect events become internal tau steps); linearizability is CSLib's IsLinearization w.r.t. snapSpec P V (update ops carry the writer, as a sequential specification does not see the caller). Since every prefix of an execution is an execution, this covers all prefixes of infinite executions.

  • The UPDATE response is modelled as the empty view (ACK).

  • Linearization. The paper orders direct scans by ⪯, borrowed scans after the direct scan they borrow from, and updates before the first scan that sees them. We give every completed scan a certificate (a successful double collect inside it; for borrowed scans obtained from the stored sview via Lemma 12) and order all scans by the vector of their certificate (a chain by Lemma 11), ties by completion time; updates are placed as in the paper. This avoids the explicit borrowing chains; Lemma 12's nesting (borrow_cert) is what makes the certificate lie inside the borrowing scan, so chains are not needed and the well-foundedness of borrowing is automatic.

  • Lemma 11 is proved for the certificates of all scans (vec_total), Lemma 13 in the form kU_le_vec_of_precedes_upd. Slip (✏️): Lemma 11's proof says "Consider any ⟨r, w⟩ ∈ W₂" for W₁. Typo: Lemma 13's proof speaks of "both stores of uop_p".

  • Updates included in the linearization: those some stored value reflects (Vis; includes all completed ones), rather than "those whose Line 83 store started" (same set up to updates whose store started but whose value is never stored — irrelevant: neither is seen by any scan).

  • Theorem 8, round bound (theorem8_collects): after the scan's Line-71 store completes at t, the scan performs at most #{p | p has an UPDATE pending at t} + 2 collects (one store before; an UPDATE adds one collect and one store). The paper says "linear in the number of nodes present when the operation starts"; its argument (and ours) counts pending updaters at t, which under churn is bounded by N(t) ≤ (1+α)²·N(start) (not formalized; linear either way). Both counts ignore a node that left with an UPDATE pending (not present, but its store may still be observed); the abstract model has no LEAVE, so this does not affect the Lean statement. The count may include q itself (through its own enclosing update). StoredOK records, for a stored value with usqno = k ≥ 1, the invocation of the k-th update before its Line-79 collect.

Not formalized

  • Lemmas 4–9, Theorems 3–4 (joining within 2D, phases complete within 2D), the operational part of Lemma 10 — moot for the algorithm as written (Theorem 6 is false); the repaired CCC is not verified.

  • Section 6.1 (max register, abort flag, set) and Section 6.3 (generalized lattice agreement from the snapshot).
