On the line `0 - 1 - ⋯ - (N-1)`, in round `i` every node `a` with `2^(i+1) ∣ a` activates the edge to `a + 2^(i+1)` (via the common neighbour `a + 2^i`), and `a + 2^i` drops its edge to `a + 2^(i+1)`. Equivalently, node `b ≠ 0` with `2^(i+1) ∣ b` jumps from its parent `b - 2^i` to its grandparent `b - 2^(i+1)`. So every snapshot is a tree; after `R = ⌊log₂ (N-1)⌋` rounds the parent of `b` is `b` with its lowest set bit cleared (the binomial tree), whose depth is at most `R + 1 ≤ ⌈log₂ N⌉`. * `exec N`: the execution (`isExec`, `exec_zero`: starts from `pathGraph N`). * `final_isDepthTree`: after `R` rounds the network is a tree of depth `≤ ⌈log₂ N⌉` rooted at `0`. * `totalActivations_le`: at most `N - 1` activations in total (`Σ_i ⌊(N-1)/2^(i+1)⌋`). This matches the lower bounds `lemma_6_1` (time `Ω(log n)`) and `lemma_6_2_total` (`Ω(n)` activations); the paper adds the deactivations implicitly (its CutInHalf only activates edges, so the final network is not a tree, only of small diameter).
