Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Actively Dynamic Networks — PODC 2020.

Paper: O. Michail, G. Skretas, P. G. Spirakis, Distributed Computation and Reconfiguration in Actively Dynamic Networks, PODC 2020 (arXiv:2003.03355v1, full version with Appendices A–D).

Errors in the paper

  1. Lemma 3.1 is false for GraphToStar as printed (GraphToStar.lemma_3_1_breaks, GraphToStar.line4_phase2). On the line 0-1-2-3 with increasing IDs, the pulling rule ("u activates uw where w is the leader that v activated an edge with in the previous phase") makes C(0) jump to node 2 in phase 2, while C(2) merges into C(3) in that same phase. In phase 3 C(0)'s target "did not activate", so C(0) enters merging. In phase 4 it merges into node 2, a follower. Afterwards lead 0 = 2 and lead 2 = 3, so the committees are not stars. In phase 5 C(3) is the only committee and enters termination while 0 is still attached only to 2 (GraphToStar.line4_terminates_wrong), so the output is not a spanning star around 3. The proof of Lemma 3.1 claims that pulling committees run TreeToStar on committees, with the root's children merging and the grandchildren becoming children. This overlooks great-grandchildren jumping onto children that dissolve in the same phase. Consequently Theorem 3.8 is not established. A natural repair is to redirect a pointer whose target merged away to the absorbing leader (one extra round, distance 2 through the former leader). It terminated with O(log n) phases in our simulations (scripts/g2s.py finds the failure, scripts/g2s_fix.py + run_fix*.py test the repair), but the repair is not proved.

  2. Lemmas 3.2–3.7 (time of GraphToStar): informal and partly garbled. They are not formalized, since the algorithm they analyse is broken (item 1).

    • Lemma 3.2's computation S(C(u_{s+log i})) = S(C(u_s)) + 2·S + 4·S + … + 2^{log(i-1)}·S = 2^{k+i} does not typecheck as written. For a pulling tree of depth j, the root gains about j·2^k, not 2^{k+j}; the intended bound needs depth ≥ 2^{j-1} after j waiting phases.

    • Lemmas 3.4 and 3.5 use inconsistent exponents (2^{k+p-4} vs 2^{k+p-3}) and phase indices (p+1 vs s+p+1).

    • Lemma 3.7 ("each phase consists of at most 2 rounds") ignores the communication between followers and leaders needed to learn neighbouring committees' UIDs and modes.

  3. Theorem 6.4 / D.12: the proof is a sketch.

    • "Corresponding states" (Def. D.7) is garbled. Lemma D.11 uses undefined i_min, i_max and "a previous proof" for k − log k. Observation 2 has no proof. The final sum is claimed "= Θ(log n)" instead of Θ(n log n).

    • The theorem implicitly assumes comparison-based algorithms, which §2.1 says of all of the paper's lower bounds; the proof uses increasing-order rings.

    Not formalized.

  4. Proposition 2.1 / A.1, "⌈log d⌉ ≤ log n": false in general. For the 6-node line (d = 5), TreeToStar needs exactly ⌈log₂ 5⌉ = 3 rounds and 3 > log₂ 6 (ceil_log_depth_le_log_false, line6_not_star_two). We prove the exact round count ⌈log₂ d⌉ (TreeToStar.star_iff).

  5. Observation 1 (App. D.1) says a Depth-log n tree forces PO_{u,v} ≤ log n. The correct bound is 2 log n: two leaves can be at distance twice the depth. Lemma D.3's final bound n − 1 − 2 log n is nevertheless what we obtain (lemma_6_2_total with d = log n).

  6. Smaller items.

    • Proof of Prop. A.1: "O(log n) total edge activations" should be O(n log n).

    • Prop. 2.2 says maximum degree 3, while §2.3 and the proof of A.2 say at most 4. We prove ≤ 3 for every snapshot (degree_le_three); 4 is only reached in the middle of a round, between activations and deactivations.

    • CutInHalf only activates edges, so its output is not a tree. We add the deactivations of the edges that become redundant.

Modelling choices and deviations

  • Model. Snapshots are SimpleGraph V and rounds are 0-indexed (E 0 is the paper's D(1) = G_s).

    • ValidRound G G' states that every edge of G' is in G or joins two nodes with a common G-neighbour. validRound_iff shows it is equivalent to the paper's E(i+1) = (E(i) ∪ E_ac(i)) \ E_dac(i) with E_ac between potential neighbours and E_dac ⊆ E(i).

    • The rules for duplicate or conflicting requests (two endpoints activating the same edge, or disagreeing) are subsumed: only the resulting graph matters.

    • Connectivity of snapshots is not part of the model. All our executions are trees at every round (isTree).

  • "Active edges per round" (Props. 2.1/2.2). We count the edges active during the round, i.e. the old edges plus the activated ones ((E t ⊔ jumpAct …).edgeSet.ncard ≤ 2n − 3). That is the count the paper's proof uses. We also prove that every snapshot has exactly n − 1 edges. The paper's separate measure "maximum activated edges" |E(i) \ E(1)| is not stated separately. It is at most n − 1 here.

  • Total activations. The paper's "O(n log n)" / "n log n" is proved in explicit form:

    • TreeToStar: ≤ (n − 2)⌈log₂ d⌉.

    • LineToCompleteBinaryTree: ≤ (N − 2)⌈log₂ d⌉ for N nodes.

    • CutInHalf: ≤ N − 1.

    All three hold over any horizon.

  • Distributed details of the subroutines. We formalize the global effect of the per-node rules (pointer jumps). Each node knows its parent; learning the grandparent's identity and child count needs one message from the parent within the same round, which is not modelled.

  • LineToCompleteBinaryTree.

    • The rule "repeat until attached to the root or the grandparent has two children" is formalized statelessly: in each round, move iff the parent is not the root and the grandparent has < 2 children. By the closed form (LineArith.Q), a node that stops never satisfies the condition again, so the stateful and stateless rules have the same executions (checked numerically up to 300 nodes, scripts/check_stateful.py).

    • The line is rooted at an endpoint, as in the paper's construction and its asynchronous variant. The paper says "any line rooted at u0"; simulations suggest that lines rooted at an interior node also work, but this is not formalized.

    • "Binary tree centered at u0" is proved in the strong form "complete binary tree in level order": node i has depth ⌊log₂(i+1)⌋, total depth ⌊log₂ N⌋ (final_edist, final_isDepthTree).

    • The time is kk n ≤ ⌊log₂ n⌋ ≤ ⌈log₂ d⌉. After that the network is constant (exec_stable), and before it the network still changes (exec_succ_ne).

  • Depth-d Tree target (IsDepthTree G r d): G is a tree and every node is within distance d of r. Leader election (the "unique leader at the root" part) is irrelevant for the centralized statements and is not modelled.

  • Lemma 6.1 / D.2.

    • Proved for every execution from the spanning line whose final snapshot has radius ≤ d: n − 1 ≤ 2^{T+1} d (lemma_6_1), hence ⌊log₂(n−1)⌋ ≤ T + 1 + ⌈log₂ d⌉.

    • The "Ω(log n)" is made explicit as ⌊log₂ n⌋ ≤ 2T + 7 for d ≤ ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1 (lemma_6_1_asymp).

    • The proof halves distances instead of using the paper's information "potential" PO.

  • Lemma 6.2 / D.3–D.4.

    • n − 1 ≤ (total activations) + 2d holds with no time restriction (lemma_6_2_total). The paper assumes O(log n) time, which is only needed for the per-round version.

    • Per round: n − 1 ≤ T · m + 2d if every round activates ≤ m edges (lemma_6_2_perRound). With T, d = O(log n) this gives m = Ω(n / log n).

    • The key fact is that one activated edge shortens any distance by at most one (ValidRound.edist_le_add; a shortest path uses each new edge once).

  • Theorem 6.3 / D.5.

    • Only the line case is formalized (CutInHalf.theorem_6_3_line): ⌊log₂(N−1)⌋ rounds, ≤ N − 1 activations, final tree of depth ≤ ⌈log₂ N⌉ rooted at 0.

    • The paper's general-graph version (Euler tour of a spanning tree used as a virtual ring of ≤ 2n nodes) is not formalized. Its depth bound would be log(2n), not log n.

    • The paper's CutInHalf indexes rounds from 1 and activates u_j u_{j+2^i} for j ≡ 1 (mod 2^i); we use 0-indexed nodes with 2^{i+1} ∣ j in round i.

  • GraphToStar model (GraphToStar.lean).

    • Committee/phase level: leaders, modes, the leader each leader last activated an edge with (tgt), and flags for "activated a leader edge in the previous phase" and "some committee activated an edge with me in the previous phase".

    • Committees are neighbours iff an edge of G_s joins their members or one leader activated an edge with the other.

    • Phases are synchronous and all decisions use the state at the start of the phase, which is how the mode descriptions read. §2.4 says the algorithms run in "asynchronous phases"; lock-step phases are one admissible schedule, and Lemma 3.1 claims correctness for every execution.

    • Under every reading of "the leader of C(v)" (the node 2, or the current leader 3 of its committee) the printed rules fail: the merging rule moves only followers and presupposes that the leader u is already adjacent to the target leader, and 0 is never adjacent to 3. At the committee level the intent (C(0) becomes a child of C(3)) is clear, but realising it needs an activation step that the algorithm does not contain.

    • The model ignores rounds within a phase, the temporary edge e₁ of the selection, and the termination phase. None of these affects the failure in phases 1–4.

  • Not formalized. Lemmas 3.2–3.7, Theorem 3.8, Section 4 (GraphToWreath, Theorem 4.2), Section 5 (GraphToThinWreath, Theorem 5.1), Appendices B and C, Theorem 6.4 / D.12, and the asynchronous LineToCompleteBinaryTree (Lemma B.4, Corollary B.5).
