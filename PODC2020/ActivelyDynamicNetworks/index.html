Actively Dynamic Networks — PODC 2020

Othon Michail, George Skretas, Paul G. Spirakis. Distributed Computation and Reconfiguration in Actively Dynamic Networks. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405744. Formalized version: arXiv 2003.03355 v1 (full version with Appendices A–D); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Lemma 3.1 is false for GraphToStar as printed: on the 4-node line with increasing IDs a pulling committee jumps onto a committee that is merging away in the same phase, it then merges into a follower, the committees stop being stars, and the output is not a spanning star. So Theorem 3.8 for GraphToStar, the first of the three distributed algorithms announced in the abstract, is not established, and neither is the distributed Ω(n log n) bound (Theorem 6.4). The basic subroutines (Propositions 2.1, 2.2, up to a small slip in the bound ⌈log d⌉ ≤ log n), the centralized lower bounds (Lemmas 6.1, 6.2) and the matching centralized upper bound on the line (Theorem 6.3, line case) hold and are proved, while the other two algorithms (Sections 4–5) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Lemma 3.1 (GraphToStar solves Depth-1 Tree), hence Theorem 3.8

On the line 0 - 1 - 2 - 3 with IDs increasing along the line, a pulling committee jumps onto a committee that is merging away in the same phase, the committees stop being stars, and the output is not a spanning star (GraphToStar.lemma_3_1_breaks, line4_terminates_wrong).

Impact. Theorem 3.8 (O(log n) time, O(n log n) activations for GraphToStar), the first of the three distributed algorithms announced in the abstract, is not established. The bug needs only a pulling chain of length 3, so it occurs on most inputs: it showed up in about 9% of random connected graphs with up to 9 nodes in our committee-level simulator.

Details

  • Phase 1: every committee selects its right neighbour. C(2) pairs with C(3) and enters merging. C(1) and C(0) selected committees that themselves selected, so they enter pulling. C(3) was selected and enters waiting.

  • Phase 2: by the pulling rule, C(1) jumps to 3. C(0) jumps to 2, the leader C(1) activated an edge with in phase 1. But in the same phase C(2) merges into C(3), so node 2 is now a follower of 3.

  • Phase 3: "the leader of C(2)" did not activate an edge in phase 2, so C(0) enters merging.

  • Phase 4: C(0) merges into node 2.

Now node 0's leader is 2, whose leader is 3. The committee invariant ("each committee is a star around its maximum-ID leader") is broken, and the network is not a spanning star (0 hangs at distance 2 from 3). In phase 5 C(3), now the only committee, enters termination mode while 0 is still attached only to 2: no rule ever connects 0 to 3. The proof's claim that a pulling tree "executes TreeToStar on committees … C_r's children merge with C_r and their children become the new children of C_r" overlooks that in TreeToStar no vertex disappears. Here a depth-3 committee jumps onto a depth-1 committee that is dissolving in the same phase.

Repair

When the target merges away, redirect the pointer to the absorbing leader (a potential neighbour through the former leader, so this costs one extra round). In simulation this repair terminated in ≈ 2–3 log₂ n phases on all tested inputs (lines, trees and random graphs, up to 256 nodes). We did not prove it, so Theorem 3.8 remains unproved.

❌ Lemmas 3.2–3.7 (time analysis of GraphToStar)

The time analysis is not a proof: it refers to the broken invariant above, and its formulas and indices are garbled.

Impact. Unestablished and not formalized, so the O(log n) time is not saved even for a repaired algorithm. Its status is unknown, not refuted.

Details

  • Lemma 3.2's size formula writes S(C(u_{s+log i})) and sums 2^{log(i-1)} terms equal to 2^{k+i}, which gives about j·2^k rather than 2^{k+j}.

  • Lemmas 3.4/3.5 have inconsistent indices (2^{k+p-4} vs 2^{k+p-3}, "phase p+1" vs "s+p+1").

  • Lemma 3.7's "each phase has ≤ 2 rounds" ignores the communication a leader needs to learn its neighbouring committees' IDs and modes.

❌ Theorem 6.4 / D.12 (distributed Ω(n log n) activations)

The proof is a sketch: several notions are undefined or garbled, and its final sum evaluates to "= Θ(log n)".

Impact. Unestablished, so the "optimal" total of O(n log n) activations has no matching bound; the symmetry argument is plausible but not formalized, and the theorem is not refuted.

Details

Undefined or garbled: "corresponding states" (Def. D.7), i_min/i_max in Lemma D.11, and the "previous proof" giving k − log k rounds. Observation 2 has no proof. The bound concerns comparison-based algorithms on increasing-order rings (stated in §2.1, not in the theorem).

🔧 Observation 1 / Lemma D.2 (distance bound off by a factor 2)

"Depth-log n tree ⇒ PO_{u,v} ≤ log n" should be 2 log n: two leaves are at distance up to twice the depth.

Impact. Bookkeeping only: the bounds of Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2 survive (lemma_6_1, lemma_6_2_total).

Details

The argument mixes information propagation with distances, but the bounds survive with a cleaner proof: one round at most halves any distance, and each activated edge shortens a distance by at most one.

✏️ Slips

  • Proposition 2.1 ("⌈log d⌉ ≤ log n rounds"): TreeToStar takes exactly ⌈log₂ d⌉ rounds (proved), and on the line with n = 6 nodes, d = 5, it needs 3 > log₂ 6 rounds (ceil_log_depth_le_log_false, line6_not_star_two). So ⌈log₂ d⌉ can exceed log₂ n, but the bound holds with ⌈log₂ n⌉.

  • Proof of Prop. A.1: "O(log n) total edge activations" should be O(n log n).

Typos

  • Prop. 2.2 states maximum degree 3, while its proof and §2.3 say "at most 4". In every snapshot the degree is ≤ 3 (proved). The value 4 only appears in the middle of a round, after the activations and before the deactivations.

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • The actively dynamic network (§2.1) is Cslib.DynNet.Active in Podc/Computability/Distributed/DynamicNetworks/ActivelyDynamic.lean. Snapshots are E : ℕ → SimpleGraph V. A round is valid (ValidRound) iff every new edge joins two nodes with a common neighbour at the start of the round; deactivations are free. validRound_iff proves this equals the paper's E(i+1) = (E(i) ∪ E_ac) \ E_dac, with activations only between potential neighbours. numActivated/totalActivations are |E_ac(i)| and their sum. The network is synchronous and deterministic, with no failures and unique IDs.

  • Lower bounds (Lemmas 6.1, 6.2) quantify over all executions of the model, i.e. every centralized strategy, on the spanning line. They only need the final snapshot to have radius ≤ d around some node; the paper's "rooted tree of depth d" (IsDepthTree) is a special case.

  • Subroutines (Props. 2.1, 2.2) are modelled by their global effect. Nodes know their parent (orientation). A moving node activates the edge to its grandparent and deactivates the one to its parent, so a round is a pointer jump jump P S of the parent map (PointerJumping.lean). We prove this is a valid round of the model. For TreeToStar the mover set is "parent ≠ root", so after t rounds the parent is parent^[2^t]. For LineToCompleteBinaryTree it is "parent ≠ root and grandparent has < 2 children", with the closed form i ↦ i − 2^{min(t, kk i)} (LineArith.lean). The message exchange needed to learn the grandparent and its number of children is not modelled: it fits in the same round.

  • GraphToStar is modelled at the committee/phase level, rule by rule (GraphToStar.lean), with synchronous phases and all decisions taken on the state at the start of the phase. This is enough to exhibit the failure. The model works on the 3-node line (line3_ok).
