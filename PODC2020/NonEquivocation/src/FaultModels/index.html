import Papers.PODC2020.NonEquivocation.Model /-! # The space of fault models (Sections 3, 4, 7.2 and Fig. 4) Madsen–Debois, *On the Subject of Non-Equivocation* (PODC 2020). Besides `byzantine`, `wne`, `sne` and `omission` (`Model.lean`) we define, in the same message-map framework, * `crash`: a faulty `y` has a *crash round* `c`; before round `c` it relays truthfully, after round `c` it is silent, and in round `c` every receiver gets either all or none of `y`'s round-`c` reports (in the full-information protocol they form one message); * `authByzLiteral`: the authenticated Byzantine fault model of Section 7.2 *exactly as printed*: `∀ p ∈ N, ∀ w' w, σ(w'|p|w) = σ(p|w) ∨ σ(w'|p|w) = ⊥`; * `authByzRep`: the first natural repair, comparing with what `p` *reports* (`rep σ(p|w)`); * `authByz`: the repaired model we use: a value about a route through a correct `p` is absent, the value `p` reported, or the omission marker `⟨⊥⟩` (nobody can forge `p`'s signature, but anybody can claim not to have received a message). All fault models include Definition 2.1 (`NonFaulty`) and constrain only routes without consecutive duplicates and of at most `k` rounds. ## Results (Fig. 4) Inclusions (for all `k F σ`): `omission_of_crash`, `wne_of_omission` (in `Model.lean`), `authByz_of_omission`, `wne_of_sne`, and the trivial `byzantine_of_wne`, `byzantine_of_authByz`. Separations (explicit message maps built with `mkσ`, `P = Fin 3` or `Fin 5`, values `Val`): * `crash_not_le_sne`: a crash during the round-1 broadcast is not strong non-equivocation; * `sne_crash_silent`: a silent faulty process is in `sne ∩ crash`; * `omission_not_le_crash`; * `sne_inter_authByz_not_le_omission` (a faulty process consistently lies about its own value; hence also `wne ⊄ omission`, `sne ⊄ crash`, `(wne ∩ authByz) ⊄ omission`); * `sne_not_le_authByz` (example (1) of §7.2; hence `wne ⊄ authByz`); * `authByz_not_le_wne` (example (2) of §7.2); * `paperEx3` (example (3) of §7.2 with `n = 5`, `t = 2`: in `wne ∩ authByz`, not in `omission`).
