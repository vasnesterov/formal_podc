import Papers.PODC2020.NonEquivocation.PSL /-! # Lemmas 4.7/4.8 fail if `v` may be `⊥`; so does line 8 of Algorithm 2 under that reading Madsen–Debois, PODC 2020, Lemma 4.7 ("no consistent majority for `⊥` can exist") and line 8 of Algorithm 2 with `v` ranging over values *and* `⊥`. Instance (`n = 3`, `t = 1`, two rounds, weak non-equivocation): processes `0, 1` are non-faulty, `2` is faulty; all private values are `v`; process `2` behaves correctly except that it omits all its messages to `0`. Then `Q = {0, 2}` is a consistent `2`-majority **for `⊥`** at process `0` (`|Q| = 2 = t + 1`, and the only admissible route is `0|2`, which carries `⊥`; the routes `0|0|2`, `0|2|2` have consecutive duplicates). The proof of Lemma 4.7 overlooks that the only non-faulty member of `Q` may be the decider itself. Meanwhile `{1, 2}` is a consistent `2`-majority for `v` at process `1` (so Lemma 4.8 fails too under this reading), and a line 8 that allows `v = ⊥` makes `0` output `⊥` (not even a value) and `1` output `v`. The type-consistent reading `v ∈ V` (line 2: "⊥ ∉ V") is the one used in `PSL.lean`, under which Algorithm 2 is correct.
