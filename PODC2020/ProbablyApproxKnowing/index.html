Probably Approximately Knowing — PODC 2020

Yoram Moses, Nitzan Zamir. Probably Approximately Knowing. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405734. Formalized version: arXiv 2007.02984 v1 (full version with appendix); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All numbered results hold: Theorems 4.2, 5.2, 6.2 (the main result), 7.1, Corollary 7.2, Lemmas 4.3, 5.1, B.1, F.1, and the numbers of Example 1. One concrete claim in §7 about Example 1 is false: that every protocol meeting the constraint μ(φ_both | fire_A) ≥ 0.99 makes Alice believe φ_both with degree ≥ 0.9 with probability ≥ 0.9 when she fires, which fails as soon as Alice fires as a mixed action. Nothing depends on it, and it holds for Alice's belief that Bob fires, which keeps the example's message.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ §7 (claim about Example 1)

The claim that every protocol with μ(φ_both | fire_A) ≥ 0.99 makes Alice's belief in φ_both reach 0.9 with probability ≥ 0.9 when she fires is false as soon as Alice fires as a mixed action: in our counterexample that probability is 0 (Example1.Mixed.section7_claim_false).

Impact. The claim illustrates the results of §7 and no numbered result uses it. With φ_both replaced by "Bob fires" (the two constraints coincide), the claim holds in every protocol-generated system (JointProtocol.section7_claim_fireB); for φ_both itself it stays false.

Details

§7 says: "Corollary 7.2 implies that in every protocol that satisfies this constraint [μ(φ_both | fire_A) ≥ 0.99], the probability that Alice's degree of belief that both are firing together when it decides to fire meets or exceeds 0.9 is at least 0.9" (emphasis in the paper). Corollary 7.2 needs φ_both to be local-state independent of fire_A, and that fails as soon as Alice fires as a mixed action. Counterexample (the Spec of Example 1 holds): if go = 1, Alice sends two messages in round 1 and fires at time 1 with probability 1/2; Bob fires at time 1 iff he received one of them. Then μ(φ_both | fire_A) = 0.99. But when Alice fires she does not yet know her coin (§2.2: "when the agent decides on the mixed step she does not know which of the actions … will actually be performed"), so β_A(φ_both) = 0.5 · 0.99 = 0.495. So the belief reaches 0.9 with probability 0, not ≥ 0.9. This is general: in every protocol-generated pps, β_i(φ ∧ does_i(α)) ≤ P_i(ℓ_i)(α), the probability of choosing α (JointProtocol.belief_and_does_le, cprob_belief_and_does_eq_zero).

This is not a typo: the sentence names φ_both, the very fact of the constraint it applies Corollary 7.2 to, and it stresses "every protocol", while mixed actions are part of the paper's model and exactly the case §§4 and 6 warn about.

Repair

The fire_B version is true in every protocol-generated system, because "j performs β" is local-state independent of every action of i ≠ j (JointProtocol.section7_claim_fireB). The paper's Lemma 4.3 does not cover this case: "Bob fires" is not past-based.

✏️ Slips

  • Abstract: it states that the expected belief when acting equals the probability that φ holds when acting, and that a threshold 1 − ε² forces belief ≥ 1 − ε with probability ≥ 1 − ε, without the hypothesis of local-state independence, without which both are false (Fig1.theorem_6_2_needs_independence): in Figure 1 with φ = does_i(α), μ(φ@α | α) = 1 but E(β_i(φ)@α | α) = 1/2, and the belief never reaches 1. The contribution list in §1 and the theorems in the body carry the hypothesis.

  • Lemma 4.3(b): in an arbitrary tree pps (§2.1), past-based facts need not be local-state independent, so the lemma silently needs a protocol-generated system with observable termination, and its proof is a sketch. Example: two initial states that i cannot tell apart, and i performs α in exactly one of them. The proof uses "the probability that i performs α is the same at all points with local state ℓ_i". This needs a pps generated by a joint protocol in which all participants choose independently, and runs that cannot end at different times while i has the same local state. We prove the lemma for protocol-generated systems with a fixed horizon via a Markov-property argument. It also holds for facts that additionally depend on the other agents' current actions (JointProtocol.lemma_4_3b, JointProtocol.lsi_of_indepOwnAction).

Typos

  • The proof of Theorem 6.2 cites "Theorem 4.1" for Definition 4.1, and so does the remark after Theorem 4.2.

  • In the proof of Theorem 7.1, β_i(φ)@α | α [r] should be β_i(φ)@α [r].

Model.

  • Abstract pps (PPS, Basic.lean). Finitely many runs with positive prior probabilities summing to 1, a common length per run, and the local state r_i(t) of every agent at every point, under synchrony (a local state determines the time). Facts are arbitrary sets of points, and an action is given by its fact does_i(α). Beliefs, φ@ℓ, φ@α, proper actions, β_i(φ)@α (0 off R_α), local-state independence and expected belief follow Definitions 3.1–6.1 literally. Every tree pps of §2.1 gives such a structure. Conversely, every such structure is a tree pps in which the environment's initial state fixes the run. So §§4–7 are proved in exactly the paper's generality, and slightly more: the action may be any fact.

  • Protocol-generated pps (JointProtocol, Protocol.lean), used for Lemma 4.3(b) and §7. A synchronous system: an initial distribution over global states, participants (agents and the environment) with local states (t, loc p g), a probabilistic protocol per participant, and independent choices in every round. There is a deterministic transition function and a fixed number N of rounds. Runs are the action sequences of positive probability; this matches the paper's assumption that the environment records the history. There are no failures and no adversary: the adversary is fixed, as in §2.

  • Examples are concrete abstract pps's: Figure 1, T̂ of Figure 2, FS, and the mixed variant.
