Recoverable Mutual Exclusion with Constant Amortized RMR Complexity — PODC 2020

David Yu Cheng Chan, Philipp Woelfel. Recoverable Mutual Exclusion with Constant Amortized RMR Complexity from Standard Primitives. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405736. Formalized version: the conference version (10 pages); proofs deferred to the full version were not used.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The algorithm of Figure 1 is correct: mutual exclusion, critical-section re-entry (wait-free), starvation freedom, wait-free recovery and wait-free recovery to exit are all proved. The RMR bound "O(m) when m passages begin" fails under the paper's literal definition of a passage, but holds in the DSM model when every crash also starts a passage, a one-clause change to the definition. The bound in the CC model, proved only in the full version, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorems 1–2 (RMR bound "O(m) when m passages begin")

Under the paper's literal definition of a passage, a single process that calls Enter() once and then crashes in the critical section over and over begins one passage but incurs unboundedly many RMRs (Literal.rmr_literal_unbounded).

Impact. The intent is evident: with the usual RME convention that a crash ends a passage and the recovery starts a new one, the bound holds (rmr_bound).

Details

The paper says a passage "begins when [the process] calls Enter() and ends when the process crashes or when it finishes its following Exit() call". Read literally, the steps of Recover() belong to no passage. Take one process: it calls Enter() once and reaches the critical section. It then crashes in the critical section over and over, and each time Recover() line 20 sends it straight back in. Every round executes D.FAI() (line 15). This is an RMR in the CC model (a non-read operation), and in the DSM model for every process whose segment does not hold D (with n ≥ 2, pick such a process and keep the others idle).

Repair

We prove at most 25·#Enter() + 76·#crashes RMRs in the DSM model, counting only crashes outside the remainder section (rmr_bound).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 2.1 (19) and Lemmas 2.4–2.6 omit the fairness hypothesis. Lemma 2.1 is stated for "any run" and (19) assumes only finitely many crashes, but take two processes and no crash: process false gets ticket 1 and grants it to itself (W = (1,true)), then stops taking steps; process true gets ticket 2 and busy-waits forever with Token stuck at 0 (Lemma2_1_19.lemma2_1_19_false). These lemmas are only used in the starvation-freedom proof, where fairness holds, and there the paper's argument goes through.

Typos

  • Section 1, definition of a super-passage: "begins when the process calls Enter() and when it completes its following Exit() call" should read "and ends when".

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, deterministic algorithm, processes of any finite type. Shared objects: FAI counters C, D; CAS objects (with read/write) A[1..∞], W[p], Exit[p], Token. A[c] is empty / claimed q / skipped (0 / q / −1).

  • One shared-memory operation per step. The local code after it is merged into that step, and the recursive Move_Token() calls are tail calls (a jump to line 34). The program counter carries the local variables.

  • Individual crash–recovery. Any process may crash at any time. Outside the remainder section a crash resets its local state and starts Recover(); in the remainder section it does nothing. Shared memory (NVM) is never lost.

  • Executions are arbitrary schedules ℕ → {step p, crash p} (an adversarial scheduler, no randomness). Liveness assumes finitely many crashes and fairness: a process outside the remainder section keeps taking steps. A step in the CS is the call of Exit().

  • RMRs (DSM only): W[p] and Exit[p] live in p's segment. Every access to C, D, Token or A is charged as an RMR, which is an upper bound for every placement.

  • Ghost state (ticket owners, c_{p,t}, crash credits, lost tickets) is computed alongside the execution and never read by the algorithm.

Main.lean

Omitted proofs. Lemma 2.1 (19 items), Lemmas 2.2, 2.4, 2.6, 2.7, 2.10 and all RMR claims come without proof. We prove all items we need, using a single inductive invariant (Inv). Lemma 2.3's proof argues backwards in time. We made it inductive, and the argument is sound. Section 3.1 claims that skipped tickets are at most twice the number of crashes. This is true (skipped_le_two_mul_crashes) and gives Lemma 2.10.

Not formalized. The CC-model RMR bound, the worst-case O(1+f) RMRs per passage, FCFS without crashes (all proved only in the full version), and the wait-free Exit() variant of Section 3.4.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Self-contained model (Mathlib + CSLib's SumUpdate and Nat.Descent); CSLib's generic crash-recovery model (SharedMemory/Recoverable.lean) is not used, since the algorithm needs an explicit program-counter model. The invariant was first checked exhaustively by an explicit-state model checker on small instances. Non-vacuity: the hypotheses Fair ∧ FinCrashes hold for the round-robin schedule (example in Spec.lean); the counterexample executions show that processes do enter the critical section and that crashes and recoveries happen in the model. CSLib candidates: the generic fairness helpers Live.NoCrash, Live.follow, Live.descent_false (LivenessTrace.lean) and card_filter_update (InvBasic.lean).
