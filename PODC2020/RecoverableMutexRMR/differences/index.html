Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Recoverable Mutual Exclusion with Constant Amortized RMR Complexity — PODC 2020.

Paper: D. Y. C. Chan, P. Woelfel, Recoverable Mutual Exclusion with Constant Amortized RMR Complexity from Standard Primitives, PODC 2020 (DOI 10.1145/3382734.3405736), conference version (10 pages; proofs of several lemmas and of the RMR bounds are deferred to a full version that we did not use).

Statement-level issues in the paper

  1. Theorems 1–2 (RMR bound) are false under the paper's literal definition of a passage (🔧). The paper defines: "A passage of a process begins when it calls Enter() and ends when the process crashes or when it finishes its following Exit() call", and claims that "in any execution in which m passages begin" the total number of RMRs is O(m). Read literally, the steps of Recover() belong to no passage. A single process that calls Enter() once, reaches the critical section and then crashes in the critical section again and again re-enters it through Recover() line 20 each time, executing D.FAI() (line 15) every time: one passage, unboundedly many RMRs (Literal.rmr_literal_unbounded, RMRLiteral.lean). Line 15 is an RMR in the CC model (a non-read operation) for any n. The Lean execution has one process and uses our DSM charging rule (item 9), which is only an upper bound: with n = 1 all of memory could sit in the process's segment. For the DSM model with n ≥ 2 the same execution works for any process whose segment does not hold D (the other processes stay in the remainder section); this variant and the CC model are not mechanized. The intended reading is the standard one of the RME literature (and the one suggested by the paper's own remark that passages and super-passages coincide only in the absence of crashes): every crash ends a passage and the recovery starts a new one. With this reading the bound holds; we prove ≤ 25·#Enter() + 76·#crashes in the DSM model (rmr_bound), where #crashes counts only crashes outside the remainder section (a crash in the remainder section has no effect and starts no passage). The fix is local (the definition of a passage) and recoverable from the paper.

  2. Lemma 2.1 (19) (and Lemmas 2.4–2.6) omit the fairness hypothesis (✏️). Lemma 2.1 is stated for "any run E", and (19) only assumes finitely many crashes. Without fairness it is false: two processes, no crash; process false obtains ticket 1 and grants it to itself (W[false] = (1,true)), then stops taking steps; process true takes ticket 2 and busy-waits forever; Token stays 0 (Lemma2_1_19.lemma2_1_19_false, Lemma2_1_19.lean). Lemmas 2.4–2.6 have the same omission. All of them are used only inside the starvation-freedom proof, where fairness holds, so this is a missing implied assumption.

No other statement of the paper that we checked is false.

Proof-level differences

  1. Lemma 2.1 has no proof in the paper ("For brevity, some proofs have been omitted"). We prove it via a single inductive invariant Inv (InvDefs.lean, proof in Inv*.lean, inv_exec). The temporal items of Lemma 2.1 are replaced by state predicates:

    • (15)+(6) "Token moves past w only after the owner of w left (w,true)" becomes done: for every x ≤ Token claimed by q, W[q] no longer holds ticket x;

    • (16) follows from grant: W[q] = (−,true) implies W[q] = (Token+1,true) and A[Token+1] = q;

    • (14) becomes future: a claimed ticket beyond Token is the owner's current ticket, or Token+1 and the owner is in Exit() after line 10 on its way to move Token (OnTrack);

    • the remaining items are local invariants per program counter (LI) or follow from them. The invariant was first validated by exhaustive explicit-state model checking (2 processes with ≤ 4 tickets and ≤ 2 crashes; 3 processes with ≤ 3 tickets and 1 crash).

  2. Lemma 2.3 (D + |I| − C ≥ 0): the paper's proof argues backwards in time (no successful line-46 CAS between the reading of C and the skip). We make it inductive with a per-process local invariant at lines 38/46 (c ≤ d and A[ℓ+1] = 0 and the owner of ℓ+1 in I imply Φ ≥ 1); same argument.

  3. Lemma 2.10 (finitely many −1 entries; proof omitted in the paper) and the claim of Section 3.1 that the number of skipped tickets is at most twice the number of crashes: proved through a second potential Ψ = Φ + #(crash credits) + #(lost tickets) with ghost variables (psi_bound, skipped_le_two_mul_crashes, lemma2_10). Each skip decreases Ψ by 1, each execution of line 15 increases it by at most 2, and Ψ ≥ Φ ≥ 0.

  4. Starvation freedom (Lemma 2.13) and Lemmas 2.4–2.6, 2.8: the paper's proof structure is kept (finitely many skips; a process avoiding rem/cs is eventually stuck at line 31, so Token is bounded, hence eventually constant; then the "last Move_Token() call" argument). Differences: finiteness of the number of calls uses a per-process call potential (needs only Token constant and nobody in the CS); the last call may precede the last crash, so the argument starts from the last event that is a call or an effective crash; the case A[L+1] = q uses a progress lemma for the owner of slot L+1. Lemmas 2.4–2.6 are not stated separately. See Liveness*.lean.

  5. RMR complexity: the paper defers all RMR proofs to the full version. We give our own proof in the DSM model (item 9) by a potential function.

Modelling choices

  1. Granularity. One shared-memory operation per step; the local computations following it (lines 6–7, 13, 17, 19–21, 27, 32, 37, 39, 43–45, 49, 50) are merged into that step. The recursive calls of Move_Token() (lines 43, 49) are tail calls and are modelled as a jump back to line 34 with the same return address. The invocation of Enter() (from the remainder section) and of Exit() (from the critical section) are separate steps without memory access. Merging line 13 into line 12 means a crash between them is not modelled (it would only send the process to Recover() and then Enter(), which is harmless).

  2. RMR model. Only the DSM model: W[p] and Exit[p] are in p's segment, every access to C, D, Token and A[·] counts as an RMR (an upper bound for every placement), invocations and crashes cost nothing. The CC model (cache invalidation by the CAS operations of line 40) is not formalized. Constants: 25 per Enter() invocation, 76 per crash outside the remainder section (not optimized). numCrashes (also used for the Section 3.1 bound skipped_le_two_mul_crashes) counts only those crashes.

  3. Crashes. Any process may crash at any time; a crash outside the remainder section resets the program counter (all local variables) and starts Recover(); a crash in the remainder section has no effect. Shared memory is never affected.

  4. Processes are an arbitrary finite type P with decidable equality (the paper's {1,…,n}); A[c] holds Slot.empty (0), Slot.claimed q (q > 0) or Slot.skipped (−1); the condition "p' > 0" of line 39 is "A[ℓ+1] is claimed".

  5. Liveness assumptions. "Finitely many crashes" = no crash event after some time. "Fair" = every process that is outside the remainder section at time t takes a step at some time ≥ t (Fair); a step in the critical section is the call of Exit(). Both hypotheses hold for the round-robin schedule (non-vacuity example in Spec.lean). Starvation freedom is stated as: a process in Enter() (resp. Exit(), Recover()) that does not crash any more reaches the CS (resp. the remainder section, leaves Recover()); a call interrupted by a crash counts as finished, as in the paper's definition of wait-freedom.

  6. Wait-freedom bounds are stated with explicit constants and own-step counting: critical section re-entry within 3 own steps (paper: "4 consecutive shared memory steps"), recovery within 3, recovery to Exit() within 2 own steps (paper: 3). The "first and last shared memory operation of Exit()" are lines 9 and 12.

  7. Ghost state (Ghost: ticket owners, c_{p,t}, the "has not incremented D" flag, crash credits, lost tickets, crash counter) is updated alongside the configuration and never read by the algorithm (ghost e t is defined from exec e t).

Not formalized

  • The CC-model RMR bound, the worst-case O(1 + f) RMRs per passage, FCFS for crash-free executions (all proved only in the paper's full version).

  • Section 3.4 (wait-free Exit() variant).

  • Lemma 2.1 items are not all restated one by one; (3), (4), (7), (8), (9), (10), (11), (16) and Lemma 2.3 have separate Lean statements (Safety.lean, Skips.lean), the others are part of Inv.
