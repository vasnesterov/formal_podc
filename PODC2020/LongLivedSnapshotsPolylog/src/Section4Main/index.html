import Papers.PODC2020.LongLivedSnapshotsPolylog.Section4 import Papers.PODC2012.LimitedUseSnapshots.SnapshotMain import Papers.PODC2012.LimitedUseSnapshots.SnapshotCost /-! # Theorem 4.4: the unlimited-use snapshot (Algorithm 2) Algorithm 2 of the paper is Algorithm 3 of Aspnes–Attiya–Censor-Hillel–Ellen [AACE] with every bounded max array replaced by Algorithm 1 and every bounded max register by the unbounded max register of Baig et al. With these objects taken as atomic (which is how the paper uses them, and what Theorem 3.7 and [12] provide), Algorithm 2 is `Snapshot.alg3 T v₀` of `Papers/PODC2012/LimitedUseSnapshots`, with unbounded components. The proof of Theorem 4.4 in the paper only says that the proof of Section 4.1 of [AACE] "holds also" here. That proof uses wrong linearization points (see `Snapshot.Counterexample.paper_linearization_points_fail`); the statement is nevertheless true, with the repaired linearization points of `Snapshot.theorem_4_2`, which needs no bound on the number of `Update`s (the arrays are unbounded families). So Algorithm 2 is an **unlimited-use** linearizable snapshot (`theorem_4_4_linearizable`). Wait-freedom and the per-operation costs: an `Update` performs `depth · (MaxUpdate + MaxScan + 3) + WriteMax + 1` weighted accesses and a `Scan` `ReadMax + 1` (`theorem_4_4_update_cost`, `theorem_4_4_scan_cost`, from Lemma 4.3 of [AACE]). Lemma 4.3 of the paper sums these per-object costs with the *amortized* costs `O(log² n)` of Algorithm 1 (Lemma 3.6) and `O(log n)` of the max register of [12]; since the total cost of the operations on each object is at most its amortized cost times the number of its operations, the total is `O((|S| + |U|) log³ n)`. The amortized costs of the black boxes are not modelled. -/ namespace Podc.LongLivedSnapshotsPolylog.Section4 open Cslib.SharedMemory Podc.LimitedUseSnapshots open Podc.LimitedUseSnapshots.Snapshot (BTree alg3 swSnap tauCost opCost Costs balanced) variable {P V : Type} [DecidableEq P] {T : BTree P} {v₀ : P → V} /-- **Theorem 4.4 (linearizability)** of [BHMT20]: Algorithm 2 (over atomic unbounded max arrays and an atomic unbounded max register) is a linearizable implementation of a single-writer snapshot, for every tree whose leaves are the processes;
