Long-Lived Snapshots with Polylogarithmic Amortized Step Complexity — PODC 2020

Mirza Ahad Baig, Danny Hendler, Alessia Milani, Corentin Travers. Long-Lived Snapshots with Polylogarithmic Amortized Step Complexity. PODC 2020. DOI 10.1145/3382734.3406005. Formalized version: the full version, HAL preprint hal-02860087v1 (June 2020, 19 pp., all proofs); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Lemma 3.3 (the linearization given by rules 1–3 respects the max-array semantics) and Lemma 3.4 (counting the MaxUpdates that start during a scan) are false, so the linearizability proof of the new max array collapses. Algorithm 1 (the unbounded 2-component max array) is still linearizable, by a new proof with certified views; in bounded-increment executions it is wait-free, given a helping period ≥ 1, which the pseudo-code's ⌊n/log² m⌋ is not for realistic n; and Algorithm 2, the unlimited-use snapshot, is linearizable and wait-free, with bounded increments (Lemmas 4.1, 4.2). The polylogarithmic amortized bounds, the paper's main claim, were checked only in their counting cores, which hold; the black-box costs and the final summation were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🛠️ Lemma 3.3 (linearization rules 1–3, with Definition 3.2 and Claim 4)

The order of rules 1–3 can put a MaxUpdate(right, 3) before a MaxScan that returns right component 0, in a 4-bounded-increment execution with n = 4 processes and m = 16 = n² (Counterexample.lemma_3_3_false).

Impact. The linearizability proof of Theorem 3.7 rests on Lemma 3.3 and Claim 4 and is broken: Lemma 3.3 is false, and so are Claim 4 and the "value of O" of Definition 3.2. Theorem 3.7's linearizability still holds; we prove it differently (alg1_linearizable). It holds in every execution with atomic MR registers, with no bounded-increment hypothesis and for every m. The history in the counterexample is itself linearizable.

Details

Process A writes MR_left = 4, 8, 12. Then:

  1. Q scans and reads r₁ = 0, l = 12.

  2. A writes MR_left = 16.

  3. S scans and reads 0, 16, 0, so its index is h = 1.

  4. B writes MR_right = 3.

  5. Q reads r₂ = 3. Its index is h = 0, and it writes MA_0 = (12, 3). Rule 3 linearizes B's update at Q's write of MA_0.right.

  6. S writes MA_1 = (0, 0) and scans it at a later step. Line 18 passes, and S returns (16, 0).

Rule 1 linearizes S at that scan, after B's update. Definition 3.2 makes the "active" array jump from MA_0 to MA_1. The right component of O's value then drops from 3 to 0, which Claim 4 excludes.

Repair

A certified view (Cert) records the time t₀ of its line-11 read and the time t₁ of its line-17 scan of MA_h. On [t₀, t₁] the high-order bits are constant, and MR(t₀) ≤ view ≤ MR(t₁). Its components are actual values of the max registers before t₁. Views are monotone in t₁ (cert_le). Every completed operation has a certificate inside its interval: a direct view, or by Claim 1 a helper's view. The max-array criterion of the PODC 2012 formalization then gives a linearization (exists_isLinearization_maxArr). The invariant is Inv in LinInv.lean.

🔧 Lemma 3.4 (MaxUpdates that start during a CompScan)

After K = 3 unsuccessful iterations of a CompScan, only 3 MaxUpdates have been invoked during it, not ⌈(K−2)m/b⌉ = 4, with n = 4, m = 16, b = 4 (Counterexample34.lemma_3_4_false).

Impact. Lemma 3.5 (wait-freedom) still holds. Its proof only needs the number of WriteMax steps during the iterations (WaitFree.lemma_3_4, WaitFree.iterations_le).

Details

The proof counts the WriteMax operations that take effect during S. It then assumes that each of them belongs to a MaxUpdate that started during S. But each of the other processes may have invoked its MaxUpdate before S and write during S. In the counterexample:

  • A, B, C have pending MaxUpdate(left, 16), MaxUpdate(right, 16), MaxUpdate(left, 20) when S starts;

  • MR_left = MR_right = 15 at that point;

  • the three pending writes plus three new MaxUpdates of C (24, 28, 32) make three iterations of S fail.

Repair

Count WriteMax steps inside I₂. With 2n+1 of them, some process writes three times there. Both CompScans between its writes then complete inside I₂, which gives Observation 5's "+2".

✏️ Slips

  • Algorithm 1, lines 23 and 25 and the text of Section 3 (helping period ⌊n/log² m⌋): with m = n², the period is 0 for every n < 256, so line 25 computes c mod 0. A floor is printed for a ceiling: the proof of Lemma 3.5 uses ⌈n/log² m⌉. We model the intended version: a period X ≥ 1, with further collects at c = 2X, 3X, … (afterFail, WaitFree.waitFreeOn_BIExec). With period 0 read as "never collect again", there is no helping.

  • Algorithm 1, line 25 also fires at c = 0, before h₀ has been collected. The text says line 25 is "not the first collect", which is what we model.

  • Lemma 4.1: the induction takes x of height h+1 but uses the bound 2^{h−1}; this is an index slip (Section4.lemma_4_1).

  • Lemma 4.1, Claim 5: (L_u, R_u) is undefined when no MaxUpdate of I touches u.MA; take (0, 0).

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 3.5 · Section 3 · Theorem 4.4

🔧 Lemma 3.5 (wait-freedom of Algorithm 1)

The interval I₁ in the proof is one iteration short, because the first collect happens in iteration X + 1; the proof also uses the false Lemma 3.4.

Impact. The statement stands for a period X ≥ 1, with the paper's bound K + 2X iterations (WaitFree.iterations_le, WaitFree.steps_le, WaitFree.waitFreeOn_BIExec). Here ⌈(K−2)m/ℓ⌉ ≥ 2n+1, i.e. 2nℓ < (K−2)m.

Repair

The off-by-one in I₁ is absorbed because I₃ needs only X − 1 iterations.

🔧 Section 3 (the bounded max arrays MA_j of [4])

Algorithm 1 uses the (m × m)-bounded max arrays of Aspnes, Attiya, Censor-Hillel and Ellen [4] (the 2015 journal version) as linearizable black boxes, but as published that max array is not linearizable (its Theorem 3.4 is false, see LimitedUseSnapshots).

Impact. The statement stands with the authors' corrected max array (its erratum), which is linearizable. That correction makes MaxUpdate₁ cost O(log² m), which eq. (4) already charges per iteration.

🔧 Theorem 4.4 (Algorithm 2 is a linearizable snapshot)

The proof only says that the proof of Section 4.1 of [4] "holds also", but that proof's linearization points are wrong, as found in the PODC 2012 formalization.

Impact. The statement stands with the corrected linearization points of Snapshot.theorem_4_2 (Section4.theorem_4_4_linearizable). No bound on the number of Updates is needed (unlimited use).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, CSLib Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl. Processes are deterministic, n processes are Fin n, and the scheduler is arbitrary. A crash is a process that stops taking steps. There is no randomization. Linearizability is CSLib's amended Herlihy–Wing definition.

  • Algorithm 1 is alg1 n m X. Its base objects are atomic, as the paper uses them as linearizable black boxes:

    • MR_left, MR_right: unbounded max registers;

    • MA_j, j ∈ ℕ: 2-component max arrays;

    • H[i]: registers.

    X is the helping period. Every program counter does one access. Collect reads H[0..n−1] one by one. Line 27 picks the least eligible j.

  • Definition 2.1 (BoundedIncr) is stated on the sequence of written values, since writes are atomic steps. We show it is equivalent to "each write exceeds the previous maximum by at most ℓ" (boundedIncr_iff_stepBounded). BIExec imposes it on MR_left and MR_right.

  • Algorithm 2 is Snapshot.alg3 T v₀ of the PODC 2012 formalization, for any tree T whose leaves are the processes. Its max arrays and root max register are atomic and unbounded.

  • CSLib has no composition theorem. "Algorithm 2 over Algorithm 1 over [12] and [4]" therefore follows only informally, by locality of linearizability. Lemma 4.1 then provides the bounded-increment hypothesis that Algorithm 1 and [12] need.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

The concrete executions are checked by decide +kernel. lemma_3_3_false holds for every X and is checked by a small tactic, kernel_decide_fvar: it calls of_decide_eq_true rfl and leaves the evaluation to the kernel.
