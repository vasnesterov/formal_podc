Given a predicate `f : A.Op → Bool` ("linearize at the invocation"), `canon f α` lists the instances of `α` in the order of their linearization points: an instance whose operation `o` has `f o = true` at its invocation (pending ones included), an instance with `f o = false` at its response (pending ones omitted). `H₁ = canon (! isUpdate)`, `H₂ = canon isUpdate`. Main results: * `canon_append`, `canon_concat`: `canon` is computed left to right. * `canon_skel`: `canon f α` only depends on the skeleton `α?` (not on the response values). * `isSkelLin_canon`: for a well-formed history, `canon f α` is a skeleton linearization. * `tau_canon_of_inv`, `tau_canon_of_ret`: the response `τ_A` assigns to an instance, in terms of the prefix of the history before its
