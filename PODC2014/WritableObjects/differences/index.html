Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Making Objects Writable — PODC 2014.

Paper: Aghazadeh, Golab, Woelfel, Making Objects Writable, PODC 2014. The PODC version is not openly available; we formalize Chapter 4 of Aghazadeh's PhD thesis (Efficient Shared Memory Algorithms for Bounding Space, U. Calgary 2018), which is the paper with full proofs. All numbering is the thesis numbering (Theorem 4.1, Lemmas 4.2, 4.5, 4.8, Claims 4.6–4.25, Figures 4.2–4.15).

Statement-level issues

None of the formalized statements is false.

  • Lemma 4.2, k ≥ 1 (degenerate): the (k,b)-array implementation computes Ver + 1 mod k; for k = 0 there is nothing to implement. We assume [NeZero k]. Not an issue.

Proof gaps

  • Lemma 4.5, proof of Claim 4.7 / construction of S_ℓ (🛠️: a step inside a proof of a true lemma). The proof takes the sub-history Λ_ℓ of the base-object operations of the calls with timestamp ℓ and says: "Subsequence Λ_ℓ is a history, and by (4.1), no A[i_ℓ].SWrite() overlaps any other operation call on A[i_ℓ]. Therefore, there is a linearization S_ℓ of Λ_ℓ." Sequential writability is a property of the histories of the object A[i_ℓ]; Λ_ℓ is only a sub-history, and a sub-history of a linearizable history need not be linearizable. The inference is false without the template's requirement that the write-index is not in use when the Write is invoked, a requirement the proof never uses. Counterexample.lean: a transcript following the template except for that requirement, satisfying (C) and ownership, with each base object's history linearizable, whose high-level history is not linearizable (notInUse_necessary); its Λ_1 is not linearizable (lambda1_not_linearizable). Repair (Lemma45.lean): take one linearization L_i of the whole history of each A[i] and show that the timestamps are sorted along L_i (IsCtrl.ret_before_of_keyPos_lt, which is where "not in use" is needed); then each block of timestamp ℓ is a contiguous segment of L_{i_ℓ} starting with its SWrite, and the legality of the block follows from that of L_{i_ℓ}.

  • Lemma 4.5, (4.1) — "no A[i].SWrite() overlaps any other operation on A[i]" — is stated from (C2) and ownership; we prove it (IsCtrl.writesIsolated). For SWrites of the owner the paper's argument ("processes are sequential") is fine.

  • Lemma 4.2, proof of Part (b1): typos — "[t_W@rsp, t_R@inv] = [t_W@64, t_R@60]" and "q reads (x_W, v_W) from A[i*] in line 60" should be line 59; "when p reads this variable in line 60 of R" should be q. The proof also does not say how pending operations are linearized; the formalization handles them (linearization points at lines 59/64/58).

  • Theorem 4.1 / Figure 4.4 "not in use" (🔧): the template requires that the write-index is not in use when chosen; the proof of Theorem 4.1 only proves (C) (Lemma 4.8). It holds (from Claims 4.22/4.23) and is part of our invariant (WF.Inv.notInUse, WF.lemma_4_8 gives Template ∧ CondC).

  • Lemma 4.8, proof of (4.32) (🔧): "p invokes more than n Write() calls during [t_E@36, t_E@39], and so q completes at least n Write() calls during this interval" — the counted Writes may include one invoked before t_E@35, outside the hypothesis of Claim 4.20; only its line 14 must come after t_E@35, which holds. Typo: "q completes" should be "p completes".

  • Claims 4.15(b), 4.16 (✏️, inside the proof): false at the level of single points — within one recycle(), line 26 may add i* to free_p at the same point at which lines 27–28 read i* from Annc and enqueue it (mechanism: example at the end of Lemma48.lean; we did not build a full execution). The uses of these claims keep one recycle() call of margin, so nothing breaks; our invariant uses "an index is held in the queues until the next read of its slot" instead.

  • Claim 4.19: "if p writes (j, s) to one of H_p[q], H'_p[q] during a Write, it writes (j, s') to the other one during the same Write" is asserted for pairs of last writes without argument; true (pairIn/pairOut in the invariant).

  • Not proved: Claim 4.14 (free_p never empty) — in the model the step free_p.remove() is disabled when free_p = ∅, which does not affect safety (linearizability); wait-freedom and the step/space bounds of Theorem 4.1 are not formalized beyond the shape of the step relation.

Modelling choices

  • Transcripts instead of composed implementations. CSLib has no composition theorem, and the base objects A[i] of the transformation are not atomic. Lemma 4.5 is therefore stated on transcripts (Ev): lists of the high-level invocations/responses, the invocations/responses of operations on the A[i], and the writes to Ptr; other steps are irrelevant to the lemma and omitted. The high-level history highH and the base-object histories lowH i are CSLib histories, so the CSLib notions (IsLinearization, Precedes) apply unchanged.

  • Sequential writability is a hypothesis on the base-object histories occurring in the transcript (SeqWritable W (lowH W i α): linearizable whenever no write overlaps another operation), which is exactly what "the objects A[i] are sequentially writable" provides.

  • Writable type (Writable): an ObjSpec with operations wr v that set the state to v; the response wresp v of Write(v) may depend on v (the paper: "returns nothing").

  • The template (Template): a per-process control automaton (PStep) records exactly what Figure 4.4 fixes: Write(v) = A[f].SWrite(v) on an owned index f, then Ptr.Write(f), return; Exec(o) (for a non-write operation o) = one A[i].Oper(o), return its response. How the indices are chosen is unconstrained. "Not in use": we require Ptr ≠ f when A[f].SWrite is invoked (NotInUse); the paper requires it at the invocation of the Write call, which implies ours (notInUse_of_atWriteInv), so our Lemma 4.5 is slightly more general.

  • Condition (C) (CondC): a point is a position between events; t* lies after the invocation event of E and no later than the invocation of Op; "no SWrite overlaps [t*, Op's response]" = each SWrite on A[i] returned before t* or was invoked after Op returned (a pending SWrite/Op never returns).

  • Initial Write(v₀). The paper starts transcripts with a Write(v₀) of process 0 in isolation and Ptr = ⊥. Our Lemma 4.5 needs no such prefix: Ptr is initially ⊥ (none), and an Exec can only satisfy (C) after some Write has put an object into use.

  • The paper's t(M) is "the latest point satisfying (C1) and (C2)"; any such point works (tsOf chooses one).

  • The algorithm of Figure 4.5 (Algorithm.lean, namespace WF): n ≥ 1 processes; indices Fin n × Fin (8n+9) with owner Prod.fst (the paper's i mod n = p up to relabelling); ctr_p kept as an unbounded counter used mod n; one shared-register access per step with the local lines attached as on p. 58–59 (lines 25–28 form one step, etc.); free_p.remove() nondeterministic; the SW objects are black boxes (invocation and response separate steps, arbitrary responses — their correctness is the SeqWritable hypothesis of Theorem 4.1); line 38 is disabled on Ptr = ⊥ (never happens after the initial write, ptr_ne_none); ghost fields (transcript, write numbers, epochs, positions) never influence guards or written values. Executions (IsExecution) start with the solo complete Write(v₀) of process 0, as in the paper, and the linearized high-level history includes it.

  • Theorem 4.1 / Corollary 4.4 are formalized for linearizability only (WF.theorem_4_1, WF.corollary_4_4). For Corollary 4.4 the objects A[i] are required to have histories of executions of the Figure 4.2 implementation (kWordImpl), up to internal steps (dropTau); CSLib has no composition theorem, so the flattened implementation is not built as one Impl.

  • Lemma 4.2: the (k,b)-array is kbArray k V v₀ with entries in an arbitrary type V (the b-bit values) and initial/reset value ⊥ = v₀; Read returns some S[i], Write/Reset return none. Base objects: CSLib registers Ver : Fin k and A[i] : V × Option (Fin k) (the version ⊥ = none). "Executed in isolation" = ResetsIsolated. Register sizes (b + O(log k) bits) are by the types; step bounds: at most 3 base-object accesses per operation.

  • Figure 4.2 (k-word register): "It is obvious that any history … is linearizable, provided that each SWrite() operation call is not executed concurrently with any other operation" — proved (kWord_seqWritable).
