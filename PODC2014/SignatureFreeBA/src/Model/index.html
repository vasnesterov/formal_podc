import Podc.Computability.Distributed.Byzantine.AsyncBroadcast /-! # The algorithm of Mostéfaoui, Moumen, Raynal (PODC 2014) as an asynchronous broadcast protocol A. Mostéfaoui, H. Moumen, M. Raynal, *Signature-Free Asynchronous Byzantine Consensus with `t < n/3` and `O(n²)` Messages*, PODC 2014 (full version: IRISA PI-2016, hal-00944019). We model Figure 1 (BV-broadcast) and Figure 2 (binary consensus with a common coin) in the interleaving model `Cslib.Byzantine.AsyncBroadcast` (asynchronous reliable authenticated channels, at most `t` Byzantine processes, chosen adaptively). * Messages: `bval r v tag` is `B_VAL[r](v)` and `aux r v` is `AUX[r](v)`. Receivers ignore `tag`. It is `none` for an echo (Figure 1, line 03) and `some d` for the broadcast of the own estimate (Figure 1, line 01, called by Figure 2, line 02). The bit `d` records whether the set `values` of the previous round was a singleton. It makes the local variables of the process (`est`, the current round, whether line 08 applies) readable from its broadcasts. * `binVal t ib r v` is `v ∈ bin_values[r]` (Figure 1, line 05): `B_VAL[r](v)` received from `2t + 1` processes. * `AuxQuorum t ib r V` is the predicate of Figure 2, line 05 with `values = V`. * `nextEst V s` is lines 06–11: the new estimate and whether `values` was a singleton. * The common coin is a fixed sequence `coin : ℕ → Bool` (`coin r` = the `r`-th bit `random()` returns, the same at every process). * Decision (line 08) is the `decide` guard: the process has started round `r + 1` with estimate `v` after a singleton `values = {v}` in round `r`, and `coin r = v`. The guards are liberal (a "wait until" may be passed later, with any set of messages that satisfies it).
