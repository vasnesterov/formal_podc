Read/Update Tradeoffs for Counters, Snapshots and Max Registers — PODC 2014

Danny Hendler, Vitaly Khait. Complexity Tradeoffs for Read and Update Operations. PODC 2014. DOI 10.1145/2611462.2611472. Formalized version: the authors' copy with complete proofs (no arXiv version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. All the abstract's results hold: the counter/snapshot tradeoff (Theorems 1–2, Corollary 1), the max-register tradeoff (Theorems 3–4) and the tree max register (Theorems 5–6). But the information-flow machinery they rest on is unsound as defined (Lemma 2 and Claim 1 are false with the paper's Definitions 1–5), so it had to be redesigned, and the proofs of Lemmas 4 and 6 and the linearization points of Theorem 5 needed new arguments. Only Theorem 4 under its printed hypothesis for M > N stays unproved.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Theorem 4 for M > N ("immediate from Theorem 3")

The paper calls Theorem 4 "immediate from Theorem 3", but with K = min(N, M) and f = log M, Theorem 3 gives only Ω(log(log N / log log M)), which is not Ω(log log N) for huge M.

Impact. We prove the theorem for M ≤ N (theorem4_fin), and for all N, M when ReadMax takes O(log min(N, M)) steps: in particular for read-optimal implementations (the abstract's claim), the generic theorem4 gives Ω(log log min(N, M)). Only ReadMax costs between c log N and c log M are affected.

Details

The bound is Ω(log log N) only while log log M is small compared with log N; for M = 2^N it gives no bound. So the case of huge M is unestablished, and we neither prove nor refute it.

🛠️ Lemma 2 and Claim 1 (erasing processes nobody is aware of)

With Definitions 1–5 a writer can be hidden although a reader saw its value: if q writes 5 twice in a row and then r reads 5, no event is aware of q, yet removing q's events leaves no execution (PaperDefs.lemma2_false, PaperDefs.claim1_false).

Impact. These are the paper's tools for erasing processes; all lower-bound proofs use them. The theorems survive: a sound awareness notion gives the same bounds, with the paper's constant 3 in Lemma 1.

Details

Definition 1 makes an event invisible in two cases:

  • it does not change the value;

  • it is overwritten by a write before any access to the object, and its process takes no step in between.

Here the first write of q is overwritten by q's own second write, so it is invisible. The second write leaves the value at 5, so it is invisible too. Then no event of r is aware of any event of q (Definition 2), even with a more generous transitive closure than the paper's. So q is hidden (Definition 5), and the E' of Lemma 2 and the E − {q} of Claim 1 both equal E without q's events. Then the reader reads 0, not 5.

The implementation is a single-writer max register (toyImpl), wait-free and linearizable (toyImpl_linearizable), so the counterexample lies in the setting of Claim 1. Nothing depends on the toy's double write: two WriteMax(5) operations with one write each, or a 2-process multi-writer max register built from per-process registers, give the same events. The paper does mean trivial writes to be invisible: the proof of Lemma 1 schedules them first and asserts that none of them is visible.

Repair

We use a data-flow semantics (Cslib.SharedMemory.Awareness):

  • awareness sets grow by the familiarity set of each object read or CAS'ed;

  • every write and every value-changing CAS records the writer's awareness set as the object's new familiarity set;

  • trivial events change nothing.

On top of it:

  • the erasure lemma arun_erase is the sound Lemma 2;

  • arun_filter_hidden / ess_erase is the sound Claim 1;

  • exists_round is Lemma 1, with the paper's three-phase schedule.

🛠️ Lemma 4 and Lemma 6 (proved under an extra invariant)

Both proofs assume that erasing hidden processes can only shrink familiarity sets, but erasing an object's last writer resurrects the previous write, possibly by another essential process.

Impact. Theorem 3 still holds. We prove both lemmas for essential sets satisfying a block invariant that the construction maintains. Whether they hold for arbitrary essential sets (Definition 7) is left open.

Details

Lemma 4 (low contention) picks processes whose objects are not familiar with other chosen processes, then erases the rest. If an erased process wrote last to an object, the object reverts to an older write. That write may come from a chosen process, which then becomes visible.

The high-contention case has a second problem. It classifies processes as read, write or value-changing CAS using o's value after E_i. The steps, however, are taken after the erasure, which may change that value, so a "trivial" CAS can succeed.

Lemma 6 has the same issue. Erasing the completed process that the reader's next object is familiar with can expose another completed process's older write to the reader.

Repair

The block invariant (BlockOK): for every object, the top run of its history written by essential processes is written by a single process. We maintain it as follows:

  • lemma4_low builds the conflict graph from block owners: out-degree ≤ 1, independent third by exists_indep;

  • lemma4_T, lemma4_W and lemma4_C erase the owner of the contended object first and classify afterwards.

lemma4 then gives ⌊√m⌋ ≤ 3(|E'| + 1), which implies the paper's √m/3 − 2 without assuming m ≥ 81.

lemma6 replaces the reader-by-reader erasure with one comparison. It runs the reader with all essential processes erased, notes the ≤ m objects it reads, and keeps exactly the completed processes owning none of them.

🔧 Definition 2 (awareness)

Definition 2 never propagates awareness through a process's local state, and its transitive clause even requires every event in a chain to access the same object, while the proof of Lemma 1 reasons with process-level awareness.

Impact. Lemma 1 holds with awareness that flows along a process's own events and without the same-object requirement (Cslib.SharedMemory.Awareness.exists_round).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 2 removes only the events of q that are aware of p, but the proof of Lemma 3 removes all events of q from the first such event on.

  • Algorithm A, line 5: n ← node.parent should be n ← n.parent.

  • Algorithm A: T_L needs N leaves (one per value v < N), not N − 1.

Typos

  • Lemma 4's proof cites "Claim 4"; only Claim 1 exists.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 5

🛠️ Theorem 5 (linearization points of Algorithm A)

Definition 9 linearizes a WriteMax when the root first "counts" it, but a completed WriteMax may never be counted (AlgA.not_counts: N = 2, WriteMax(3) by p₀, then WriteMax(2) by p₁).

Impact. The statement is true, with a new proof (AlgA.linearizable).

Details

A WriteMax is never counted when all its CASes succeed trivially, when its leaf write is trivial, or when it returns early at line 16. The proof of Invariant 1 treats a trivial CAS as a failed one.

Repair

We linearize WriteMax(v) at the first moment in its interval at which the root is ≥ v. Lemma 8 (lemma8) and the double-refresh guarantee (root_ge_of_wDone) are proved as state invariants.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • System. CSLib's asynchronous shared memory (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl). Processes are deterministic and each step is one atomic access to a read/write/CAS register (RWC ι V v₀), with arbitrary value and index types. There are no crashes; the adversary is the scheduler, which chooses every interleaving. There is no randomness.

  • Implementations. Implementations are linearizable (CSLib Impl.Linearizable).

  • Read bound. The lower bounds assume only that the reader's operation completes within R solo steps from every reachable configuration (SoloBound). This is weaker than obstruction-freedom plus a worst-case bound.

  • Updates. Updaters need no progress guarantee. Our conclusion is that some update operation has already taken the stated number of steps.

  • Objects. Counters are counterObj; single-writer snapshots are CSLib snapObj n. The max register is CSLib maxRegObj, with 0 playing the role of −∞; it is M-bounded through allowed.

  • Generic theorem. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 are instances of update_lower_bound, which applies to any object whose read reveals all updaters. Corollary 1 is proved directly, not through the paper's reduction.

Proof remarks. The o(·)/Ω(·) steps of Theorems 1 and 3 are made exact. Theorem 1 becomes "R · 3^k + 1 < N ⇒ some update takes k + 1 steps". Theorem 3 becomes the recursion |E_{i+1}| ≥ ⌊√(|E_i| − m)⌋/3 − 1 with the explicit threshold max(2m, 10^6); the paper's Eq. (4) iterates a hidden Ω constant i times. Lemma 5 is folded into lemma6.

Not formalized.

  • Theorem 4 for M > N with ReadMax above O(log min(N, M)): see the mistake above.

  • Lemmas 4 and 6 without the block invariant.

Trust. There is no sorry. All listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Non-vacuity:

  • the counterexample implementation toyImpl is a genuine wait-free linearizable max register;

  • Algorithm A satisfies the hypotheses of the max-register theorems (it is linearizable and its ReadMax takes one step).
