The paper's proof erases, before each step of the reader, the (unique) completed hidden process the accessed object is familiar with. Erasing the last writer of an object can however *resurrect* an older write by another completed hidden process, which the reader then sees; the paper does not account for this. We prove the lemma for executions satisfying the block invariant (`Ess`, field `blocks`), by a single comparison: run the reader in the execution with *all* essential processes erased, and keep exactly the completed processes owning none of the (at most `m`) objects that the reader accesses there. -/ namespace Podc.ReadUpdateTradeoffs open Cslib.SharedMemory Cslib.SharedMemory.Awareness
