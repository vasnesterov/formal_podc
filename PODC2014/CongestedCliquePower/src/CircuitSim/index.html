Programs are `Prog V Bool σ ρ` (`Phases.lean`) and their round count at bandwidth `B` is `Prog.roundsAt B` (`Bandwidth.lean`). The input `x : X → Bool` is partitioned by `part : X → V`: initially player `v` knows exactly the bits `x i` with `part i = v` (`inputState`). The protocol (proof of Theorem 2), given an assignment `I : C → V` of the gates to the players and a set `H` of heavy gates: * step 0: the owner of every input bit sends it to the owner of its input gate; * stage `r = 0, …, D`: the owner of every non-input gate `G` of rank `r` computes its value: - if `G` is heavy, every player `u` sends to `I G` the `b` bits `g_u(values of the in-neighbours of G owned by u)` (separability for the partition of `in(G)` induced by `I`); - if `G` is light, the owner of every in-neighbour `G'` sends the value of `G'` to `I G`. All patterns are fixed in advance, so each step is executed by oblivious routing (`Prog.exists_slots`), in two rounds of bandwidth `b + 4s + 3`.
