On the Power of the Congested Clique Model — PODC 2014

Andrew Drucker, Fabian Kuhn, Rotem Oshman. On the Power of the Congested Clique Model. PODC 2014. DOI 10.1145/2611462.2611493. Formalized version: the 10-page author copy (no arXiv version; the "full version" cited for several proofs is not online).

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The circuit simulation (Theorem 2, after repairing a counting error in its proof), the reduction from set disjointness (Lemma 13) and the lower bounds for cliques (Theorem 15) and cycles (Theorem 19) hold. One of the three lower-bound families is lost: the K_{ℓ,m} gadget of Lemma 21 is wrong for every ℓ ≠ m, and the case ℓ = m was not checked. The degeneracy upper bound (Claim 6, Theorem 7) is proved only for connected H, and the consequences for triangle detection (Sections 2.1 and 3.6) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Lemma 21 and Theorem 22 for ℓ ≠ m (the K_{ℓ,m} lower-bound gadget)

The construction in the proof of Lemma 21 contains unintended copies of K_{ℓ,m} for every ℓ ≠ m, so Lemma 21 and Theorem 22 are unestablished there (lemma_21_counterexample).

Impact. A broken proof, not a refuted statement: Lemma 21 only claims that some lower-bound graph exists, and in the smallest case other gadgets exist. Nothing we proved replaces the step, so the K_{ℓ,m} lower bound stands without a proof for ℓ ≠ m. The lower bounds for cliques and cycles do not use it.

Details

The proof claims that each side of a copy of K_{ℓ,m} contains at least two nodes of V_A ∪ V_B, "as |W^L| = ℓ − 2 and |W^R| = m − 2 (and no mixing between W^L, W^R is possible)". But in a non-induced copy, nodes of W^R can sit on the ℓ-side. Smallest case ℓ = 2, m = 3, so W^L = ∅ and W^R = {w}. Take F = the path i – k – i' with i, i' ∈ L and k ∈ R (bipartite, C₄-free, 2 edges, at least ex(3, C₄)/2). Then {w, u_k} × {u_i, u_{i'}, v_k} is a K_{2,3}: w is adjacent to φA(L) ∪ φB(R), u_k to its F_A-neighbours and to v_k. Its four nodes in V_A ∪ V_B are not of the form φA(e) ∪ φB(e), so condition II of Definition 10 fails. The copy uses only Alice's edges and fixed edges, so with Bob's input empty the reduction graph contains K_{2,3}: Observation 11, and with it the reduction, breaks.

The same happens for every m = ℓ ± 1 as soon as F has a vertex of degree 2 on the relevant side, which every F with ≥ ex(N, C₄)/2 = Θ(N^{3/2}) edges does for large N (counting argument, not formalized). For |ℓ − m| ≥ 2 there is even a copy of K_{ℓ,m} avoiding all of F_A and F_B, so every input looks "intersecting". We do not know whether a lower-bound graph exists for ℓ ≠ m in general. For the 2-edge F above, a brute-force search finds valid (K_{2,3}, F)-lower bound graphs on 2N + ℓ + m − 4 = 7 vertices, so that instance of the lemma is true. (lemma_21_counterexample, lemma21_fails_succ, lemma21_fails_succ', lemma21_fails_free)

⚠️ Claim 6 and Theorem 7 for disconnected H

The proof of Claim 6 needs H connected, but the paper states Claim 6 and Theorem 7 for every graph and also applies Theorem 7 to forests.

Impact. Proved only for connected H with at least one edge, which covers cycles, cliques, complete bipartite graphs and trees (claim_6, claim_6_isDegenerate, theorem_7). Disconnected forests are not covered.

Details

The proof uses that ex(n, H) is non-decreasing in n and that ex(2n, H) ≥ 2 ex(n, H) (two disjoint copies of an extremal graph). Both fail for disconnected H, e.g. H = K₂ ∪ K₁ has ex(2, H) = 1 > ex(3, H) = 0 (extremalNumber_edge_plus_point). For forests the doubling step fails too: ex(3, 2K₂) = 3 (a triangle) but ex(6, 2K₂) = 5 (a star).

🔧 Lemma 18 for ℓ = 5, N = 4 (lower-bound graphs for cycles)

Lemma 18 claims an extremal C_ℓ-free F with a (C_ℓ, F)-lower bound graph for every even N; for ℓ = 5, N = 4 none exists (lemma_18_false, no_lowerBoundGraph_C5_K4).

Impact. One condition changes and the construction stays. For N < ℓ every graph on N vertices is C_ℓ-free, and Theorem 19 (asymptotic in n, ℓ fixed) never uses this range. Theorem 19 is proved from a corrected Lemma 18.

Details

For odd ℓ the proof takes F = K_{N/2,N/2} "since ex(N, C_ℓ) = N²/4". That holds only for large N. For ℓ = 5, N = 4 the unique extremal C₅-free graph is K₄, and no (C₅, K₄)-lower bound graph exists on any number of vertices. The witness cycle for an edge {u, v} has a single vertex outside V_A ∪ V_B, so it contains a direct edge A_x B_y with x, y ∈ {u, v}. Two such edges for the disjoint edges {0,1} and {2,3} of K₄ close a 5-cycle through three vertices of V_A, which violates condition II. (not_lemma18Statement)

Repair

With "extremal" replaced by "C_ℓ-free, with all edges between the two halves when ℓ is odd" the construction is correct for all N; we prove this (including condition II for every cycle, which the paper does not argue) and derive Theorem 19 from it. (lemma_18)

🔧 Lemma 18 (proof), the bound for even ℓ

The proof claims "ex(N, C_ℓ) = Ω(N^{3/2})" for every even ℓ; for ℓ ≥ 6 this contradicts Bondy–Simonovits (ex(N, C_{2k}) = O(N^{1+1/k})).

Impact. The claim is never used: deleting it saves everything, and the even case is proved without it (lemma_18_even).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 22 is stated "for any ℓ, m ≥ 1", but for ℓ = 1 (and, symmetrically, m = 1) one round with one-bit messages suffices, not Ω(√n/b) (theorem_22_false_of_l_eq_one, starAlg): K_{1,m} is a star, and G contains it iff some node has degree ≥ m. Lemma 21 assumes ℓ, m ≥ 2, so this is evidently a typo.

  • Theorem 4 says "depth c·R" with c > 1; the simulation gives depth R / c (theorem_4).

  • After Theorem 7 the paper says that for a fixed tree or forest ex(n, H) = O(1). It is O(n), which is what the stated running time O(log(n)/b) needs.

  • Lemma 14's construction connects u_1, …, u_{ℓ−1} to everything; it should be u_{ℓ−4}.

  • Lemma 18's index ranges for the path nodes u_{i,j} give ⌊ℓ/2⌋ (resp. ⌈ℓ/2⌉) internal nodes; it should be ⌊ℓ/2⌋ − 2 (resp. ⌈ℓ/2⌉ − 2), consistent with the stated path lengths and the (ℓ−4)N/2 extra nodes.

Typos

  • Lemma 21's proof says "assume n = 4N + ℓ + m − 4"; it should be 2N.

  • Section 3.6 prints the bound as Ω(n/(e^{O(√n)} b)), while the introduction has e^{O(√log n)}.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 2

🔧 Theorem 2 (circuit simulation)

The proof's count of heavy gates is off by a factor 2: the total weight is 2N, not ≤ n² s.

Impact. Only the thresholds change, and the rest of the proof stands (theorem_2).

Details

The proof calls G heavy if w(G) = |in G| + |out G| ≥ ns (N = n² s wires) and claims that "the number of heavy gates cannot exceed n". It also claims that the greedy assignment of light gates never gets stuck with load 2ns. Both rest on "total weight ≤ n² s", but the total weight is 2N. With n = 2, s = 1, a path of 5 gates has 4 = n² s wires and 3 heavy gates (heavy_count_counterexample).

Repair

Heavy iff w ≥ n(2s + 1) (counting input gates with weight +1) and light load ≤ 2n(2s + 1). With these thresholds the simulation runs with bandwidth b + 4s + 3 in 2D + 4 rounds. We use oblivious routing (König splitting) instead of Lenzen's algorithm; all communication patterns are fixed in advance. (theorem_2)

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CLIQUE-UCAST (Section 2): deterministic synchronous rounds on n players with known identities, no failures, free local computation. A program is a sequence of communication steps (Prog, staged in Podc/). A step whose longest per-pair message has w bits costs ⌈w/B⌉ rounds at bandwidth B (Prog.roundsAt). Each player starts with its own input bits; the protocol may depend on the circuit and the input partition.

  • CLIQUE-BCAST (Section 3): synchronous rounds; every node broadcasts b bits per round (Bcast.Algorithm, staged). The input of a node is its incident edges. Randomized algorithms get private coins, uniform over a finite set.

  • CONGEST-UCAST: CSLib's deterministic CONGEST (Cslib.Congest.Algorithm) on the input graph.

  • Detection convention: all nodes accept iff the input graph is H-free. Copies of H are Mathlib Copys, i.e. not necessarily induced subgraphs, as in the paper.

  • Randomized lower bounds take the randomized communication complexity of set disjointness as a hypothesis (RandDisjLB). Deterministic bounds are unconditional (CSLib's CC(DISJ_K) ≥ K).
