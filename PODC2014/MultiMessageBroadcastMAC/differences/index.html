Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Multi-Message Broadcast with Abstract MAC Layers and Unreliable Links — PODC 2014.

Paper: Ghaffari, Kantor, Lynch, Newport, Multi-Message Broadcast with Abstract MAC Layers and Unreliable Links, PODC 2014, arXiv:1405.1671v1 (numbering of the arXiv version).

Model

  1. One transition system instead of composed timed I/O automata. BMMB and the MAC layer are modelled together (step). User well-formedness (one broadcast at a time) and BMMB's zero-delay bcast are built in: the message being broadcast is always the head of the queue.

  2. BMMB state. log i is the list of messages i got, in order. It plays the role of rcvd and of the order of bcastq. acks i counts acknowledged broadcasts. Then bcastq = log.drop acks, R_i = log, C_i = log.take acks, and the "sent set" of §3.1 is C_i.

  3. Cause function. It is implicit. A message instance is identified by (sender, message), which is sound because BMMB broadcasts each message at most once per node (Inv.nodup). heard v records the receive events at v.

  4. Receive and acknowledgment correctness are enforced by the guards of step:

    • rcv u v needs G'.Adj u v and that v did not receive this instance before. It happens only before the instance's ack.

    • ack u needs every G-neighbour to have received the instance.

    Disabled events are no-ops, so every event sequence is an execution.

  5. No aborts, no ε_abort, no crashes (standard model; BMMB never aborts). The enhanced model is not modelled.

  6. Time.

    • Event n happens at real time T (n+1), with T 0 = 0, T monotone and T → ∞ (admissibility).

    • Trajectories are not modelled: states only change at events.

    • The final state at time t is configuration last t, the last one with T n ≤ t.

    • F_ack, F_prog ≥ 0 is assumed (the paper says positive).

  7. Acknowledgment bound (AckBound). Every broadcast is acknowledged at most F_ack after the configuration in which it starts (Fresh). This includes the termination property.

  8. Progress bound (ProgBound). It is required only for closed fragments from the final state at a time t ≥ 0 to the final state at a time t' > t + F_prog. The paper requires it for every closed fragment. These are the only fragments used by the proofs (Lemmas 3.9, 3.10), and the weaker hypothesis makes the theorems stronger.

    • connect(α', j): instances of G-neighbours of j that started by the first state and are not acknowledged by the last state.

    • contend(α', j): instances of G'-neighbours not acknowledged by the first state.

    • Condition (c) counts receive events by the end of the fragment, including those before it, exactly as in the paper.

  9. Arrivals. arrive i m can happen at any time. BMMB delivers m and appends it to the queue only if m ∉ rcvd. The paper's pseudocode does not check rcvd on arrive; the two coincide in MMB-well-formed executions, and we do not assume MMB-well-formedness at all.

  10. Nodes and messages are arbitrary types (V need not be finite; ids are irrelevant for BMMB). There is no bound on the message size: each broadcast carries one MMB message, as in BMMB.

  11. The topology assumption G ≤ G' is a hypothesis of Lemmas 3.3, 3.9, 3.10 and the r-restricted results only. Theorems 3.1 and 3.4 hold for any G'.

  12. r-restricted (RRestricted G G' r): every G' edge {u, v} has G.edist u v ≤ r. N^z_G(j) = {j' | G.edist j j' ≤ z}. Distances use Mathlib's edist/dist. "v at distance d from u" is G.edist u v = d, which implies reachability.

Problem statement

  1. MMB specification (SolvesMMB).

    • (a) Every message that arrives at i is delivered at every node of i's connected component of G. §3.2.2 says "every process j"; we follow §2, which does not assume G connected.

    • (b) At most one deliver(m)_j per node and message, and it comes no earlier than some arrive(m)_i event (the same event when i = j).

  2. Time bounds.

    • As in §3.1 and §3.2.3, all arrive events happen at time 0 (ArrivalsAtZero).

    • "k messages": the arriving messages lie in a Finset K with #K ≤ k.

    • k is not known to the algorithm (BMMB does not use it).

Statements

  1. Theorem 3.4 (theorem_3_4) is proved for online arrivals. It assumes only the acknowledgment bound: nothing about G' and no progress bound.

  2. Theorem 3.1 (theorem_3_1, theorem_3_1_diam).

    • The explicit bound is: v has acknowledged (hence received) m by (d_G(u, v) + k) F_ack.

    • The diameter form gives (D + k) F_ack for every v within distance D of u in G.

    • No assumption on G' or F_prog is needed.

  3. Theorem 3.1, proof. The key claim (theorem_3_1_key) is "m ∈ C_v((d+ℓ) F_ack) or |C_v((d+ℓ) F_ack)| ≥ ℓ" for all ℓ ≥ 0. "Contains ℓ other messages" is replaced by |C_v| ≥ ℓ when m ∉ C_v. The paper's base case h = 0 is vacuous for ℓ ≥ 1; we start at ℓ = 0.

    • The induction is on ℓ (outer) and d (inner), equivalent to the paper's induction on h = d + ℓ.

    • The d = 0 case is the queue argument (mem_C_or_le_card).

  4. Lemma 3.5 holds for all real t ≤ t' (no t ≥ 0 needed). Part 3 is stated for G-neighbours i, i'.

  5. Lemma 3.6 is stated as an equality of Finsets plus Nodup of the queue.

  6. Lemma 3.7, part 1: positions are 1-based (queue[k-1]? = some m').

  7. Lemma 3.9 is derived from Lemma 3.10 (footnote 9 of the paper notes this).

  8. Lemma 3.10 needs G ≤ G'. Its hypothesis 1 is applied to j, which is a G'-neighbour of i only because G ⊆ G'.

  9. Lemma 3.11 is stated with an explicit ℓ > 0. The "closest j″" in the proof is not needed: any j″ ≠ j with |R_{j″}| ≥ ℓ works.

  10. Lemma 3.12.

    • t_{d,ℓ} is not defined in the paper. We define tdl r F_prog F_ack d ℓ = d F_prog + (ℓ−1)((r+1) F_prog + r F_ack). It has t_{0,1} = 0 (used in Lemma 3.13) and t_{D,k} ≤ t₁ (Theorem 3.16). The formula (d + (r+1)ℓ − 2) F_prog + r(ℓ−1) F_ack suggested by Theorem 3.16 has t_{0,1} = (r−1) F_prog ≠ 0 for r > 1.

    • Parts 2 and 3 are stated with shifted indices: paper ℓ = ours ℓ + 2; paper d ≥ 1 = ours d + 1 in part 3. Part 2 needs no d ≥ 1; part 3 needs r ≥ 1 and F_ack ≥ 0.

    • Part 5 is false (lemma_3_12_5_false). It fails for every definition of t_{d,ℓ} that satisfies part 4 and t_{D,k} ≤ t₁ (lemma_3_12_5_false_any: r = 1, k = ℓ = 2, F_prog = 1, F_ack = 3), so the failure is not an artefact of our choice of tdl. We prove (ℓ−1) F_ack ≤ t_{0,ℓ} instead (lemma_3_12_5', needs r ≥ 1). This is exactly what Lemma 3.14 uses.

    • Our increment t_{0,ℓ+1} − t_{0,ℓ} = (r+1) F_prog + r F_ack is not tailored: parts 2–4 force an increment ≥ max(r F_prog + r F_ack, (r+1) F_prog + F_ack), and with it t_{D,k} = t₁ − (r−1) F_prog (equal for r = 1).

  11. Lemma 3.13 is proved for our t_{d,ℓ}, with t_{0,1} = 0 as in the paper. Part 2 follows from Lemma 3.7.3 with k = 1.

  12. Lemma 3.14 needs r ≥ 1 (through the repaired part 5).

  13. Lemma 3.15 follows the paper's double induction, with paper ℓ ≥ 2 = ours ℓ + 1 and paper d ≥ 1 = ours d + 1. Typos repaired:

    • Case 1 derives m′ ∈ R_{j*} \ C_{j*} (the paper writes j″).

    • Case 2 cites Lemma 3.5, not 3.15.

  14. Theorem 3.16.

    • theorem_3_16 is the per-component statement: m reaches every j with d_G(i₀, j) ≤ D by t₁.

    • theorem_3_16_connected is the paper's statement R_j(t₁) = K, assuming G connected with all distances ≤ D, and K the exact set of arriving messages. It is false for disconnected G.

  15. Theorem 3.2 (theorem_3_2) gives the explicit bound D F_prog + 3 r k F_ack, for every j within distance D of the source, with no relation between F_prog and F_ack. For F_prog ≤ F_ack it follows from Theorem 3.16. Otherwise it follows from Theorem 3.1. The paper's t₁ alone is not O(D F_prog + r k F_ack) when F_prog ≫ F_ack.

Not formalized

  1. §3.3 lower bounds (Theorem 3.17, Lemmas 3.18–3.20). They need a model of arbitrary randomized algorithms, the message-size restriction and the grey-zone geometry. Informal observation, not checked in Lean: the proof of Lemma 3.20 assumes each broadcast carries a single MMB message.

    • With capacity c ≥ 2, node b_{i+1} learns m₀ from the cross delivery of a_i's broadcast.

    • Its later broadcast "containing m₁" can then carry m₀ to a_{i+2} over a G' edge. The schedule breaks: against "send everything you know" it costs only O(F_ack + D F_prog).

    • Repeated delivery of the same instance at the end of every F_prog sub-interval is also not allowed by receive correctness. This one is harmless: one delivery suffices, because condition (c) counts receive events before the fragment.

  2. §4 (enhanced abstract MAC layer, FMMB, the MIS/gather/overlay subroutines, Theorem 4.1) and the sphere packing Lemma 4.2.
