Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Gracefully Degrading Consensus and k-Set Agreement under Dynamic Link Failures — PODC 2014.

Paper: Biely, Robinson, Schmid, Schwarz, Winkler, Gracefully Degrading Consensus and k-Set Agreement in Directed Dynamic Networks, arXiv 1501.02716v1 (full version of the PODC'14 brief announcement by Schwarz, Winkler, Schmid, Biely, Robinson; Sections 6–7 are the PODC part, Sections 4–5 the SIROCCO'12 part).

Model

  • Full-information deterministic algorithms (Alg): every process broadcasts its state; the paper's sending/transition functions are a special case, so the impossibility results cover all algorithms of the paper. Algorithms may know n, D, H, k. Decisions are irrevocable (dec_stable), as in the paper.

  • Round indices start at 0 (ρ t is the paper's G^{t+1}). Intervals keep their length.

  • The causal-distance bound cd_x(p, q) ≤ k is the CSLib journey Journey ρ x p (x + k) q; we never use cd as a number.

  • k-agreement is stated as "every finite set of decided values has at most k elements" (no ncard of a possibly infinite set).

  • Definition 15 (iii) is ambiguous ("1 ≤ ℓ ≤ k H-network-bounded VSRCs exist simultaneously"): VSRCk is the literal reading (some ℓ stable root components), VSRCkAll/VSRCkInf the strong reading (all root components of the window are among them). Impossibilities are proved for the strong reading, which implies the literal one (VSRCkAll_subset, theorem7_weak).

  • Definition 16 (influence "before or at the beginning of I_j"): a journey from time b_i + 1 to time a_j. Definition 17's formula cd_{s_cur+1}(p, q) ≤ r_suc − s_cur would also allow the first round of I_j; for singleton (deaf) roots the two coincide.

  • Def. 9/10 for infinite windows (d = ∞): HNetBoundedFrom.

Section 4 (consensus)

  • Definition 13 is unsatisfiable as written (not_almostBounded): "a unique q with cd ≤ H from all p ∈ R" contradicts "all other q' have cd ≤ H − 1" (they also have cd ≤ H). Hence VSRC'(H − 1) = ∅ (vsrcPrime_eq_empty), the first claim of Theorem 4 is false (theorem4_prime_false), and Lemma 8's inclusion VSRC(H) ⊆ VSRC'(H − 1) is false (lemma8_false). Definition 13 also uses "for all p ∈ R" where Definition 10 uses "for some p", so Lemma 8 fails under the intended reading as well.

  • Lemma 9, cases (ii)/(iii), make the "distant" process q the single root right after J, so q never hears from R: this contradicts Definition 13 under its intended reading (q must be reached within H rounds from every p ∈ R). The intended VSRC'(H − 1) impossibility is not established. Under the weakest reading of Definition 13 (no condition on q), Lemma 9 (ii) works, but the induction has the gaps of the next item and our re-proof does not cover VSRC'(H − 1); unproved either way (❌ for the first claim of Theorem 4, the literal unsatisfiability alone would be a slip).

  • Theorem 4's induction never checks that the forever-bivalent run is feasible: Lemma 10's intermediate graphs are arbitrary single-root graphs, so item (ii) of Definition 12 (every VSRC H-network-bounded) can fail on the constructed run, and the window J is fixed "anywhere" so a VSRC of the prefix may extend into J and force q to be reached. We re-prove Theorem 4 for VSRC(H − 1) with a product sub-adversary (famT4): J = the first H − 1 rounds with root {a}, afterwards only "fast" graphs with roots of at most two processes (prodAdv_famT4_subset), chain connectivity by single-process changes through stars (chain_rootedAt, chain_famF), and the Lemma 9 strategy iso_famT4 (line a → c₁ → ⋯ → q during J, then q the eternal root). The generic bivalence argument is not_solvesConsensus_of_chain_isolation.

  • Implicit hypotheses of Theorem 4: 2 ≤ H (for H = 1 the window d = 0 is meaningless) and H ≤ n − 1 (needed for the distant process; the paper's H is a causal diameter, ≤ n − 1 by Lemma 4). For large H (H − 1 ≥ 4(n − 1) + 2) the claim would contradict the paper's own Theorem 5 with H' = n − 1.

  • Lemma 10 is used in the form "consecutive graphs differ in the in-neighbourhood of one process" (OneChange), which is what the valency argument needs; our chains stay inside the families above.

  • Not formalized: Theorems 1–3, Lemmas 1–7 (except what is used), Section 5 (Algorithms 1–2, Theorem 5).

Section 6 (k-set agreement impossibilities)

  • Theorem 7: the parenthetical "(and only root component R_k is vertex-stable for at most min{n − k, H} − 1 rounds)" is not part of our statement: in our runs (as in the paper's proof via Theorem 4) the remaining group may end with an eternal single root {q}.

  • Theorem 7: proved (theorem7) for all D ≥ 1, H ≥ 2, 1 ≤ k, k + 2 ≤ n, |V| > k, even with k − 1 singleton roots stable forever. The paper's k = 1 case reduces to Theorem 4 "with item (ii) dropped", i.e. for a larger adversary than claimed; our runs satisfy item (ii) of Definition 15 for every D ≥ 1 (all roots are cliques of at most two processes, dBounded_of_clique).

  • The generic Theorem 6 (Theorem 1 of [7]) is not used as a black box: its literal statement is false and needs repairs (see Papers/PODC2011/KSetAgreement). We prove the reduction directly (restrict_solvesConsensus): the processes of D are isolated forever and decide their own pairwise distinct inputs chosen to avoid two disagreeing values of D̄; validity of A|D̄ follows by setting the inputs of D to an input of D̄ (the missing condition (E) of the repaired Theorem 1).

  • |V| > k is the paper's standing assumption; we use it as an injection Fin (k + 1) → V.

  • Lemma 17 as stated (∃ r' > 0) is trivial (r' = 1: no communication at all; lemma17). The proofs of Theorems 8 and 9 need it for every fixed r' (lemma17_fixed), which we prove by the finite-horizon chain argument exists_lossy_disagree instead of citing [52, Lemma 3] and [50].

  • Theorem 9: the pair Dᵢ must disagree after a lossy period whose length is fixed by the earlier phases; "A|Dᵢ cannot have solved consensus" only gives some bad pattern. Fixed with exists_lossy_disagree, choosing each pair's pattern after its length is known. Our schedule is simpler than the paper's: groups {2g − par k, 2g + 1 − par k} (group 0 a singleton when k is even); group g has a lossy link in rounds [0, T g), is deaf in [T g, T (g+1)) and afterwards hears everybody; processes outside the groups always hear everybody; from the end of the last phase the graph is complete (single root, constant). Every root of every round is a clique (D-bounded for all D ≥ 1), at most ⌈k/2⌉ + 1 roots per round. Inputs: x_i = i for the k + 1 deciding processes (needs |V| > k, Fin (k + 1) ↪ V), the hypothesis 1 < k is not used.

  • Theorem 8 is derived from Theorem 9 (VSRC(⌈k/2⌉+1, ∞) ⊆ VSRC(k, ∞) for k ≥ 2), not by the paper's separate argument (which is the same construction with one pair).

  • Typo in the proof of Theorem 8: "(i < 0 < k)" for 0 < i < k.

Section 7.1 (Theorem 10)

  • Algorithm 3 in full-information form; the state carries the round counter. When several heard processes have decided, line 7 adopts the decision of one of them chosen by a choice function (the paper: "some p_j"). After round n processes keep running with frozen decisions ("terminate"). [Inhabited V] only fills an unreachable branch.

  • The paper's inductive proof leaves implicit why p_i decides x_i (we derive it from injectivity of the decision map and "decision ≥ own input") and why a line-11 decision in round n happens in a deaf round.

  • Non-vacuity of Σ_{n−1}-MAJ (complete_mem_sigmaMaj) is weak: the complete graph has no singleton roots.

Section 7.2–7.4 (Algorithm 4, Theorem 11)

  • Theorem 11 is false (❌): theorem11_false (n = 5, D = H = 1, k = 1; tie of two locks with the same creation round, fallback of line 32 picks a fresh value) and theorem11_false' (n = 8, D = 2, H = 1, k = 1; a virtual lock is the unique most frequent lock, no tie); the same run with H = D = 2 is theorem11_false'_H2 (the paper assumes H ≥ D, p. 23). Lemma 26 fails: Definition 19 does not constrain locks spread during a VSRC's own interval nor virtual locks. Found by simulation only (scripts in the agent work folder, not in Lean): a re-lock run (D = 2, n = 6) where a member of R_cur releases (Corollary 2 does not cover a VSRC of exactly 2D + 1 rounds) and re-locks with a newer lock of another value; and an off-by-one in Lemma 22 (decisions in round a + 3D may miss the 3D + H window; periodic run).

  • Definition 19 literally includes R = R_cur and is then unsatisfiable (majInfLit_false); MAJINF(k) then only bounds |V_{2D+1}| (mem_MAJINFLit_iff). We use MajInf: R ≠ R_cur and R_cur ends before R_suc starts (otherwise a later VSRC can majority-influence an earlier one, contradicting Lemma 18).

  • Definition 17 uses the paper's formula cd_{s_cur+1}(p, q) ≤ r_suc − s_cur (chains may use the first round of I_suc); IS(R₁, R₂) = ∅ unless R₁ ends before R₂ starts. V_d: maximal VSRCs, [a, ∞) allowed.

  • Algorithm 1 interpretation (Alg4.lean I1–I3): the vertex set of A_p|t contains p; C_p|t is the strongly connected component of p in A_p|t if no known edge enters it (read literally, "A_p|t if strongly connected" breaks Lemma 13 and Corollary 2 as soon as outside processes report edges leaving the root; observed in simulation); InStableRoot is ∅ on intervals containing a round < 1.

  • Algorithm 4 interpretation (I4–I9): line 15 adds every newly learned lock to hist_i[i][r] (stored as earliest rounds, exact for line 26); the multiplicity in S is the number of members of R knowing the lock by round r − 2D; mfrq_latest exactly as line 27; fallback = max value over all of S; τ_create = current round; smallest decision adopted if several arrive; V_p omitted (unused).

  • The counterexample runs satisfy the strong reading of Definition 15 (iii) (VSRCkAll), hence the literal one.

  • Not formalized: Theorem 11's positive parts (Lemmas 19–25), Definition 20, Section 3.3.
