import Papers.PODC2014.GracefulKSetAgreement.Model /-! # A generic bivalence argument for consensus under product message adversaries This is the skeleton of the proof of Theorem 4 (Lemmas 9 and 10 and the induction on rounds), in a form that makes the admissibility of the constructed runs explicit. The message adversary is a *product* `ProdAdv Fam`: in round `t + 1` the adversary may choose any graph of `Fam t`, independently of the other rounds. Two properties of the families suffice for consensus to be impossible: * **connectivity** (Lemma 10): any two graphs of `Fam t` are joined by a chain of graphs of `Fam t` in which consecutive graphs differ only in the in-neighbourhood of one process (`OneChange`); * **isolation** (the adversary strategy of Lemma 9): for every round `t` and process `p` there is a process `q ≠ p` and an admissible continuation from round `t` in which no information from `p` ever reaches `q`. `not_solvesConsensus_of_chain_isolation`: under these two properties, with at least two input values, no algorithm solves consensus. The proof follows the paper: valency (`Val`), Lemma 9 (`exists_mem_val_inter`), an initial bivalent configuration (`exists_biv_init`), preservation of bivalence (`exists_biv_succ`), and a forever-bivalent admissible run, contradicting termination. -- CSLib candidate (generic bivalence for product message adversaries). -/ namespace Podc.GracefulKSetAgreement open Cslib.HeardOf
