Gracefully Degrading Consensus and k-Set Agreement under Dynamic Link Failures — PODC 2014

Manfred Schwarz, Kyrill Winkler, Ulrich Schmid, Martin Biely, Peter Robinson. Brief announcement: Gracefully degrading consensus and k-set agreement under dynamic link failures. PODC 2014. DOI 10.1145/2611462.2611506. Formalized version: the full version, Biely, Robinson, Schmid, Schwarz, Winkler, Gracefully Degrading Consensus and k-Set Agreement in Directed Dynamic Networks, arXiv 1501.02716 v1; numbering follows it. Its Sections 6–7 are the PODC'14 announcement, Sections 4–5 the SIROCCO'12 consensus part. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The paper's central algorithm is wrong. Algorithm 4, the "first gracefully degrading consensus algorithm", violates agreement: with k = 1 (consensus) there are runs under VSRC(n, 3D + H) + MAJINF(1) in which two values are decided, so Theorem 11 is false. The impossibility results hold: Theorems 7, 8 and 9, and the consensus lower bound Theorem 4 they rest on. Proving Theorems 4 and 7 needed new arguments. Theorem 10 (set agreement) is correct.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 11 (Algorithm 4, k-agreement)

Algorithm 4 does not solve k-set agreement under VSRC(n, 3D + H) + MAJINF(k). Two runs with k = 1 decide two different values:

  • n = 5, D = H = 1 (theorem11_false);

  • n = 8, D = 2, H = 1 (theorem11_false'); the same run with H = D = 2 (theorem11_false'_H2), as the paper assumes H ≥ D.

Impact. The paper's main positive result, the k-uniform, gracefully degrading k-set agreement algorithm, is refuted, already for consensus. The impossibility results of Section 6 and Theorem 10 are not affected.

Details

The proof breaks in Lemma 26. It claims that, by majority influence, no lock is known to more members of R_suc than the lock L of the majority-influencing R_cur. It also claims that equally frequent locks are older than L. But Definition 19 counts only causal chains that start after a VSRC's stability interval. It constrains neither locks spread during the interval nor virtual locks (initial values).

  • First run (theorem11_false, processes a, b, c, y, s, inputs 1..5).

    • {a} is a root in rounds 1–3. {b} and {c} are roots in rounds 5–7 and 5–8. From round 10 on the graph is the static star from s. Every other root lasts one round.

    • K = {{a}}, {a} ↪m {b}, {a} ↪m {c}, {b} ↪m {s}.

    • a decides 1. b and c lock ({b}, 1, 7) and ({c}, 1, 7).

    • c's lock reaches s through y in round 8, inside c's interval, so IS({c}, {s}) = ∅.

    • In s's GetLock the two locks tie in multiplicity and creation round. The fallback of line 32 takes the largest value of all known locks, and s decides its own input 5.

  • Second run (theorem11_false'): no tie and no fallback.

    • {a} is a root in rounds 1–5, then R_suc = {1, 2, 3} from round 7 on, with {a} ↪m R_suc.

    • The virtual lock of process h is known to all three members of R_suc, and a's lock only to two.

    • R_suc locks and decides h's input 2, while a decided 1.

Both runs are verified against the formal adversary (Scenario1.mem_VSRCkAll, Scenario1.mem_MAJINF, and the same for Scenario2). The decisions are computed by evaluating the algorithm in the kernel. Definition 19 is taken in its charitable reading (next item), and Algorithm 1's InStableRoot in its sound reading (see differencies.md).

Further problems, found by simulation only (not in Lean):

  • A run with D = 2 breaks agreement in another way. A member of R_cur releases and re-locks with a newer lock of another value, which a successor VSRC adopts.

  • Lemma 22 (termination) is off by one. A VSRC decides only in round a + 3D, so its decision can miss the 3D + H window.

✏️ Definition 19 (majority influence)

Read literally, R ranges over all of V_{D+1}, including R_cur itself, where IS(R_cur, R_cur) = ∅ requires |IS(R_cur, R_suc)| > |IS(R_cur, R_suc)|. So ↪m never holds (majInfLit_false), and MAJINF(k) degenerates to "at most k VSRCs in V_{2D+1}" (mem_MAJINFLit_iff).

Impact. R ≠ R_cur is clearly meant. Without the requirement that R_cur precedes R_suc, which the text implies ("by some earlier VSRC"), the antisymmetry claimed in Lemma 18 fails. We use the reading with both (MajInf); Theorem 11 fails under it.

❌ Theorem 4, first claim, Definition 13 and Lemma 8 (VSRC'(H − 1))

The sharper impossibility under VSRC'(H − 1) fails as stated and its proof does not work for the reading the text describes.

Impact. The sharper impossibility under VSRC'(H − 1) is not established. The claim used later, Theorem 4 for VSRC(H − 1), holds (proof-gap item below, theorem4).

Details

  • Literally (a slip on its own): Definition 13 asks for a unique process q with cd_x(p, q) ≤ H while every other process has cd_x(p, q') ≤ H − 1 ≤ H, so it is unsatisfiable with two processes. Hence VSRC'(H − 1) = ∅, every algorithm solves consensus under it, and Theorem 4's first claim and Lemma 8's VSRC'(H − 1) ⩾ VSRC(H) are false (vsrcPrime_eq_empty, theorem4_prime_false, lemma8_false).

  • Reading of the text ("guarantees one process to be reached from the processes in R within H rounds"): all but one process are reached within H − 1 rounds, the last one within H. The proof fails: Lemma 9's cases (ii) and (iii) make the distant process q the single root right after the window J, so q is never reached from R. In fact, if p ∈ R changes state at the start of J, the change reaches every process within H rounds, so no process can serve as Lemma 9's witness. Lemma 8 also fails under this reading: Definition 13 quantifies "for all p ∈ R" where Definition 10 has "for some p".

  • Weakest reading (the condition on q is void: all but at most one process are reached within H − 1 rounds): Lemma 9 (ii) goes through, but the induction of Theorem 4 has the gaps of the item below, and our re-proof does not cover it (its window graphs need not reach anybody within H − 1 rounds). Not proved either way.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 4 needs 2 ≤ H ≤ n − 1. For H = 1 the window d = H − 1 = 0 is meaningless, and the distant process needs H + 1 processes. For large H the claim would contradict the paper's own Theorem 5 with H' = n − 1. The paper's H is a causal diameter, at most n − 1 by Lemma 4.

  • Lemma 17 as stated ("some round r' > 0") is trivial: r' = 1 gives two processes that never communicate (lemma17). The proofs of Theorems 8 and 9 need the version for every fixed r' (lemma17_fixed).

Typos

  • Proof of Theorem 8: "Di (i < 0 < k)" should read 0 < i < k.

Proof gaps in true statements (4)Theorem 4 · Theorem 7 · Theorem 9 · Theorem 10

🛠️ Theorem 4 (no consensus under VSRC(H − 1))

The bivalence induction never checks that the forever-bivalent run is feasible. Lemma 10's intermediate graphs are arbitrary single-root graphs, so item (ii) of Definition 12 (every VSRC H-network-bounded) may fail. A VSRC of the prefix may also run into the window J and force the distant process to be reached.

Impact. Statement true for 2 ≤ H ≤ n − 1; re-proved (theorem4).

Repair

A product sub-adversary famT4 (prodAdv_famT4_subset):

  • the window J is the first H − 1 rounds, and every graph there has root {a};

  • after J the adversary uses only "fast" graphs, with a root of at most two processes that every other process hears directly.

The rest of the argument:

  • Lemma 10 is proved inside these families via stars (chain_rootedAt, chain_famF).

  • Lemma 9's strategy (iso_famT4): during J, a's information goes down a line a → c₁ → ⋯ → q of length H, and then q is the eternal root.

  • The bivalence skeleton is generic (not_solvesConsensus_of_chain_isolation).

🔧 Theorem 7 (case k = 1 and use of Theorem 6)

For k = 1 the paper appeals to Theorem 4 "with item (ii) dropped". That is a larger adversary than VSRC(1, H − 1), so it does not give the claimed impossibility. For k > 1 it uses the generic Theorem 6, whose literal statement is false (see Papers/PODC2011/KSetAgreement).

Impact. Statement true for every D ≥ 1, H ≥ 2, |V| > k, even with k − 1 roots stable forever (theorem7). It also holds for the literal reading of Definition 15 (iii) (theorem7_weak).

Repair

All runs of famT4 have only roots that are cliques of at most two processes, so item (ii) holds for every D ≥ 1 (dBounded_of_clique). The reduction to consensus among D̄ is proved directly (restrict_solvesConsensus):

  • the processes of D decide their own inputs, chosen to avoid the two values on which D̄ disagrees;

  • validity of A|D̄ follows by giving D an input of D̄. This is the condition (E) that the repaired Theorem 6 needs.

🔧 Theorem 9 (finite-horizon lossy link)

The proof uses Lemma 17 ("A|Dᵢ cannot have solved consensus") to get a bad link pattern for each pair. But the pattern must have a prescribed length: the pair's lossy period ends when the earlier phases end.

Impact. Statement true (theorem9); Theorem 8 follows from it (theorem8).

Repair

The chain argument for a fixed horizon L (exists_lossy_disagree): if the two processes agree after every lossy pattern of length L, the decision does not depend on the pattern, and the two one-directional patterns force their two inputs. Each pair's pattern is chosen after the length of its lossy period is known.

🔧 Theorem 10 (Algorithm 3, the process with the largest input)

The inductive proof does not justify why pᵢ decides xᵢ. It also leaves implicit why a process deciding by line 11 in round n was deaf.

Impact. Statement true (theorem10).

Repair

If n values are decided, the decision map is a permutation of the inputs, and each decision is at least the decider's input. Induction on the rank then forces every process to decide its own input in a deaf round. A causal chain from one such round to a later one would make the later process adopt a decision first.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous lock-step rounds, n processes with unique identifiers, no process failures.

  • An omniscient message adversary chooses the directed graph of every round. Message adversaries are arbitrary sets of graph sequences (Model.lean, CSLib Cslib.HeardOf.Graph).

  • Deterministic full-information algorithms (Alg) with irrevocable decisions. These cover all algorithms of the paper, which may know n, D, H, k. No randomness.

  • Inputs are arbitrary and independent of the graphs. k-agreement, validity and termination are required in every run.

  • Root components are CSLib's Digraph.IsSourceComponent. Causal-distance bounds are CSLib journeys (Cslib.DynNet.Journey).

  • Definitions 9, 10, 12, 15 and 16 are in Model.lean. Definition 15 (iii) is ambiguous: VSRCk is the literal reading and VSRCkAll/VSRCkInf the strong one (all roots of the window are stable). Impossibilities are proved for the strong reading, which is the smaller adversary.
