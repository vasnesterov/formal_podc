Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Linear-in-Δ lower bounds in the LOCAL model — PODC 2014.

Paper: Göös, Hirvonen, Suomela, Linear-in-Δ lower bounds in the LOCAL model, PODC 2014; formalized version arXiv 1304.1007v1.

Scope

  1. Randomized algorithms (Section 2, Appendix B) are not formalized. Theorem 1 is proved for deterministic LOCAL algorithms only (theorem1). The derandomization of Appendix B (Lemma 10, finite Ramsey sets I_n ⊆ S_n) is not formalized. This is a scope limitation, not an issue found in the paper.

  2. Explicit constant. The paper states Ω(Δ). We prove t ≥ ⌊Δ/2⌋ − 1 for every Δ (the bound is vacuous for Δ < 4). The constant comes from the paper's own chain: an EC lower bound t ≥ k − 1 for k colours, and EC with k = ⌊Δ/2⌋ colours embeds into degree 2k ≤ Δ.

  3. Lemma 4, algebraic proof (A.1). Not formalized. Only the combinatorial proof (A.2) is formalized, rephrased as a positive cone on reduced words (Cslib.FreeGroupOrder).

  4. The general simulations of Section 5 are formalized only where they are used. The paper states EC ⇝ PO for all graphs, and PO ⇝ OI for all PO-checkable problems. We formalize the composite simulation simEC (EC ⇝ PO ⇝ OI ⇝ ID) and prove it correct on loopy EC-graphs (Setup.simEC_isMaxFM), which is all that Section 5.5 uses. Separate notions of PO- and OI-algorithms, PO-checkability and Corollary 9 for A_OI as a standalone algorithm are not introduced.

Modelling choices

  1. ID model. An ID-graph is a SimpleGraph ℕ with finitely many edges; identifiers are node names, unbounded and arbitrary (strictly local algorithms do not know n). A G v u is the weight node v outputs for its edge to the neighbour with identifier u. Run-time t is CSLib's GAgree G G' v t: the output depends only on the edges incident to nodes at distance < t. This is the paper's τ_t (an edge {u,w} is at distance min + 1, so τ_0 contains no edges). Correctness (IDAlg.Solves Δ) is required on all finite graphs of maximum degree ≤ Δ, as in the paper. IDAlg.HasRunTime t asks for locality on all graphs (also infinite ones or ones of degree > Δ), while the paper only uses graphs of maximum degree Δ. This is no loss of generality: an algorithm that is t-local on finite graphs of degree ≤ Δ can be replaced by A'(G, v) := A(τ_t(G, v), v) (and 0 when τ_t(G, v) is infinite or has degree > Δ), which is t-local on all graphs and agrees with A on the paper's graphs.

  2. EC model. An EC-algorithm is a function of the view, a colour system ⊆ FreeCox k (CSLib Cslib.ColourSystem), with outputs Fin k → ℝ. Lift-invariance (eq. (2)) is built in. Outputs on multigraphs are read off the universal cover, and a loop counts once in the load (Section 3.5). ECAlg.SolvesLoopy requires correctness on finite loopy multigraphs, in this multigraph semantics. The paper requires correctness on simple graphs and transfers it to multigraphs via lifts (discussion before Lemma 2). This does not matter for Theorem 1: the hypothesis is derived from ID-correctness on finite simple graphs (Setup.simEC_isMaxFM).

  3. Loopiness. IsLoopy G means that every node of G carries a half-loop. This is a sufficient condition for the paper's Definition 1, which asks for a loop at every node of the factor graph F_G. The paper's k-loopiness of G_i is tracked as "at most i + 1 non-loop colours per node" (Cfg.Good). For PO-views we use IsLoopyF U: every node has a neighbour with the same re-rooted view.

  4. PO-views. The universal cover of the PO-graph of an EC-graph is cover G a ⊆ F_k: the reduced words that can be followed letter by letter (letters c and c⁻¹ both follow the c-edge). The tree T of Section 5.3 is the Cayley graph of F_k = FreeGroup (Fin k). Its edge colouring and orientation are the letters.

  5. Lemma 2 is immediate in the multigraph semantics, where a loop is an edge with both endpoints at v (Cfg.Good.load_eq_one). The paper's lift argument (Figure 4) is not needed.

Proofs: changes and details supplied

  1. Lemma 5 (Naor–Stockmeyer) — finite instead of infinite Ramsey. Mathlib has no hypergraph Ramsey theorem. We use the finite one (Podc/Foundations/Combinatorics/HypergraphRamsey.lean, Cslib.exists_homogeneous), iterated over the finitely many shapes X ⊆ B_t(F_k) (exists_homogeneous_family, exists_homog). This gives a finite set I that is homogeneous for the saturation indicator on every shape and is as large as needed. The paper itself notes that a finite version suffices (Appendix B).

  2. Lemma 7 — no sparse set J, no extension of labellings. The paper labels only B_t(v) with identifiers from a sparse J ⊆ I, then extends two labellings to B_{2t+1}(v) with values in I. We instead compare two order-preserving I-labellings of the same ball U ∩ B_{2t+2} (Setup.out_eq). The one-node-change chain stays inside I (chain_induction). The EC-algorithm simEC labels a fixed big ball of F_k by I once and for all (labelOf B I). Its run-time t is then immediate (simEC_hasRunTime), and its outputs are identified with those of A on a bigger labelled ball through Lemma 7 (Setup.big_out).

  3. Radius 2t + 2 instead of 2t + 1 in the proof of Lemma 7. The radius 2t + 1 occurs only in the proof, not in the statement. The saturation of a node u ∈ B_{t+1}(v) needs all neighbours of u, which lie in B_{t+2}(v); this is inside B_{2t+1}(v) iff t ≥ 1. Also, the saturation indicator A* of step (i) needs radius ≥ 1 to see its own edges. For t = 0 the hypothesis of Lemma 7 (a 0-round ID-algorithm finding a maximal FM) is unsatisfiable (not_hasRunTime_zero: such an algorithm fails on a 3-node path), so Lemma 7 is vacuously true there. Not a statement-level issue; at most a degenerate case of the proof (✏️).

  4. Walk in the proof of Lemma 7 (Fact 8). The paper walks from v "away from v*". We take, among the nodes w in the shadow of the root as seen from v* (|v*⁻¹ w| = |v*| + |w|) whose parent edge carries a disagreement, one of maximal depth. This is the same argument (Setup.out_eq_of_single).

  5. Fact 3 / propagation walk in Section 4. The paper walks until it reaches a loop. We choose, among the nodes with an "incoming disagreement", one farthest from g (propagation, Inv.step_core). This is the same argument.

  6. Base case of Section 4 (terse in the paper). The paper says the pair (G_0, H_0 = G_0 − e) "satisfies (P1–P3)". (P1) needs a loop of a colour c_0 present at both nodes, so c_0 cannot be the colour of the removed loop e. Figure 5 indicates the intended c_0. The detail supplied: H_0 is loopy, so it is saturated too (needs Δ ≥ 2). Both loads are 1 and y(e) ≠ 0, so the common loops cannot all keep their weights (exists_inv_zero). Only y(e) ≠ 0 is used, not positivity.

  7. Equation (3) needs a separate nested induction (🔧). The paper says the critical case is g* = g, and that τ_i(GG, ĝ) ≅ τ_i(GH, h) follows "by our induction assumption" τ_i(G, g) ≅ τ_i(H, h). GG is a lift of G, but GH is not a lift of H: walks in the views can cross the colour-c edge between the two halves several times (through loops), so τ_i(GH, h) is not τ_i(H, h) and the induction assumption does not apply as cited. The formal proof inducts on the radius r ≤ i + 1, simultaneously for all nodes of the G-part (GG vs GH) and of the H-part (HH vs GH). It uses ball_view_succ_of_step_eq: the radius-(r+1) view is determined by the edges at the root and the radius-r views of the neighbours. The paper's idea is correct, but the justification given is not, so this is a real (if small) repair of the proof of Step 1, not a routine detail.

  8. The "analogous" case of the inductive step (weight of {g,h} differs from that of f) is handled by the symmetry mix_swap: D.mix C c is C.mix D c re-rooted at c.

  9. Typo in the proof of Lemma 6. "By order homogeneity, τ_t(U_G, ≺, v) ≅ τ_t(U_G, ≺, v)": the right-hand side should be τ_t(U_G, ≺, u).

Summary of what was checked

Checked statements: Theorem 1 (deterministic part), Lemma 2 (multigraph form), Step 1, Fact 3/8, Lemma 4, Lemma 5 (finite form), Lemma 6, Lemma 7 (for labellings of a slightly larger ball), Corollary 9 / Section 5.5 (as simEC_isMaxFM). All are true as written. The proof of Step 1 needs a repair at equation (3) (item 16: the cited induction hypothesis does not give the claimed view isomorphism; a nested induction is needed). Items 15 and 17 supply routine details, item 12 concerns only the proof and only the vacuous case t = 0, item 18 is a typo inside a proof. No statement is wrong. Item 16 (🔧) is a gap because the paper's stated justification ("by our induction assumption") does not apply to GH.
