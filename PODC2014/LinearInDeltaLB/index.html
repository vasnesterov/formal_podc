Linear-in-Δ lower bounds in the LOCAL model — PODC 2014

Mika Göös, Juho Hirvonen, Jukka Suomela. Linear-in-Δ lower bounds in the LOCAL model. PODC 2014. DOI 10.1145/2611462.2611467. Formalized version: arXiv 1304.1007 v1; numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Theorem 1 is proved for deterministic LOCAL algorithms: no algorithm finds a maximal fractional matching in o(Δ) rounds, explicitly t ≥ ⌊Δ/2⌋ − 1. The whole chain is formalized (the unfold-and-mix lower bound in the EC model, the homogeneous order on the free group, the Ramsey step, and the identifier-elimination Lemmas 6–7) and every checked statement is true, but one step of the Section 4 proof (equation (3)) is justified by an induction hypothesis that does not apply, and needs a short nested induction. The randomized extension (Appendix B) was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Proof of Lemma 7: the radius 2t + 1 is too small for t = 0. The radius occurs only inside the proof, not in the statement of Lemma 7. For t ≥ 1 the neighbours of the nodes of B_{t+1}(v) lie in B_{2t+1}(v) and the argument works. For t = 0 they do not, and the saturation indicator A* needs radius ≥ 1 to see its own edges. But for t = 0 the hypothesis of the lemma (a 0-round algorithm finding a maximal FM) cannot hold: such an algorithm already fails on a 3-node path (not_hasRunTime_zero). We use radius 2t + 2 throughout.

Typos

  • In the proof of Lemma 6, "τ_t(U_G, ≺, v) ≅ τ_t(U_G, ≺, v)" should end with u.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Equation

🔧 Equation (3) (Section 4.3, unfold-and-mix step)

The paper justifies τ_i(GG, ĝ) ≅ τ_i(GH, h) "by our induction assumption" τ_i(G, g) ≅ τ_i(H, h), which does not apply because GH is not a lift of H.

Impact. Statement true; repaired by a nested induction (Inv.step_core). The paper's idea is right, but the step as written is a gap a careful referee would flag.

Details

The paper reduces (3) to the "critical case" g* = g. GG is a lift of G, but in GH the colour-c edge at h leads into the copy of G − e, and walks can cross that edge several times by going around loops. So τ_i(GH, h) ≅ τ_i(H, h) needs its own argument.

Repair

The formal proof inducts on the radius r ≤ i + 1, simultaneously for all nodes of the G-half (GG vs GH) and of the H-half (HH vs GH). (Inv.step_core)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Deterministic LOCAL with unique identifiers. Graphs are SimpleGraph ℕ with finitely many edges; identifiers are arbitrary naturals. A G v u is the weight v outputs for its edge to u. Run-time t means the output of v depends only on τ_t(G, v), the edges at nodes of distance < t (CSLib Cslib.Local.GAgree). A.Solves Δ asks for a maximal fractional matching (consistent, nonnegative, loads ≤ 1, every edge has a saturated endpoint) on every finite graph of maximum degree ≤ Δ. The algorithm may depend on Δ.

  • EC model (intermediate): algorithms are functions of the view, a colour system of CSLib's Cslib.ColourSystem, so lift-invariance holds by construction. Multigraph outputs are read through the universal cover, and a loop counts once. "Loopy" means every node carries a loop. This is a sufficient form of Definition 1.

  • PO / OI (intermediate): PO-views are subsets of the Cayley tree of the free group F_k, ordered by a left-invariant linear order (Lemma 4). ID-instances are finite pieces of PO-views labelled order-preservingly by identifiers.

  • Lemma 5 is used in a finite-Ramsey form. Lemma 7 compares labellings of one ball of radius 2t + 2 instead of extending labellings from a sparse set J. This changes no statement used in Section 5.5.

Implicit detail. The base case of Section 4 leaves the choice of the loop colour c_0 implicit. (P1) needs a loop colour c_0 present in both G_0 and H_0 = G_0 − e, so c_0 cannot be the colour of e (Figure 5 shows the intended choice). Such a c_0 exists because H_0 is loopy (Δ ≥ 2), hence saturated, and y(e) ≠ 0 (exists_inv_zero).

Not formalized. Randomized algorithms (Appendix B), the algebraic proof of Lemma 4 (A.1), and the general forms of the simulations EC ⇝ PO and PO ⇝ OI for arbitrary PO-checkable problems.
