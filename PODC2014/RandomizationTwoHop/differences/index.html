Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Anonymous Networks: Randomization = 2-Hop Coloring — PODC 2014.

Paper: Y. Emek, C. Pfister, J. Seidel, R. Wattenhofer, Anonymous Networks: Randomization = 2-Hop Coloring, PODC 2014 (authors' copy, 10 pages, with proofs).

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 9 / correctness of A* as written: unestablished (🔧). The proof of Lemma 9 claims that "the first k rounds in η for a single node v are fully determined by L_k(v, I) and the first k − i bits of each node u ∈ H_i(v)". With L_1 = root only (the paper's definition), k rounds depend on the view of tree depth k (L_{k+1}) even without ports. In the paper's port-numbering model the message from u to x may depend on the port number at u, which depends on u's other neighbours, so k rounds depend on L_{k+2} (locality_gap: equal L_2, different states after one round, colour-sorted ports, 2-hop coloured instances). A* compares L_p(v, I^p) with candidates and simulates up to l ≤ p − 1 rounds (all bit strings have length ≤ p − 1 in phase p). For l = p − 1 the output of v* in the simulation is not shown to equal the output of v in η. The paper also does not say which port numbering the simulations use; with any numbering that is a local function of the colours the gap remains. We neither prove nor refute Lemma 9 for the original A*. A full counterexample needs a problem in GRAN with an adversarial Las Vegas algorithm A_R and an analysis of A*'s selection among all candidate graphs; we did not attempt it. Repair (used in the formalization): Update-Graph uses the view L_{p+1}(v, I^p) (tree depth p) in condition C2 and for v* (AStar.Lv). This costs p rounds, which is the paper's own round count for this step ("requires p rounds"). With it, Lemmas 5–9 and Theorem 1 hold. Scope of the repair. Lemma 9 is verified only for A* with Update-Graph's view changed from L_p to L_{p+1}, i.e. for a changed algorithm (the false locality claim inside the proof is not the only issue; the repair had to change the algorithm, not just the argument). The change is a local index fix whose intent is recoverable from the paper (Update-Graph is said to take p rounds, which is what L_{p+1} costs). Lemma 9 for the original A* is unestablished, not refuted. Theorem 1 (existential) is proved. The paper's §1.3 says randomized algorithms need no port numbers, and for a port-oblivious A_R the original depth suffices, but A* is stated for an arbitrary A_R that solves Π in the port model. Note also that the bits b^p encode information from beyond the view radius, so the original A* may well be correct by a different argument; we have neither such a proof nor a counterexample.

Proof gaps and proof differences (statements true)

  1. Lemma 3. The proof establishes "every prime factor is isomorphic to G∞" but not that G∞ is prime (needed for existence and used in Lemma 7). Repaired: distinct nodes of G∞ have distinct views, so factorizing maps out of G∞ are injective (lemma_3).

  2. Lemma 7. Compares views of nodes in two different graphs. Theorem 3 (as stated, from Norris) is about one graph; the printed proof is terse, truncating subtrees of equal depth-p views and using Corollary 1 inside each graph. We take another route, colour refinement on the disjoint union (teq_stable_two): agreement to tree depth m + n − 1 suffices, and p ≥ 2n ≥ n + n'. Our proof then shows the candidate's set of views equals that of I^p (AStar.eq_of_mem_Fam) instead of the truncating-map argument.

  3. Corollary 2 is said to follow from Theorem 3. Theorem 3 gives depth |V|, Corollary 2 needs depth |V∞|. It follows by applying Theorem 3 to G∞ together with Fact 1 (corollary_2).

  4. Corollary 3 is said to follow from Corollary 1; it follows from Fact 1 and Lemma 2 (AStar.phase_spec + lab_eq_of_trunc_eq; we use it in the form "the view of v* equals the node's view").

  5. Lemma 1 depends on how ties and orders are resolved. We make every choice a function of the canonical data (V∞, the selected view set), so Lemma 1 holds by construction.

Modelling choices and changed definitions

  1. Port numbering model. CSLib's PNGraph, ports 0 … deg − 1, two-sided (receiver learns the sender's port). Solvability is required for every simple port numbering of every instance.

  2. Randomness. One bit per node and round, delivered with the round's messages (RAlg.recv). An algorithm that needs the bit before sending can be simulated with one round of delay, so the class GRAN does not change. Deterministic = recv ignores the bit (IsDeterministic).

  3. Irrevocable outputs: out : State → Option O. A node's output is its first output (decided), so later changes are ignored instead of being forbidden.

  4. Las Vegas solvability. The proofs use Solves: every finite prefix of bits extends to a run in which all nodes output, and every run in which all nodes output is valid. The paper's definition (termination with probability 1, validity of every labelling of positive probability, fair independent coins) is SolvesAS. SolvesAS.solves proves it implies Solves. The validity parts are equivalent; Solves' termination is weaker. Hence our GRAN is weaker than the paper's (GRAN_AS.gran), and Theorems 1 and 2 are stated for both (theorem_1_AS, theorem_2_AS).

  5. Inputs and degrees. The paper assumes that the input label of every node contains its degree. We do not need this (more general): nodes learn their degree from the port numbering, and all view comparisons are of trees whose branching encodes degrees.

  6. Problems are arbitrary predicates on labelled graphs plus arbitrary validity predicates. No isomorphism invariance is assumed (GRAN's decider forces it for the instance set anyway).

  7. Colours come from a type with a linear order ([LinearOrder C]), used for the colour-sorted port numbering of the simulations. The paper's colours are bit strings. theorem_2 also assumes [Nonempty Out] (the infinity-model function must be total).

  8. Local views. We define equality of views (TEq, ViewEq) recursively, never the trees themselves. L_∞(u) = L_∞(v) is defined as equality at all finite depths, which for finitely branching trees is equality of the infinite trees. In 2-hop coloured graphs the view is represented canonically by the set of label words of walks (lang, forall_teq_iff_lang). This is the representation of V∞ and of the input of A∞.

  9. Infinity model. A∞ is a function of the walk language ⟪X⟫ v, which is determined by L_∞(v) (lang_eq_of_forall_teq). So it is an infinity-model algorithm in the paper's sense.

  10. Infinite/finite view graph. G∞ is viewLGraph, with node type the set of walk languages. The finite view graph G* is not constructed. Corollary 2 is stated as "depth-|V∞| views separate exactly the infinite-view classes", which is what makes f_n : V∞ → V* a bijection. A* simulates on the infinite view graph of a canonical realizer of the selected view set, which is isomorphic to the paper's finite view graph.

  11. Orders and choices in A* and A∞. The paper's total orders (on V∞ via canonical tree encodings, on bit assignments, on finite view graphs via bit-string encodings) are replaced as follows. A view set with the fewest nodes is selected, with ties broken by a fixed well-order (select). The successful assignment and the smallest extension are picked by choice functions of the canonical data. The analysis only uses that the choices are functions of the data all nodes share, and Lemma 7 shows the minimum is unique for p ≥ 2n.

  12. Port numbering of the simulations (unspecified in the paper): colour-sorted (colorPN). Coverings preserve it (runC_localIso), and it is local (decided_local).

  13. Message-passing implementation of A*. The paper's phase p lasts p rounds. We implement A* as a full-information algorithm (floodAlg): nodes flood their truncated walk languages and in round r evaluate phase ⌊√r⌋ on their current knowledge. Phase p needs depth p(p+1)/2 ≤ p² (Db, outPhase_depthLocal). The outputs are the same as in the paper's schedule, only the rounds at which they are produced differ. Messages are elements of an arbitrary type; the flooded truncated walk languages are finite sets, so messages have finite size as the paper requires (no bound is imposed, as in the paper). Round complexity is irrelevant in the paper.

  14. Computability of local computations (e.g. deciding C3, enumerating candidates) is not modelled. The paper's motivation for A* (replacing infinite views by finite ones) is about finite rounds, which we do model.

  15. Phase 0. bL 0 = [] and phase 0 is vacuous (no candidate has ≤ 0 nodes), matching the paper's phases 1, 2, ….

Not formalized

  • The introduction's claims: 2-hop colouring, MIS and colouring are in GRAN, and the k-hop variant of colouring is not in GRAN for k > 2. Also the remark |V| = m |V'| for factors.
