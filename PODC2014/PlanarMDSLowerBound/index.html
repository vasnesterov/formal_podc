Local Approximability of MDS on Planar Graphs — PODC 2014

Miikka Hilke, Christoph Lenzen, Jukka Suomela. Brief Announcement: Local Approximability of Minimum Dominating Set on Planar Graphs. PODC 2014. DOI 10.1145/2611462.2611504. Formalized version: arXiv 1402.2549 v1 (3 pages, complete proof); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. The main theorem holds as stated: no deterministic T-round LOCAL algorithm finds a (7 − ε)-approximation of a minimum dominating set on planar graphs, and the Ramsey argument of Lemma 1 and the construction of Figure 1 work as described. The only flaw is that the last step of the proof of Lemma 1 needs internal areas of at least 3 × 3, while the lemma says "for any m". The paper fixes m ≫ T just before, and the theorem takes m large anyway.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 1, last step: "the common output cannot be 0" needs an internal node whose whole closed neighbourhood is internal, i.e. m − 2T ≥ 3, while the lemma says "for any m". The paper has already fixed m ≫ T, and we prove Lemma 1 under 2T + 3 ≤ m (lemma_1); see Proof remarks.

Typos

  • The graph G and the set D* are given only by Figure 1. The paper says D* contains 1/7 of the internal nodes, but it must also dominate the border of the whole grid; the bound |D*| ≤ (1/7 + ε′)n is right once these O(rm) extra nodes are added.

Model.

  • Deterministic LOCAL model with unique identifiers, extensional form (CSLib Cslib.Local.IsLocal T). The nodes of a network are its identifiers Fin n = {0, …, n − 1}. An algorithm is A : SimpleGraph (Fin n) → Fin n → Bool, and its output at v may depend only on the edges at nodes within distance < T of v and on the identifiers within distance ≤ T. An algorithm that reads the whole induced radius-T ball is (T + 1)-local, so it is covered too, because the theorem holds for every T. There is no randomness and no failures. The algorithm may depend on n (A n in no_local_approx_planar); in main_theorem n is fixed and A is arbitrary for that n.

  • The identifier space is exactly {0, …, n − 1}, the most restrictive choice. A lower bound for this choice implies the bound for any larger identifier space.

  • Instances: G = grid (r·m) m is the rm × m triangulated grid: the triangular lattice (x, y) ~ (x ± 1, y), (x, y ± 1), (x + 1, y + 1), (x − 1, y − 1). It has r blocks of m × m nodes side by side, as in Figure 1a. An instance is an identifier assignment σ : GV W H ≃ Fin n, and the network is lab σ (the grid relabelled by σ).

  • Planarity: Mathlib has no planarity, so we use straight-line planar drawings (IsStraightLinePlanar: injective points in ℝ², edges as segments meeting only at common endpoints, no vertex on a non-incident edge). By Fáry's theorem this is equivalent to planarity for finite graphs. We prove that every piece of the triangular lattice has such a drawing.

  • The theorem is stronger than the paper's in two ways. In main_theorem, A only needs to be correct on the relabelled copies of one grid. The grid sizes W, H (and hence n) depend only on T and ε, not on A.

In the explicit form we take m = ⌈7(4T + 72)/ε⌉ + 2T + 3, ℓ = N((2T+1)², m²) + 1 and r = (m + 1)ℓ. Then |A(G)| ≥ ℓm(m − 2T)² and 7|D*| ≤ rm² + 35rm, which gives (7 − ε)|D*| < |A(G)|.

Proof remarks. ℓ. ℓ = o(r) can be taken as a constant N(K, m²) that does not depend on r.

Lemma 1, last step. "The common output cannot be 0; otherwise there would be nodes that are not dominated" needs an internal node whose whole closed neighbourhood is internal, i.e. m' = m − 2T ≥ 3. For m' ∈ {1, 2} (and T ≥ 1) this inference fails in G alone: the boundary rows can dominate the internal nodes. Whether Lemma 1 itself still holds there depends on the reading of "radius-T neighbourhood".

  • (a) If the view is the edges at distance < T plus the identifiers at distance ≤ T (our model) or the induced radius-T ball, the lemma still holds: an internal node of G then has the same view as a deep interior node of a taller planar grid H on n nodes, so c is also the colouring of H. Every monochromatic set of size (2T + 3)² therefore has colour 1, and the Ramsey step with that size goes through.

  • (b) If nodes also see the degrees of the nodes at distance T, the lemma fails for m' = 1: "output 0 iff your view is exactly that of the middle node of a (2T+1)-high strip" dominates every planar graph, and it puts no internal node of G into the output.

In either case the paper has already fixed m ≫ T ("Pick sufficiently large m ≫ T"), so the assumption is implied. The theorem is unaffected. We prove Lemma 1 under 2T + 3 ≤ m (lemma_1). Our lemma_1 assumes correctness only on relabellings of G. Under that weaker hypothesis Lemma 1 fails for m' ∈ {1, 2} already in model (a), via "join iff a horizontal border is visible at distance < T". Neither argument is formalized.

Not formalized. Fáry's theorem itself (equivalence of straight-line and topological planarity), and the prose arguments about Lemma 1 for m − 2T ≤ 2.
