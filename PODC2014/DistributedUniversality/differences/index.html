Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed Universality — PODC 2014.

Paper: Raynal, Stainer, Taubenfeld, Distributed Universality: Contention-Awareness; Wait-Freedom; Object Progress, and Other Properties, PODC 2014 (BA); full version IRISA PI-2014 (hal-00947657v2).

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 2 / abstract property (4), contention-awareness — false (❌). Counterexample theorem_2_not_contention_aware (ContentionCex.lean): a sequential execution (in the paper's sense: an operation is in progress from its first proposal to its return, Section 3.4) in which a process running alone invokes kSC[1].

    • Other readings. The claim holds for the weaker notion "only one process ever takes steps" (fig2_solo_from_init_no_sc). That is not the paper's notion: it says "interval contention" and "no contention during the execution of an operation". Under the other conceivable reading, where an operation is pending from next(), every process that ever started is in contention forever (Figure 1 fetches the next operation, at line 11/22, before returning the previous one at line 31), so the property would reduce to the weak one. That reading also clashes with the paper's well-formedness assumption (Section 2.2: no new operation on m is invoked before the previous one terminated), which Figure 1 would violate at line 22.

    • A reader taking the paper at its word, with long-lived objects and sequential use, is misled substantively. The authors' own justification ("adopt … This means that there is contention", Section 4.1) is exactly the false step.

    • The title names contention-awareness, the abstract lists it as property (4) of the proposed construction, and Theorem 2 names the algorithm of Figure 2.

    • Robustness. The run satisfies the strong reading of the adopt-commit no-conflict property (strongAC = true), so it is valid under both readings. Its line-N1 responses at AC[1][0] coincide with those of the standard register implementation (ACImpl.adopt_on_stale). Under the weak reading (satisfied by the standard implementation, which violates the strong one: ACImpl.adopt_after_solo_commit) even a fully sequential run, in which no two proposed operations overlap, invokes kSC[1] (theorem_2_not_contention_aware_sequential). Under the strong reading, sequential runs do not use SC objects; the concurrent prefix of the main counterexample is needed, and interval contention (the paper's explicit notion) allows it.

  2. Section 4.3, line 4M — typo (✏️). The text proposes oper_i[m]; Figure 2 proposes ac_op_i[m]. The literal text is unsafe (Typo4M.section_4_3_line_4M_unsafe). The intent is evident: Section 4.3 says the algorithm is Figure 2 with only the SC objects replaced. We formalize Figure 2's version (Mode.Good).

  3. Lemma 5 is stated "∀ r ≥ 1" without requiring that some process reaches the end of round r's adopt-commit part. We require it (Lemma5.lemma5_general, hypothesis DoneAC); in infinite executions it always holds (Exec.round_commits). This is a degenerate case (✏️).

  4. Theorems 6 and 7 (Appendix G.2) — not formalized and not checked. For ℓ < k their proofs seem to rely on "(k,ℓ)-universal ⇒ wait-free implementation of any single object", which does not obviously follow; a possible counterexample via the set-consensus hierarchy is only a lead (README).

Proof gaps (statements true; repaired)

  • Lemma 3. The "valid history" definition and its consequences (i), (ii) presuppose that an operation is never committed in non-consecutive rounds. This is argued only inside the proof, and case (a) of that argument overlooks proposers that adopted the operation while already having it in their history. We give a full invariant proof (InvB: chain property hist_G, knowledge know, proposal invariant PIa, collect invariant CI, birth records GS1/GS3/GS4; core step chain_next).

  • Lemma 6. The proof counts rounds with a commit instead of distinct operations, and assumes the committer survives until it writes GSTATE. Repaired via NoRepeat (an operation committed in round ρ is never proposed from round ρ + 2 on) and the knowledge invariant.

  • Theorem 3. The proof's final sub-case is left open. We repaired it with a new argument: the helping window Wst opens at the first moment the object's sequence reaches a length ≡ j mod n (Wst.start, Wst.step, Wst.close).

  • Theorem 4(b). The register-based proof fails when the UC is a black box (Abstract.register_counterexample). Repaired with sticky (first-value) objects (Abstract.scFromUC).

  • Lemma 4 under helping. With L1–L5, a helper may propose p_j's operation before p_j itself does, so an operation can be linearized before the paper's start point (first proposal by its owner). We use the invocation time (next(), ghost invAt) as the start, which is standard linearizability.

  • Adopt-commit validity. Section 2.1 says only "v has been proposed by a process". All proofs (paper and ours) need "proposed before the response", the standard meaning. This is our acOK.

Modelling choices

  • Black-box base objects. Adopt-commit and (k, ℓ)-SC objects have separate invocation and response steps; the adversary chooses any response allowed by the specification. The no-conflicting-values property is taken in the weak reading (it constrains the returning process only); the strong reading is available (Mode.strongAC) and used in the counterexample. Responses always exist (NonVacuity.enabled).

  • (k, ℓ)-SC returns exactly ℓ pairs with distinct objects (Section 4.3). ℓ = 1 is k-SC.

  • Atomicity. Each step performs at most one base-object access or one register read.

    • GSTATE[i] is one register holding all k histories, as in the paper.

    • The local computation of lines 6–13 is one step; lines 16–28 take one step per object, including the read of LAST_OP[j][m] (L1).

    • A process's writes to its own LAST_OP registers, implicit in next(), are merged with the surrounding local step. They are single-writer registers on which these steps commute with other processes' accesses; the merge is not formally justified.

  • Loop orders. "For each m" loops run in increasing order. Lines 3M are executed on the decided objects in increasing order, before lines 4M (the paper allows any order within 3M; we fix one).

  • Line 7 ("longest history containing ℓ_hist") is a deterministic left fold over g_state[1..n]; since all histories are prefix-comparable (proved), every resolution gives the same result.

  • Operations and outputs. my_listᵢ[m] is static: operation c of pᵢ on m is (i, c) (operations unique, as the paper assumes). Outputs are represented by the history they are computed from (retH): we prove it is a prefix of the common sequence containing the operation, so the output is the sequential specification applied to the operations preceding it. Dynamic lists are not modelled.

  • Linearizability is stated with explicit linearization points (Lemma 4's "first write of a GSTATE register containing the operation", ghost lin) and a real-time corollary, not via a general Herlihy–Wing history notion. Per-object; locality is not formalized.

  • Executions. Infinite executions are sequences of enabled steps (Exec). A correct process takes infinitely many steps; a crashed one stops. "An object progresses forever" means its sequence G x grows unboundedly. "Process j completes infinitely many operations on x" means retAt j x c is set for arbitrarily large c (CompletesInf). The ℓ-wait-freedom of Appendix G is theorem_4a.

  • Ghost state (time, G, born, opRec, invAt, retH, retAt, lin, firstL, finR, acFree) never influences the algorithm.

  • Round numbering. The paper's AC[r] (Figure 1) and AC[2r−1], AC[2r] (Figure 2) are acI r false m / acI r true m; Figure 1 uses only the true instance.

  • Process indices are 0-based: L1's j = |ℓ_hist| mod n + 1 becomes |ℓ_hist| mod n.

Not formalized

  • Theorem 5 (needs composition of implementations), Theorems 6–7, Section 4.4's equivalence k-SA ≡ k-SC (cited from [1]).

  • Generosity / obstruction-freedom remarks (Section 3.4, Appendix F).

  • Property (3) of the abstract (an object that stops progressing stops in the same state at all correct processes). It follows from the knowledge invariant but is not stated.

  • Appendices A (Gafni–Guerraoui construction and its flaws), C (state-based variant), E (fewer SC uses).
