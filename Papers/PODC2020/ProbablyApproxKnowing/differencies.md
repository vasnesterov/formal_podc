# Differences between the formalization and the paper

Paper: Y. Moses, N. Zamir, *Probably Approximately Knowing*, PODC 2020 (arXiv:2007.02984v1, full
version with appendix). Verdict rules: `.claude/skills/formalize-paper/verdicts.md`.

## Errors and imprecisions in the paper (statement level)

1. **§7, application of Corollary 7.2 to Example 1: false (❌, R1/R3/R4/R5; borderline, see below).**
   Claim: "Corollary 7.2 implies that *in every protocol that satisfies this constraint*
   [`μ(φ_both | fire_A) ≥ 0.99`], the probability that Alice's degree of belief that both are firing
   together when it decides to fire meets or exceeds 0.9 is at least 0.9" (the italic bold emphasis
   is the paper's). Read with Definition 3.1, the belief is `β_A(φ_both)` at the firing point, and the
   claim fails for protocols in which Alice's firing is a mixed action.
   `Example1.Mixed` is a 5-run pps. If `go = 1`, Alice sends two messages in round 1, each lost with
   probability 0.1, and fires at time 1 with probability 1/2. Bob fires at time 1 iff he got a
   message. This satisfies the Spec of Example 1 and gives `μ(φ_both@fire_A | fire_A) = 0.99`. Yet
   `β_A(φ_both) = 0.495` at both firing runs, so `μ(β_A(φ_both)@fire_A ≥ 0.9 | fire_A) = 0`, and `φ_both`
   is not local-state independent of `fire_A` (`Mixed.section7_claim_false`). Robustness: in every
   protocol-generated pps, `β_i(φ ∧ does_i(α)) ≤ P_i(ℓ_i)(α)` at every point
   (`JointProtocol.belief_and_does_le`). So any protocol in which Alice fires with probability ≤ 1/2
   at each local state is a counterexample (`JointProtocol.cprob_belief_and_does_eq_zero`).
   *Which label.* It is a statement (R1: a concrete claim about an example in the main text), not a
   headline (R5: §7's illustration, not a result named in the title or abstract), so ❌ or ⚠️.
   Not excused by R4: the missing assumption (Alice fires deterministically, or local-state
   independence) is exactly what the paper's emphasized "in every protocol" denies, and mixed actions
   are part of the paper's model (§2.2, first contribution bullet). Not excused by R3: the sentence
   names `φ_both`, the fact of the constraint that Corollary 7.2 is applied to, so the intended
   version with `fire_B` is not evident from the text, and the paper's one-line argument reasons with
   `φ_both`. Hence ❌. What can be said in the paper's favour: the hypothesis
   `μ(φ_both@fire_A | fire_A) = μ(fire_B@fire_A | fire_A)` is the same for both facts, and with `fire_B`
   ("Bob is firing") in place of `φ_both` the claim holds in every protocol-generated system, because
   `fire_B` is local-state independent of every action of Alice
   (`JointProtocol.section7_claim_fireB`, via `lsi_of_indepOwnAction`, a generalization of Lemma
   4.3(b)). §1 calls `β_A(fire_B)` Alice's belief that Bob fires, and for deterministic firing (FS) the
   two beliefs coincide at firing points. Note that the paper's own Lemma 4.3 would not give the
   `fire_B` version either (`fire_B` is not past-based).
2. **Abstract omits local-state independence (⚠️, R3).** The abstract says that
   the expected belief when acting equals the probability that `φ` holds when acting, and that the
   threshold `1 − ε²` forces belief `≥ 1 − ε` with probability `≥ 1 − ε`. Neither mentions local
   state independence. Without it both are false: in Figure 1 with `φ = does_i(α)`, `μ(φ@α | α) = 1`
   (`= 1 − 0²`) but `β_i(φ) = 1/2` whenever `α` is performed (`Fig1.theorem_6_2_needs_independence`).
   The contribution list in §1 ("if ϕ and α satisfy the independence property mentioned above") and
   the theorems (6.2, 7.1, 7.2) state the hypothesis, and the paper itself shows it is needed (§6),
   so the intended statement is recoverable and the fix is local.
3. **Lemma 4.3(b) is implicitly about protocol-generated pps with observable termination (⚠️, R4; proof gap).** In an
   arbitrary tree pps of §2.1, a past-based fact need not be local-state independent. Example: two
   equiprobable initial states that `i` cannot distinguish, with `i` performing `α` in the first and
   `α' ≠ α` in the second. With `φ` = "the initial state is the first one" (past-based),
   `μ(φ@ℓ | ℓ) · μ(α@ℓ | ℓ) = 1/4` but `μ([φ ∧ α]@ℓ | ℓ) = 1/2`. The paper's proof uses that `i`'s action is
   drawn from `P_i(ℓ_i)` independently of everything else at the node, which holds for systems
   generated by a joint protocol (§2.2: "We will restrict attention to systems…"). We count this as
   the paper's standing assumption (R4). The proof is a two-line sketch. It also needs
   independence of the participants' choices within a round, which the paper never states, and that
   `ℓ_i` cannot occur at a leaf in one run and at an inner node in another. Otherwise the fact
   "the run has ended" is a past-based counterexample, possible if runs can end at different times
   unnoticed by `i`; the paper leaves termination unspecified. We prove the lemma for
   protocol-generated systems with a fixed horizon `N` by a Markov-property argument
   (`JointProtocol.sum_weightAux_mul_ind`). It extends to facts that also depend on the other
   participants' actions at the current time (`JointProtocol.lsi_of_indepOwnAction`).
4. **Typos.** The proof of Theorem 6.2 cites "Theorem 4.1" for Definition 4.1, and so does the remark
   after Theorem 4.2. In the proof of Theorem 7.1, `β_i(φ)@α | α [r]` should be `β_i(φ)@α [r]`.
   Lemma B.1 says "local-state independent of `does_i(α)`" where Definition 4.1 says "of `α`" (same
   meaning). The §8 value 0.99899 is `990/991 = 0.998990…` truncated (`FS.cprob_both_noNo`).

No other errors: Theorems 4.2, 5.2, 6.2, 7.1, Corollary 7.2, Lemmas 4.3(a), 5.1, B.1, F.1, the
"necessary and sufficient" remark after 6.2 and the `p' = 1 − √(1 − p)` remark after 7.2 hold as
stated. So do the Figure 1 claims and the numbers of Example 1 (0.99; beliefs 1/0/0.99; 0.009 and
0.991).

## Modelling choices

* **Abstract pps.** `PPS Ω Agent L` keeps only what §§3–7 use: a finite run type, positive run
  probabilities summing to 1, run lengths, local states `loc i r t`, and synchrony (`sync`: a local
  state determines the time). The tree is not represented. Every tree pps yields a `PPS`. Conversely,
  a `PPS` is the pps of the tree whose root children are the runs themselves, with the environment's
  state determining the run, so the general theorems are exactly as general as the paper's. The
  proofs of §§4–7 never use synchrony or the tree; it is kept for faithfulness.
* **Actions are facts.** A "proper action `α` of `i`" is any fact `A` (meant to be `does_i(α)`) that
  holds at some point and at most once per run. The theorems hold for every such fact, which is
  slightly more general. `β_i(φ)@α[r] = 0` when `α` is not performed, as in §3.1.
* **Conditional probability** `μ(Q | Y) := μ(Q ∧ Y)/μ(Y)`, which is `0` when `μ(Y) = 0`. This only
  matters for local states that never occur, where all quantities in Definition 4.1 are `0`.
* **Protocol-generated pps** (`JointProtocol`, used only for Lemma 4.3(b) and §7):
  * participants `P` include the environment, which the paper treats separately but with the same
    shape of protocol;
  * action sets are finite types (the paper only requires finite support);
  * local states are `(t, loc p g)`, i.e. the time is part of every local state, as in §2.1. The
    protocol is a function of this local state, and the transition function may depend on `t`
    (equivalent to a clock in the global state);
  * all participants choose independently in each round;
  * fixed horizon `N` (all runs have `N` rounds), and runs are pairs (initial state, joint-action
    sequence) of positive probability. The node of the tree at time `t` is determined by the initial
    state and the actions before `t`, as with the paper's "history" component of the environment's
    state. `does_i(α)` holds at `(r, t)` iff `t < N` and `i`'s action in round `t` is `α` (the paper
    reads it from `r_e(t+1)`).
* **Theorem 5.2** is proved with `T̂` as an abstract `PPS` (`Fig2.T`), with runs `r, r', r''`,
  probabilities `1 − p, p − ε, ε`, local states as in Figure 2, and `i` acting at time 1. It is a pps
  in the sense of §2.1, which is what the theorem quantifies over; we did not also build it as a
  `JointProtocol`. As in the paper, the case `ε ≥ p` is reduced to `ε' = min(ε, p/2) < p`.
* **Example 1** pps's (`Example1.lean`) record only the random outcomes Alice's beliefs depend on.
  FS uses "Bob got at least one message" (0.99) and "Bob's reply was delivered" (0.9). Runs of the
  full tree that differ only in which of Alice's two messages arrived are merged, and Bob's local
  state is coarsened accordingly. This changes no probability or Alice-belief of the facts considered.
  The time-`t` facts `fire_A`, `fire_B` are read as in §2.3 (`does` at time 2 for FS, time 1 for
  `Mixed`).

## Proof-level differences

* Theorem 6.2: the paper's chain (10)–(23) becomes two fibrewise sums over the local state at which
  `α` is performed (`PPS.sum_mu_beliefAt`, `Finset.sum_fiberwise_of_maps_to`). Theorems 4.2, 7.1,
  Lemmas 5.1, F.1 and Corollary 7.2 are derived from it, as in the paper. Theorem 7.1 is proved by
  Markov's inequality (`PPS.markov_beliefAt`) rather than the paper's strict-inequality contradiction
  argument; this is equivalent and also covers `ε, δ` outside `(0, 1)`.
* Lemma 4.3(a): the paper shows `μ(α@ℓ | ℓ) ∈ {0, 1}` from determinism. We use synchrony to compare
  points at the same time, as the paper's definition of a deterministic action does.
