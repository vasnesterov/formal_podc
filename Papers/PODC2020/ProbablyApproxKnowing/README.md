# Probably Approximately Knowing — PODC 2020

Yoram Moses, Nitzan Zamir. *Probably Approximately Knowing.* PODC 2020. DOI
[10.1145/3382734.3405734](https://doi.org/10.1145/3382734.3405734). Formalized version: arXiv
[2007.02984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02984) v1 (full version with appendix); numbering follows it.
Copy of the paper: [paper.pdf](paper.pdf).

**Verdict: ❌ False claim.** All numbered results hold: Theorems 4.2, 5.2, 6.2, 7.1,
Corollary 7.2, Lemmas 4.3, 5.1, B.1, F.1, and the numbers of Example 1. The one false statement is
a concrete claim in §7 about Example 1: that *every* protocol meeting the constraint
`μ(φ_both | fire_A) ≥ 0.99` makes Alice believe `φ_both` with degree `≥ 0.9` with probability
`≥ 0.9` when she fires. It fails as soon as Alice fires as a mixed action. It holds for Alice's
belief that Bob fires. Smaller issues: the abstract states the main theorem without its hypothesis
(local-state independence), and Lemma 4.3(b) silently needs a protocol-generated system with
observable termination.

## Mistakes in the paper

* **§7 claim about Example 1 is false (a secondary, non-headline statement).** §7 says:
  "Corollary 7.2 implies that ***in every protocol that satisfies this constraint*** [`μ(φ_both | fire_A)
  ≥ 0.99`], the probability that Alice's degree of belief that both are firing together when it
  decides to fire meets or exceeds 0.9 is at least 0.9" (emphasis in the paper). Corollary 7.2 needs
  `φ_both` to be local-state independent of `fire_A`, and that fails as soon as Alice fires as a
  *mixed* action. Counterexample (the Spec of Example 1 holds): if `go = 1`, Alice sends two messages
  in round 1 and fires at time 1 with probability 1/2; Bob fires at time 1 iff he received one of
  them. Then `μ(φ_both | fire_A) = 0.99`. But when Alice fires she does not yet know her coin (§2.2:
  "when the agent decides on the mixed step she does not know which of the actions … will actually
  be performed"), so `β_A(φ_both) = 0.5 · 0.99 = 0.495`. So the belief reaches 0.9 with probability
  0, not ≥ 0.9 (`Example1.Mixed.section7_claim_false`). This is general: in every protocol-generated
  pps, `β_i(φ ∧ does_i(α)) ≤ P_i(ℓ_i)(α)`, the probability of choosing `α`
  (`JointProtocol.belief_and_does_le`, `cprob_belief_and_does_eq_zero`). We do not count it as a
  typo: the sentence names `φ_both`, the very fact of the constraint it applies Corollary 7.2 to,
  and it stresses "every protocol", while mixed actions are part of the paper's model and exactly
  the case §§4 and 6 warn about. A repaired claim holds: replace `φ_both` by "Bob fires" (the two
  constraints coincide). This version is true in every protocol-generated system, because "`j`
  performs `β`" is local-state independent of every action of `i ≠ j`
  (`JointProtocol.section7_claim_fireB`). The paper's Lemma 4.3 does not cover this case: "Bob
  fires" is not past-based.
* **The abstract drops the hypothesis of the main theorem (excused: stated everywhere else).** The
  abstract says that the expected belief when acting *equals* the probability that `φ` holds when
  acting, and that a threshold `1 − ε²` forces belief `≥ 1 − ε` with probability `≥ 1 − ε`. Neither
  mentions local-state independence, without which both are false: in Figure 1 with
  `φ = does_i(α)`, `μ(φ@α | α) = 1` but `E(β_i(φ)@α | α) = 1/2`, and the belief never reaches 1
  (`Fig1.theorem_6_2_needs_independence`). The contribution list in §1 and the theorems in the body
  carry the hypothesis.
* **Lemma 4.3(b): true under implicit assumptions; the proof is a sketch.** In an arbitrary
  tree pps (§2.1), past-based facts need not be local-state independent. Example: two initial states
  that `i` cannot tell apart, and `i` performs `α` in exactly one of them. The proof uses "the
  probability that `i` performs `α` is the same at all points with local state `ℓ_i`". This needs a
  pps generated by a joint protocol in which all participants choose independently, and runs that
  cannot end at different times while `i` has the same local state. We prove Lemma 4.3(b) for
  protocol-generated systems with a fixed horizon via a Markov-property argument. It also holds for
  facts that additionally depend on the *other* agents' current actions (`JointProtocol.lemma_4_3b`,
  `JointProtocol.lsi_of_indepOwnAction`).
* Typos: the proof of Theorem 6.2 cites "Theorem 4.1" for Definition 4.1, and so does the remark
  after Theorem 4.2. In the proof of Theorem 7.1, `β_i(φ)@α | α [r]` should be `β_i(φ)@α [r]`.

## The formalization

**Model.**
* *Abstract pps* (`PPS`, `Basic.lean`). Finitely many runs with positive prior probabilities summing
  to 1, a common length per run, and the local state `r_i(t)` of every agent at every point, under
  synchrony (a local state determines the time). Facts are arbitrary sets of points, and an action is
  given by its fact `does_i(α)`. Beliefs, `φ@ℓ`, `φ@α`, proper actions, `β_i(φ)@α` (0 off `R_α`),
  local-state independence and expected belief follow Definitions 3.1–6.1 literally. Every tree pps of
  §2.1 gives such a structure. Conversely, every such structure is a tree pps in which the
  environment's initial state fixes the run. So §§4–7 are proved in exactly the paper's generality,
  and slightly more: the action may be any fact.
* *Protocol-generated pps* (`JointProtocol`, `Protocol.lean`), used for Lemma 4.3(b) and §7. A
  synchronous system: an initial distribution over global states, participants (agents and the
  environment) with local states `(t, loc p g)`, a probabilistic protocol per participant, and
  independent choices in every round. There is a deterministic transition function and a fixed
  number `N` of rounds. Runs are the action sequences of positive probability; this matches the
  paper's assumption that the environment records the history. There are no failures and no
  adversary: the adversary is fixed, as in §2.
* Examples are concrete abstract pps's: Figure 1, `T̂` of Figure 2, FS, and the mixed variant.

**Trust.** No `sorry`; the listed theorems use only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound`. Hypotheses are non-vacuous: Figure 2's `T̂` satisfies all hypotheses of the general
theorems (proper, deterministic, hence local-state independent), and so does FS. The run
distribution of `JointProtocol` sums to 1 (`toPPS`). The one-step independence argument uses CSLib's
`Cslib.Probability.piExpect` (`Cslib/Probability/Finite/Product.lean`). CSLib candidates (marked
`-- CSLib candidate`): the abstract `PPS` with posterior beliefs and Theorem 6.2 (`Basic.lean`), and
protocol-generated run distributions with their Markov property (`JointProtocol`,
`sum_weightAux_mul_ind`, `Protocol.lean`).
