# Restorable Shortest Path Tiebreaking — PODC 2021

Greg Bodwin, Merav Parter. *Restorable Shortest Path Tiebreaking for Edge-Faulty Graphs.* PODC 2021.
DOI [10.1145/3465084.3467913](https://doi.org/10.1145/3465084.3467913). Formalized version: arXiv
[2102.10174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10174) v4; numbering follows it.
Copy of the paper: [paper.pdf](paper.pdf).

**Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix.** The main result is proved in full: every graph has a consistent, stable,
`f`-restorable replacement-path tiebreaking scheme, generated by antisymmetric tiebreaking weights
(Theorems 2, 19, 20, 23). So are the `C₄` impossibility of symmetric restorable schemes (Theorem 37) and
the correctness of the preserver, labeling and `+4`-spanner applications, after local repairs: one constant in
Corollary 22's union bound, the minimum in Theorem 23's proof, and the connectivity hypothesis of Definition 17,
which Theorem 2 already prints. The sizes of the applications for `f ≥ 1`, Theorem 27 and all running-time and
CONGEST claims were not checked.

## Mistakes in the paper

### ✏️ Slips
- Definition 17 and Theorem 19 (missing connectivity hypothesis): Definition 17 asks for a replacement path for every
  nonempty fault set `F` and every pair `s, t`, and does not require that `s` and `t` remain connected in `G \ F`.
  When `F` cuts `s` from `t`, no replacement path exists. So no scheme is restorable on any graph in which some `F`
  with `|F| ≤ f` separates two vertices: any graph with a bridge (`f ≥ 1`), and any disconnected graph with an edge.
  Read literally, Theorem 19 fails, e.g., on the single-edge graph `K₂`, which does have ATW functions
  (`theorem19_literal_false`). Theorem 2 states the correct hypothesis ("an `s ⇝ t` path remains"). We add it to
  Definition 17 (`IsRestorable`) and prove Theorem 19 (`theorem19`).
- The proof of Corollary 22 says "path lengths change by `< 1/(2n)`". That is the per-edge change; a path changes by
  `< 1/2`, as in Theorem 20's proof (`isATW_of_coeff`).
- The first bullet of Theorem 23's proof says a non-shortest path is "at least 1 less" than a shortest path, where
  "at least 1 more" is meant (`theorem23`).
- Theorem 21 (isolation lemma) is stated for a set of paths but needs distinct edge sets: a path and its reverse
  always tie. In the only use all paths go from `s` to `t` (`Cslib.Isolation.card_not_uniqueMin_le`).
- The proof of Theorem 30 says "restorable `f`-RPTS" where an `(f+1)`-restorable `(f+1)`-RPTS is needed
  (`theorem30_correct`).
- The proof of Lemma 32 omits replacement paths that contain no clustered vertex. In the missing case every edge of
  the path is in `H` (`lemma32_correct`).
- Appendix B (Theorem 27, not formalized) has several slips, none affecting the asymptotics:
  - the bipartite weights should be `1 + (λ−j)/n⁴`;
  - Observation 1(c) states `N(f, d) = 2^f · d²` where `≤` is meant;
  - labels are undefined for the last copy at each level, so only a constant fraction of the bipartite
    edges is forced.

### Typos
- Appendix B: `v*` is never defined.

## Proof gaps (statements true)

### 🔧 Corollary 22 (union bound over `s, t, F`)
The proof bounds the number of choices of `(s, t, F)` with `|F| ≤ f` by `n^{f+2}`, but there are about
`n² · |E|^f`, up to about `n^{2f+2}` (`paper_count_false`).

**Impact.** Statement true after changing the constant `W` in the proof; it still gives `O(f log n)` bits per
weight (`corollary22`).

**Details.**
- Already `K₄` with `f = 1` has `16 · 7 = 112 > 4³` choices with ordered pairs (`84 > 64` excluding
  `s = t`).
- With the paper's `W = n^{f+4+c}`, the union bound therefore only gives about `n^{f−c}`, not `n^{−c}`.

**Repair.** `W = n^{2f+4+c}` repairs the bound and keeps `O(f log n)` bits per weight. We prove the correct
bound `n² · N_f · |E| / (2W+1)` with the isolation lemma. The final arithmetic (choice of `W`,
`O(f log n)` bits) is not stated in Lean. (`paper_count_false`; repaired: `corollary22`)

### 🔧 Corollary 22 (signed weights in the isolation lemma)
Applying the isolation lemma to signed directed weights needs that all shortest `s ⇝ t` paths cross each edge in the
same direction and that the weight distribution is symmetric under negation; the proof does not say so.

**Impact.** Statement true; the two facts are added at this step (`corollary22`, via `tau` and a sign flip).

### 🔧 Theorem 23 (deterministic weights)
The uniqueness argument picks the minimum-ID edge of `π₁ \ π₂` instead of the whole symmetric difference.

**Impact.** Statement true; repaired (`theorem23`).

**Details.** The proof also speaks of arbitrary paths, ignoring lengths; the `±2r(e)` terms of oppositely
traversed edges are harmless for large `C`.

[differencies.md](differencies.md).

## The formalization

**Model.**
- **Graphs and faults.** A finite undirected unweighted graph `G : SimpleGraph V`. Edge faults
  `F : Finset (Sym2 V)` (deterministic, arbitrary). `G \ F` is `G.deleteEdges F` and distances are
  `edist`.
- **Paths.** Selected paths are walks of `G` (`G.Walk s t`). A replacement path is `IsShortestIn F`: it
  avoids `F` and is a shortest walk of `G \ F`.
- **Schemes.** A scheme `π : Finset (Sym2 V) → ∀ s t, Option (G.Walk s t)` selects `π(s, t | F)`.
- **Weights.** `G*` has dart weights `1 + r(u,v)` (`wstar`). Our `IsATW` reads Definition 18 with lightest
  *walks*; `IsATWPath` reads it with lightest *simple paths*. The two are proved equivalent.
- **Randomness.** Only in Theorem 20 (uniform product measure on `[-ε, ε]^{Sym2 V}`) and Corollary 22
  (uniform `{−W,…,W}^{Sym2 V}`, counting form).
- **Not modelled.** No distributed or computational model is formalized; running times, bit lengths and
  the CONGEST results are outside the formalization.

**Not formalized.**
- The size bound of Theorem 26 (cited from [9], proof sketch only) and therefore the sizes in
  Theorems 5, 7, 10, 30, 31 and 33 for `f ≥ 1`.
- The expected-size part of Lemma 32.
- The lower bound of Theorem 27 (checked numerically only: `scripts/thm27.py` confirms the corrected construction
  for `f ≤ 3` and small `d`). Open point: the weights `W` do not break all ties, so a further generic perturbation is
  needed to obtain a full RPTS. The forced paths stay unique; we did not prove the extension.
- All running-time, bit-complexity and CONGEST results: Theorems 3, 8, 28, 29 (runtime), 35, Corollary 9
  and Lemmas 34–36.

**Trust.** No `sorry`. All theorems in the table use only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

Non-vacuity:
- ATW functions exist for every finite graph (`theorem23`), so `theorem19` is not vacuous.
- Theorem 2 is an existence statement.
- `theorem37` and `theorem19_literal_false` are refutations on concrete graphs (`C₄`, `K₂`).

General graph infrastructure is in CSLib, `Cslib/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/ShortestWalks.lean`. It covers:
- walks avoiding a fault set and shortest walks of `G \ A`;
- splitting at a dart;
- orientation of edges on shortest walks;
- distinct shortest walks have distinct edge sets;
- directed dart weights and the bypass weight bound.

The isolation lemma is in CSLib, `Cslib/Foundations/Combinatorics/IsolationLemma.lean`.
