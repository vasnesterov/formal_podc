import Papers.PODC2010.EventualLinearizability.Layer import Papers.PODC2010.EventualLinearizability.Defs /-! # Implementations on top of a consistency layer (Section 4.1, Appendix B) A general model of shared-object implementations that use *only* a consistency layer, as in Theorems 2 and 3 of the paper. * Every process runs a deterministic automaton (`Impl`). Its inputs are high-level invocations, deliveries of the consistency layer (sequences of submitted items) and local ticks (local computation steps, e.g. "upon periodic tick"). On every input it may change its state, submit items (arbitrary messages) to the layer, and return the response of its pending operation. * An item is `(submitter, index of the submission at the submitter, payload)`, so items are unique. * A run is driven by an adversary (`Adversary`): at every time `t + 1` it chooses, as a function of the time and the current configuration, an action: nothing, a high-level invocation at a process, a delivery of a sequence of items to a process, or a tick of a process. The adversary plays both the environment (invocations), the scheduler (asynchrony, crashes) and the consistency layer (deliveries); the layer's specification is imposed on the resulting `LayerRun` (`Admissible`). * `hist`: the high-level history of a run; `layerRun`: the consistency-layer run (`S(i, t)` = the last sequence delivered to `p_i`; submission times from the log). * `Admissible spec allowed adv correct`: crashed processes take no step after their crash, correct processes tick infinitely often, invocations respect the environment (`allowed`, e.g. single writer / single reader) and happen only at processes without a pending operation, and the layer run satisfies `spec` (e.g. Eventual Consistency, Eventual Consensus) for the set `correct`. * `Terminates`: every operation invoked by a correct process eventually completes (an implied requirement of the paper; without it, an implementation that never responds would be eventually linearizable). * `EventuallyLinearizable`: there is a single time `t_l` such that, in every admissible run, every finite prefix of the high-level history is weakly consistent, and, if all processes are correct, `t_l`-linearizable (the late operations being those not yet invoked at time `t_l`). This is weaker than the paper's Eventual Linearizability, uniform or not, so impossibility results for it are stronger. -/ namespace Podc.EventualLinearizability open Cslib.SharedMemory.HerlihyWing
