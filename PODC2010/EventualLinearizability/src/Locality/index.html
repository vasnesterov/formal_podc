We prove this for arbitrary sets of "late" operations (`A ⊆ A_x` for all `x` corresponds to `t_max ≥ t_x`). Histories are assumed well-formed (as in the paper). The "only if" directions additionally need the empty history to be legal for every object (`hnil`): with CSLib's `Legal`, a sequential history of operations of `x` is legal only if its (empty) restriction to every other object is in that object's specification. ## Differences with the paper's proofs * **Lemma 1, "if" direction.** The paper reuses, for an operation `o` of `p_i` on `x`, the sequential history `τ_x(i, o)` of `H|x` as `τ(i, o)` for `H`. This violates condition (iii) across objects: for an earlier operation `o'` of `p_i` on another object `y`, `τ(i, o') = τ_y(i, o')` consists of operations of `y` and is not contained in `τ_x(i, o)`. We use instead (`wcGlue`) the concatenation, over all objects `y ≠ x`, of `τ_y(i, b_y)` where `b_y` is the last operation of `p_i` on `y` that precedes `o` in real time (`wcLast`; nothing if there is none), followed by `τ_x(i, o)`. * **Lemma 2, "if" direction.** Instead of the paper's minimal-cycle argument, we merge the `t_x`-linearizations `L_x` by a stable sort on a key (`tKey`): the key of an entry `a` of `L_x` is the largest invocation time of a late operation at or before `a` in `L_x`. Keys are monotone along each `L_x` and strictly increase along `<_{H,t}` (`tKey_lt`), so the sorted concatenation (`tMerge`) contains each `L_x` as its subsequence of operations of `x` and respects
