import Papers.PODC2010.EventualLinearizability.Runs import Mathlib.Tactic.FinCases import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.SharedMemory.HerlihyWing.SeqLin /-! # Theorem 2: no eventually linearizable register on top of Eventual Consistency **Theorem 2** of the paper: there is no implementation of an eventually linearizable single-writer single-reader binary register that uses only an eventually consistent layer. We prove a stronger statement (`not_eventuallyLinearizable_eCons`): the impossibility holds even on top of **Eventual Consensus** (Eventual Consistency + Eventual Stability), and even for our weakened Eventual Linearizability (one `t_l` for all runs, `t_l`-linearizability required only in runs without crashes, see `ImplModel.lean`). This **refutes the sufficiency part of Theorem 3** of the paper (Eventual Consensus suffices to implement an eventually linearizable object): with Eventual Consensus as the layer specification, no terminating implementation of a register is eventually linearizable. `theorem2` (the paper's statement, for Eventual Consistency) follows since Eventual Consensus implies Eventual Consistency. The model is the one of `ImplModel.lean`: deterministic process automata reacting to invocations, deliveries of the layer and ticks; the adversary schedules the actions and plays the layer; termination (`Terminates`) is a hypothesis (without it, an implementation that never responds would be vacuously eventually linearizable). `localImpl_terminates` shows that the hypothesis is satisfiable. ## The argument (the paper's proof, with infinite admissible runs) Fix `I`, `t_l` and termination. `w` = the write(true) of `p0`, `r` = the read of `p1`. All runs end with the round-robin layer of `Runs.lean` (snapshots of the log), so they satisfy Eventual Consensus. * `R0` (`soloAdv 0 (write true) t_l`, only `p0` correct): idle up to `t_l`, `w` is invoked at `t_l + 1`, then round robin for `p0`. By termination `w` completes, by some time `t_w`. * `R1` (`soloAdv 1 read t_w`, only `p1` correct): idle up to `t_w`, `r` is invoked at `t_w + 1`, then round robin for `p1`. By termination `r` completes, by some time `t_r`; by weak consistency `r` returns `false` (`resOf_read`): its `τ(r)` contains only `r`. * `R2` (`glue`, no crash): copies `R0` up to `t_w` and `R1` from `t_w` to `t_r`, then round robin for both processes. Since every action of `R1` in `[t_w, t_r)` is an action of `p1`, and the reaction of `p1` depends only on its local state, `p1` behaves in `R2` as in `R1` (`glue_sim`): the history of `R2` at `t_r` is `H(R0, t_w) ++ H(R1, t_r)`. `R2` is admissible: before `t_r` deliveries are snapshots of `R0`'s log or of `R1`'s log (a part of `R2`'s log), and from `t_r` on the round-robin layer gives Eventual Consensus (stable from the end of the first round after `t_r`). * In `R2`, `w` completes before `r` is invoked and `r` is invoked after `t_l`, so a `t_l`-linearization orders `w` before `r` and gives `r` its result `false`; but a legal sequential history with `write(true)` before `read` makes the read return `true` (`no_tlin`).
