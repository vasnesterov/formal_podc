The k-Bakery — PODC 2010

Robert Danek. The k-Bakery: Local-Spin k-Exclusion Using Non-atomic Reads and Writes. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835707. Formalized version: the author's 9-page copy (no arXiv version); the proofs the paper omits (Theorem 9, the CC part of Lemma 7, Theorem 10) are taken from Danek's PhD thesis (Toronto, 2010, Chapter 5: Figures 5.3, 5.5, 5.6, Theorems 5.9, 5.20, 5.33). Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The non-atomic algorithm of Figure 3 — the variant named in the title — violates k-exclusion: with safe registers, 3 processes and k = 1, two processes end up in the critical section together (NonAtomic.counterexample), and no repair is proved. The two algorithms for atomic reads and writes are correct: Theorems 8 and 9 (k-exclusion, starvation freedom with up to k − 1 crashes, k-FCFS, FIFE, Θ(N) RMRs per passage in the DSM and CC models) are fully proved.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 10 (Figure 3, non-atomic reads and writes)

Figure 3 does not satisfy k-exclusion: for N = 3 processes y, p, r and k = 1 there is a legal execution with safe registers after which y and p are both in the CS (NonAtomic.counterexample).

Impact. The title's claim of a k-exclusion algorithm with non-atomic reads and writes is refuted for the paper's algorithm. The atomic results (Theorems 8 and 9) are unaffected. The thesis' Theorem 5.33 (same algorithm, Figure 5.6) is false for the same reason.

Details

Ticket is a safe register too (the paper duplicates Want and Capture, but not Ticket), so a read of Ticket[r] that overlaps a write of r may return any value, even 0. Line 44 keeps an old capture value Capture[r][p], written in an earlier passage of r, until p's ticket catches up with it. The execution (246 steps):

  1. y starts its first passage and begins writing Ticket[y] := 1; meanwhile r chooses its ticket, its read of Ticket[y] returns 100, so r gets ticket 101.

  2. y (ticket 1) goes through the CS and leaves. Then r goes through the CS; at line 45 it captures y and p with Capture[r][·] := 101.

  3. r starts a second passage and stops in the middle of writing Ticket[r] := 102.

  4. y starts its second passage; its read of Ticket[r] returns 0, so y gets ticket 2 and enters the CS (all announcements it sees are larger).

  5. p starts its first passage; its read of Ticket[r] returns 0, so p gets ticket 3. p cannot remove y (whose pair (2, y) is smaller), but it reads the stale Capture[r][p] = 101 > 3, leaves the loop as captured, and enters the CS too.

The thesis' proof breaks at Lemma 5.21 ("if Cp → Cq then tp < tq"): "the value that q reads from Ticket[p] is no smaller than tp" fails when p is rewriting Ticket[p] in a later passage (step 4–5: C_r → C_p but t_p = 3 < t_r = 101). Lemma 5.24 then allows a capture by a stale value, and the induction of Lemma 5.25 ("r was CS-qualified earlier, so r violates the lemma earlier") no longer applies, because the processes that block p started their passages after r's passage.

Scope. The counterexample uses one particular legal schedule, foreach loops in increasing index order (the paper leaves the order open), and the safe-register semantics the paper states ("if a non-atomic read and write access the same variable concurrently, then the read returns an arbitrary value"). It does not depend on crashes.

Repair

Not formalized. Making Ticket an atomic or at least a regular register (a read overlapping a write returns the old or the new value) restores Lemma 5.21, since tickets only grow; whether the rest of the thesis' proof then goes through was not checked.

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, N processes of a finite linearly ordered type P (ids break ticket ties). Figure 2 is one transition system with a flag fife: false is the unshaded algorithm (Theorem 8), true the full algorithm (Theorem 9). One shared access per step; the program counter carries the local variables. The adversary schedules processes and picks the next index of every foreach (any order).

  • Crash failures: a process crashes iff it takes finitely many steps and stops outside the NCS; a process resting in the NCS forever is not faulty; a scheduled process in the CS leaves it (so "the CS is finite" for non-faulty processes). No fairness assumption beyond this.

  • Executions are infinite schedules (every process always has an enabled step); safety is also proved for all reachable configurations. Deterministic algorithm, no randomness.

  • RMRs: DSM with Ticket[p], Want[i][p], Capture[i][p] in p's module; CC write-through / write-invalidate caches (cached), reads miss iff no valid copy, every write is an RMR and invalidates the other copies.

  • Figure 3: separate model (NonAtomic.lean) with safe registers: writes take two steps (begin, end), a read overlapping a write returns an adversary-chosen value. This is a sub-model of the paper's interval semantics, so its executions are legal executions of the paper's model.

Proof remarks. The proofs replace the paper's interval reasoning (→, ⇢) by inductive invariants. Safe s q v r says that q cannot get priority over the pair (v, r) before its next ticket; the set U s v r of unsafe processes only shrinks while v ≤ Ticket[r]. A process past its loop has fewer than k unsafe processes: either its predecessor set is small, or it was captured with a pair (v, r) whose own unsafe set is small (card_U_le_of_capture). This also replaces the thesis' induction on qualification times (Lemmas 5.13–5.14). Starvation freedom follows the paper's minimal-ticket argument. The paper's sketch skips processes that leave and re-enter; these are handled by a measure argument (reach_good). The CC bound is an amortized potential argument. While p waits, q has at most 4 more writes to Want[q][p] and at most 2 more to Capture[q][p] before these are good for p forever (Bw, Bc). This gives the thesis' "5 RMRs per Want[q][p], 3 per Capture[q][p]".

Explicit constants: per passage, DSM exactly 4(N − 1) / 5(N − 1) RMRs (unshaded / full algorithm), CC at most 9N − 6 / 13N − 6 and at least 3N − 1; FIFE within 4N + 7 own steps after q is in the CS.

Not formalized. The rest of Theorem 10 (starvation freedom, FIFE, Θ(N) RMRs for Figure 3) was not examined, as k-exclusion already fails. The scenarios of Section 5 (Figure 2 unshaded violates FIFE) and Section 6 (Figure 2 is incorrect with non-atomic registers); Table 1; the CC write-back variant; the ticket-resetting algorithms of the thesis.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The counterexample is checked by kernel evaluation (decide +kernel, no native_decide). Non-vacuity: in the round-robin execution every process enters the CS (rrRun_enters_cs). The model is paper-local (no CSLib model of RMRs or crash-prone mutual exclusion fits); it imports only Mathlib. CSLib candidates: exists_good_of_measure (Progress.lean), sum_cost_le (RMRCC.lean).
