import Papers.PODC2010.KBakery.Basic /-! # Invariant definitions for the k-Bakery The central notion is `Safe s q v r`: process `q` does not have priority over the pair `(v, r)` (ticket `v` of process `r`), *now and until `q` chooses its next ticket*: `q` is in the NCS, at line 14 or in the exit protocol (its next ticket will be read after `Ticket[r] ≥ v`), at line 15 with a prospective ticket `> v`, or it holds a ticket with `(v, r) < (Ticket[q], q)`. `U s v r` is the set of processes that are not safe for `(v, r)`. This replaces the interval reasoning of the paper (Lemma 3, Claims 3.1–3.2 and the "CS-qualified" induction of the thesis' Lemma 5.14) by inductive invariants: * `s1`: a process `p` in the waiting loop only removed safe processes from its predecessor set; * `s3`: a captured process was captured with a pair `(v, r)`, `v > Ticket[p]`, for which fewer than `k` processes are unsafe; * `s4`: a process past the loop has fewer than `k` unsafe processes; * `s5`: every capture value `Capture[r][x]` was written by `r` past its loop. `U s v r` can only shrink as long as `v ≤ Ticket[r]`. -/
