import Papers.PODC2010.KBakery.Progress /-! # Starvation freedom (Lemma 5 of the paper; Lemma 5.5 of the thesis) *If a non-faulty process `p` is in the trying protocol and at most `k − 1` processes crash, then `p` eventually enters the CS.* Proved for both variants. Proof structure (following the paper): among the non-crashed processes that stay in the trying protocol forever, take `m` with the smallest final `(ticket, id)`. Every other non-crashed process `q` eventually keeps `Want[q][m]` "good" for `m` forever (`(Ticket[m], m) < (Want[q][m], q)`): * a stuck `q` has a larger final pair; * a `q` that takes finitely many steps rests in the NCS (`Want = ∞`); * any other `q` leaves the trying protocol infinitely often and, after `m`'s ticket is fixed, its next passage chooses a ticket larger than `m`'s (`reach_good`). Then the next full scan of `m` removes all non-crashed processes from its predecessor set, which then has fewer than `k` elements, so `m` leaves the loop and enters the CS — a contradiction. -/
