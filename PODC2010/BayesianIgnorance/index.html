Bayesian Ignorance — PODC 2010

Noga Alon, Yuval Emek, Michal Feldman, Moshe Tennenholtz. Bayesian Ignorance. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835785. Formalized version: the full version of May 9, 2010 (17 pp., with appendix; no arXiv version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The numbered results we checked hold: Observations 2.1–2.2, Lemmas 3.1, 3.2, 3.3 ("ignorance is bliss"), 3.6, 3.7, 3.8, Proposition 4.1, Lemma 4.2 and the reduction of Lemma 3.5. Rosenthal's potential fails with the paper's ∞ costs but works on connecting profiles, which saves existence of equilibria and Lemma 3.8, and the other mistakes are local proof repairs. Lemma 3.4, the online lower bound behind Lemma 3.5 and the directed variants of Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 End of Section 2; proof of Lemma 3.8 (Rosenthal's potential for Bayesian NCS games)

The claim that Q(s) = ∑_t p(t) ∑_e c(e) H(|{i : e ∈ s_i(t_i)}|) is a Bayesian potential is false under the paper's definitions, where actions are arbitrary edge sets and an agent whose set misses her path pays ∞.

Impact. The identity holds between profiles in which every agent connects her terminals, which is what the paper evidently means (in the NCS literature strategies are paths). Existence of equilibria and Lemma 3.8 (best-eq^P ≤ H(k) · opt^P) survive with that restriction (exists_isBayesEq, lemma_3_8), although the proof of Lemma 3.8 takes a global minimiser of Q, which is the empty profile.

Details

A deviation from a connecting edge set to a non-connecting one raises the agent's cost by ∞ but changes Q by a finite amount, so C_i(s) − C_i(s') = Q(s) − Q(s') fails. The global minimiser of this Q is the all-empty profile (Q = 0), in which every agent with a non-trivial type pays ∞; it is not an equilibrium. Lean counterexample on the one-agent, one-edge network 0 → 1: RosenthalCounterexample.no_finite_bayesPotential (no finite-valued function is a Bayesian potential) and RosenthalCounterexample.empty_minimises_not_isBayesEq.

Repair

The per-state Rosenthal identity between two connecting profiles (payment_add_potential_update) gives the potential identity between feasible profiles (every agent connects whenever possible); a minimiser of Q over feasible profiles is a Bayesian equilibrium (isBayesEq_of_isMin, exists_isBayesEq). For Lemma 3.8, minimise Q over feasible profiles; turn an optimal profile into a feasible one without changing it on the support of the prior; the case of an unreachable destination of positive probability (opt^P = ∞) is trivial.

✏️ Slips

  • The appendix proofs of Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7 are swapped: the "proof of Lemma 3.6" shows worst-eq^P/worst-eq^C = O(1/k), which is Lemma 3.7's claim, and the "proof of Lemma 3.7" shows Ω(k), which is Lemma 3.6's claim. Each is proved by the other's construction (lemma_3_6, lemma_3_7; the two constructions are worstConstructionA and worstConstructionB). In the "3.6" game every Bayesian equilibrium costs 3/2 + ε while the underlying game with agent k+1 at v has a Nash equilibrium of cost k+2, so worst-eq^P ≤ 3/2 + ε and worst-eq^C ≥ (k+2)/2. In the "3.7" game a Bayesian equilibrium costs k + 2 while worst-eq^C = O(1).

  • "Unique" equilibria in the proofs of Lemmas 3.2, 3.3 and the appendix are unique only up to zero-cost edges and strategies at types of probability 0. In Lemma 3.3 agents may add zero-cost edges (z, y_j); the equilibrium cost 1 + ε is unique, which suffices.

  • The "Alternatively" conditional form of Bayesian equilibrium in Section 2 is not equivalent to the ex-ante one when an agent's ex-ante cost is ∞; the equivalence holds when the cost is finite (Cslib.GameTheory.BayesianGame.IsBayesEq.cond_le).

  • Proof of Lemma 3.7: the unique Nash equilibrium when agent k+1 stays at u has agents 1, …, k buy the edge (u, w), not (u, v).

  • Proof of Lemma 3.7: the cost of an agent under s is 1 + 2/k − 1/k², not 1 + 2/k − 2/k². The chosen range 2/k − 1/k² < ε is exactly what the correct value needs.

Typos

  • Proof of Lemma 3.2: the prior is not said to be uniform, and u_ℓ, u_p are written for v_ℓ, w_p.

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 3.3 · Appendix, "Proof of Lemma 3.6" · Lemma 3.2, proof

🔧 Lemma 3.3 (the equilibrium analysis on Anshelevich's graph)

The proof never fixes ε, and it replaces the argument that s is the Bayesian equilibrium by "by induction on i".

Impact. The lemma stands; repaired (Lemma33.lemma_3_3, Lemma33.worstEqP_le).

Details

Agent i joins (x, z) only if ε < 1/(2i+1), so the claimed equilibrium needs ε < 1/(2k−1); for larger ε it is not an equilibrium. The induction must also rule out agents buying other agents' direct edges.

Repair

For ε(2k − 1) < 1 we show by strong induction on the agent index that every Bayesian equilibrium has all agents on (x, z), using that no agent buys a positive-cost edge she does not need (not_isBayesEq_of_removable); hence worst-eq^P ≤ 1 + ε. Every Nash equilibrium of the state where agent k stays at x costs ≥ H(k−1) (hnum_le_stateCost).

🔧 Appendix, "Proof of Lemma 3.6" (uniqueness of the Bayesian equilibrium)

The uniqueness claim is only argued against the all-via-v profile; profiles with some agents on each route have to be excluded too.

Impact. The bound on worst-eq^P needs all equilibria; it stands, since a simple share comparison excludes the mixed profiles (triA_socialCost).

🔧 Lemma 3.2, proof

"Agent i buys the right edge with probability 1/m" should be 1/(m+1) (a point lies on m+1 lines), and K(s) = m(1 − 1/m) forgets agent k's edge and holds only as a lower bound.

Impact. The conclusion Ω(k) is unchanged (Lemma32.lemma_3_2).

Repair

The correct bound is opt^P ≥ 1 + m²/(m+1) ≥ k − 1.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • A Bayesian game: finitely many agents, finite action and type spaces, a common prior p on type profiles, pure strategies s_i : T_i → A_i, ex-ante costs C_i(s) = ∑_t p(t) C_{i,t}(s(t)), pure Bayesian equilibria (no profitable unilateral deviation in ex-ante cost) and pure Nash equilibria of the underlying complete-information games. No randomness other than the prior; no dynamics.

  • Costs are ℝ≥0∞-valued (the NCS cost is ∞ when an agent's edge set misses her path); expectations are finite sums with 0 · ∞ = 0. opt, best-eq, worst-eq are ⨅/⨆ over ℝ≥0∞ (an empty best-eq is ∞, an empty worst-eq is 0; Bayesian NCS games always have an equilibrium, exists_isBayesEq).

  • NCS games on an abstract network (step e u v, non-negative edge costs) covering directed and undirected (multi)graphs; types T_i = V × V, actions A_i = 2^E, fair cost sharing, as in the paper.

  • Ratios are stated multiplicatively (worst-eq^P ≤ k · opt^C), asymptotic claims with explicit constants and explicit vertex counts.

  • Section 4 uses its own model with strictly positive real costs and distributions in stdSimplex, as in the paper.

Proposition 4.1 is proved in the stronger form that the RoE and EoR bounds are equivalent for every r (a reparametrisation of the simplex), which avoids the appendix's tacit assumption that sup_p min_i is attained.
