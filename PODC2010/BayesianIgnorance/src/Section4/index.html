import Mathlib /-! # Bayesian Ignorance, Section 4: public random bits as a substitute for the common prior Formalization of Section 4 (and the appendix proof of Proposition 4.1) of [Alon–Emek–Feldman–Tennenholtz, *Bayesian Ignorance*, PODC 2010]. ## Setting A *cost tuple* `φ = ⟨k, {A_i}, {T_i}, {C_{i,t}}⟩` consists of finitely many agents `ι`, finite nonempty action sets `A i`, finite nonempty type sets `T i` and strictly positive costs `C i t a`. A (pure) strategy profile is `s : ∀ i, T i → A i`; on the type profile `t` it has social cost `K s t = ∑ i, C i t (s · (t ·))`, and `Kmin t = min_s K s t`. Distributions are points of `stdSimplex ℝ _`. * `RoE(φ) = r` (ratio of expectations): `r` is the least real such that every common prior `p` admits a strategy profile `s` with `E_p[K s] / E_p[Kmin] ≤ r`. * `EoR(φ) = r` (expectation of ratios): `r` is the least real such that every `p` admits `s` with `E_p[K s / Kmin] ≤ r`. ## Main results * `Podc.BayesianIgnorance.proposition_4_1` — Proposition 4.1 (`r* = r**`), for a positive matrix `M` and a positive vector `v`. We prove the stronger pointwise statement `roeBound_iff_eorBound : RoEBound M v r ↔ EoRBound M v r` for **every** `r`, via the bijection `p ↦ p · v / ⟨p, v⟩` of the simplex (inverse `p ↦ (p / v) / ⟨p, 1/v⟩`), which maps the ratio of expectations at `(p, i)` exactly to the expectation of ratios at `(p', i)`. We also prove that the minimum in the definition of `r*`/`r**` exists (`exists_isLeast_eorBound`). * `Podc.BayesianIgnorance.CostTuple.roe_eq_eor` — `RoE(φ) = EoR(φ)` (both exist and coincide). * `Podc.BayesianIgnorance.CostTuple.lemma_4_2` — Lemma 4.2: a single mixed strategy profile `q ∈ Δ(S)` (chosen with public random bits, without knowing the prior) achieves the ratio `RoE(φ)` against every common prior, and no `q` achieves any smaller ratio. The abstract matrix versions are `exists_mixed_roeBound` and `exists_lt_mixed_ratio`. ## Notes on the paper's proofs * The appendix proof of Proposition 4.1 picks a prior `p` "such that for all `i` the ratio is `≥ r*`", i.e. it silently assumes that `sup_p min_i ratio(i, p)` is attained. This is true (continuity on a compact set) but not argued; our proof avoids it entirely. * The paper says "`r*` is the minimum real such that …" without showing that the minimum exists; we prove it (`exists_isLeast_eorBound`): the set of admissible `r` is a nonempty, bounded below, closed set (an intersection of finite unions of closed rays). * The paper's index sets `[m]`, `[n]` (and the type/action sets) must be nonempty; otherwise `r*` does not exist. We assume `Nonempty` explicitly, and also `Nonempty ι` (at least one agent, `k ≥ 1`), so that `K(s,t) = ∑_i C_{i,t}(·) > 0`. * Proposition 4.1 only needs `v > 0` for the equivalence `RoEBound ↔ EoRBound`; `M > 0` is used only to show that the minimum exists (boundedness from below). * In the proof of Lemma 4.2, the step "it suffices to consider priors concentrated on single type profiles" is exactly the summation in `exists_mixed_roeBound`: from `∑_s q(s) K(s,t) ≤ r · Kmin(t)` for every `t` one gets, multiplying by `p(t)` and summing, the bound for every prior `p`. * The minimax step uses Sion's minimax theorem from Mathlib (`Sion.exists_isSaddlePointOn`) for the bilinear payoff `f(q, p) = ∑_s q(s) ∑_t p(t) K(s,t)/Kmin(t)` on `Δ(S) × Δ(T)`. -/ namespace Podc.BayesianIgnorance open
