Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Bayesian Ignorance — PODC 2010.

Paper: Alon, Emek, Feldman, Tennenholtz, Bayesian Ignorance, PODC 2010 (full version of May 9, 2010, 17 pp., with appendix).

Model

  1. Costs are ℝ≥0∞-valued (Cslib.GameTheory.BayesianGame). The paper's general model allows real (possibly negative) costs C_{i,t} : A → ℝ; its NCS games have costs in ℝ≥0 ∪ {∞}. All quantities the paper studies (ratios of social costs) only make sense for non-negative costs, and every result of Section 3 is about NCS games, so nothing is lost. Section 4 uses its own real-valued model (positive costs), as the paper does (CostTuple).

  2. Expectations are finite sums ∑_t p(t) · X(t) in ℝ≥0∞ with 0 · ∞ = 0: type profiles of prior probability 0 never matter. The paper's conditional form of the equilibrium condition (E[X_i(s) | t_i] ≤ …) is replaced by the ex-ante form (the paper's primary definition); the conditional form is derived when the agent's ex-ante cost is finite (IsBayesEq.cond_le). With infinite costs at another type of the same agent the two forms are not equivalent (⊤ ≤ ⊤); the paper calls them equivalent. This only matters in the degenerate case where some destination of positive probability is unreachable (then opt^C = ∞ and all of Section 3 is trivial). Not a mistake of any numbered statement.

  3. Ratios are stated multiplicatively, e.g. worst-eq^P ≤ k · opt^C instead of worst-eq^P / opt^C ≤ k. This is the paper's meaning when the denominator is in (0, ∞) and avoids 0/0, ∞/∞.

  4. Minima/maxima over equilibria are ⨅/⨆ in ℝ≥0∞ (best-eq = ⊤, worst-eq = 0 if no equilibrium exists). The paper restricts attention to games with pure equilibria; we prove existence for every Bayesian NCS game (exists_isBayesEq), and Observation 2.2 assumes an equilibrium exists.

  5. Exact potentials in additive form (C_i(s) + Q(s') = C_i(s') + Q(s)), equivalent to the paper's difference form for finite values.

  6. Networks are abstract: a step relation step e u v and costs c : E → ℝ≥0 (Cslib.GameTheory.Network); this covers directed and undirected (multi)graphs at once, so Lemmas 3.1 and 3.8 are proved for both. "a_i contains an (x_i, y_i)-path" is reachability inside a_i (Connects); the empty set connects x to x.

  7. Type spaces T_i = V × V, action spaces A_i = 2^E, exactly as in the paper.

  8. Asymptotic statements (Ω(k), O(1/log k), Θ(k) vertices) are made explicit: for every k₀ there is k ≥ k₀ and a game with explicit vertex count and an explicit inequality with explicit constants. The paper's "k-agent" games of Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7 have k + 1 agents (as in the paper's proofs).

Section 2

  1. Observation 2.1 is proved verbatim (additive form, observation_2_1). Observation 2.2 (observation_2_2) is proved for games with a pure Bayesian equilibrium, as stated; we also prove opt^C ≤ best-eq^C ≤ worst-eq^C (the latter when every underlying game of positive probability has a pure Nash equilibrium).

  2. The Rosenthal potential of Bayesian NCS games (end of Section 2) is not a potential as written. The paper defines the cost of an agent whose edge set does not contain her path as ∞, with A_i = 2^E, and then claims (via Observation 2.1) that Q(s) = ∑_t p(t) ∑_e c(e) H(|{i : e ∈ s_i(t_i)}|) is a Bayesian potential. A deviation from a connecting to a non-connecting edge set changes the agent's cost by ∞ but Q by a finite amount, so the identity C_i(s) − C_i(s') = Q(s) − Q(s') fails. Worse, the global minimiser of this Q is the all-empty profile (Q = 0), which is not an equilibrium. The intended restriction is to path-containing actions, standard in the NCS literature where strategies are paths (🔧). Repair: the identity holds between feasible profiles (every agent connects whenever possible), and a minimiser of Q over feasible profiles is a Bayesian equilibrium (isBayesEq_of_isMin, exists_isBayesEq), via the per-state identity payment_add_potential_update. Lean counterexample (one agent, one edge 0 → 1 of cost 1, point prior): RosenthalCounterexample.no_finite_bayesPotential (no finite-valued function is a Bayesian potential of that game) and RosenthalCounterexample.empty_minimises_not_isBayesEq (the empty profile minimises Q and is not an equilibrium). The same applies to the per-state sentence "this potential function turns out to be q(a)" (cited from [4], whose strategies are paths).

Section 3

  1. Lemma 3.1 (lemma_3_1): proved as stated (multiplicatively), for directed and undirected graphs. The paper's proof uses the conditional equilibrium condition per type; we deviate at all types simultaneously to cheapest connecting sets, which needs no conditioning (see item 2).

  2. Lemma 3.2 (Lemma32.lemma_3_2, Lemma32.lemma_3_2_prime): proved, in the stronger form stated by the paper (opt^P ≥ (k−1) · worst-eq^C, with worst-eq^C = 1), plus the consequences (k−1) · opt^C ≤ opt^P and (k−1) · best-eq^C ≤ best-eq^P; 2(k−1)² + (k−1) + 1 vertices.

    • We use affine planes over ZMod q, q prime (the paper: any prime power); enough for "for every k₀". The prior is uniform over (line, permutation), as the paper intends (it does not say "uniform" explicitly).

    • Proof slip (typo-level): "agent i buys the right edge with probability 1/m" should be 1/(m+1) (a point lies on m+1 lines); "K(s) = m(1 − 1/m) = m − 1" forgets agent k's edge and is only a lower bound (agents may buy more). Correct bound: opt^P ≥ 1 + m²/(m+1) ≥ m = k − 1.

    • "The unique Nash equilibrium" is unique only up to zero-cost edges (v_ℓ, w_p); every Nash equilibrium costs exactly 1 (isNashEq_inl), which is what is needed.

  3. Lemma 3.3 (Lemma33.lemma_3_3): proved, with worst-eq^P ≤ 1 + ε, best-eq^C ≥ H(k−1)/2 and ln k · worst-eq^P ≤ 4 · best-eq^C; graph with k + 1 vertices (Θ(k)).

    • The paper never fixes ε (it appears only in Figure 1). The claimed equilibrium needs ε < 1/(2i+1) for the agent i joining (x, z), i.e. ε < 1/(2k−1); we assume ε(2k−1) < 1 (worstEqP_le) and ε > 0 (half_hnum_le_bestEqC). For larger ε the profile is not an equilibrium. Proof gap (🔧).

    • "s is the unique Bayesian equilibrium" is false literally (✏️): agents may additionally buy zero-cost edges (z, y_j). All equilibria have the same social cost 1 + ε, which is what we prove (and what the lemma needs). Same for "the unique Nash equilibrium" when agent k stays at x: unique up to zero-cost edges; we prove every Nash equilibrium there costs ≥ H(k−1).

    • lemma_3_3 also states best-eq^C(G) ≠ ∞, so the ratio bound is not satisfied trivially.

    • The paper's "induction on i" is made precise: strong induction on the agent index, using that no agent buys an unneeded positive-cost edge (not_isBayesEq_of_removable).

    • The claim of Section 3 / the introduction that in this game "the worst Bayesian equilibrium achieves the expected cost of the globally optimal outcome" and "opt^C(G) = worst-eq^P(G) = O(1)" is proved for k ≥ 3 (ignorance_is_bliss: worst-eq^P = opt^C = 1 + ε). For k = 2 it fails (opt^C = 1 + ε/2), a case irrelevant to the asymptotic claim.

  4. Lemma 3.4 (opt^P/opt^C = O(log n) in undirected graphs, via FRT tree embeddings and Gupta's Steiner-point removal) is not formalized (FRT is not in Mathlib/CSLib).

  5. Lemma 3.5: only the paper's reduction is formalized (Lemma35.lemma_3_5): for every distribution q over request sequences, opt^P(G^q) ≥ α · opt^C(G^q) whenever every deterministic online Steiner tree algorithm (valid on the support of q) pays at least α · E_q[OPT] in expectation. The cited distributional Imase–Waxman Ω(log n) lower bound on the diamond graph is a hypothesis (hLB), not proved. Request sequences are Fin n → Option V (holes allowed), a harmless generalization of V^{≤n}; the reduction holds for any network. We prove opt^C(G^q) ≤ E_q[OPT], the direction the reduction needs. The remark after Lemma 3.5 (best-eq version on the diamond graph, Euclidean Ω(log k / log log k) via Alon–Azar) is not formalized.

  6. Lemmas 3.6 and 3.7 (lemma_3_6, lemma_3_7, Lemma36.lean): both proved, stated as (k+1) · worst-eq^C ≤ 4 · worst-eq^P with 0 < worst-eq^C, worst-eq^P ≠ ∞ (Ω(k)), resp. (k+1) · worst-eq^P ≤ 4 · worst-eq^C with 0 < worst-eq^P, worst-eq^C ≠ ∞ (O(1/k)), on the 3-vertex undirected triangle G_worst, with k + 1 agents (k ≥ 3, resp. k ≥ 2; the paper states no range).

    • The appendix proofs are swapped (✏️, statements true): the "proof of Lemma 3.6" gives worst-eq^P = 3/2 + ε and worst-eq^C ≥ (k+2)/2, i.e. ratio O(1/k) = Lemma 3.7 (worstConstructionA); the "proof of Lemma 3.7" gives worst-eq^P ≥ k + 2 and (1−1/k)(1+ε) ≤ worst-eq^C ≤ (1−1/k)(1+ε) + (k+3+ε)/k, i.e. ratio Ω(k) = Lemma 3.6 (worstConstructionB). Each lemma is proved by the other's construction.

    • "Proof of 3.6": uniqueness of the Bayesian equilibrium is argued only against the profile where all agents route via v; mixed profiles must be excluded too (done in triA_socialCost). The equilibrium is unique on types of positive probability; off-support strategies are arbitrary.

    • "Proof of 3.7": the unique Nash equilibrium at destination u buys (u, w), not "(u, v)" (typo); the agent cost is 1 + 2/k − 1/k², not 1 + 2/k − 2/k² (arithmetic slip; the paper's range 2/k − 1/k² < ε is exactly what the correct value needs, and ≤ suffices).

    • Directed variants ("a trivial modification") are not formalized.

  7. Lemma 3.8 (lemma_3_8): proved as stated. The paper's proof minimises the potential over all strategy profiles, whose minimiser is the empty profile (item 10); we minimise over feasible profiles, and handle unreachable destinations (then opt^P = ∞). Proof repair (🔧), consequence of item 10.

  8. Table 1 universal bounds derived from the lemmas: table_upper_k (opt^P/opt^C, best-eq^P/best-eq^C, worst-eq^P/worst-eq^C are ≤ k), bestEqC_le_hnum_mul_bestEqP (best-eq^P/best-eq^C ≥ 1/H(k), via the price of stability H(k) of Anshelevich et al., which we re-derive as the point-mass case of Lemma 3.8: bestNash_le_hnum_mul_opt) and worstEqC_le_card_mul_worstEqP (worst-eq^P/worst-eq^C ≥ 1/k, via the point-mass case of Lemma 3.1, worstNash_le_card_mul_opt). The O(min{k, log k log n}) entry needs Lemma 3.4 (not formalized).

Section 4

  1. Proposition 4.1 (proposition_4_1): proved, together with the stronger pointwise statement roeBound_iff_eorBound (for every r, the RoE bound holds iff the EoR bound holds) via the bijection p ↦ p·v/⟨p,v⟩ of the simplex. The appendix proof assumes that sup_p min_i ratio is attained ("by definition, there exists some p such that for all i the ratio is ≥ r*"), true by compactness but not argued; and "r* is the minimum real such that …" presupposes that the minimum exists, which we prove (exists_isLeast_eorBound). Routine remarks. Nonempty index sets (and at least one agent) are assumed explicitly. Only v > 0 is needed for the equivalence; M > 0 is used for the existence of the minimum.

  2. RoE(φ) = EoR(φ) (CostTuple.roe_eq_eor, isRoE_iff_isEoR): proved.

  3. Lemma 4.2 (CostTuple.lemma_4_2): proved, using Sion's minimax theorem from Mathlib in place of von Neumann's. The existence part only needs that the bound r holds, not that it is least. The remark that the conclusion also holds "in the limit" when some costs are 0 (with 0/0 = 1) is not formalized.
