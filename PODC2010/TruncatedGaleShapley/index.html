Almost Stable Matchings in Constant Time — PODC 2010

Patrik Floréen, Petteri Kaski, Valentin Polishchuk, Jukka Suomela. Brief announcement: Almost stable matchings in constant time. PODC 2010, pp. 281–282. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835765. Formalized version: the full paper Almost Stable Matchings in Constant Time, arXiv 0812.4893 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All three theorems hold with the paper's constants: truncated Gale–Shapley gives an ε-stable matching in T ≤ 4 + 2Δ²/ε rounds (also with ties), a (2+ε)-approximate maximum-weight matching in T ≤ 4 + 2Δ/ε rounds, and a constant-query estimator of the size of a stable matching. Lemmas 1–5 are true. Lemma 6 is false as stated for Δ = 1, i = 1, and the proofs of Theorems 1 and 2 inherit this; it needs i ≥ 2, as in Lemmas 4 and 5, which its proof uses, and this condition changes no constant.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 6 for Δ = 1, and its use in the proofs of Theorems 1 and 2

Lemma 6 claims f_i(R) ≤ γ w_i(B) for all γ > 0 and i ≥ 1 + (Δ-1)/γ, which for Δ = 1 allows i = 1, where the bound fails already on a single edge K₂ (lemma_6_false_at_one).

Impact. The intent is evident: the proof combines Lemmas 4 and 5, which are stated only for i ≥ 2, and with the extra hypothesis i ≥ 2 the lemma holds (Bicoloured.lemma_6). No constant of Theorems 1–3 changes.

Details

After round 1 nothing is matched (w_1(B) = 0), while every red node has a pending proposal (f_1(R) > 0). K₂ has maximum degree 1 and no isolated nodes. For Δ ≥ 2 the hypothesis forces i ≥ 2. The proofs of Theorems 1 and 2 inherit the mistake: for Δ = 1 they allow i = 1, and M_1 = ∅ is neither ε-stable nor an approximation.

Repair

Add i ≥ 2 to Lemma 6. In Theorems 1 and 2, whose statements are true, choosing i = max(2, ⌈…⌉) keeps T = 2i within the claimed bounds (theorem_1, theorem_2).

✏️ Slips

  • Proof of Lemma 5: "w({r,b}) < w_j(b)" should be ≤. The weights respect the preferences only weakly, and with w ≡ 1 equality holds. Only ≤ is needed.

Typos

  • §6.3 treats Y (the matched nodes among the first N red samples) as a binomial variable without saying that the analysis conditions on the sample containing N red nodes. The bound is correct. We prove it with a martingale (exponential-moment) argument over the sequential sampling. (card_firstHits_abs_sub_ge_le in Podc/Probability/Finite/MultiplicativeChernoff.lean)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Distributed model (Theorems 1, 2): CSLib's anonymous port-numbering model (Cslib.PortNumbering). It is synchronous and deterministic, with no failures and no identifiers. A node knows its degree, its colour (red/blue input) and T (built into the algorithm). Its ports are numbered by preference (port 0 = favourite). The graph is any simple port-numbered graph whose input colouring is proper; isolated nodes are allowed. One GS round is two communication rounds (blue turn, red turn), so T = 2i. Messages are full local states (Algorithm.ofLocal), but each node only uses the information the 'propose', 'accept', 'reject' and 'break' messages of §4 carry. The output is the port of the partner. For Theorem 1 with ties the paper's "analogous" hides nothing: the ports refine the weak preferences, and every edge unstable for the weak preferences is unstable for the refinement (theorem_1_ties).

  • Centralised analysis: Bicoloured V is a finite bicoloured graph with strict preferences, given as ranks that are injective on the neighbours. run I i is the state at the end of round i, a global synchronous transition. dec_localRun proves that after 2i communication rounds the port-level run decodes exactly to run I i.

  • Theorem 2: each node's input is its colour and the weights (nonnegative reals) of its edges, given per port. The node ranks its ports by decreasing weight, breaking ties by port number, and then runs the Theorem 1 algorithm. M* ranges over all matchings.

  • Theorem 3: randomness is uniform on (Fin N → V) × (Fin K → V) (sampling with replacement, probability by counting), and the algorithm knows n. The oracle is not modelled interactively. Instead we prove the three facts the query count rests on: locality (whether r is matched in M_j depends only on the local data within distance 2j), the bound < 3(Δ-1)^{2j} on the size of such a ball, and the arithmetic bound on N + K + N·3(Δ-1)^{2j}. M is the stable matching M_∞ reached by Gale–Shapley, as in the paper's proof.

For the query count of Theorem 3, the invariance of the run under order-preserving re-ranking is argued informally and not stated in Lean, and the oracle is not modelled interactively.

Trust. No sorry; all listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The distributed model is CSLib's Cslib.PortNumbering. Hypotheses are non-vacuous: k2 (Counterexample.lean) and the path and triangle examples are concrete instances. New shared code, all CSLib candidates: Podc/Probability/Finite/MultiplicativeChernoff.lean, a multiplicative Chernoff bound by counting with a "first N hits" variant, and Podc/Foundations/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/BallCard.lean, the ball size in bounded-degree graphs. Paper-local CSLib candidates: the incomplete-preference stable-matching notions (PrefGraph, PrefGraph.forced), and card_filter_fst/card_filter_snd (Theorem3.lean).
