import Papers.PODC2010.MultiVersionSTM.Commute /-! # Theorem 2: MV-permissiveness rules out weak disjoint-access parallelism Section 6.1 of Perelman–Fan–Keidar (PODC 2010). The proof runs the two schedules of Figure 3, * `H1 = w₁(o₂) c₁ r₂(o₁) w₃(o₁) c₃ r₂(o₂) c₂`, * `H2 = r₂(o₁) w₃(o₁) c₃ w₁(o₂) c₁ r₂(o₂) c₂`, where `T₁, T₃` are update transactions and `T₂` is read-only, run by three distinct threads. 1. MV-permissiveness forces every step to succeed (only `T₁` and `T₃` could abort, but they access different objects, and `T₂` is read-only); since reads return written versions, the first read of `H2` returns the initial version of `o₁`. 2. Weak DAP, applied to the prefix `α = w₁ c₁ r₂(o₁) w₃ c₃` of `H1`, in which `T₁` is disjoint-access with `T₂` and with `T₃`: the base steps of `T₁` do not contend with those of `T₂, T₃`. 3. Hence (`STM.run_comm`) the steps of `T₁` commute with those of `T₂, T₃`: after the prefix `β = r₂(o₁) w₃ c₃ w₁ c₁` of `H2` the configuration is the same as after `α`, and `r₂(o₂)` returns the same version in `H1` and in `H2`. 4. In `H1`, strict serializability forces `r₂(o₂)` to return `T₁`'s version (`fig3_H1`). 5. Then `H2` is not strictly serializable (`fig3_H2`): `T₂ < T₃` (it read the initial `o₁`), `T₁ < T₂` (it read `T₁`'s `o₂`) and `T₃ < T₁` (real-time order). **On the paper's step 2.** The paper derives the absence of contention in `H1` from an auxiliary history `H3` in which `T₂` commits right after its first step, and claims that `thr(T₃)` does not distinguish `H1` from `H3` after `s₃`. This is not right as stated: in `H3` the commit of `T₂` precedes `s₃`, and its base steps may change base objects that `s₃` reads (and the step `s₃` itself may then differ). The detour is unnecessary: in the prefix `α` of `H1` itself, `T₁` is disjoint-access with `T₂` and with `T₃` (every transaction of `α` accesses a single object), so weak DAP applies to `α` directly.
