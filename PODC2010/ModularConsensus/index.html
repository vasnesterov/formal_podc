A Modular Approach to Shared-Memory Consensus — PODC 2010

James Aspnes. A modular approach to shared-memory consensus, with applications to the probabilistic-write model. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835802. Formalized version: the author's full version of May 14, 2011 (18 pp., with proofs; no arXiv version); numbering follows it. The author's erratum (ratifiers are Gafni's adopt-commit objects) only fixes credit. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. The only mistakes are two slips in intermediate sentences: the composition lemmas (Lemmas 1–3, Theorem 4), the safety of the conciliator/adopt-commit consensus construction, the multi-valued adopt-commit object (Theorems 8 and 10), the quorum optimality via Bollobás's theorem (Theorem 9) and the new probabilistic-write conciliator (Theorem 7: agreement probability (1 − e^{−1/4})/4, 2⌈lg n⌉ + 5 individual work, O(n) expected total work) all hold as stated. The paper's end-to-end consensus result was not fully checked: the probabilistic part of Section 4 (termination with probability 1, the expected-cost bound, Theorem 5) is proved only conditionally, because the per-stage bound "a reached conciliator fails with probability ≤ 1 − δ" is a hypothesis, not derived from Theorem 7 (see Model).

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • §5.2: "the loop is never executed for more than ⌈lg n⌉ + 1 iterations" should be ⌈lg n⌉ + 2 (= ⌈lg 2n⌉ + 1): the write probability 2^k/(2n) reaches 1 only at k = ⌈lg n⌉ + 1. For n = 1 the loop runs twice (attempts_tight). The paper's bound 2⌈lg 2n⌉ + 3 = 2⌈lg n⌉ + 5 uses the right count and is attained.

  • §6.1, sentence before Theorem 8: the prose condition "R_{v′} includes some register written in W_v that is not written in W_{v′}" is not sufficient, since it does not exclude R_v ∩ W_v ≠ ∅, and then a process sees its own write and convergence fails. The precise condition is printed in Theorem 8, and we follow it (AC.theorem8_safety).

Model.

  • Adopt-commit objects (Theorems 8 and 10) use CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl). Processes are deterministic and there may be any number of them, even infinitely many. Memory consists of atomic multi-writer registers, and the scheduler chooses an arbitrary interleaving. A crash is a process that stops taking steps. Safety (validity, coherence, convergence) is proved for every finite, possibly partial, one-shot execution (Impl.Solves). Termination is CSLib's wait-freedom, and individual work is the number of register accesses of an operation.

  • Deciding objects and composition (Section 3) are modelled by the input/output pairs of their executions (DObj). Executions may be partial; inputs are P → Option V and outputs P → Option (Decision × V). (X; Y) combines an execution of X with an execution of Y whose participants adopted from X. Composition is also built operationally on CSLib Impls, together with a projection theorem (SeqCompose.lean, ComposeBridge.lean).

  • Theorem 7 (probabilistic-write model) uses a separate model with one register r : Option V and n processes. The k-th write attempt of a process takes effect with probability min 1 (2^k/(2n)). The coin is flipped when the scheduled write step is taken. The adversary is any function of the full history, including all coins flipped so far, and of the current configuration. It may also idle or stop processes. This is at least as strong as the paper's location-oblivious adversary. Executions are Mathlib PMFs.

  • The probabilistic termination of the consensus construction (Section 4) is formalized abstractly. The deterministic part is proved for executions: if a conciliator's outputs agree, every process commits in the next adopt-commit object. The probabilistic part is a lemma about measures. It assumes that a reached conciliator stage fails with probability at most 1 − δ (μ(E (k+1)) ≤ (1 − δ) μ(E k)) and that the cost of an execution is bounded stage-wise by constants T(A), T(C), and derives termination with probability 1, at most 1/δ expected stages, and the cost bounds. No probabilistic semantics of composed randomized objects is built, so these hypotheses are not discharged for any concrete conciliator, in particular not for Theorem 7's (whose agreement bound is proved for a single, stand-alone instance with fixed inputs). Theorem 5 and the Section 4.1 termination claim are therefore verified only modulo this step, which the paper also treats informally.

Proof remarks.

  • Theorem 8. The paper's timing argument is right. We prove it with an invariant on configurations, AC.Inv. Its key clause is Inv.k2: suppose p is executing line 10 with preference v and has read a set S ⊆ R_v of registers as 0. Then no process with input ≠ v that read ⊥ from proposal has a register of S in its write quorum. Once S = R_v, the hypothesis W_w ∩ R_v ≠ ∅ for w ≠ v shows that every writer of proposal has input v. Termination needs finite (in Lean: uniformly bounded) quorums, which the paper takes for granted.

  • Theorem 7 is proved with a potential function (a submartingale) instead of the paper's "all-failures path" argument. This avoids the cases the paper skips: the index t with ∑_{i ≤ t} pᵢ ≥ 1/4 may not exist, because the adversary may stop scheduling, and the min 1 cap in 2^k/(2n). The paper's two estimates appear in the proof as attemptProb_le_sum and one_sub_sum_le_prod.

  • Section 4. The paper's "with probability 1 some Cᵢ produces agreement" requires each conciliator's δ-guarantee to hold conditionally on the history at the moment its first participant arrives. Its "for any adversary strategy" naturally covers this, but we keep it as the hypothesis of stages_prob.

Not formalized. Theorem 6 (CoinConciliator from a weak shared coin; "does not give any new results"), the noisy/priority schedulers of §4.2, the cheap-collect variant (§6.2 item 4), the Θ lower bound in the abstract's "lg m + Θ(log log m)" (the upper bound and the optimality among quorum systems are proved), and the abstract's end-to-end consensus bound for the probabilistic-write model (O(log m + log n) individual work, O(n log m) total work): it combines Theorems 7 and 10 through Section 4, whose probabilistic step is only assumed (see Model). Open points: termination with probability 1, the E[T(U)] bound and Theorem 5 are proved only under the per-stage failure hypothesis, and it was not checked that Theorem 7 gives this hypothesis conditionally on the history with adaptively chosen inputs. Our cost hypothesis also bounds stage costs by constants, so conciliators with only an expected O(n) total-work bound (a Wald-type argument) are not covered.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: concrete executions of the binary AdoptCommit, one committing and one adopting (Quorums.lean); the bit-vector and middle-layer quorums satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem 8 (bit_hWR, mid_hWR); the middle-layer family attains Bollobás's bound; for Theorem 7, an adversary under which disagreement has positive probability (Impatient.disagreement_prob_pos).
