Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of A Modular Approach to Shared-Memory Consensus — PODC 2010.

J. Aspnes, A modular approach to shared-memory consensus, with applications to the probabilistic-write model, PODC 2010; full version of May 14, 2011 (author's copy, 18 pp.). The erratum note (ratifiers = Gafni's adopt-commit objects) is a credit fix with no technical content.

Model and definitions

  • Shared memory model. Procedure AdoptCommit (Theorem 8, Section 6) is formalized in CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl): deterministic processes, any type P of processes (finite or infinite, "unbounded number of processes"), atomic multi-writer registers, arbitrary interleavings. Crashes are processes that stop taking steps; every safety property is proved for every finite, possibly partial, one-shot execution (Impl.Solves), which covers crashed and still-running processes. Termination is CSLib's wait-freedom (Impl.WaitFree). Returning is a separate (non-memory) step, as in CSLib.

  • Line 10 reads with early exit. Procedure AdoptCommit reads the registers of R_preference one at a time (in Finset.toList order) and stops at the first 1. The paper only says "if rᵢ ≠ 0 for some rᵢ ∈ R_preference". Reading the remaining registers would not change the outcome; the step bound uses the worst case (all of R_preference read).

  • Specifications as input/output relations. Validity, coherence, convergence and agreement are predicates on the inputs P → Option V and outputs P → Option (Decision × V) of a (possibly partial) execution (Basic.lean). Convergence for partial executions reads "if all inputs (of the processes that invoked the object) equal v, every output given is (commit, v)", which is the paper's property applied to every prefix.

  • Composition (Section 3.3) is formalized twice:

    • relationally (DObj.seq): an execution of (X; Y) is an execution of X plus an execution of Y whose participants are processes that adopted from X, with the adopted value as input. Lemmas 1 and 3 and Theorem 4 (validity + coherence) are proved for this relation. Associativity (seq_assoc) needs the middle object to be well-formed (only processes that invoked it get an output), which every real execution is;

    • operationally, as a CSLib Impl built from two Impls (Impl.seqComp, Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/SeqCompose.lean): every valid one-shot execution of the composite projects to valid one-shot executions of the components (seqComp_project, assuming Y allows every input it can receive), so its input/output pair lies in DObj.seq (ComposeBridge.seqComp_mem_seq) and Lemmas 1, 3 and Theorem 4 transfer (seqComp_solves_weakConsensus). Only one-shot executions are projected.

  • Termination of compositions (Lemma 2) is deterministic wait-freedom with additive step bounds, for components whose termination is witnessed by a potential (strictly decreasing at each access; bounded after invocation for Y) (seqComp_waitFree, seqComp_tauSteps_le). A general WaitFree X → WaitFree Y → WaitFree (X; Y) would need projecting infinite executions and is not proved. The probabilistic "terminates with probability 1" of randomized components is not modelled at the level of objects (see below).

  • Wait-freedom of AdoptCommit is proved under a uniform bound ρ on the read-quorum sizes (always true for finitely many values, and for all quorum systems of Section 6.2). With infinitely many values of unbounded quorum sizes each operation still finishes, but CSLib's potential method needs a uniform natural-number bound. The paper's "Termination is immediate from the lack of unbounded loops" implicitly assumes finite quorums.

  • Infinite consensus chain U (Section 4.1) is formalized relationally (ChainExec): one execution per stage, linked by the adopt values. Safety (validity, agreement) is proved for any chain of weak consensus objects.

  • Probability in Section 4 is abstract. The paper's argument "with probability 1, some Cᵢ eventually produces agreement" and E[T(U)] ≤ 2T(A) + (1/δ)(T(C)+T(A)) are formalized as: (deterministic) if the outputs of a conciliator stage agree and the next stage is convergent, nobody reaches the following stage (no_next_of_agreement); (probabilistic, stages_prob, expected_cost_le, expected_cost_bounded_le) if Pr[reach stage k+1] ≤ (1-δ)·Pr[reach stage k], then termination has probability 1, the expected number of stages is ≤ 1/δ, and the expected cost bound holds. The hypothesis Pr[reach k+1] ≤ (1-δ) Pr[reach k] is the step the paper leaves informal: it needs the conciliator's agreement guarantee to hold conditionally on the history at the moment its first participant arrives, against an adversary that also controls the arrivals. This is the natural reading of "for any adversary strategy" but no probabilistic execution model of objects is built here. (No error: just not formalized at this level.) Consequences, made explicit in the README:

    • The hypothesis is not discharged for any concrete conciliator. Discharging it for Theorem 7's conciliator would need (a) a probabilistic semantics of the composed protocol and (b) Theorem 7 with adaptively chosen inputs: in U, a late process's input to Cᵢ is its adopted value from Aᵢ₋₁, which the adversary can steer after seeing coins of Cᵢ. agreement_prob fixes the inputs up front. (The potential argument does not depend on the inputs, so this extension is expected to go through; the paper does not discuss it.)

    • The cost hypothesis hT bounds the cost of each stage by constants T(A), T(C) pointwise. This covers worst-case measures (individual work of Theorem 7's conciliator, 2⌈lg n⌉ + 5) but not stage costs that are bounded only in expectation (the O(n) expected total work of Theorem 7), for which a Wald-type argument would be needed. So the "expected total work" half of Theorem 5 is covered only for conciliators with a worst-case total-work bound.

    • The abstract's combined consensus bound for the probabilistic-write model (O(log m + log n) individual, O(n log m) total work) is therefore not formalized end to end.

  • Theorem 5 is covered by expected_cost_bounded_le (fallback term (1-δ)^(k+1) T(K), slightly better than the paper's (1-δ)^k T(K) because the stages are counted from A₋₁) and exists_k_fallback_le_one (k = ⌈ln M / δ⌉ makes the fallback term ≤ 1 when T(K) ≤ M, so k = O(log n) for polynomial T(K)). The "bounded space" part is immediate (finitely many objects) and not stated separately.

  • Fast path (Sections 4.1.1, 4.2). The indistinguishability argument "p cannot distinguish this execution from one in which all processes have the same input" is formalized as: the prefix ending with p's response is an execution in which all inputs are v; convergence applies to it (fastPath, for every implementation of an adopt-commit object in CSLib's model).

Theorem 7 (ImpatientFirstMoverConciliator)

  • Separate model (ImpatientConciliator.lean): one register Option V, n ≥ 1 processes Fin n, every step one register operation; the coin of a write attempt is flipped when the step is taken; executions are PMFs of N adversary decisions (run). The adversary is an arbitrary function of the history (scheduled processes and the outcomes of all coins already flipped) and of the configuration, and may idle (crash processes). This is at least as strong as the paper's location-oblivious adversary, so the agreement bound is a stronger statement.

  • Agreement is stated for the processes that have returned after N steps, for every N (the paper's "all return values are equal").

  • Proof: a potential-function (submartingale) argument instead of the paper's "all-failures path" with index t; it covers the cases the paper's text does not mention (t need not exist if the adversary stops; the min 1 cap on 2^k/(2n)). The paper's estimates are attemptProb_le_sum, one_sub_sum_le_prod.

  • Expected total work: the explicit bound 7n (paper: O(n)).

Statement-level remarks

  • ✏️ Section 5.2, iteration count. "the loop is never executed for more than ⌈lg n⌉ + 1 iterations": the write probability 2^k/(2n) reaches 1 only at k = ⌈lg n⌉ + 1, so there can be ⌈lg n⌉ + 2 iterations (k = 0, …, ⌈lg n⌉ + 1). The paper's final bound 2⌈lg 2n⌉ + 3 = 2⌈lg n⌉ + 5 counts ⌈lg n⌉ + 2 iterations and is correct. The sentence is the paper's proof of Theorem 7's work bound ("The bounds on individual work and total work have already been established"). Nothing needs repairing: the very next formula 2⌈lg 2n⌉ + 3 is exactly 2·(⌈lg 2n⌉ + 1) + 1, i.e. the proof already computes with the correct count ⌈lg 2n⌉ + 1, and ⌈lg n⌉ + 1 is a typo for it (lg n for lg 2n).

  • Theorem 10 for m = 1. "O(log m) registers and O(log m) individual work" is literally O(0) for m = 1, while every implementation needs at least one operation. Degenerate; we state exact bounds 2⌈lg m⌉ + 1 registers and 2⌈lg m⌉ + 2 operations.

  • Abstract: "lg m + Θ(log log m) space and individual work". We prove the upper bound k ≤ ⌈lg m⌉ + ⌈lg(2⌈lg m⌉ + 2)⌉ for the middle-layer construction (exists_k_le) and its optimality among all quorum systems with |W_v| + |R_v| ≤ k (card_le_choose_of_quorums, from Theorem 9). The matching lower bound C(k,⌊k/2⌋) ≤ 2^k/√k-type estimate behind the Θ is not formalized.

  • ✏️ Section 6.1 prose. "This works as long as for every two distinct values v and v', R_{v'} includes some register written in W_v that is not written in W_{v'}" is a different (and, without W_v ∩ R_v = ∅, insufficient) condition than the hypothesis of Theorem 8; we follow Theorem 8.

  • Theorem 9 is cited from Jukna; we prove it (Cslib.SetPairs.sum_inv_choose_le_one) together with the optimality corollary and its tightness.

Not formalized

  • Theorem 6 (CoinConciliator from a weak shared coin). The paper notes it gives no new result. Its proof is correct in substance, with one point a careful proof must make explicit: every "skip" decision (reading 0 from r_¬v) happens before the first process enters the shared coin (both r₀, r₁ are set by then), so the skip value is fixed before the coin's randomness is used, which is what lets the coin's δ-bound apply to it.

  • Section 4.2 (noisy / priority-based schedulers), Section 6.2 item 4 (cheap-collect model).
