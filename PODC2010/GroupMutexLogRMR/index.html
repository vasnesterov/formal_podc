Group Mutual Exclusion in O(log n) RMR — PODC 2010

Vibhor Bhatt, Chien-Chung Huang. Group Mutual Exclusion in O(log n) RMR. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835708. Formalized version: the authors' copy of the PODC'10 paper (10 pages, https://www.di.ens.fr/~cchuang/work/gmx.pdf); no full version exists (the paper defers the proof of P5' "to the full version" and gives its Invariants 1–8 "without their formal proofs"). Numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The final algorithm (Figure 3) is not a correct GME algorithm in the claimed sense: it deadlocks — two processes and two sessions suffice for a fair execution in which a process waits at line 8 forever although nobody else is active — and its RMR cost per attempt is unbounded (four processes), so Theorem 1's starvation freedom (P2) and its O(min(log n, k)) RMR bound both fail. Mutual exclusion (P1) and bounded exit (P3) do hold; P1 needed a new inductive invariant of about thirty clauses, since the paper's Invariants 1–8 are unproved and partly false.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 1 / Lemma 3 (starvation freedom, P2)

The algorithm deadlocks: with two processes and two sessions there is a fair execution in which p₁ waits at line 8 forever while p₀ stays in the remainder section and nobody is in the CS (starvationFree_false).

Impact. P2, the basic liveness property of every exclusion algorithm, fails for the paper's final algorithm; with it fall Lemma 3, Claim 3 (claim3_false), concurrent entering in the paper's own formulation (Lemma 2, concurrentEnteringPaper_false) and Invariant 8 (invariant8_false), on which the proofs of Lemmas 2 and 3 rest. We did not find (or prove) a repaired algorithm.

Details

Sessions 0 and 1, S = (0,0,0) initially (deadSched, deadCfg in Deadlock.lean):

  1. p₀ (session 0) joins side 0, enters and leaves the CS and stops in Promote-Exit after line 16.5 (after its LL(Ex) at line 16.3), still counted in C[0,0].

  2. p₁ (session 0) also joins side 0, passes line 8 (Out[0] = 0), finds Ex = false at line 9 and performs a successful SC(Ex, false) at line 10 — which kills p₀'s link on Ex. In its exit it sees C[0,0] = 1 (because of p₀) at line 16.8 and leaves without promoting anybody.

  3. p₁ starts a new attempt with session 1 (side 1). Promote-Try sees C[0,0] = 1 and does nothing; p₁ waits at line 8 for Out[1] = 1.

  4. p₀ finishes: C[0,0] = 0, Min(Q) = (1,1), but its SC(Ex, 0) at line 16.9 fails (step 2), so it returns to the remainder section without switching S.

Now S = (0,0,0), Out[1] = 0, and nobody will ever write Out[1]. The proof of Claim 4 treats the case "the successful SC [on Ex] was performed by some process at Line 10" as happening after q[16.6]; here it happens between q[16.3] and q[16.6], by a fellow process of q that is the actual reason why q is the last of its side. The mechanism of lines 9–10 (invalidating lingering exiters) thus also disables the exiter that is responsible for the next switch. Line 10 is not essential: p₁'s own successful SC(Ex, false) at line 16.2 (after its LL(Ex) at 16.1) also falls between q[16.3] and q[16.9] and would kill p₀'s link just as well; the paper's sentence "If q fails at Line 16.9 then … some process performed a successful SC on Ex since q[16.6]" should read "since q[16.3]", and the window (q[16.3], q[16.6]) is not covered. (Under the stricter, Hadzilacos-style reading of concurrent entering — no conflicting process during the whole attempt — our model checker found no violation; the paper's Lemma 2 and Claim 3 use the weaker "no conflicting process active at time t".)

❌ Theorem 1 / Lemma 5 (RMR complexity O(min(log n, k)))

With n = 4 processes and two sessions, a single attempt can incur any number of remote memory references at line 8 (rmr_unbounded, not_bounded_rmr).

Impact. The headline complexity claim of the paper (and of its title) fails for the printed algorithm: no bound in terms of n holds at all. A plausible repair (writing Out[s] at line 7 only when it differs) was not checked.

Details

Lemma 5 argues that the busy-waiting loops cost 2 RMRs "by Claims 6 and 8", i.e. because Out[s] does not change once it equals p's side. But line 7 rewrites Out[s] (with the other side) every time a fellow process finds S = (s, d̄, s), and in the CC model every such write invalidates the waiting process's cached copy. Execution (RMR.lean): r (session 0) takes time-stamp 1 and pauses before enqueuing; q (session 1, time-stamp 2) and p (session 0, side 1, time-stamp 3) wait at line 8; r enqueues, writes Out[0] ← 0 at line 7 and stays in the CS. Then, round after round, f (session 0) joins side 0 via lines 1.4–1.6 (Min(Q) is r's element), writes Out[0] ← 0 at line 7, passes the CS and leaves without flipping In[0] or promoting; after each round p's re-read of Out[0] is remote. The rounds leave the configuration unchanged except for G, so K rounds give K remote reads in one attempt of p; rmr_completes checks for K = 3 that the attempt then completes (r leaves, q runs, p enters the CS).

❌ Invariant 8 and Claim 3

Both are false (invariant8_false, claim3_false): in the deadlocked configuration of the first item, p₁ is at line 8 with Min(Q) = (1,1), S = (0,0,0), C[0,0] = 0, no process of session 0 active, and Out[1] ≠ 1.

Impact. These are the steps of the proofs of Lemmas 2 and 3 that fail; see the first item.

✏️ Slips

  • Invariant 1 ("no process of session s and side d active ⇒ C[s,d] = 0") is false for processes at lines 1.2–1.3, which are counted but have no side yet. We use the exact count (Inv.gC).

  • Invariant 6 requires the exiting winner to be at lines 16.10–16.13; it may be at 16.14 (with Exclear[s] still false). Our clause win uses 16.10–16.14.

  • Invariant 7 is false as stated (a process in LE at line 16.9 may find S already switched by Promote-Try); it holds when the process is also in LS (LI.e1, LI.e2).

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 1

🛠️ Theorem 1 (mutual exclusion, P1)

"Mutual exclusion (P1) follows easily from Invariant 5", but Invariant 5 is not proved and is far from inductive: it depends on the time-stamp order (it fails if Min(Q) may return any element of Q, as our model checker shows), on the Ex/Exclear protocol of Promote-Exit, and on Invariants 2–4.

Impact. Statement true for every number of processes and sessions; proved with a new inductive invariant (mutual_exclusion, with Invariant 5 invariant5 and Invariant 3 invariant3 as by-products).

Details

Inv (Inv.lean) has 7 global and 26 per-process clauses: exact counters, the queue, the relation In[s] ≠ Out[s] unless S is of session s with side Out[s] (g4), who may hold In[s] = S.2.1 (g5), all waiting processes of a session not in S are on side In[s] (g7), Lemma 1 with time-stamps (l1), the facts remembered by a process holding a valid LL on S or Ex (a1–a3, e1–e3, x1–x3), the pending writes of Out (p5, p11, p13, g8), and the Ex "winner" protocol (w1–w5, win, winS). It was found and checked with an explicit-state model checker before the Lean proof.

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. Asynchronous shared memory, n processes (Fin n), sessions of an arbitrary type J (finite or not); no crashes outside the remainder section. Each numbered line of Figure 3 is one atomic step (line 1.8 is merged with 1.7, the CS is one step). G, C[s,d] and the priority process-queue Q are atomic objects (the paper's Theorems 2–3 provide linearizable implementations); Q is one slot per process and Min(Q) returns the element with least (tm, p). LL/SC on S and Ex: per-process link bits, cleared by every successful SC. A process in the remainder section starting an attempt chooses its session (adversary). Fairness for P2: every process takes infinitely many steps or stays in the remainder section from some time on. RMRs: the write-invalidate CC model, restricted to the reads of Out at line 8 (a lower bound).
