import Papers.PODC2010.GroupMutexLogRMR.Model /-! # A deadlock of the final algorithm (Figure 3): starvation freedom fails Two processes, two sessions `0, 1` (`S = (0,0,0)` initially). 1. `p₀` (session 0) runs alone through its whole attempt: it joins side 0 (lines 1.5–1.6, because `S = (0, 0̄̄, −)`), enters and leaves the CS, and stops just before line 16.6, i.e. after its `LL(Ex)` at line 16.3. It is still counted in `C[0,0]`. 2. `p₁` (session 0) runs a whole attempt: it joins side 0 as well and passes line 8 (`Out[0] = 0`). At lines 9–10 it finds `Ex = false` and performs a *successful* `SC(Ex, false)`, which kills `p₀`'s link on `Ex`. In its exit section it finds `C[0,0] = 1` (because of `p₀`) at line 16.8 and returns to the remainder section without promoting anybody. 3. `p₁` starts a new attempt with session 1 (side 1). In `Promote-Try` it finds `C[0,0] = 1` and does not touch `S`; it waits at line 8 for `Out[1] = 1`. 4. `p₀` finishes: `C[0,0] = 0`, `Min(Q) = (1,1)`, so it tries `SC(Ex, 0)` at line 16.9 — which fails because of `p₁`'s `SC` at line 10. `p₀` returns to the remainder section. Now `S = (0,0,0)`, `Out[1] = 0`, and the only active process `p₁` waits at line 8 forever: nobody will ever write `Out[1]`. The paper's proof of Claim 4 (case "the successful SC was performed by some process at Line 10") assumes that SC happened after `q[16.6]`; here it happened between `q[16.3]` and `q[16.6]`. -/
