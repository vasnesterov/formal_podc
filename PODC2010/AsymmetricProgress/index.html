Asymmetric Progress Conditions — PODC 2010

Damien Imbs, Michel Raynal, Gadi Taubenfeld. On Asymmetric Progress Conditions. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835709. Formalized version: the extended IRISA research report PI-1952 (May 2010, HAL inria-00486977), whose numbering matches the PODC paper and adds the proofs of Appendix A. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. All results hold for n ≥ 2 processes: the impossibility of (n,1)-liveness from (n−1)-port objects (Theorem 1), the (n,x)-liveness hierarchy (Theorems 2, 3, Corollary 1), the fault-freedom variant (Theorem 4), the arbiter (Theorem 5) and the group-based consensus algorithm (Theorem 6). The proof of Theorem 2, on which the hierarchy rests, had to be redone in a new model of base objects that may withhold responses, with a new adversarial object. Theorems 1 and 4 and Lemmas 3–9 are false only in the degenerate one-process case, and the proofs of Lemma 6 and Theorem 6 need only local repairs.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorems 1 and 4, Lemmas 3–9: with n = 1 these statements are false, because one process that decides its own input is a wait-free consensus algorithm without any shared object, and it has no bivalent run (theorem1_false_one_process, lemma3_false_one_process). The paper never states n ≥ 2, but every proof needs two processes (Lemma 3 picks two processes with different inputs). With n ≥ 2 all of them are proved.

  • The text of Section 6.1 describing Figure 4 says "the owners win the arbitration and p_i updates consequently WINNER to guest (line 03)"; it should say owner, as line 03 does.

Typos

  • Lemma 10 says "y ∈ [1..n]"; it should be [1..m] (groups). Its end marker reads "Lemma 11".

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Theorem 2 · Lemma 6 · Theorem 6

🛠️ Theorem 2 (blocking base objects)

The proof applies Lemma 6 (developed only for atomic events) to implementations from non-wait-free (n,x)-live base objects and lets processes "access o simultaneously" forever, without saying what a step on such an object is.

Impact. Statement true (for every 0 ≤ x < n − 1); repaired (theorem2). The upper bound of Theorem 3 and Corollary 1 rest on it.

Repair

Base objects are deterministic, caller-aware objects that may withhold a response (BObj). Lemmas 1–6 are proved in this model. The adversary lets every (n,x)-live base object behave as advCons: wait-free ports are answered at once, any other process only at a step that directly follows its own previous step on the object. This is a legal (n,x)-live consensus object (advCons_isLive). At the configuration of Lemma 6 take a process w wait-free for the implementation but not for o (it exists since x + 1 > x) and another process r ∉ X_o (it exists since x + 1 < n). Alternating w and r keeps both blocked forever, so w violates wait-freedom.

🔧 Lemma 6 (induction step)

The proof applies Lemma 1 to x and z (and xq, zq, y), claiming that all objects p can access have the same state there, although the steps of s in z − x may write registers that p can access.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (lemma6, CritK.extend). Lemma 9 has the same proof and the same repair.

Details

Lemma 1 needs equal states of every object p may touch in its solo run, not only of the object of its next step. So the hypothesis of Lemma 1 fails as soon as s writes a register before reaching o.

Repair

The step of p on o commutes with the steps of s, which avoid o. So z p = x p (z − x) is an extension of x p, and z q p = x q p (z − x) one of x q p. Compatibility follows without Lemma 1 (run_comm_of_avoids, Avoids.step_of_poised).

🔧 Theorem 6 (use of the arbiter's validity)

The proofs of Lemma 10 (Case 2) and Lemma 11 (Case 2) need "a guest (owner) invoked arbitrate() before the return", but the arbiter specification of Section 6.1 only says "if no guest (owner) invokes arbitrate(), the value guest (owner) cannot be returned".

Impact. Statement true; repaired. The Figure 4 implementation satisfies the real-time version (arbiter_validity_owner, arbiter_validity_guest), and Theorem 6 is proved for the inlined algorithm (group_agreement, group_termination).

Details

The non-⊥ argument for ARB_VAL[y+1] (and VAL[y]) needs an invocation that happened before the read. An invocation later in the run would have written the register too late.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, n deterministic processes, crash-stop. Infinite executions are schedules ℕ → Fin n; a process is correct iff it is scheduled infinitely often. No randomness; the adversary chooses the schedule and, for non-wait-free base objects, a legal behaviour.

  • Theorems 1–4: every process proposes a binary input at time 0. This only restricts the executions, so the impossibility results transfer to the paper's setting. Valency is on configurations, since the system is deterministic and outputs persist.

  • Base objects (BObj): deterministic, possibly caller-dependent, possibly withholding the response. Atomic objects always respond. "Atomic register" is generalized to read/write-like objects (every operation is read-only or a blind overwrite). Ports are a property of the algorithm (RespectsPorts).

  • (n,X)-live consensus (Live A X): agreement and validity in all finite executions, obstruction-freedom of every process (solo runs from reachable configurations decide), wait-freedom of the processes in X. Fault-freedom (FFree): a process decides in every execution where all processes are correct.

  • Section 6 (Arbiter.lean, GroupConsensus.lean): a separate concrete model with proposals of an arbitrary type V. A process participates iff it takes a step. Registers are atomic, and GXCONS[g] and the arbiters' XCONS (wait-free (x,x)-live objects accessed only by group g) are atomic consensus objects. Figure 4 is inlined for every ARBITER[g]. The two tasks of a process alternate (T1, T2, T1, …). Deciding does not stop T1, which only adds executions.

Theorems 1 and 4 are proved in a more general form than stated. The objects with at most n − 1 ports may be arbitrary, not only (n−1, n−1)-live consensus objects; this covers the abstract's "any wait-free objects with n − 1 ports". Theorem 1 allows wait-freedom of any nonempty set of processes. Theorem 2 allows different wait-free sets for different base objects and also x = 0. Lemmas 4–6 are proved once from "a bivalent configuration with a decider", which Lemma 4 (wait-freedom) or Lemma 7 (fault-freedom) supplies, so Lemmas 8 and 9 come for free. corollary1_top rests on a general lemma (wFree_of_wFree_others): if every process but r is wait-free and r is obstruction-free, then r is wait-free. This lemma is also the lower bound of Theorem 3.

Theorem 5 validity and Theorem 6 validity are proved in the real-time form ("invoked before the return", "proposed by a process that took a step before the decision").

Not formalized. Section 3.5 (Common2 objects; relies on external results) and the remarks of Sections 1.2 and 6.3.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: advCons_isLive shows that the adversarial base objects are legal (n,X)-live consensus objects. atomicCons_live shows that one n-port atomic consensus object solves wait-free consensus, so Live is satisfiable and the n − 1 port bound of Theorem 1 is needed. The model is paper-local (CSLib candidate: caller-aware blocking objects and the valency toolkit); registers come from CSLib's ObjSpec and RWLike (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/{Spec,ObjClasses}.lean).
