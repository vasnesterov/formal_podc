Finding mobile data with searching costs — PODC 2010

Amotz Bar-Noy, Panagiotis Cheilaris, Yi Feng, Asaf Levin. Finding mobile data under delay constraints with searching costs. PODC 2010. DOI 10.1145/1835698.1835774. Formalized version: the PODC'10 paper (8 pages, author copy at corelab.ntua.gr/~philaris); no full version exists, so the proofs omitted there (Lemmas 5, 6, Theorem 5) were supplied by us.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The headline results hold. FRO is an 8/7-approximation for two rounds (Theorem 4), and a (7-2√7)/(28-10√7) = (4+√7)/6 ≈ 1.108-approximation for typical tokens (Theorem 5); both bounds are tight. The paper's proof of both theorems does not work: its reduction to N = 3, 4 boxes (Lemma 4) has a false step, and the cases N = 3, 4 (Lemmas 5, 6) are omitted. We proved both theorems for every N with a new argument. Sections 2–4 (Propositions 1–3, Lemmas 1–3, Corollary 1) hold; the proof of Lemma 1 misses a degenerate case (identical or zero-probability boxes).

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 7 and "the ratio 8/7 is attainable" (Theorem 4): the tight instance has a tie p₁/w₁ = p₂/w₂. FRO reaches 8/7 only if it breaks the tie adversarially (box 1 before box 2); with the other order FRO is optimal on this instance, and then 8/7 is only a supremum. Section 5.2 states the adversarial convention for typical tokens; we use it throughout (lemma7, theorem4_tight, lemma8).

  • Lemma 1, proof: from j < i it concludes the strict p_i < p_j, w_i > w_j, so the swap is strictly cheaper and every optimal strategy respects the order. Both fail in a degenerate case: the swap has equal cost exactly when C_i, C_j are identical boxes or both have probability 0 in a round of probability 0. The statement holds (lemma1_isOptimal): a weak exchange step (cost_comp_swap_le) and the potential ∑ k · A(k) give a monotone strategy at least as cheap as any strategy (exists_monotone_cost_le). The proof also uses, without saying so, that a non-respecting partition into non-empty rounds has an inversion between adjacent rounds (true).

Typos

  • Section 4, before Lemma 3: "X = (1 + Y)/2" with X the load-balancing and Y the search objective; by Proposition 3 it is Y = (1 + X)/2 (the proof of Lemma 3 uses the correct relation).

  • Lemma 1, proof: "does not respect the non-decreasing order of the ratios" should be non-increasing.

  • Lemma 3, proof: the load-balancing cost is "greater than 1/D and smaller than 1"; it is ≥ 1/D and ≤ 1, as written in the next sentence.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorems 4 and 5

🛠️ Theorems 4 and 5 (Lemma 4 reduction to N = 3, 4; Lemmas 5, 6 omitted)

The proof of Lemma 4 claims that FRO on the merged 4-box instance I₄ = {C_A, C_B, C_C, C_D} outputs ⟨{C_A, C_B}, {C_C, C_D}⟩, hence cost(FRO₄) ≥ cost(FRO_N). This is false: with p = (8,1,6,5,1), w = (1,1,7,6,4) (strict ratio order), FRO costs 246 and OPT 243, but on the merged instance FRO costs 245 and OPT 243, so the ratio drops from 246/243 to 245/243 (lemma4_proof_step_fails).

Impact. Statements true. We proved Theorem 4 (theorem4) and Theorem 5 (theorem5) for every N, so Lemmas 5 and 6 follow (lemma5, lemma6), and Lemma 4's statement holds too (lemma4), because of Lemma 7.

Details

FRO on I₄ minimises over all prefixes of the ratio order of I₄. Merged boxes keep FRO's split A ∪ B | C ∪ D among these prefixes, so in fact cost(FRO₄) ≤ cost(FRO_N), the wrong direction. The other prefixes, such as {C_A} or {C_A, C_B, C_C}, are not prefixes of the original order and can be cheaper. In the counterexample FRO₄ = ⟨{C_A, C_B, C_C}, {C_D}⟩ with A = {1}, B = {2}, C = {4}, D = {3, 5} (1-based). The proof of Theorem 5 is an outline that reuses Lemma 4 and leaves its own N = 3, 4 cases unproved. A merge-based reduction only works for runs of boxes that are consecutive in the ratio order and on the same side of OPT, and that does not bound N.

Repair

A direct argument for every N (bracket_gen, TwoRounds.lean). For two rounds, cost(S) = P·W − p(S)·w(Sᶜ). Let S be any first round and k the largest index with w(C₁…C_k) ≤ w(S). Then the FRO candidates {C₁…C_k} and {C₁…C_{k+1}} already satisfy 7 · min cost ≤ 8 · cost(S). The ratio order bounds p(S) by the line through the critical box C_{k+1}. Integrality enters as a case split: either C_{k+1} ∉ S (so p(S) ≤ P − p_{k+1}) or C_{k+1} ∈ S (so w(S) ≥ w_{k+1}). Rescaling then reduces everything to two polynomial inequalities in three variables (ineq_case1, ineq_case2, key_real). For typical tokens the same bracketing gives (4+√7)/6 (typ_real).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Centralized combinatorial optimization; no distributed model, no failures, no randomness beyond the known placement distribution. Boxes are Fin N, p i ≥ 0 (not necessarily summing to 1), w i ≥ 0. A D-round strategy is a map A : Fin N → Fin D, box i unlocked in round A i. Empty rounds are allowed; this changes no optimum.

  • cost p w A = ∑ i, p i · ∑_{A j ≤ A i} w j is the expected unlocking cost. Proposition 1 shows it equals both of the paper's formulas.

  • FRO: the boxes are indexed in non-increasing order of p i / w i (RatioSorted, stated without division). IsFRO means minimum cost among monotone strategies, i.e. those respecting the order. Every theorem quantifies over all RatioSorted indexings, so it holds for every tie-breaking. For typical tokens (p = w) every order is a ratio order, matching the paper's adversary.

  • Theorems 4 and 5 hold for every N (the paper says N > 2) and allow zero costs (the paper assumes positive costs; Lemma 8 uses its "0/0 = 1" convention, which we need not).
