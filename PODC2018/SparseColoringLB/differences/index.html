Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Lower bounds for colouring sparse graphs — PODC 2018.

Paper: Aboulker, Bonamy, Bousquet, Esperet, Distributed Coloring in Sparse Graphs with Fewer Colors, PODC 2018; arXiv 1802.05582v4 (numbering as there). Only the lower bounds of §2 (Observation 2.4, Theorems 2.5, 2.6, 1.5) are formalized; the algorithm of Theorem 1.3 (§3–§6) is not.

Model

  1. LOCAL model. An algorithm on n-node networks is a function A : SimpleGraph V → V → β, |V| = n: node names are the identifiers (the paper: identifiers in {1, …, n}, n known — here V is any type with n elements, so n is known). It is r-round (BallLocal r A) if the output of a node only depends on the subgraph induced by its closed ball of radius r, with identifiers (BallAgree). This is the paper's own description ("the output of each vertex v only depends on the labelled ball of radius r"). It gives the nodes slightly more information than r synchronous rounds really do (edges between two nodes at distance exactly r), so our lower bounds are (slightly) stronger than lower bounds for genuine r-round algorithms; BallLocal.of_isLocal transfers them to CSLib's Cslib.Local.IsLocal r (genuine r rounds), and BallLocal.isLocal_succ shows BallLocal r ⊆ IsLocal (r + 1).

  2. "A colours Γ" (ColoursGraph): for every bijective identifier assignment W ≃ V, adjacent nodes output different colours. The algorithm may depend on Γ (it is only required to work on copies of Γ), which makes "no algorithm colours Γ" stronger than needed.

  3. Class statements ("no algorithm can 3-colour every planar triangle-free graph on n vertices in o(n) rounds", etc.): formalized as "for every r-round algorithm on n identifiers there is an n-node network of an explicit family on which it fails", with explicit constants instead of o(·). The networks are copies of an explicit graph (H_{2r+2}, the (2r+3)×(2r+3) grid, a cut torus cutTorus (2r+3) c) plus isolated nodes (IsCopyOf), so they are disconnected when n is larger than the explicit graph. Adding isolated vertices keeps planarity, triangle-freeness and bipartiteness.

  4. Planarity is not formalized (Mathlib has no planarity). The explicit families are planar by their drawings: H_k = C₅ □ P_k is k nested pentagons (Figure 2, right); grids; cutTorus w c is a triangulated annulus (w + 1 nested triangles, each consecutive pair joined by a triangulated band). Triangle-freeness of H_k and bipartiteness of the grid are likewise not stated in Lean. The theorems are therefore literally about these explicit families.

Observation 2.4

  1. The paper says "each ball of radius at most r + 1 in H is isomorphic to some ball of radius at most r + 1 in G". As stated (unrooted isomorphisms) this is not enough for the argument; we assume rooted isomorphisms of the subgraphs induced by the balls of radius r + 1 (observation_2_4). The conclusion is formalized as: an r-round algorithm colouring G with colours β yields a colouring of H with colours β.

  2. The proofs of Theorems 2.5, 2.6, 1.5 do not go through Observation 2.4 but through a sharper form of the same indistinguishability argument (coloring_of_views, coloring_of_views_family): it suffices that the radius-r views of the two endpoints of each edge of H are realised simultaneously by one identifier assignment of G. This is what gives the paper's bound in Theorem 2.5 (see 8).

Theorem 2.5 (H_k)

  1. H_k is only drawn for k = 2ℓ (Figure 2); we define H_k = C₅ □ P_k for all k.

  2. The paper's proof does not give the stated bound (statement true). The paper applies Observation 2.4 with H = G_{5,2ℓ+1} (Klein bottle) and G = H_{2ℓ}. (a) G_{5,2ℓ+1} has 10ℓ + 5 vertices, more than H_{2ℓ} (10ℓ), so the hypothesis |V(H)| ≤ |V(G)| fails. (b) "Balls of radius < ℓ" with Observation 2.4 (radius r + 1) give only r ≤ ℓ - 2, not r < ℓ. We prove the stated bound r < ℓ exactly (theorem_2_5), with klein 5 (2ℓ) (same size as H_{2ℓ}) and the edge-view argument (6), and more generally 2r + 2 ≤ k (hGraph_not_colours; for odd k this is r < (k-1)/2, the odd case is not claimed by the paper).

  3. Second sentence: with explicit constants, n ≥ 10(r + 1) ⇒ the algorithm fails on H_{2r+2} plus isolated nodes (theorem_2_5_family), i.e. r ≥ n/10 - 1 is needed.

Theorem 2.6 (grid)

  1. The first sentence is false for every odd k ≥ 3. "No distributed algorithm can 3-colour the k × k grid in less than k/2 rounds": for k = 2t + 1, t < k/2 rounds suffice, even for a proper 2-colouring, in genuine t-round LOCAL (CSLib IsLocal t): a node counts the nodes at distance exactly t (or the whole distance profile up to t), which determines x + y mod 2. Lean: theorem_2_6_false_odd (every odd k = 2t + 1, using only the number of nodes at distance exactly t; Theorem26General.lean), and the instances theorem_2_6_false_3, …, …_11 by kernel computation. The counterexample refutes the statement in the paper's own sense (a fixed graph, every identifier assignment, as in Observation 2.4 and item 2; nodes know n = k²); the algorithm is tailored to the grid. A stronger "uniform" reading (an algorithm that 3-colours every planar bipartite graph needs ≥ k/2 rounds on the k × k grid) is not refuted by it and is not addressed here. The error is one round in an exact bound; the Ω(√n) content stands.

  2. What the paper's argument proves, in its sharp form, is 2r + 3 ≤ k (theorem_2_6, via grid_not_colours with the Klein bottle grid klein m m, m the largest odd number ≤ k). For odd k this is tight by 10. For even k the paper's bound 2r + 2 ≤ k remains open here (neither proved nor refuted; the Klein-bottle argument cannot give it, since it needs an odd side ≥ 2r + 3 and at most k² vertices). The paper's own bookkeeping (balls of radius < k in G_{2k+1,2k+1}, Observation 2.4 with radius r + 1) gives only r ≤ k - 2 for the (2k+1)-grid.

  3. Second sentence (o(√n) for planar bipartite graphs): n ≥ (2r + 3)² ⇒ the algorithm fails on the (2r+3) × (2r+3) grid plus isolated nodes (theorem_2_6_family). True as stated.

Gallai's theorem (cited)

  1. The paper cites Gallai: G_{2k+1,2ℓ+1} is 4-chromatic. We prove only the needed half, χ > 3, but for all odd m ≥ 3 and all n ≥ 2 (klein_not_colorable; winding-number argument). The upper bound χ ≤ 4 is not formalized. Our Klein bottle grid glues (i, n-1) ~ (-i, 0) (reflection i ↦ -i of ℤ/m).

Theorem 1.5 and Fisk's theorem

  1. The triangulation of Figure 3 is given only as a picture ("versions of this graph on n vertices clearly exist for any n ≡ 1 (mod 3)"). We define an explicit family torus w (3w + 1 vertices: rows of lengths w + 1, w, w; all degrees 6 except 4, 7, 7, the two degree-7 vertices adjacent) matching the figure up to drawing. That it is a triangulation of a closed surface (every edge in exactly two of the 6w + 2 triangular faces, links are cycles) and the degree pattern were checked numerically for 3 ≤ w ≤ 8, not in Lean; they are not needed.

  2. Fisk's theorem is cited, not proved, in the paper. We prove non-4-colourability of torus w directly (torus_not_colorable, all w ≥ 2) by Fisk's parity argument specialised to this family: a transfer argument over the 24 proper colourings of a column triangle, with a 6-class invariant preserved between consecutive columns and changed across the defect (finite checks by decide). The general Fisk theorem is not formalized.

  3. Instead of "balls of radius ≤ (n-1)/6 - 3 are planar" + Observation 2.4, we compare torus w with the cut tori cutTorus w c (delete the edges between columns c and c + 1, choose c opposite to the edge). Constant: n ≥ 6r + 10 suffices (theorem_1_5), i.e. 4-colouring all n-vertex planar graphs needs r ≥ (n - 10)/6 rounds, slightly better than the paper's (n-1)/6 - 4.

Not formalized
