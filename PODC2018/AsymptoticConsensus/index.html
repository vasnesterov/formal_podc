Tight Bounds for Asymptotic and Approximate Consensus — PODC 2018

Matthias Függer, Thomas Nowak, Manfred Schwarz. Tight Bounds for Asymptotic and Approximate Consensus. PODC 2018. DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212762. Formalized version: arXiv 1705.02898 v2 (the JACM 2021 version was not consulted). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The contraction-rate lower bounds (Theorems 1, 2, 3, 5, 6) and the approximate-consensus bounds (Theorems 8–11) are correct, and Theorems 1 and 2 are tight (Algorithm 1 and the midpoint algorithm). Theorem 4 (⇐) is false: "valencies singleton or disconnected ⇒ exact consensus solvable", the converse of the paper's topological characterization of exact consensus, fails for two agents in {H₀, H₁, H₂}. It becomes true once the asymptotic algorithm is required to have a continuous consensus function.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 4 (⇐: valencies singleton or disconnected ⇒ exact consensus solvable)

For two agents in N = {H₀, H₁, H₂} the "first deviation" algorithm solves asymptotic consensus with every valency finite (a singleton or disconnected), yet exact consensus is unsolvable in N (theorem4_false, fd_not_vanishing).

Impact. Only the converse direction fails: (⇒) holds as stated. Adding continuity of the consensus function (valency diameters vanish along every execution of every submodel) makes (⇐) true (theorem4_mpr_repaired).

Details

H₀ lets both agents hear each other, and in H₁/H₂ one of them is deaf. The "first deviation" algorithm: before any non-H₀ round the output is x₀. At the first non-H₀ round t, the deaf agent decides its own value if t is odd and the other's value if t is even. The other agent copies that decision, or applies the same parity rule if it is itself deaf in round t+1. This solves asymptotic consensus, and every limit is an initial value, so every valency Y*_{N'}(C) is finite. Yet by Theorem 1 an exact algorithm would have contraction rate 0.

The proof fails at "δ_{N'}(C_t) → 0 in any execution, by Convergence and Agreement". That holds only if the consensus function (pattern ↦ limit) is continuous, and the paper itself notes in §2.1 that it need not be. Here, along the all-H₀ execution, both x₀ and x₁ stay reachable forever. The refutation also covers binary inputs.

Theorem 4 quantifies over network models N' ⊆ N, which the paper defines to be nonempty (§2); this matters, since for N' = ∅ the valency would be empty.

Repair

With continuity, the full equivalence follows, conditional on the cited characterization of Coulouma et al. [12] (theorem4_repaired).

✏️ Slips

  • Definition 15 (α-relation) writes In_S(G) as a union of node sets, but [12, Def. 4.7] and every later use need the per-agent version In_i(G) = In_i(H) for all i ∈ R(K); under the literal reading Lemma 20 and Theorem 5 fail (lemma20_literal_false, theorem5_literal_false). The α-diameter of {H₀,H₁,H₂} would be 1. Lemma 20 would fail for Algorithm 1 from (0, 1), since the valencies after H₁ and H₂ lie in [0, 1/3] and [2/3, 1]. Theorem 5 would claim rate ≥ 1/2 for Algorithm 1, whose rate is 1/3. The remark D = 2 for {H₀,H₁,H₂} and the proofs of Lemmas 20 and 24 use the per-agent version, under which Lemma 20 and Theorem 5 hold.

  • The proof of Theorem 11 writes log₃ for log_{D+1}.

  • The proof of Theorem 11 writes δ_N(C₀) ≤ Δ/n for ≥ Δ/n.

  • Lemma 24 swaps the endpoints of its chain relative to its own definition of H_r.

Typos

  • The cited Theorem 1 of [8] and the continuity theorem of Section 2.2 share their numbers with the main Theorems 1 and 2.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Finitely many agents; synchronous, communication-closed rounds (Heard-Of style). A pattern is any sequence of communication graphs from the model N (oblivious adversary). Self-loops are implicit. No failures beyond what the graphs encode.

  • Algorithms are deterministic and full-information-capable: arbitrary state, agent-dependent initial state, outputs need not lie in the hull of received values. This is a larger class than the paper's, so the lower bounds are stronger.

  • Asymptotic consensus: in every execution all outputs converge to a common limit in the hull of the inputs. Values lie in any nontrivial real normed space (covers ℝ^d); the midpoint algorithm is on ℝ.

  • Valency = set of limits reachable from a configuration (equal to the paper's Y* on reachable configurations). Diameters and rates live in ℝ≥0∞. Snapshots and properties (§6.1) are replaced by block patterns.

  • Approximate consensus: ε, Δ are fixed parameters, and only time-T decisions are constrained (weaker than the paper's spec, so stronger lower bounds).

  • Section 8: only the network model N_A ("wait for n − f messages") is formalized, not the reduction from asynchronous crash executions.
