Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of On Local Distributed Sampling and Counting — PODC 2018.

Paper: W. Feng, Y. Yin, On Local Distributed Sampling and Counting, PODC 2018 (arXiv:1802.06686v1).

Errors in the paper (statement level)

  1. Corollary 5.2 is false as stated (❌; the paper lists it under "Minor results"). Claim. A class of locally admissible, local Gibbs distributions has SSM with exponential decay at rate α in TV distance iff it has SSM with exponential decay at rate α in multiplicative error. Here err(μ, μ̂) = max_x |ln μ(x) − ln μ̂(x)|, eq. (2), with only ln 0 − ln 0 = 0 as a convention, and exponential decay means δ_n(t) = poly(n)·α^t for all t (Definition 5.1). Counterexample. Take the hardcore model with fugacity 1 on the perfect matching with m ≥ 1 edges (matchingGraph m, n = 2m). Every member is locally admissible (hardcore_locallyAdmissible) and has constraints of diameter ≤ 1 (hardcore_gibbsLocal). The class has TV-SSM with δ_n(d) = 2·(1/2)^d (matching_ssm). But pin a neighbour u of v to 1 (σ) and to 0 (τ). Then D = {u} is at distance 1, μ^σ_v(1) = 0 and μ^τ_v(1) > 0, so err = ∞. Hence there is no multiplicative SSM with any real-valued rate (hardcore_err_infinite, hardcore_not_mssm, which hold for any graph with an edge; corollary_5_2_false). Not degenerate. The counterexample satisfies every hypothesis of the paper and is not degenerate: the class covers every even n, and the hardcore model is the paper's flagship application. The repair is not local. Restricting to distances > ℓ is not enough, because soft constraints with weights like e^{e^n} give finite but super-polynomial err at distance 1. The true statement must only constrain err where poly(n)·α^d is small, i.e. at distances ≳ log n. That changes the definition of exponential decay the corollary is about. Second problem (❌, the proof route). Even at large distances, the paper's proof (Theorem 5.1 ⇐, then the boosting Lemma 4.1 with locality 2t + ℓ, then Theorem 5.1 ⇒ for err) yields err ≤ poly(n)·(√α)^d, not poly(n)·α^d. A direct pinning argument, with the pinned shell placed close to D and lower bounds on the marginals obtained from SSM, should recover rate α at large distances. This is not formalized, and we make no claim about it. Distance 0. Read literally, multiplicative SSM fails for every distribution with |Σ| ≥ 2 already at dist(v, D) = 0: if v ∈ D, then μ^σ_v, μ^τ_v are point masses at different values and err = ∞. Our MSSM inherits this (at d = 0 the pinnings may differ at v), so ¬ MSSM on its own would be a weak statement. The counterexample does not use this case: hardcore_err_infinite has v free in both pinnings, which differ only at a neighbour u. So it also refutes the natural readings "v ∉ Λ" and "dist(v, D) ≥ 1". Another reading. One could read Definition 5.1 for err as constraining only distances where poly(n)·α^d is small, which would make the hardcore counterexample disappear. We do not adopt this reading: the paper never states such a restriction, it says that the known multiplicative SSM results for independent sets have exactly this form, and the needed repair changes the notion of exponential decay itself (not a local fix). True part. Multiplicative SSM ⇒ TV SSM with the same rate, since d_TV ≤ err for probability vectors (tv_le_of_multClose, ssm_of_mssm). This direction needs neither local admissibility nor locality.

  2. Algorithm local-JVV and the statement of Lemma 4.8: acceptance and rejection swapped (✏️). §4.2.1: "vᵢ samples F'_{vᵢ} = 1 with probability q_{vᵢ}" and "fails locally if F'_{vᵢ} = 1". Lemma 4.8: "fails with probability at most ∑ E[F'_{vᵢ}] = 1 − O(1/n)". As written, with q_{vᵢ} ≥ e^{−5/n²}, the algorithm fails almost surely. The proof of Lemma 4.8 uses Pr[F'_{vᵢ} = 1 | Y = σ] = q_{vᵢ}(σ) together with Pr[success | Y = σ] = ∏ q_{vᵢ}(σ). The intent is therefore recoverable: accept with probability q_{vᵢ}, and fail with probability ≤ 1 − e^{−5/n} = O(1/n). The fix is local. We formalize the intended version.

  3. Theorem 5.1 (⇒): δ_n(t) = 2 min{δ | t(n, δ) ≤ t − 1} (✏️). The minimum need not be attained (δ ranges over the open set δ > 0), and for t ≤ t(n, δ) for all δ the set is empty, so δ_n(t) is undefined although Definition 5.1 requires δ_n : ℕ → ℝ≥0. Intended reading: infimum, with inf ∅ = ∞. We use [0, ∞]-valued rates in SSM and prove the statement with the infimum (theorem_5_1_forward).

Proof gaps and repairs

  1. Lemma 4.1, Claim (🔧). μ_{Λᵢ∪{v}}(τᵢᶜ) = μ_{Λᵢ₋₁∪{v}}(τᵢ₋₁ᶜ) · μ^{τᵢ₋₁ᶜ}_{vᵢ}(cᵢ) ≥ μ^{τᵢ₋₁ᶜ}_{vᵢ}(cᵢ) is wrong (the first factor is ≤ 1). Only positivity is needed. The first factor is positive by induction, and the second is ≥ 1/q − 3ε/(5nq) > 0 by (4) and (5). Our proof uses exactly this (pinSeq_spec). The quantitative bound (3) is correct as stated (lemma_4_1_core). The numeric step e^{−ε/n} ≤ 1 − 2ε/(5n − ε) and 1 + 2ε/(5n − ε) ≤ e^{ε/n} is checked in ratio_bound.

  2. Lemma 4.1: |p(c) − q(c)| ≤ d_TV(p, q) (used for (5)) requires both p and q to be probability vectors. We assume, as the paper's definition of approximate inference does, that the TV-approximate algorithm returns probability vectors on feasible instances (hacc).

  3. Lemma 4.1, the pinned case v ∈ Λ ("otherwise the inference problem is trivial"): our boostAlg returns the point mass at τ(v), which is exact (marg_of_pinned).

  4. Theorem 3.2, coupling. "By a coupling argument, it can be verified that d_TV(μ̂, μ^τ) ≤ δ." We prove the ℓ¹ version by induction on the scan (sum_abs_seqProb_sub_le). The proof notes one subtlety: after an approximation error the sampler may reach infeasible pinnings, on which the inference algorithm has no guarantee. Our statement lets the oracle behave arbitrarily there, requiring only nonnegative answers of total mass ≤ 1.

  5. Claim 4.6. Γ = {vⱼ | vⱼ ∉ B_t(v) or j ≤ i − 1} should read B_t(vᵢ). The pinnings τ₁ = σᵢ₋₁(Γ) and τ₂ = Y(Γ) must be read together with τ (feasibility "w.r.t. μ^τ"). We formalize the core step: equal oracle answers plus multiplicative error give positivity of the needed marginal (claim_4_6_core). The vertex bookkeeping is not formalized.

  6. Small slips. In the Claim of Lemma 4.1, "dist_G(v, v_{i−1}) > t" and "at node v_{i−1}" should read vᵢ. In the proof of (3), "m = |Λ| ≤ n" should be m = |Γ| ≤ n. In the statement of Lemma 4.1 the round count is 2t + ℓ with t = t(n, ε/(5qn)), which the paper writes as O(t(n, ε/(5qn))).

Modelling choices

  1. Per-instance LOCAL model. We fix one instance (G, x) (graph, Gibbs factors) and model an inference algorithm as a map Pin V S → V → (S → ℝ). IsLocalAlg G r A requires that the answer at v depends only on the pinning within distance r. True LOCAL locality also restricts the dependence on (G, x), so assuming IsLocalAlg is weaker (Theorem 5.1 ⇒ and the hypothesis of Lemma 4.1 become stronger results). For the constructed algorithms (ssmAlg, boostAlg) we prove IsLocalAlg. Their outputs are given by the local formula marg (gibbsWOn F B) (restrictPin τ' B) v, which uses only the factors inside the ball B, and the choices of the extension depend only on factors inside B_{t+2ℓ}(v).

  2. Randomness. Inference algorithms are deterministic (Proposition 3.3, not formalized; its key step, convexity of TV distance, is tv_mix_le). Samplers are modelled by their exact output distributions (seqProb). The JVV third pass is modelled by its acceptance probabilities. Random bit strings, the SLOCAL model and the network-decomposition simulation (Lemmas 3.1, 4.4, cited from Ghaffari–Kuhn–Maus) are not formalized, so round complexities appear only as localities.

  3. Weights instead of normalized distributions. Joint distributions are given by nonnegative weights w (any μ, or a Gibbs weight); μ^τ is cond w τ. A partial configuration τ ∈ Σ^Λ is a pinning V → Option Σ. Feasibility is Z w τ > 0.

  4. SSM (Definition 5.1). SSM G w R says: for feasible σ, τ with the same domain that agree on all u with dist(v, u) < d, d_TV(μ^σ_v, μ^τ_v) ≤ R d. For non-increasing R this is the paper's ≤ δ_n(dist(v, D)). When D is unreachable from v, the paper's dist = ∞ case, our version requires ≤ R d for every d. Rates take values in [0, ∞] (see item 3). MSSM is the multiplicative variant with real rates, as in the paper.

  5. Theorem 5.1 (⇐) parameters. We assume R t ≤ δ, where R is evaluated at the shell radius t. The paper's t = min{t' | δ_n(t') ≤ δ} satisfies this. The algorithm has locality t + 2ℓ, as in the paper. The extension on the shell is any one passing the local test (14) (Classical.epsilon); the paper enumerates.

  6. Vertex order in Lemma 4.1. The paper orders Γ by IDs; we use any fixed enumeration (Finset.toList). The result holds for every order.

  7. Theorem 3.4 is formalized as its inequality chain d_TV(μ̃_v, μ^τ_v) ≤ d_TV(μ̃, μ^τ) ≤ d_TV(μ̂, μ^τ) + Pr[fail] for the mixture μ̃ = (1 − p) μ̂ + p ν (theorem_3_4_tv). Reconstructing μ̃_v at v by enumerating random bits is not formalized.

  8. Not formalized. Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 4.3 as compositions. The locality 3t + ℓ of q_{vᵢ} (eqs. (11), (12)). Corollary 5.3 and the applications (they rely on cited SSM results). A repaired Corollary 5.2.
