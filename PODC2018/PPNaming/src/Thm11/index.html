By Lemma 2 and Proposition 6 the mobile-mobile rules are null except between homonyms, and homonyms reduce to the sink `m`. We work in a population of `P` mobile agents with *reduced* configurations (`Red`: no homonyms except in `m`) and *reduced steps* (`rstep`: the leader meets an agent, and a homonym pair it creates is immediately reduced to `(m, m)`). **Proof (repaired).** Fix `s ≠ m`. Two worlds with the same leader state are related (`Rel`) if the *plus* world has exactly one `s`-agent where the *minus* world has an extra `m`-agent (multisets: `plus + {m} = minus + {s}`, no `s` in minus). Every reduced step of the minus world can be mirrored in the plus world (`mirror`): the relation is kept, with roles exchanged exactly when the minus world creates an `s`-agent (then the plus world gets a second `s`-agent and reduces it). By weak fairness every reachable reduced configuration reaches a named one by reduced steps (`exists_rreach_injective`); in particular a minus world eventually creates `s` (`drive`). Our adversary always lets the *minus* world drive: the actual execution makes the interaction required by weak fairness when it is the minus world, and otherwise follows the other world until it creates `s`, after which it is the minus world again. Hence the actual execution is weakly fair and has two `m`-agents infinitely often — it never names the agents. The paper's proof instead fixes one execution `e` from `C₁⁻ˢ` and claims (Theorem 11, "e is chosen such that ... a particular s-state agent y_i does not interact") that the plus phases of `e` can avoid the `s`-agent; this step is not justified (it fails for Protocol 1 under global fairness, where the leader must meet the `s`-agent).
