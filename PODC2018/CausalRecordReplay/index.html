Optimal Record and Replay under Causal Consistency — PODC 2018

Russell L. Jones, Muhammad S. Khan, Nitin H. Vaidya. Brief Announcement: Optimal Record and Replay under Causal Consistency. PODC 2018. DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212789. Formalized version: arXiv 1805.08804 v2 (full version with all proofs); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. All optimality results hold under strong causal consistency: the offline records for both RnR models are good and least (Theorems 5.3, 5.4, 6.6, 6.7), and the online record for Model 1 is good and least among online schemes (Theorems 5.5, 5.6). Definition 6.4 and the notion of online record need small fixes, both available from the paper's own proofs, and the proofs of two central results, necessity in Theorem 6.7 and sufficiency through Lemma C.1(b), needed new arguments.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 5.6 (notion of "online record")

"R is an online record of V" is defined for one fixed execution, and in that reading any record of V is online, so Theorem 5.6 would contradict Theorem 5.3.

Impact. The intended reading quantifies over recording schemes whose decisions depend only on the recording process's knowledge, uniformly over all executions. In that reading Theorem 5.6 holds (theorem_5_6).

Details

The paper also lets process i "check if (o₁, o₂) ∈ SCO(V)". We restrict i's knowledge to the prefix of its own view and, for each observed write of j, the prefix of V_j up to it (SameKnow). That is exactly the paper's bound "process i is aware of (o₁, o₂) ∈ V_j only if it has observed some o³ⱼ with o₁ <_{V_j} o₂ ≤_{V_j} o³ⱼ", it determines every SCO pair between operations i has observed, and it is all the proof of Theorem 5.5 uses. A scheme online for less knowledge is online in our sense, so this is the strongest form of Theorem 5.6. An oracle answering SCO queries about writes i has not observed yet would exceed this bound and would break Theorem 5.6. This remark is argued on paper only, not in Lean. Example: processes 1, 2, 3 write w₁, w₂, w₃; V₁ = w₁w₂w₃, V₂ = w₂w₁w₃, V₃ = w₁w₃w₂. Then (w₁, w₂) is a B₁ edge outside SCO₁ ∪ PO. When 1 observes w₂, the answers (w₁,w₃) ∈ SCO and (w₂,w₃) ∉ SCO reveal w₁ <_{V₃} w₂. The scheme "record V̂_i \ (SCO_i ∪ PO) except the B_i edges the oracle reveals" contains the offline record of Theorem 5.3, so it is good, yet it omits (w₁, w₂).

✏️ Slips

  • Definition 6.4 (and hence Theorem 6.6) omits w₃ ≠ w₄. Read literally, C¹_i(V, o₁, w₂) contains the self-loop (o₁, o₁) whenever o₁ is a write of i, so B_i(V) swallows such edges and Theorem 6.6's record is not good: two processes each write x once, both views order w₁ before w₀, the record is empty, yet swapping both views is a valid replay with other data races (theorem_6_6_literal_false). The intent is evident: the proof of Lemma C.1(a) uses "w³ ≠ w⁴₁, by Definition 6.4", and the definition's wording "two write operations" suggests distinct ones. With w₃ ≠ w₄ added at every level, Theorems 6.6 and 6.7 hold (theorem_6_6, theorem_6_7).

  • Lemma C.1(a), Case 1 of the proof: the record is written Â₁(V) \ (SWO(V) ∪ PO ∪ B₁(V)). The lemma's statement, and Theorem 6.6, have SWO₁(V). With SWO the four cases miss the edges in SWO(V) \ SWO₁(V), and for that record the lemma would indeed fail (lemma_C_1_SWO_record_false). With SWO₁, as stated, the case split is complete.

Typos

  • Theorem 6.7, proof, read case: (o³, w⁴) should be (w³, w⁴).

Proof gaps in true statements (4)Theorem 6.7 · Lemma C.1(b) · Theorem 5.6 · Lemma A.1(b), Case 4

🛠️ Theorem 6.7 (construction of the replay)

The proof's U₁ = A₁(V) \ {(o₁,o₂)} ∪ {(o₂,o₁)} ∪ C₁(V,o₁,o₂) is not a partial order whenever o₁ is a write of another process and o₂ a write of process 1, because then (o₁,o₂) ∈ C¹₁ (take w₃ = o₁, w₄ = o₂).

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a different set of forced constraints (theorem_6_7).

Repair

The forced constraints F are computed in the new order of process i: F⁰ uses A_i \ {(o₁,o₂)} instead of A_i, and so do the propagation steps at process i. Then F ⊆ C, so ¬B_i gives acyclicity. Every F-target z satisfies (o₂, z) ∈ F, so (o₂,o₁) closes no cycle. The strong causal pairs of U lie in SWO(V) \ {(o₁,o₂)} ∪ F. The paper's own case "o₂ a read" fits as F ⊆ SWO.

🛠️ Lemma C.1(b) (bad write pairs, Claims C.2 and C.3)

The reduction from a violated B_i edge (o, w′) to the "bad write pair" (wᵢ^min, w′) silently changes the m = i clause of Definition 6.5 (which removes a different pair), and the cycle surgery in Claim C.3 builds cycles that may use the removed pair (wᵢ¹, w²).

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a new proof (cov_preserved, lemma_C_1_b).

Repair

Pick a violated cover of some A_i(V) whose wᵢ^min u is SWO(V)-maximal, preferring one that starts at u. Claim C.2 then holds as in the paper (stepA). For Claim C.3 (stepB), one shows by induction on the level of C (lemmaX) that no cycle of (A_i \ {(u,w)}) ∪ C enters u through a C edge. Hence every A_m(V) segment of the cycle starts at a C-target other than such a u and is preserved in V'.

🔧 Theorem 5.6 (indistinguishable execution)

The proof argues that "both orders are valid for future observation" but never builds a strongly causal execution indistinguishable to process i in which the edge is not a B_i edge, which is what is needed to invoke Theorem 5.4.

Impact. Statement true (in the uniform reading above); repaired (exists_indistinguishable).

Repair

Keep every known prefix. Order all unknown operations by one global strict total order that puts known writes first, extends SCO(V) and PO, and orders w₂ before w₁. This order exists by extend_partialOrder.

🔧 Lemma A.1(b), Case 4

The proof uses the maximality of wᵢ¹ with respect to SCO(V), but derives only (wᵢ¹, o^{ρ,j}) ∈ SCO(V′).

Impact. Statement true; (wᵢ¹, o^{ρ,j}) ∈ SCO(V) holds as well, because o^{ρ,j} is a write of process 1 after wᵢ¹ in V₁ (lemma_A_1_b).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Shared memory with read/write operations on variables (Section 2). There is a finite set of operations, each with a process, a kind and a variable. Program order is a strict total order on each process's operations and is fixed across executions. Processes are an arbitrary type.

  • An execution is a writes-to map. A view of process i is a strict total order on Obs i (own operations and all writes) in which every read returns the last write before it (IsViewOf, Explains).

  • Strong causal consistency (Definitions 3.3, 3.4): SCO(V) and V_i ⊇ (SCO(V) ∪ PO|Obs i)⁺.

  • Records and replays are those of RnR Models 1 and 2 (Section 4). A replay is any execution explained by strongly causal views respecting the record. Its read values are unconstrained, and every family of total orders explains some execution (exists_explains).

  • The transitive reduction Â is the cover relation Cov. For finite strict partial orders it is the unique minimal relation generating the order (transGen_cov_eq, cov_le_of_transGen_eq).

  • Online setting (Section 5.2): a record scheme is online if its decision for (o₁, o₂) at i depends only on i's knowledge when it observes o₂ (SameKnow, IsOnline).

  • No failures, no randomness; the adversary chooses the original and the replay views.

Counterexamples.lean

Not formalized. The examples of Figures 2–10 (Sections 5.1, 6.1) and the negative examples for causal consistency (Sections 5.3, 6.2) are not in Lean. We only tested them with brute-force Python scripts, which are not part of this folder, and found that all claims there hold. This is evidence, not a verification. Before the Lean proofs were finished, the same scripts also tested Theorems 5.3/5.4 and 6.6/6.7 on all programs with up to 3 processes and 4 operations. The Lean proofs do not depend on those tests.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The counterexamples are concrete strongly causal executions, so the hypotheses of Theorems 6.6 and 6.7 are satisfiable. The model is self-contained (Mathlib only). The generic order lemmas in Basic.lean (covers, chain induction) and Completion.lean (one-process linear extension preserving SCO pairs) are CSLib candidates.
