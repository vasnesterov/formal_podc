Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Partially Replicated Causally Consistent Shared Memory — PODC 2018.

Paper: Z. Xiang, N. H. Vaidya, Partially Replicated Causally Consistent Shared Memory: Lower Bounds and An Algorithm (brief announcement, PODC 2018), full version arXiv:1703.05424v3 (29 May 2019); numbering follows the full version.

Errors in the paper

  1. Lemma 4 is false (Appendix B). 🛠️ It claims: if u' (from k to i, timestamp T') happened before u (from j to i, timestamp T), then T[e_ki] ≥ T'[e_ki] (k ≠ j).

    • As stated it is ill-typed when e_ki ∉ E_j (then T has no entry e_ki); random testing (modelcheck/lem4.py) shows that this case occurs constantly. If the missing entry is read as 0, the lemma is false there.

    • It is also false when e_ki ∈ E_j, the only reading under which it is well-typed: Lemma4.lemma_4_false / Lemma4.not_lemma4Statement (Lemma4.lean). Replicas A, B, C, D, I with X_A = {f, e, b}, X_B = {e, a}, X_C = {a, g}, X_D = {g, b}, X_I = {f, a, g}. A writes f (u'), then e; B applies and writes a; C applies and writes g; D applies and writes g (u). Then u' ↪ u, e_AI ∈ E_D (triangle D, I, A), e_AI ∉ E_C (since X_C ⊆ X_I, condition (iii) fails for every loop through C), and T[e_AI] = 0 < 1 = T'[e_AI].

    • The intended use survives: Lemma 5 and Theorem 3 are true (lemma_5, theorem_3_safety). In the example I cannot apply u before u' because B's and C's updates (on a, g) also go to I and must be applied first.

    • The proof of Lemma 4 also assumes that a causal chain from k to j together with i forms a simple loop (chains may revisit replicas or pass through i), and it applies the induction hypothesis to loops whose replicas do not carry e either.

  2. Proof of Lemma 5 (and hence of Theorem 3) has a gap (🛠️; the gap is the use of Lemma 4). Lemma 5's proof uses T[e_ki] ≤ τ_i[e_ki] from the predicate of Step 4, which only checks edges in E_i ∩ E_j, together with Lemma 4. Our replacement (Chain.lean, chain_main): strong induction on the length of a causal chain a ↪ v₀ ↪ ⋯ ↪ v_m = u.

    • A replica occurring twice ⇒ shorten the chain.

    • An intermediate update on a register of X_i ⇒ it is applied at i first (induction), and so is a (safety of the earlier step).

    • Otherwise either every replica of the chain has e_ji ∈ E_{p_q} and the counter is carried to u's timestamp, or the chain plus i is a simple loop that is not a (p_q, e_ji)-loop. Each of the three failing conditions of Definition 6 then yields an update of the chain (or a) that is sent directly to a later replica of the chain, i.e. a shorter chain.

  3. Footnote 2 is false ❌ ("our proposed algorithm in Section 3.2 actually satisfies this constraint", i.e. Constraint 1: the timestamp is a function of the causal past set). Footnote2.footnote_2_false: six replicas, X₀ = {e, b}, X₁ = {a, b}, X₂ = {a, c}, X₃ = {c, d}, X₄ = {e, d}, X₅ = {c, e, d}. In two executions replica 0 ends with the same causal past {u_c, u_d, u_e⁴, u_e⁵} (one update per writer), but τ₀[e_25] = 1 in one and 0 in the other. e_25 ∈ E₀, e_25 ∉ E₄, and u_c reaches 0 either through 5 (tracks e_25) or only through 4 (does not). Consequence: the lower bound of Section 4 (which assumes Constraint 1) does not in general apply to the paper's own algorithm. The special cases where the paper claims tightness (trees, cycles, full replication) are not formalized.

    • Identity of updates (a reading we had to choose). Updates are identified across executions by issuer, register and (unique) value, not by the attached timestamp T. If the message tuple update(i, T, x, v) (with T) were the identity, footnote 2 would be true: τ_i is the entrywise max of the timestamps of the updates applied at i, and by safety these are exactly the members of the causal past on registers of X_i. That reading does not fit Section 4. The paper calls the causal pasts produced by different executions (with algorithm-dependent timestamps) "identical" (Appendix D, Cases 1–2), and its conflict graph H_i is meant to be independent of the algorithm. So we use the value-based identity.

Theorem 1 (necessity)

Statement true; the proof needs redesigned executions (🛠️). Formalized as Necessity.theorem_1 (Necessity/Theorem1.lean).

  • Class of algorithms. "Implements causal consistency by storing and attaching metadata … when to apply is determined only using the local metadata and the attached metadata" is formalized as deterministic metadata algorithms (Necessity.Alg). The decision may also see the sender and the register, and the full metadata is attached (attaching a function of it is a special case). In this respect the class is larger, so the result is stronger. Two implicit restrictions: replicas are deterministic, and their metadata changes only on a write or an apply (receipt is merged into apply; there are no other messages). The paper's model (Section 2: replicas exchange only update messages and decide when to apply them) does not mention other algorithms. Its proof of Theorem 1 (and of Lemma 2) also treats the timestamp as fixed by the writes and applies. We judge the class faithful. Implements = safety in every reachable state and liveness (Definition 5) in every weakly fair execution. The paper's algorithm is in the class and implements causal consistency (Necessity.paperAlg_implements), so the hypothesis is satisfiable.

  • Obliviousness. The paper's definition quantifies over graphs R₁, R₂ with R₀ < R₁, R₀ < R₂, where < ranges over arbitrary executions passing through R₀. We fix a state s₀ and quantify over its extensions (Necessity.Oblivious). This is a weaker hypothesis, so the theorem is stronger. Updates are identified across executions by (value, issuer, register), using the paper's unique-value assumption. "Differ only in updates on e_jk" (DiffOnly) is the paper's (i) and (ii).

  • Which R₀. The theorem says "must not be oblivious" without fixing R₀; the proof (Appendix A) starts from an execution in which all issued updates have been applied. We prove it for every reachable quiescent state s₀ (Quiescent). [Fintype ι] (finitely many replicas) is assumed, as in the paper (R replicas).

  • Gap in Case 2. "R₁ and R₂ only differ in updates on e_ji" is false in general: applying u₁ also brings j's causal past (which may contain updates on other edges) into i's graph. Repair: a pre-phase in which j writes and i applies, then the paper's argument with two further updates u_A, u_B.

  • Gaps in Case 3. The proof says all other messages are "delayed indefinitely", but its liveness arguments then fail, because postponed dependencies are never applied. Moreover an earlier update u'_p of the path l₁ … l_s may already be sent to l_s, and in Case 3.2 the execution without u₀ loses j's causal past, so the two graphs differ by more than u₀. Repair: both executions share j's first update u_A, which k applies. Execution E1 adds one more update u_B of j on the same register. Every intermediate replica applies all updates sent to it (a deterministic closure, which liveness forces to succeed). The critical update is the first u'_p whose register is stored at k. The two executions are compared by an explicit simulation (LogSim, MdSim).

  • The paper's informal "timestamp" is the whole metadata of the replica.

Modelling choices (all statement-level choices are faithful or more general)

  • Execution model. Asynchronous, reliable, non-FIFO channels, no failures. A received update sits in pending_i until the predicate of Step 4 holds; we merge "receive" into "apply": an update may be applied at any time after it was issued. This gives exactly the paper's set of behaviours (any delivery order and delay). Liveness assumes weak fairness: an update that stays applicable is eventually applied (Execution.Fair). This encodes reliable channels plus "the replica performs Step 4 when the predicate holds".

  • Values and reads are omitted. Neither affects updates, timestamps or Definition 5.

  • ↪ (Definition 4) is defined through ghost data: each update stores the set pre of updates applied at its issuer before it was issued (condition 1), and HB is its transitive closure (condition 2). The issuer's own write counts as "applied" at the issuer, as in the algorithm (Step 2(a)).

  • Timestamps are stored as total functions ι → ι → ℕ; the algorithm only reads and writes entries in E_i (entries outside stay 0). The predicate τ_i[e_ki] = T[e_ki] − 1 is written τ_i[e_ki] + 1 = T[e_ki] (equivalent, since T[e_ki] ≥ 1 for an update of k sent to i).

  • Definition 6 (loops) is formalized with lists l = [l₁, …, l_{s−1}], r = [r₂, …, r_t]; l_s = k, r₁ = j, r_{t+1} = i; simplicity is Nodup, adjacency is List.IsChain Adj.

  • Safety (Definition 5) is stated for every reachable state; liveness as theorem_3_liveness (applied within finite time after all dependencies are applied) and the corollary eventually_applied (every update is eventually applied at every replica storing its register).

  • Lemma 3 is formalized as stated (lemma_3); internally we prove the stronger invariant that replica i has applied exactly the first τ_i[e_ji] updates on e_ji (Inv.count).

Not formalized

  • Lemma 2 and Theorem 2 (timestamp-space lower bound σ_i(m) ≥ χ(H_i) under Constraint 1). "Possible causal past", the identity of updates across executions and the spanning-tree constructions of Appendix D are informal. Theorem 2 is a one-line consequence of Lemma 2 (a colouring argument). The "Implication" bullets (trees, cycles, full replication) are stated without proof.

  • Section 3.3 (comparison with Hélary–Milani), Appendix C (numerics), Appendix E (optimizations), Section 5 / Appendix F (client–server architecture, Theorem 4).
