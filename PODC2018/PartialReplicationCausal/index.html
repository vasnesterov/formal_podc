Partially Replicated Causally Consistent Shared Memory — PODC 2018

Zhuolun Xiang, Nitin H. Vaidya. Brief Announcement: Partially Replicated Causally Consistent Shared Memory. PODC 2018. DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212790. Formalized version: arXiv 1703.05424 v3 (full version, …: Lower Bounds and An Algorithm); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The paper's necessary and sufficient condition survives: the edge-indexed timestamp algorithm achieves replica-centric causal consistency (Theorem 3), and tracking every edge of the timestamp graph is necessary (Theorem 1). Both take new arguments: Lemma 4, on which the correctness proof rests, is false (also in its only well-typed reading), and the executions in the proof of Theorem 1 had to be redesigned. Footnote 2's claim that the algorithm satisfies Constraint 1 of the lower-bound section is also false, but it is a side remark nothing is derived from, and the paper's third contribution, the timestamp-size lower bound, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Footnote 2 (the algorithm satisfies Constraint 1)

Footnote 2's claim that the algorithm's timestamp is a function of the causal past (a set of updates) is false: on six replicas, two executions give replica 0 the same causal past but different τ₀[e_25] (Footnote2.footnote_2_false).

Impact. The lower bound of Section 4 (which assumes Constraint 1) does not apply to the paper's algorithm in general. Nothing is proved in place of the footnote.

Details

Take six replicas with X₀ = {e,b}, X₁ = {a,b}, X₂ = {a,c}, X₃ = {c,d}, X₄ = {e,d}, X₅ = {c,e,d}. Replicas 2, 3, 4, 5 write once each. In both executions replica 0 ends up with the same causal past (all four updates). In one, 2's update reaches 0 through 5, which tracks e_25; in the other, only through 4, which does not. So τ₀[e_25] is 1 vs. 0.

🛠️ Lemma 4 (Appendix B, timestamps along causal chains), and with it the proofs of Lemma 5 and Theorem 3

Lemma 4 (if u' from k to i with timestamp T' happened before u from j to i with timestamp T, then T[e_ki] ≥ T'[e_ki]) is false even when e_ki ∈ E_j, its only well-typed reading, on five replicas (Lemma4.lemma_4_false, Lemma4.not_lemma4Statement).

Impact. The correctness proof of Theorem 3 rests on it, through Lemma 5, but Theorem 3 is true: we prove it by a new argument (chain_main, lemma_5, theorem_3_safety). Safety is not violated in the counterexample.

Details

When e_ki ∉ E_j, T has no such entry, and the proof of Lemma 5 nevertheless applies the lemma there. Even when e_ki ∈ E_j it fails. Take five replicas with X_A = {f,e,b}, X_B = {e,a}, X_C = {a,g}, X_D = {g,b}, X_I = {f,a,g}. A writes f (u', delayed on its way to I), then e. B applies e and writes a; C applies that and writes g; D applies that and writes g (u). Then u' ↪ u and e_AI ∈ E_D (triangle D, I, A). But e_AI ∉ E_C (X_C ⊆ X_I, so condition (iii) fails on every loop through C), so C drops the information and T[e_AI] = 0 < 1 = T'[e_AI]. Safety is not violated: B's and C's updates also go to I and must be applied first. The lemma's proof also assumes that causal chains form simple loops.

Repair

We replace it by a strong induction on the length of a causal chain a ↪ v₀ ↪ ⋯ ↪ v_m = u. If a replica repeats, or an intermediate update also goes to the receiver i, the chain gets shorter (the latter by safety of earlier steps). Otherwise, either every replica on the chain tracks e_ji and the counter reaches u's timestamp, or the chain closes with i into a simple loop that is not a (p_q, e_ji)-loop. Each of the three failing conditions of Definition 6 then exhibits an update sent directly further down the chain, which again shortens it.

In Case 2 applying u₁ also brings j's whole causal past into i's graph, so the two graphs differ in more than updates on e_ji, and Case 3 delays all other messages "indefinitely", which breaks its liveness arguments.

Impact. The statement holds for every deterministic metadata algorithm and every quiescent state R₀ (Necessity.theorem_1), with redesigned executions.

Details

Also, in Case 3 an earlier update on the path l₁ … l_s may already reach k, and in Case 3.2 the execution without u₀ loses j's causal past.

Repair

Both executions share a first update u_A of j, which k applies, and one execution adds a second update u_B. Every relay applies all updates sent to it (liveness forces this). The critical message is the first path update stored at k. The two executions are compared by an explicit simulation.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Peer-to-peer architecture with R replicas (ι), registers Reg, and replica i storing X i. Asynchronous, reliable, non-FIFO channels, no failures, deterministic replicas. The scheduler (delivery order, delays, client writes) is adversarial. Liveness is under weak fairness: an update that stays applicable is eventually applied.

  • "Receive" is merged into "apply": an update can be applied at any time after it was issued, if the predicate of Step 4 holds. Values and reads are omitted (irrelevant to Definition 5).

  • ↪ (Definition 4) is defined through a ghost set pre per update: the updates applied at the issuer before it was issued.

  • Theorem 1 quantifies over all deterministic metadata algorithms (Necessity.Alg). A replica keeps metadata, attaches it to its updates, and decides when to apply a received update from its metadata, the sender, the register and the attached metadata only (Section 2). The decision may see sender and register, which enlarges the class. The class assumes deterministic replicas whose metadata changes only when they write or apply an update (not on receipt). The paper's model (Section 2) does not discuss other algorithms, and its proofs assume this.
