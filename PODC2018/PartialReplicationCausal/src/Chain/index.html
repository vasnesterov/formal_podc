The paper argues via Lemma 4 (which is false, see `Lemma4.lean`). We argue on a causal chain `a ↪ v₀ ↪ v₁ ↪ ⋯ ↪ v_m = u` of direct links, by strong induction on its length `m`: * if a replica occurs twice on the chain, or some intermediate update of the chain writes a register stored at `i`, the chain can be shortened (using safety of earlier steps); * otherwise either every replica of the chain has `e_ji` (`j` the issuer of `a`) in its timestamp graph — then the counter `τ[e_ji]` carries the information about `a` along the chain up to `u`'s timestamp, and the predicate of Step 4 forces `a` to be applied first — or some replica `p_q` of the chain does not; the chain then forms a simple loop `(p_q, p_{q+1}, …, p_m, i, j = p_0, p_1, …, p_{q-1})` which is not a `(p_q, e_ji)`-loop, and each of the three failing conditions of Definition 6 yields a shorter chain.
