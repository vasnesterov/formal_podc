Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Beeping a Time-Optimal Leader Election — PODC 2018.

Paper: F. Dufoulon, J. Burman, J. Beauquier, Beeping a Time-Optimal Leader Election, PODC 2018 brief announcement (DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212779). Formalized version: the full version Beeping a Deterministic Time-Optimal Leader Election, DISC 2018 (LIPIcs 121, paper 20); numbering follows it. Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 are "adopted" from [5] = Casteigts, Métivier, Robson, Zemmari (DISC 2016; we used arXiv:1605.01903v4, Deterministic Leader Election Takes Θ(D + log n) Bit Rounds, Lemmas 2, 5, 6, Corollary 7, Theorem 8).

Mistakes (labels of mistakes-labeling.md)

  1. 🔧 Lemma 1 is false for Algorithm 1 as printed. The paper evaluates rule 1 strictly before rule 2; [5] (rule R1) applies whichever of R1.1/R1.2 deletes more letters. With the paper's order, on the 7-node path with identifiers 152, 1, 3, 2, 144, 4, 148, after 18 phases nodes 2 and 3 hold 111111110100110 and 111111110100100 (same length 15, differing at bit 14), whereas Lemma 1 allows differences only from bit 15 on (Counterexample.lemma_1_false, not_lemma1_at_18). They still differ only in the last 6 bits, so the looser reading "identical except in the last ≤ 6 bits" survives here. Repair: take the largest deletion among rules 1 and 2 (Priority.maxDelete), as in [5]. Then Lemma 1 (lemma_1, via the five forms of Lemma 6 of [5], form_step, invariants), Theorem 2 and Theorem 3 hold. Whether Theorem 2 (or the looser Lemma 1 that Section 3.1.3 needs) holds for the printed priority is unchecked. In random simulations (20 000 runs, paths, trees and sparse graphs with up to 12 nodes) the looser relation and Theorem 2's bound were never violated, but our per-edge proof method does not apply: the per-edge abstraction for the printed order is not closed (it diverges).

  2. ✏️ Section 3.1.2, decoding formula of k-balanced messages. The printed formula m_u = m_v + r_u − r_v − ⌊(r_u − r_v)/(k+1)⌋·M (floor) is wrong whenever the floor and the truncation toward 0 differ. Example from the paper's own Table 2 (k = 4, m_v = 19, r_u = 0): it gives 27, not 18 (decodeFloor_table2, decodeFloor_false); also m_v = 17, m_u = 19 gives 28 (decodeFloor_wrap; exact characterization decodeFloor_eq). Table 2 uses truncation, and with truncation the formula is correct for all |m_u − m_v| ≤ k (decodeTrunc_correct). Slip: the intended version is in the paper (Table 2).

Modelling choices and other differences

  • Words, order, α. Words are List Bool, most significant bit first. The paper's "higher" order (x ≻ y: y is a proper prefix of x, or x has 1 where y has 0 at the first difference) is exactly the lexicographic order on List Bool (a total order), used as Mathlib's LinearOrder (List Bool). α(i) = 1^{|bin i|} 0 bin i; proved: strictly monotone (alpha_lt_alpha_iff), prefix-free (alpha_prefix_alpha), |α(i)| = 2⌊log₂ i⌋ + 3, decoding alphaInv.

  • Rule ties not specified by the paper. When several neighbours trigger rule 1 (or rule 2) with different amounts, we delete the largest amount (as [5] does). The paper's counterexample run has no such tie (each rule's amount is unique at each step), so the counterexample does not depend on this choice.

  • Phase-level model. A diffusion phase is modelled as: every node receives the set received G C v of (prefix, suspicious) pairs of its neighbours (no senders, no multiplicities), then applies ruleStep. The beep-level implementation of Section 3.1.3 is proved to simulate it (see README).

  • Candidates. A node becomes a follower as soon as any of rules 1–5 fires (the paper's text). [5] makes a node a follower only on a change (R2), but between a deletion and the following change nothing else can happen, so this does not matter for the prefixes.

  • Followers with no rule triggered. The paper's prose says a node whose prefix is locally highest "appends a bit from its α-id" without restricting this to candidates; we let only candidates append (as R5 of [5] does). The counterexample run is unchanged under the other reading (and under either tie rule), checked by an independent simulation.

  • Own bit. A candidate with no rule triggered appends α(id)[|prefix|] while prefix is a proper prefix of α(id) (the paper: "appends a bit from its α-id"). The output leader is candidate ∧ prefix = α(id) (lines 9–10) and leaderId = α⁻¹(prefix).

  • Theorem 2 constant. We prove the bound |α(X)| + 2r (the constant of [5], Theorem 8), which implies the paper's |α(X)| + 6r (theorem_2_paper). Theorem 2 needs no uniqueness of identifiers for the max-identifier node l (only ident v ≤ ident l for all v); the "unique candidate" part and Theorem 3 assume injective identifiers.

  • Lemma 1 needs nothing. lemma_1 holds for every graph (connected or not) and every identifier assignment.

  • Identifier range. The paper assumes ids in {1, …, N}, N = n^c; we state complexities in terms of X (the maximum id): |α(X)| = 2⌊log₂ X⌋ + 3 = O(log N).

Not formalized

  • Section 3.2 (Algorithm 2: overlays, termination detection; Lemmas 4, 5; Theorem 6: uniform explicit leader election in O(D + log n) rounds) — unchecked. Only informal evidence: a phase-level simulation of Algorithm 2 under our reading of the informal description (border nodes after more than 6 phases in the overlay, relays one level per phase, roots decide from phase |α(id)| + 6 on) elected exactly the maximum-identifier node in 7 000 random runs (both rule priorities), and joins happened exactly at phase |α(id_r)| + 6d (Lemma 4's claim) in 3 000 runs.

  • Section 3.3 (arbitrary id space, subsets of candidates, non-unique ids), the wake-on-beep variant of Section 2.2, and Section 4 (randomized LE, MIS and 5-colouring of trees, multi-broadcast).

  • Lower bounds quoted from the literature (Ω(D + log n)).
