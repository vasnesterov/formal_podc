Beeping a Time-Optimal Leader Election — PODC 2018

Fabien Dufoulon, Janna Burman, Joffroy Beauquier. Brief Announcement: Beeping a Time-Optimal Leader Election. PODC 2018. DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212779. Formalized version: the full version Beeping a Deterministic Time-Optimal Leader Election, DISC 2018 (LIPIcs 121, paper 20); numbering follows it. Lemma 1 and Theorem 2 are adopted from Casteigts, Métivier, Robson, Zemmari [5]; for their proofs we used arXiv:1605.01903v4.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Lemma 1 is false for Algorithm 1 as printed, because rule 1 is given priority over rule 2. With the rule of [5] (delete the larger amount) Lemma 1, Theorem 2 (even with 2r instead of 6r phases) and the O(D + log n)-round eventual leader election of Theorem 3 are proved, including the O(1)-round beeping implementation of a diffusion phase. The decoding formula for k-balanced messages has a floor that should be a truncation. The paper's headline result, the terminating (explicit) election in O(D + log n) rounds of Section 3.2 (Lemmas 4–5, Theorem 6), was not checked; the label covers Section 3.1 (Algorithm 1, the eventual election it builds on) only.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 1 (Algorithm 1, rule priority)

With the paper's priority (rule 1 strictly before rule 2), Lemma 1 fails: on the 7-node path with identifiers 152, 1, 3, 2, 144, 4, 148, after 18 phases the neighbours 2 and 3 hold 111111110100110 and 111111110100100, two words of length 15 that differ at bit 14, while Lemma 1 allows differences only from bit 15 on (lemma_1_false).

Impact. Theorem 2's proof and the constant-size communication phase of Section 3.1.3 both rest on Lemma 1. With the deletion rule of [5] (the larger of the two deletions) all of Section 3.1 is proved.

Details

In phase 18, node 3 holds 1111111101001000. Its neighbour 4 is suspicious and holds 111111110100100, a proper prefix, so rule 1 deletes one letter. Its neighbour 2 holds 11111111010011, which branches higher two letters before the end, so rule 2 would delete two. The paper applies rule 1, and node 2 appends a bit meanwhile. [5] (rule R1) deletes the larger amount, and then node 3 would hold 11111111010010. The paper says it uses "the same constant-size set of changes" as [5], so Lemma 8 of [5] would carry over, but it changed how rules R1.1 and R1.2 combine. The two words still differ only within their last 6 bits, so the looser reading "identical except in the last ≤ 6 bits" survives in this run. Whether that looser relation (which Section 3.1.3 needs) and Theorem 2 hold for the printed priority is unchecked. Random simulations found no violation, but the per-edge invariant method below does not apply to it.

Repair

Rules 1 and 2 delete the larger of their amounts (Priority.maxDelete). The five forms of neighbouring prefixes of [5] (Lemma 6) are invariant (form_step, invariants). Lemma 1 then holds on every graph (lemma_1), and so do Theorem 2 (theorem_2, theorem_2_paper) and Theorem 3 (theorem_3_phases, theorem_3).

✏️ Slips

  • Section 3.1.2: the decoding formula m_u = m_v + r_u − r_v − ⌊(r_u − r_v)/(k+1)⌋·M is wrong with a floor. On the paper's own Table 2 (k = 4, m_v = 19, r_u = 0) it gives 27 instead of 18 (decodeFloor_table2, decodeFloor_eq). Table 2 rounds toward zero, and with truncation the formula is correct (decodeTrunc_correct).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous beeping model (BEEP) with a synchronous start, deterministic, no failures. In each round a node beeps or listens. A listener learns only whether at least one neighbour beeped; a beeping node learns nothing. The graph is static and arbitrary (SimpleGraph V, V finite). Nodes have identifiers (positive, unique where stated) and know nothing about n, D or N.

  • Phase level (Algorithm.lean): in a diffusion phase every node receives the set of its neighbours' (prefix, suspicious) pairs, without senders or multiplicities, and applies rules 1–5 (ruleStep). Priority.paper is the printed rule order, Priority.maxDelete the repair.

  • Beep level (Beeping.lean): a diffusion phase is a communication phase of c = 1664 rounds. A node beeps in the slot of its message (|prefix| mod 13, last 6 bits, suspicious), listens in the others, and decodes the heard slots relative to its own prefix. A node cannot hear neighbours that use its own slot, which is harmless because its own pair never triggers a rule (ruleStep_erase_self). Decoding is correct whenever Lemma 1 holds (decode_encode), and the beep-level run reproduces the phase-level run (beepRun_phase).

  • The paper's order "higher" is the lexicographic order on List Bool, which is total, so the rules and the α-encoding use it consistently (alpha_lt_alpha_iff, alpha_prefix_alpha).
