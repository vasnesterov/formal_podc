Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Replicated List: the Jupiter Protocol Revisited — PODC 2018.

Paper: Wei, Huang, Lu, Specification and Implementation of Replicated List: The Jupiter Protocol Revisited, arXiv:1708.04754v4 (full version of the PODC 2018 brief announcement).

Statement-level issues

  1. 🔧 Out-of-range positions break CP1 (Section 2.3 + Section 2.4 + Figure A.1). Section 2.3 explicitly allows Ins(a, p) / Del(p) with p beyond the list size ("insertion/deletion at the end"). Section 2.4 says that Figure A.1 gives "OT functions satisfying CP1". The clients send the user operation unchanged (Alg. B.5 line 9, Alg. C.5). With such positions CP1 fails (cp1_fails). Jupiter and CJupiter then diverge, so Theorem 11, Theorem 18 and the solution of the conjecture are false for the literal model (jupiter_raw_counterexample, cjupiter_raw_counterexample). The failure of A_weak holds for every well-formed abstract execution complying with the run, not only for our choice of vis (cjupiter_raw_not_weak_any, jupiter_raw_not_weak_any): c₁'s final Read misses c₁'s own insertion. (The failure of A_cp uses vis = happened-before; with an arbitrary vis the two Reads need not see the same updates.) Theorem 12 (equivalence) is not refuted: both protocols diverge in the same way (cex_run, cex_run_cjupiter). The fix is local: normalise the position when the operation is generated (genOp), i.e. one extra line in Do. The paper itself shows that this is intended: it requires transformed operations to "insert (resp. delete) the element a", and the OT functions it cites assume in-range positions. With the fix all statements hold. The paper's proofs only use CP1 abstractly, the fix is a missing case in the Do pseudocode, and the intended version can be recovered from the paper, although the out-of-range convention is stated explicitly: the paper states the intended behaviour itself (Appendix A: a transformed Ins(a, p)/Del(a, p) still inserts/deletes the element a, which presupposes in-range positions), and no proof of the paper reasons with out-of-range positions (CP1 is cited, not proved). The convention is the paper's own definition and the fix changes the protocol, but we judge the normalisation too evident to be a protocol design change.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. Lemmas 22–25 / Theorem 26. These are sketches: 🛠️ Lemma 23's inductive step; 🛠️ Lemma 24 Case 2.2 ("step ladder"); Lemma 22 ("parallel transitions"). 🔧 The one-step claim in the proof of Lemma 25 requires that the operation "not correspond to any element of the list in v". This excludes deletions of common elements, which the induction needs. We replace Section 5's argument (see README):

    • the vertices of the global space are the causally closed sets of the form "a ≺s-prefix plus operations of one client" (Sched.Vert);

    • these sets are closed under intersection (Sched.Vert.inter), so V₁ ∩ V₂ plays the role of the unique LCA with disjoint paths (Lemmas 23–24);

    • lists along a path preserve the relative order of surviving elements (Sched.sublist_of_subset, Lemma 25);

    • common elements of two lists were inserted in V₁ ∩ V₂ (Sched.mem_L).

  2. 🔧 Remark after Definition 19. "By definition, lo is transitive on {a | a ∈ w}" is false without pairwise compatibility. It follows from Theorem 26 (used in Cfg.GI.weak).

  3. Proposition 9 / Lemma 5 / Propositions 14, 16. These are proved by induction sketches; the hard case of Prop. 9 (Case 2, an operation in the middle of the client's sequence) is only illustrated. We prove instead:

    • CJupiter: every replica's state space is sound and complete with respect to the global space (CJup.CPI, CJup.walk);

    • Jupiter: every edge of every 2D state space is an edge of the global space (Jup.JPI), and the edges each xForm needs exist.

    We do not prove the literal statements "CSS_s = ⋃ DSS_s^i" and "DSS_ci ⊆ CSS_ci" as equalities or inclusions of graphs. Our invariants imply what Theorems 12 and 18 need.

  4. ✏️ Footnote 8. It defines the LCAs as an antichain of common ancestors and does not require them to be lowest. Our proof does not need LCAs.

Modelling choices

  1. Elements. Inserted elements are the operation identifiers (client, seq). The paper assumes unique elements "achieved by attaching replica identifiers and sequence numbers"; user content plays no role in the specification.

  2. Priorities. The priority of an insertion is its client id, and Figure A.1's rule is taken literally (larger pr is shifted). Appendix A's prose says the smaller id has the higher priority, which contradicts that rule. With our convention, the schedule of Figure 1 ends in ab instead of ba. Any fixed convention works.

  3. OT with NOP. Figure A.1 omits these cases. We use OT(NOP, o) = NOP and OT(o, NOP) = o.

  4. Del on an empty list. It is recorded as Del(⊥, k) in the do event and sent as NOP. The paper leaves this case undefined.

  5. Read. It generates a NOP operation that goes through the server, as Do does in Algorithms B.5/C.5 for every user operation. Reads do not change any list.

  6. Visibility. The abstract execution uses vis(e, e') ⇔ e is earlier and its operation was processed by the replica of e' when e' happened. We prove that this is a well-formed visibility relation (AbsExec.WF). We do not prove that it coincides with Lamport's happened-before, which is the vis the proof of Theorem 18 picks. The specification only asks for some well-formed vis.

  7. Executions. Executions are finite sequences of enabled events. FIFO reliable channels are built into the model, and fairness is not modelled. Non-vacuity: every handler succeeds whenever its channel is non-empty (*_enabled). The replicas agree once all channels are empty (jupiter_quiescent).

  8. Data structures.

    • A state space is the list of its edges plus the current vertex.

    • Locate is implicit: the loop starts from op.ctx.

    • In Jupiter, "the edge of u in dimension d" is the latest added matching edge, which models the array overwrite of Algorithm C.4.

    • In CJupiter, firstEdge returns the edge that Compare ranks before all other edges of the vertex. Compare is not a priori a total order.

    • The loops run with fuel |cur|; we prove the fuel suffices.

  9. Theorem 12 ("behaviors are the same"). It is formalized as follows: for every event sequence, both protocols accept it or neither does, and they reach configurations with the same do events (replica, operation, returned list), the same knowledge, server order and identifiers in transit, and the same list at every replica (equivalence). Since this holds for every prefix, the behaviours coincide step by step.

  10. Proof device. The global state space (Sched.T, Sched.L) is defined directly from a schedule. The schedule's ranks come from the final configuration of the execution (a prophecy), not from a constructed graph.
