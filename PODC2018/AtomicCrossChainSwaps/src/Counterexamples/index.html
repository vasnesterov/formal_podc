import Papers.PODC2018.AtomicCrossChainSwaps.Liveness /-! # Counterexamples for the protocol of §4.5 read literally 1. `literal_not_live`: on the paper's own three-party example (3-cycle `A → B → C → A`, leader `A`), with the paper's hashkey timeouts `(diam(D) + |p|)Δ` (`B = diam D = 3`, or `B = 2` under the reading "longest path between distinct vertices"), the all-conforming execution triggers no arc at all: Alice can present her own hashkey `(s, (A), sig)` only at `4Δ`, after it expired at `3Δ`. (Lemma 4.6 / Theorem 4.7 / the first clause of uniformity fail.) 2. `literal_unsafe_refwd` (an *ambiguity*, not an error of the paper: it relies on a leader forwarding foreign keys while still in Phase One, which the proof of Theorem 4.9 rules out): even with the timeouts enlarged by one `Δ` (`B = diam + 1 = 4`), on the two-leader digraph of Figure 7 (complete digraph on `A, B, C`, leaders `A, B`), a single deviating leader `A` (no front-running) makes the conforming leader `B` end Underwater: `A` unlocks its hashlock on `B → A` before publishing its contract `A → B`, so `B` forwards `A`'s key "the first time" while `(A, B)` has no contract yet, and never again. 3. `literal_unsafe_late`: with `B = diam + 1 = 4` on the three-party example, a coalition `{B, C}` publishes `C → A` at the last moment; Alice (conforming) releases her secret too late, her own call fails, but it is public, and `B` uses it with a longer (later-expiring) hashkey path `(B, C, A)` to collect Alice's asset (no front-running needed).
