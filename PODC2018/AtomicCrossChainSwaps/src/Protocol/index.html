import Papers.PODC2018.AtomicCrossChainSwaps.Atomicity /-! # §4: the hashed-timelock swap protocol as an executable model **Time.** Discrete, in units of `Δ`; the protocol starts at time `0`. In round `t` every party observes the state of all blockchains at time `t` and submits actions; they take effect at time `t + 1` (one `Δ` = publish + confirm, as in Figures 1–2 of the paper: Alice's first contract is in place at `Δ`). Conforming parties are thus lock-step. **Contracts.** One contract per arc, published by the arc's head; we only model correct contracts (a conforming party treats an incorrect one as missing). Every arc carries one hashlock per leader `ℓ ∈ L`. A call `unlock(ℓ, p)` on arc `(u, v)`, made by the counterparty `v`, succeeds iff the contract is already published, `p` is a path (paper's §2.1 sense) from `v` to `ℓ`, and the call takes effect no later than the deadline `B + |p|` (the paper has `B = diam D`; Figure 2 triggers contracts exactly at their timeouts, so the deadline is inclusive). An arc is triggered when all its hashlocks are unlocked (`claim` has no deadline). **Keys and the adversary.** Hashkeys are symbolic: a key for `ℓ` is the path `p` (the secret `s_ℓ` with the nested signatures of the vertices of `p`). Every submitted key is public (the contract call is on a public ledger), including keys of failed calls, and so are all its suffixes (the inner signatures). The deviating coalition `C` shares knowledge and can extend a key it knows by signing as any of its members, and knows the secrets of its leaders; it can never sign for a conforming party (unforgeable signatures). It publishes contracts on its own arcs whenever it likes (or never) and makes arbitrary unlock calls on arcs whose counterparty it controls. If `rush` is set, its calls in round `t` may already use keys that conforming parties submit in the same round (front-running). **Conforming behaviour** (§4.5). A leader publishes contracts on all its leaving arcs in round `0`; a follower does so in the first round in which all its entering arcs have contracts. A leader releases its secret (calls `unlock(ℓ, (ℓ))` on all entering arcs) in the first round in which all its entering arcs have contracts. A party `v` that sees hashlock `ℓ ≠ v` unlocked on a leaving arc by key `p` (for the first time) calls `unlock(ℓ, v + p)` on its entering arcs. Two variants: `fixed = false` is the paper's protocol read literally (the forwarding is done once, at the first observation, on the entering arcs that have contracts at that moment). `fixed = true` is our repair: a leader releases its secret only if its own key can still meet its deadline (`t + 1 ≤ B`), and forwarding is repeated for entering contracts that appear later (while the forwarded key is
