The origin (processor `0`, the paper's processor 1) sends its secret at initialization and then forwards its first `n - 1` incoming messages; a normal processor buffers: upon each of its first `n` incoming messages it sends the content of its one-message buffer (initially its own secret) and stores the new message. After `n` incoming messages a processor checks that the `n`-th one is its own secret (else it aborts with `⊥`) and outputs the sum of the `n` incoming messages modulo `n`. As sequences, both kinds of processors send `d i` followed by their first `n - 1` incoming messages; they differ only in timing (the origin is one message ahead).
