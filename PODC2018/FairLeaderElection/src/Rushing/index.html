import Papers.PODC2018.FairLeaderElection.Characterization /-! # Rushing attacks on `A-LEADuni` (Section 4 of [YM18]): the generic attacker The attacks of Lemma 4.1 and of the Cubic Attack (Theorem 4.3) are instances of one *rushing* adversary strategy with parameters `F` (number of forwarded messages), `z` (number of zeros sent after them) and the length `l` of its honest segment. An adversary `a` never draws a secret: 1. it forwards its first `F` incoming messages (immediately); 2. after its `F`-th incoming message (at wake-up if `F = 0`) it sends `z` zeros; 3. after its `(n-k)`-th incoming message (`k = |C|`; it then knows all `n-k` honest secrets) it sends `M = w - (sum of everything else it sends)`, then `n - F - z - 1 - l` zeros, then the last `l` of its incoming messages (the secrets of its own segment, in the right order), and terminates with output `w`. Lemma 4.1 is `F = n - k`, `z = 0`; the Cubic Attack is `F = n - k - l`, `z = k - 1`. This file proves the general theorem `rushing_outcome`: if the origin is honest and the parameters satisfy the content conditions `n - k - l_a ≤ F_a ≤ n - k`, the budget `F_a + z_a + 1 + l_a ≤ n`, and the liveness conditions (some adversary `a*` has `n - k ≤ F_{a*} + z_{a*}`, and every other adversary `a` passes enough messages to the next one: `F_{next a} ≤ F_a + z_a`), then the outcome is `w` for every choice of the honest secrets.
