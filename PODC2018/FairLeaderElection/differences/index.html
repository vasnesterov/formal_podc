Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Fair Leader Election for Rational Agents — PODC 2018.

Paper: A. Yifrach, Y. Mansour. Fair Leader Election for Rational Agents in Asynchronous Rings and Networks. PODC 2018; arXiv:1805.04778v2 (the version formalized; numbering as there).

Model (Section 2)

  1. Asynchrony. The unidirectional ring is the shared model Cslib.UniRing (Podc/Computability/Distributed/UniRing/Basic.lean): deterministic processors whose behaviour is a function of the received history (cumulative sent list, monotone; optional termination output), FIFO links, oblivious schedules σ : ℕ → Fin n (a step delivers the next pending message to σ t, a no-op if there is none), fair = every processor scheduled infinitely often. Every fair asynchronous execution is of this form. The paper's remark "all message schedules are equivalent" on a unidirectional ring is proved (exists_le_run, terminates_iff, outcomeUnder_eq), and most statements are about the schedule-free outcome (equal to outcomeUnder σ for every fair σ).

  2. Wake-up. Every processor runs its initialization (it may send at wake-up), as in the paper's pseudocode where every processor has Init(). The paper's text says only the origin wakes up spontaneously; honest normal processors send nothing at initialization, so this only gives the adversaries more power, and it is used by the Cubic Attack (see item 12).

  3. Messages and identities. Messages are values in Fin n (honest processors reduce incoming values mod n anyway); processors are Fin n, the origin is 0 (the paper's processor 1); ids are consecutive along the ring (as the paper assumes for A-LEADuni in Sections 3–5).

  4. Randomness. Each honest processor draws its secret d i uniformly from Fin n; probabilities are counted over Fin n → Fin n. A protocol is a family of profiles indexed by a finite uniform randomness type (Game.lean).

  5. Deviations are deterministic (adversary strategies do not depend on any randomness). All our negative results exhibit deterministic deviations, which is enough. The positive results (claim_D_1, isUnbiased_aLead_one, isResilient_aLead_one) are proved against deterministic deviations only; for unbiasedness this is equivalent to allowing randomized deviations (a randomized deviation is a mixture of deterministic ones), for resilience (Def. 2.3, which asks for some coalition member not to gain) it is in general weaker.

  6. Outcome. As in the paper, outcome = v iff all processors (including adversaries) terminate with output v; otherwise FAIL.

  7. Resilience / unbiasedness (Def. 2.3 and the definition before Lemma 2.4) quantify over fair schedules, rational utilities (u FAIL = 0, values in [0,1]), coalitions of size exactly k and deviations. FLE: every leader with probability exactly 1/n under every fair schedule. The paper defines an FLE protocol as a symmetric protocol; IsFLE does not require symmetry (no restriction, since strategies may depend on the id, and A-LEADuni is a fixed protocol).

Section 2

  1. Lemma 2.4 is proved as stated, with the implicit side conditions made explicit: the second direction needs k ≥ 1 (a coalition member must exist) and ε ≥ 0 (lemma_2_4).

  2. New observation (used below): for every FLE protocol, rational utility and deviation, the gain is at most 1 − 1/n (expU_le_add_one_sub), so every FLE protocol is (1 − 1/n)-k-resilient for every k ≥ 1 (isResilient_one_sub_inv).

Section 3 and Appendix A

  1. The origin's pseudocode (Appendix A) is off by one: round starts at 1, so the origin checks its (n−1)-th incoming message against d_1 and sums only n − 1 values; the honest execution would then abort whenever d_1 ≠ d_2 (for n ≥ 2). We follow the verbal description of Section 3 (the origin sends d_1, forwards n − 1 messages, and validates its n-th incoming message), i.e. round should start at 0. Not formalized as a counterexample (typo-level).

  2. Lemma 3.3 is imprecise as stated. (a) "The execution succeeds (outcome ≠ FAIL)" must be read as "all honest processors terminate with the same valid output": outcome also requires the adversaries to terminate with that output, which conditions 1–3 do not constrain. (b) "Sends n messages" means at least n, and "its last l_j messages" means the messages n − l_j + 1, …, n (honest processors process only their first n messages). (c) "Sums of all the outgoing messages" means sums of the first n messages. (d) The proof's case analysis swaps the numbering of conditions 1 and 2. Our lemma_3_3 states: all honest processors terminate with output v iff in some reachable configuration (equivalently, lemma_3_3_run, at some time of any fair run) every exposed adversary has sent ≥ n messages, the first n sum to v, and the messages n, n−1, …, n−l+1 are the secrets of its segment. Requires a nonempty coalition (for C = ∅ the honest execution succeeds, outcome_aLead).

  3. Lemmas 3.4/3.5 are formalized in list form (lemma_3_4, lemma_3_5: the histories of the segment processors are seg m e s) and used in Lemma 3.3 through segment_hist/segment_pump.

  4. Correctness of A-LEADuni (implicit in the paper) is proved: the honest execution elects ∑ d_i (outcome_aLead) and the protocol is FLE (isFLE_aLead).

Section 4 and Appendix C

  1. Lemma 4.1 needs the origin to be honest. The paper states it for every coalition with segments of length ≤ k − 1, but its attack (all adversaries just forward) never starts if the origin is an adversary: nobody sends anything and the outcome is FAIL (lemma_4_1_attack_deadlocks). We prove Lemma 4.1 under the extra hypothesis 0 ∉ C (lemma_4_1). We believe a modified attack (the adversarial origin injects one dummy message) proves the lemma in general, but this is not formalized. Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 are unaffected (the coalition is chosen with the origin honest, as the paper does in Section 4.3).

  2. Theorem 4.2 is proved as stated (theorem_4_2' with √n ≤ k, theorem_4_2 with n ≤ k²), for k ≤ n. The "equally spaced" coalition is replaced by any placement with segments of length ≤ k − 1, the origin honest (greedy segment lengths fillGaps, positions place); for k = n the coalition is everybody and outputs w at once (outcome_univ).

  3. Theorem 4.3 (Cubic Attack) is proved as stated (theorem_4_3' with 2∛n ≤ k, theorem_4_3 with 8n ≤ k³, k ≤ n). Differences in the construction: the segment lengths satisfy the paper's relaxed conditions (l_k ≤ k − 1, l_i ≤ l_{i+1} + k − 1, total n − k), chosen greedily from the end below the caps (k − 1)(k + 1 − i); all k adversaries attack (the paper picks a minimal k ≤ k' and lets the other k' − k adversaries play honestly — not needed). Minor slips in the paper: ∑_{i=1}^{k'−1}(k'−1)i is written (k'−1)k'(k'+1)/2 instead of (k'−1)²k'/2 (harmless); the proof of Lemma 4.4 starts with "since l_1 = max_i l_i", which does not follow from the relaxed conditions (our liveness proof does not need it).

  4. Cubic Attack pseudocode (Appendix C): the k − 1 zeros are sent only when count == n − k − l_i after incrementing count, which never happens when n − k − l_i = 0; the attack then deadlocks (e.g. n = 4, k = 2, l = (2, 0)). The verbal description ("transfer n − k − l_i messages, then send k − 1 zeros") sends them at wake-up, which we do.

  5. Lemmas 4.4 and 4.5 are proved for a general rushing attack (forward F_a messages, send z_a zeros, after n − k messages send M, zeros and the last l_a messages) under explicit content/budget conditions (Params) and liveness conditions (Live); both Lemma 4.1's attack and the Cubic Attack are instances (rushing_outcome, lemma_4_4_4_5). The termination proof (Lemma 4.4) is a counting argument on the stuck configuration (reach_finalCfg), not the paper's round-by-round narrative.

  6. Corollary 4.6 is false at the boundary ε = 1 − 1/n: the paper states it for 0 ≤ ε ≤ 1 − 1/n, but by item 9 A-LEADuni (like every FLE protocol) is (1 − 1/n)-k-resilient (corollary_4_6_boundary_false). Proved for 0 ≤ ε < 1 − 1/n (corollary_4_6, corollary_4_6'; Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 2.4).

  7. Appendix C (randomly located adversaries, Theorem C.1) and Claim B.1 are not formalized.

  8. Conjecture 4.7 is not addressed.

Section 5 and Appendix D

  1. Theorem 5.1 (∜n-resilience) is not formalized.

  2. Claim D.1 omits the bound k < n/2 (false as stated, with no bound on k; the claim is a standalone warm-up, not used elsewhere): for n ≥ 3 the block of all processors but the origin forces any outcome by Lemma 4.1 (claim_D_1_false). The proof uses that the honest segment is longer than the coalition ("length k > l" in the text should read l > k), and the introduction states the result of [4] for k < n/2. We prove it for consecutive coalitions with 2k + 1 ≤ n (claim_D_1), with any position of the origin (the paper assumes it honest), against deterministic deviations. The proof formalizes the information-flow argument by a simulation (reach_tau): until the coalition has received the secret of the first honest processor, changing that secret does not change what the coalition sees. Consequence: A-LEADuni is 0-1-unbiased and 0-1-resilient for n ≥ 3 (isUnbiased_aLead_one, isResilient_aLead_one).

Sections 6, 8 and Appendices E, G, H

  1. PhaseAsyncLead (Theorem 6.1, w.h.p. over a random function, Appendix E), Appendices G and H are not formalized.

Section 7 and Appendix F

  1. Model for Section 7 (TreeModel.lean): general asynchronous message passing on a finite simple graph; a processor is a function of its received history of (sender, message) pairs (cumulative sends per neighbour, monotone; optional output); FIFO links; oblivious schedules are sequences of directed links (no-op if nothing pending), fair = every directed edge infinitely often; protocols need not be symmetric; random inputs from finite nonempty sets, uniformly (the paper: infinite random strings — a restriction). Deviations may be arbitrary strategies of the coalition members (deterministic given their own inputs). Two-party protocols are modelled as the unidirectional ring of size 2 (TreeTwoParty.lean), where executions are schedule independent.

  2. Definition 7.1 as written is degenerate (a wording slip: the sentence after it describes the intended notion) (IsKSimTreeLiteral.card_fiber_le_one: a homomorphism into a loopless tree with connected fibres has singleton fibres). We use the intended notion IsKSimTree: a partition into connected parts of size ≤ k, every edge inside a part or between tree-adjacent parts.

  3. Theorem 7.2 is false at ε = 1/n for n = 2 (thm_7_2_boundary_false: K₂ is a 1-simulated tree, k2FLE is FLE, and every FLE protocol is (1 − 1/n)-resilient, isResilient_one_sub). For n ≥ 3 at ε = 1/n the status is open (the method gives gain only 1 − |A|/n).

  4. The reduction FLE → coin toss has a gap (Theorem 8.1 and the last step of the proof of Theorem 7.2): the parity coin can succeed (all processors output leaders of the same parity) while the election fails, so Pr(coin = 0) ≤ Σ_{i even} Pr(leader = i) does not follow; the proof also assumes n even. Replaced by not_resilient_of_assures: a coalition of size ≤ k assuring that the leader lies in a proper subset contradicts ε-k-resilience for every ε < 1/n, every n.

  5. Lemma F.2 is proved in a generalized form (lemma_F2): arbitrary outputs and arbitrary target sets Q₁, Q₂, finite input sets; the hypothesis "bounded amount of messages" is derived from termination with finite inputs (exists_bdd). Its hypothesis that every honest execution succeeds (implicit in the paper) is explicit.

  6. Lemma F.3 and Corollary F.4 are not formalized; their proofs have gaps. The "super-node" b simulating T ∖ {a} is not a processor of the model: its behaviour toward a depends on the interleaving of a's messages with internal events, so executions of P are not executions of the two-party P_a, and neither "a assures in P_a ⇒ a assures in P" nor "b is a dictator in P_a" transfers to single processors; moreover, for a super-node, termination under all fair schedules does not bound the number of messages (ping-pong until a third processor's message arrives), so Lemma F.2's boundedness hypothesis is genuinely missing. Connected fibres simulating tree nodes (Corollary F.4) raise the same issue. Theorem 7.2 is proved only for n = 2 (thm_7_2_two, all 1 ≤ k ≤ 2, ε < 1/n); for n ≥ 3 it is neither proved nor refuted.

  7. The remark of Section 7 that every graph is simulated by a tree of size 2 with parts of size ≤ ⌈n/2⌉ is false (K₁,₃); Claim F.5 (a star partition) is correct but not formalized.

  8. Theorem 8.1 is not formalized (see item 28).
