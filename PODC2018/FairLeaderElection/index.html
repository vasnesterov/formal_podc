Fair Leader Election for Rational Agents — PODC 2018

Assaf Yifrach, Yishay Mansour. Fair Leader Election for Rational Agents in Asynchronous Rings and Networks. PODC 2018. DOI 10.1145/3212734.3212767. Formalized version: arXiv 1805.04778 v2; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The attacks on A-LEADuni, the paper's main focus, hold: Lemma 2.4, the success characterization (Lemmas 3.3–3.5), Lemma 4.1 (honest origin), Theorem 4.2 (k ≥ √n), the Cubic Attack (Theorem 4.3, Lemmas 4.4–4.5), Corollary 4.6 (ε < 1 − 1/n) and Claim D.1 (k < n/2) are proved. But Theorem 7.2 (no resilient FLE on k-simulated trees) is proved only for n = 2, its status for n ≥ 3 is open, and the claimed equivalence of Fair Leader Election and Fair Coin Toss (Theorem 8.1) has a wrong step and no repair. The resilience side was not checked: Theorem 5.1 (∜n-resilience) and Section 6 (PhaseAsyncLead) are not formalized.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 7.2 for n ≥ 3 (no resilient FLE on k-simulated trees)

The proof's Lemma F.3 and Corollary F.4 treat b, which "simulates the rest of the tree", as a processor of the model, but it is not one, so Theorem 7.2 is unestablished for n ≥ 3; we prove it for n = 2 (Trees.thm_7_2_two).

Impact. For n ≥ 3 the theorem is neither proved nor refuted, and n = 2 is only the two-party case, none of the claimed generalization to trees. The attacks on A-LEADuni (Sections 3–4) are unaffected.

Details

The super-node's behaviour toward the leaf depends on how the leaf's messages interleave with internal events. So neither "a assures o in P_a ⇒ a assures o in P" nor "b is a dictator" transfers. Also, for a super-node, termination under every fair schedule does not bound the number of messages (ping-pong until a third processor's message arrives), and Lemma F.2 needs such a bound. The same issue affects connected fibres in Corollary F.4.

Repair

For n = 2 only. We prove Lemma F.2 itself, for two processors, in a generalized form: arbitrary output sets, finite inputs, and no message bound needed, since it follows from finite inputs (Trees.lemma_F2). With Trees.not_resilient_of_assures (see the next item) this gives Theorem 7.2 for n = 2, all 1 ≤ k ≤ 2, ε < 1/n.

❌ Theorem 8.1, first direction (also the last step of the proof of Theorem 7.2)

The step Pr(coin = 0) ≤ Σ_{i even} Pr(leader = i) is wrong, because the parity coin can succeed while the election fails: processors may output different leaders of the same parity.

Impact. The claimed equivalence of Fair Leader Election and Fair Coin Toss is not established. Theorem 8.1 is neither repaired nor formalized; its status is unknown, not refuted.

Details

The last step of the proof of Theorem 7.2 goes through this coin toss (leader parity), and it also covers only even n.

Repair

For the use in Theorem 7.2 only. We replace the coin-toss step by a direct lemma: a coalition that forces the leader into a proper subset breaks ε-k-resilience for every ε < 1/n and every n (Trees.not_resilient_of_assures).

⚠️ Lemma 4.1 (adversarial origin)

Lemma 4.1 is stated for every coalition whose segments have length ≤ k − 1, but its attack deadlocks when the origin is an adversary: only the origin wakes up spontaneously and every adversary just forwards, so nobody ever sends and the outcome is FAIL (lemma_4_1_attack_deadlocks).

Impact. Unestablished for an adversarial origin; the statement is probably still true with a modified attack. We prove the lemma for an honest origin, which is all Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 need (lemma_4_1).

🔧 Theorem 7.2 at the boundary ε = 1/n

For n = 2 and ε = 1/n Theorem 7.2 is false: K₂ is a 1-simulated tree, and the protocol in which one processor draws a bit and both output it is ½-1-resilient (Trees.thm_7_2_boundary_false).

Impact. An edge case of the stated range ε ≤ 1/n: every FLE protocol is (1 − 1/n)-resilient (see Corollary 4.6 under Slips), and for n = 2, 1 − 1/n = 1/n. For n ≥ 3 and ε = 1/n the status is open.

Repair

The condition ε < 1/n, under which we prove the case n = 2 (Trees.thm_7_2_two).

✏️ Slips

  • Claim D.1 claims Pr(outcome = j) ≤ 1/n for every consecutive coalition, but for n ≥ 3 the block of all processors except the origin forces any outcome by the attack of Lemma 4.1 (claim_D_1_false). That block leaves one honest processor; its segment has length 1 ≤ k − 1. The proof needs the honest segment to be longer than the coalition (the text says "length k > l", meant as l > k), and the introduction presents Appendix D as a proof of the result of Abraham et al. for k < n/2. The claim is not used elsewhere. We prove it for 2k + 1 ≤ n and any position of the origin (claim_D_1). Consequence: A-LEADuni is 0-1-resilient for n ≥ 3 (isResilient_aLead_one).

  • Corollary 4.6 is stated for 0 ≤ ε ≤ 1 − 1/n, but every FLE protocol, in particular A-LEADuni, is (1 − 1/n)-k-resilient for every k (corollary_4_6_boundary_false): no deviation can raise an expected rational utility by more than 1 − 1/n, since the deviation gets at most max u and the honest run already gets (1/n)·Σ u ≥ max u / n. Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 themselves use the strict ε < 1 − 1/n, and we prove the corollary for ε < 1 − 1/n (corollary_4_6).

  • Lemma 3.3 says "the execution succeeds iff conditions 1–3", but outcome also needs the adversaries to terminate with the same output, which conditions 1–3 do not control. The intended reading is "all honest processors terminate with the same valid output"; we prove it, and its version for every fair schedule (lemma_3_3, lemma_3_3_run). Also, "sends n messages" should be "at least n", and the sums are over the first n messages.

  • Definition 7.1 asks for a graph homomorphism f with {(f(x), f(y)) | (x, y) ∈ E} ⊆ E_T. A tree has no loops, so every connected fibre is a singleton (Trees.IsKSimTreeLiteral.card_fiber_le_one). The next sentence describes the intended notion (a partition into connected parts of size ≤ k whose quotient is a tree), which we use.

  • The Section 7 remark "Every graph can be simulated by a tree of size 2 with parts of size ≤ ⌈n/2⌉" is false: K₁,₃ has no such partition. Claim F.5, which uses a star, is fine (not formalized).

  • Appendix A: the origin starts with round = 1. It then validates its (n−1)-th incoming message against d₁ and sums only n − 1 values, so the honest run aborts whenever d₁ ≠ d₂. The verbal description (validate the n-th message) is what we formalize.

  • Appendix C: in the Cubic Attack the zeros are sent only when count == n−k−lᵢ after incrementing count. This never happens if n − k − lᵢ = 0, and the attack deadlocks (e.g. n = 4, k = 2, l = (2, 0)). Following the text, we send them at wake-up.

  • Proof of Theorem 4.3: Σ_{i=1}^{k'−1}(k'−1)i is written (k'−1)k'(k'+1)/2; it is (k'−1)²k'/2 (harmless).

  • Proof of Lemma 4.4: "since l₁ = maxᵢ lᵢ" does not follow from the relaxed conditions l_k ≤ k−1, lᵢ ≤ lᵢ₊₁ + k − 1; the lemma holds anyway, and our liveness proof does not use it.

Typos

  • Proof of Lemma 3.3: the case analysis swaps the numbering of conditions 1 and 2.

Model.

  • Asynchronous unidirectional ring of n processors (Fin n, origin 0), with reliable FIFO links. Processors are deterministic and event driven: a processor is a function of its received history (shared model Cslib.UniRing, Podc/Computability/Distributed/UniRing/Basic.lean). Every processor may send at wake-up (honest normal processors send nothing then). Schedules are oblivious sequences of processors; fair = each one scheduled infinitely often. We prove that on a unidirectional ring the schedule does not matter (terminates_iff, outcomeUnder_eq).

  • Randomness: honest secrets d i uniform in Fin n; probabilities by counting. Messages are values in Fin n.

  • Outcome v iff all processors (adversaries included) terminate with output v; otherwise FAIL.

  • Coalitions of size exactly k run arbitrary deterministic strategies (Def. 2.2). Rational utilities, ε-k-resilience (Def. 2.3), ε-k-unbiasedness and FLE are as in Section 2, for every fair schedule. Deterministic deviations suffice for all negative results. Our positive results (Claim D.1 and its corollaries) are proved against deterministic deviations only (see differencies.md, item 5).

  • Section 7 (Tree*.lean, namespace Podc.FairLeaderElection.Trees) uses a general asynchronous model on a graph. Processors see the arrival order across their links, links are FIFO, schedules are sequences of directed links, and fair = every directed edge infinitely often. Protocols need not be symmetric; random inputs come from finite sets, uniformly. Two-party protocols are the unidirectional ring of size 2.
