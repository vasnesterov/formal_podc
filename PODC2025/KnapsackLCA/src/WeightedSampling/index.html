The only property of `rQuantile` the paper uses is its accuracy guarantee (Theorem 4.5): `ẽ_k` is a `τ`-approximate `(1 - kq)`-quantile of the profit-weighted efficiency distribution of `S(I) ∪ G(I)`, i.e. `Pr[eff ≤ ẽ_k] ≥ 1 - kq - τ` and `Pr[eff ≥ ẽ_k] ≥ kq - τ` (as used in the proof of Lemma 4.6). **Counterexample.** `uniformInstance N`: `N` items, each of (normalised) profit and weight `1/N` (integer weights `1`, weight limit `N/2` before normalisation), weight limit `1/2`. For `ε = 1/m`, `m ≥ 2` (`m ≥ 30` in `theorem_4_1_false`), `N ≥ m²`, all items are small with efficiency `1`, so the efficiency distribution is a point mass: * no equally partitioning sequence (Definition 4.3) of length `≥ 1` exists (`no_eps_uniform`), refuting Lemma 4.6 (which claims that the computed sequence is an EPS with probability `≥ 1 - 13ε/36`); * whenever the quantiles are
