Interplanetary and Relativistic Distributed Computing — PODC 2025

Siddhartha Jayanti. On Interplanetary and Relativistic Distributed Computing. PODC 2025. DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733563. Formalized version: the proceedings version (no arXiv version exists); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The Central Theorem (Theorem 6.7) and the Equivalence Theorem (Theorem 6.8) are saved by local changes: finite executions in two definitions, a rule for ties in frames, and a citation in place of one wrong step of Lemma 6.3. The only false case, uncountably many processors, is a corner the paper never uses. Theorem 6.7 holds for countably many processors, and Theorem 6.8 holds with no assumption at all once its three notions of linearizability are all read over finite executions.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 6.8 (linearizability over finite vs. all executions)

Theorem 6.8 combines "linearizable" (Def. A.3), which quantifies over finite classical executions, with "relativistically/computationally linearizable" (Defs. 5.3, 6.1), which quantify over all executions.

Impact. The printed proof does not go through unchanged. With all three notions read over finite executions, Theorem 6.8 holds with no assumption on Π (theorem_6_8).

Details

The proof instantiates Theorem 6.7 with no finiteness. Taken literally, (1) ⟹ (3) would need linearizability of infinite histories to follow from that of finite ones. This is delicate (cf. Guerraoui–Ruppert, "linearizability is not always a safety property"), and Def. A.3 does not pin down the infinite case.

Repair

Add "finite" to the two other definitions, as Def. A.3 already has it, and restrict Theorem 6.7 to finite executions (theorem_6_7_on, theorem_6_8).

🔧 Def. 5.3 and the Central Theorem ("the total ordering according to the local time of frame R")

This order is not total when two steps are simultaneous in R, which happens in the paper's own §7 scenario (e₁ =_A e₂).

Impact. One condition is missing. We formalize both readings: all tie-breakings (RelativisticallySatisfies) and only frames without ties (RelativisticallySatisfies'). Both are equivalent to (1) and (2) for countable executions.

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 6.3 (⇐), Lemma 6.4 (second half), Corollary 6.5, Theorem 6.7 (1) ⟹ (2): uncountably many processors. With processors indexed by the "double arrow" ℝ ×ₗ Bool, each taking one step and sending no messages, the lexicographic order is a total ordering of the (empty-relation) computational execution that satisfies P1–P3 but does not embed into (ℝ, <), so it is not classical (lemma_6_3_false, lemma_6_4_false, corollary_6_5_false, theorem_6_7_false, theorem_6_7_false_frames). This case is degenerate, though the paper never excludes it: all four statements hold for executions with countably many steps, hence for every execution when Π is countable (lemma_6_3_of_countable, lemma_6_4, theorem_6_7) and for any countable class of executions, e.g. finite ones (theorem_6_7_on).

    • The order does not embed into (ℝ, <): there would be a rational between the images of (x, false) and (x, true) for every real x, all distinct.

    • Placing the steps at distinct points of the t = 0 slice of Minkowski spacetime makes the same order a total ordering of a relativistic execution, which refutes Corollary 6.5.

    • For the property "the order embeds into ℝ", statement (1) of Theorem 6.7 holds trivially and (2) fails. Statement (3) also fails if observers may perceive simultaneous steps in any order, and holds if only frames without simultaneous steps count.

    • We also proved that "classical ⟺ every observer in every relativistic execution, frames without ties" holds for any Π (classical_iff_frames).

  • Φ2 ("a step can occur at π only if its current state is σ"). Φ2 presupposes that each processor's steps form a sequence, but a real-valued time map alone allows Zeno behaviour, e.g. steps at 1 − 1/n followed by one at time 2. We make "the steps of each processor, in time order, form a rooted consecutively permissible sequence (order type ≤ ω)" part of Φ2. The paper's proofs of Lemmas 6.2 and 6.3 ("by CP1 / RP2 these steps can be ordered into a sequence") rely on exactly this.

Typos

  • "A predicate on classical executions" (Theorem 6.7) must really be a predicate on arbitrary totally ordered step sets, since (2) and (3) apply it to orders that are not a priori classical; the introduction has the right wording.

  • Def. 6.1, axiom A1: "the transitive closure of the relation satisfying the following axioms" is circular, since A2 refers to ◁_S; the items (a), (b) of the proofs show the meaning.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 6.3

🔧 Lemma 6.3 (⇐), used by Lemma 6.4: the map t(sᵢ) = i assumes order type ≤ ω

Lemma 6.3 lists the steps as s₁, s₂, … in <_S order, but a total ordering of an infinite execution need not have order type ω, even with two processors (omegaPlusOne_classical, omegaPlusOne_not_seq).

Impact. The statement is true for countably many processors, and Lemma 6.4 inherits the gap via Lemma 6.3.

Details

Example: processor 0 takes infinitely many steps and processor 1 takes one step after all of them. This is a classical execution of order type ω + 1, with times 1 − 1/(n+1) and 1, and no indexing by ℕ exists (omegaPlusOne_classical, omegaPlusOne_not_seq).

Repair

Every countable linear order embeds into ℚ ⊂ ℝ (Cantor), so a time map exists anyway (exists_real_embedding).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Algorithms are state machines per processor (Def. 4.1). A step is (π, σ, e, σ') with δ_π(σ, e, σ') (Def. 4.2). Events may be sends (π, send, m, π') or receives (π', receive, m). The model is asynchronous, with arbitrary (possibly infinite) step sets and arbitrary processor sets.

  • An execution is a set S of steps with a relation on the subtype ↥S. The conditions that do not depend on the order are message validity and at-most-once delivery (MsgValid). The conditions that depend on the order are: per-processor steps form a rooted, consecutively permissible sequence (ProcSeq: a strict total order with finitely many predecessors), and messages are oriented forward (MsgForward).

  • Classical execution: the order is induced by an injective time map S → ℝ (CP1, CP2). Computational execution: ◁ = TransGen(program order ∪ message edges) as a fixed-point equation (A1–A3). Relativistic execution: the order is induced by an injective map into an abstract Spacetime. That structure has a strict causal order (RP1), real time coordinates for each frame that refine it (RP1), world lines that are chains (RP2), and one body at rest whose world line is parametrised by frame time (the physical fact used in Lemma 6.6). Messages go forward in ≺ (RP3).

  • Instance: Minkowski spacetime ℝ × ℝᵈ with c = 1. Causal order: ‖Δx‖ ≤ Δt. Inertial frames have velocity ‖v‖ < 1, and frame time is t − ⟪v, x⟫ (the Lorentz time up to the factor γ_v).

  • Linearizability is CSLib's Cslib.SharedMemory.HerlihyWing.Linearizable (single object), applied to the history of a finite execution.
