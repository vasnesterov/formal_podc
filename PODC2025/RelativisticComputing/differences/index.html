Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Interplanetary and Relativistic Distributed Computing — PODC 2025.

Paper: S. Jayanti, On Interplanetary and Relativistic Distributed Computing, PODC 2025 (DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733563), proceedings version.

Errors in the paper

  1. Uncountably many processors: Lemma 6.3 (⇐), Lemma 6.4 (second half), Corollary 6.5 and Theorem 6.7 (1) ⟹ (2) are false. False only in a degenerate case. The paper places no restriction on Π. Take Π = ℝ ×ₗ Bool and the trivial algorithm: one state, one event, no messages, each processor takes its single step. Let S be all these steps and R = ∅. Then R is a computational execution (isComputational_S₀). The lexicographic order lt₀ of the processors is a total ordering of R and satisfies P1–P3. It is not a classical execution, because it does not embed into (ℝ, <) (lt₀_not_real: rationals between the images of (x, false) and (x, true) would give an injection ℝ → ℚ).

    • Lemma 6.4: lemma_6_4_false. Lemma 6.3: lemma_6_3_false.

    • Corollary 6.5: place step (x, b) at the event (0, ±eˣ) of 1+1 Minkowski spacetime. This gives a relativistic execution with ≺_S = ∅ of which lt₀ is a total ordering (corollary_6_5_false).

    • Theorem 6.7: for P := "the order embeds into ℝ", (1) holds and (2) fails (theorem_6_7_false). (3) in the tie-breaking reading also fails, because the observer at rest sees all steps of the execution above as simultaneous (theorem_6_7_false_frames). (3) in the tie-free reading holds, since it is equivalent to (1) for every Π (classical_iff_frames).

    • Fix: assume the executions are countable. This holds automatically for countable Π, because every per-processor sequence is countable (ProcSeq.countable). It also holds for every finite execution, which is all Theorem 6.8 needs (theorem_6_7_on).

  2. Proof of Lemma 6.3 (⇐), used by Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.7: t(sᵢ) = i assumes order type ≤ ω. Proof gap; the statement is true for countable executions. Counterexample to the step: two processors, a counter algorithm; processor false takes steps at times 1 − 1/(n+1) for all n, processor true takes one step at time 1. This is classical (omegaPlusOne_classical), and its order has no embedding into ℕ (omegaPlusOne_not_seq). Repair: Cantor's theorem, every countable linear order embeds into ℝ (exists_real_embedding, via Mathlib's Order.embedding_from_countable_to_dense). The gap does not make Lemma 6.4 false for countable executions, infinite ones included. The lemma is false only for uncountable ones (item 1).

Under-specified definitions (resolved)

  1. Reference-frame order with simultaneous steps (Def. 5.3, Theorem 6.7 (3), Def. A.4). The chronological order <_R of a frame is only a total preorder on the steps: the paper's own relativity-of-simultaneity discussion and §7 have e₁ =_A e₂. The paper speaks of "the total ordering of the execution according to the local time of R". We give two readings: RelativisticallySatisfies (every strict total order refining the frame order, i.e. ties broken arbitrarily) and RelativisticallySatisfies' (only frames in which no two steps are simultaneous). Both are equivalent to (1) and (2) for countable executions. For uncountable ones only the tie-free reading stays equivalent to (1).

  2. Finite vs infinite executions in Theorem 6.8. Def. A.3 calls 𝒜 linearizable if the history of every finite classical execution is linearizable. Defs. 5.3/A.4 (relativistic) and 6.1 (computational) quantify over all executions. The proof of Theorem 6.8 instantiates Theorem 6.7 with "the execution is linearizable" and no finiteness. Taken literally, (1) ⟹ (3) would need linearizability of infinite histories to follow from that of their finite prefixes. That is not true in general: for some object types the set of linearizable histories is not limit-closed (Guerraoui–Ruppert, "Linearizability is not always a safety property", 2014). Def. A.3 also does not say what a sequential history over an infinite set of events is. We define all three notions over finite executions (IsLinearizable, IsCompLinearizable, IsRelLinearizable). With this reading Theorem 6.8 holds for every Π.

  3. Typing of 𝒫 in Theorem 6.7. The paper calls 𝒫 "a predicate on classical executions", but (2) and (3) evaluate 𝒫 on total orderings of computational and relativistic executions. We take 𝒫 to be a predicate on arbitrary pairs (S, <_S) (Property).

  4. "Sequence" in Φ2 / P2 / A2. A real-valued time map (or a world line) does not force a processor's steps to form a sequence s₁, s₂, …. Zeno behaviour has order type ω + 1 at one processor, and then the "current state" of Φ2 is undefined. The proofs of Lemmas 6.2 and 6.3 assert "by CP1/RP2 these steps can be ordered into a sequence". We make this part of Φ2: the steps of each processor, in physical order, form a rooted consecutively permissible sequence. This is ProcSeq: a strict total order on S_π in which every step has finitely many predecessors, the first step starts in an initial state, and each step starts in the state its immediate predecessor ends in. The same predicate is used for P2 and A2.

  5. Definition 6.1, A1 is circular ("◁_S is the transitive closure of the relation satisfying A2, A3", where A2 refers to ◁_S). We formalize it as a fixed-point equation ◁ = TransGen((same processor ∧ ◁) ∪ message edges), together with A2 (ProcSeq for ◁) and A3. This matches items (a) and (b) in the proofs of Lemmas 6.2 and 6.4. Acyclicity is implied by A2.

Modelling choices

  1. Physics. RP1–RP3 are abstracted into the structure Spacetime: a strict causal order; real frame times that are strictly monotone along ≺; world lines are ≺-chains. The proof of Lemma 6.6 needs one more physical fact, which we make a field of Spacetime: some body is at rest in some frame F, and its world line is γ : ℝ → Pt with time F (γ τ) = τ and τ < τ' → γ τ ≺ γ τ'. As in the paper, all processors may share that world line. RP3's "iff" is used only in the direction "permissible transmissions go forward in ≺". The postulate "every reference frame's order is total" is weakened to a real time coordinate, which can have ties (item 3). Minkowski spacetime (minkowski d) is an instance. Its frame time t − ⟪v, x⟫ is the Lorentz time γ_v (t − ⟪v, x⟫) up to the positive factor γ_v, which does not change the order.

  2. Algorithms. All processors share one type of states and one type of events (Σ_π, E_π of the paper are encoded through init π and δ π). A send event carries its message and intended recipient, and a receive event carries its message. "Messages are instances" (uniqueness of a send) and "every received message was sent" are not imposed. The paper does not impose them either. These are order-independent conditions on S, identical in all three kinds of executions, so they do not affect any result.

  3. Executions as orders on ↥S. Orders are relations on the subtype of steps of S. "X is extracted from 𝒳" (Def. 4.4) is ∃ t, IsClassicalPhys A S t ∧ ∀ a b, lt a b ↔ t a < t b, and similarly for relativistic executions.

  4. Linearizability. We do not re-define Appendix A. We use CSLib's Cslib.SharedMemory.HerlihyWing.Linearizable (Herlihy–Wing with the Sela–Herlihy–Petrank amendment, one object, operations identified by (process, index)) on the history of a finite execution (IsHistoryOf: invocation/response events listed in <_S order). Theorem 6.8 is the Central Theorem instantiated with this property. The proof does not depend on what linearizability is, so any other history-level definition would do equally well.

  5. Section 7. Only the geometry is formalized (observation1_geometry). Two operations happen in the ε-neighbourhoods of (0, −u) and (0, u) with ε < 1/3. Then the observer moving with velocity u/2 sees the second operation entirely before the first, and the observer moving with −u/2 sees the opposite. The JTUF algorithm and the resulting linearization orders are not modelled.
