The paper claims that `W` weakly-one decides `L` and that every process reports `NO` infinitely often if `x(E) ∉ L` ("no entry of `C` ever stabilizes, hence all processes report NO infinitely often"). This is false: an entry can change infinitely often without changing between two consecutive snapshots of a given process. Counterexample (`fig4_fails`): `n = 2`, `L = ∅`, `V = altAlg` (every process alternately reports `YES` and `NO`). `V` weakly-one (and weakly-all) decides `∅`. In the execution where `p₀` runs two iterations, then `p₁` runs two iterations, and so on, the algorithm `W` of Figure 4 always reports `YES` at both processes, so `W` neither weakly-one nor weakly-all decides `∅`.
