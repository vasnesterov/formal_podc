Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Round and Communication Efficient Graph Coloring — PODC 2025.

Paper: Chang, Mishra, Nguyen, Salim, Round and Communication Efficient Graph Coloring, PODC 2025, arXiv 2412.12589v2.

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 5.3 is false for Δ = 0. The lemma has no hypothesis Δ ≥ 1. With Δ = 0 every vertex has degree 0, the degree-0 vertices are trivially independent, and no matching covers them: one isolated vertex is a counterexample (lemma_5_3_false_of_zero). This is a degenerate parameter corner that Algorithm 2, with Δ ≥ 8, never uses (✏️). We prove lemma_5_3 for Δ ≥ 1 in a stronger form: only "maximum degree ≤ Δ" is needed for an injective choice of neighbours (Hall). The independence hypothesis is used separately, to show the chosen edges are disjoint (lemma_5_3_matching, Plan.M_unique).

Proof-level remarks

  1. Theorem 3, deferral. "Sequentially identifies edges connecting two vertices of degree Δ" must use the current degree in the remaining graph. Then the deferred edges form a maximal matching between vertices of party-degree Δ, and a single colour of the other palette suffices. With the static reading the deferred edges need not be Δ-edge-colourable (Alice holds K₃, Δ = 2). We formalize the sequential reading, the same procedure as Algorithm 2 lines 3–5 with threshold Δ instead of Δ − 1 (exists_deferral). The statement of Theorem 3 is true.

  2. Lemma 5.3, proof. The paper uses integrality of the bipartite fractional matching polytope [Sch03]. We use Hall's theorem directly (double counting). Same content.

  3. Theorem 2, proof. The proof says "for Δ < 7 use Lemma 5.1, otherwise Δ ≥ 8", which misses Δ = 7. Our protocol uses Lemma 5.1 for every effective degree bound ≤ 7.

  4. Lemma 5.2. It says "the maximum degree of DG is 2" where "at most 2" is meant (Plan.defer_le_two).

  5. Lemma 6.2, proof. The last display writes E_A ∩ E_B ∩ E_1 ∩ E_2 for E_A ∩ E_B ∩ E'_A ∩ E'_B (typo, checked on the rendered PDF). In Case 1 our proof keeps one fixed Bob input, which gives the weaker loss bound 1/2205 instead of the paper's 1/105. The paper's 1/105 is also correct, and both are ≥ 1/11025, so the final bound is the paper's.

  6. Lemma 6.2, labels. Case 2 uses |L_A(v_i)| ≥ 2 for v_i ∉ V_A, which tacitly needs L_A(v_i) ≠ ∅. This holds because some input contains v_i and one of the 3 colours then has probability ≥ 1/3 ≥ 1/5 (label_nonempty). Routine.

Modelling choices

  1. Degree bound. Inputs satisfy "maximum degree ≤ Δ" (ValidInput) instead of "maximum degree exactly Δ". This is more general, and all protocols work under it.

    • In Theorem 2 the protocol internally uses the effective bound Δ' = min(Δ, n). It is a public parameter, valid because degrees are < n. With it, colour codes have O(log n) bits even when the given Δ is huge.

    • The colours used are < 2Δ' − 1 ≤ 2Δ − 1.

  2. Rounds. The paper claims O(1) rounds; Lemma 5.5 is stated with two rounds and Lemma 5.4 with one. We prove Theorem 2 with one round of simultaneous messages. The information of Lemma 5.5 (which of the first seven own colours are used at each vertex) and the cover data of Lemma 5.4 depend only on the sender's own graph. So everything can be sent at once:

    • Lemma 5.4's cover is computed for all vertices of sender-degree ≤ Δ/2, not only the "match vertices";

    • the receiver colours its deferred graph after reading the message.

  3. Communication model.

  • A one-round protocol (OneRoundProtocol) has two message functions (List Bool) and two output functions. Each party's output depends on its own input and the received message. Cost is the total message length.

  • Variable-length messages could in principle carry information in their lengths. To exclude this, theorem_2_tree converts the protocol into a CSLib protocol tree. There each party sends one symbol from an alphabet of size 2^(L+1), where L = 112 n bounds its message length, and the cost is ≤ 224 n + 2.

  • Bits are counted exactly. Constants: Algorithm 2 messages have ≤ 112 n bits per party, the Lemma 5.1 message 13 n bits.

  1. Encoding of Lemma 5.4.

    • The paper sends the colours c₁, …, c_t and the bit arrays V₁, …, V_t (|V_i| = |U_i|).

    • We send t in unary, the colours in binary with ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1 digits, and for every vertex v its level ℓ(v) in unary. Here v is first covered by c_{ℓ(v)+1}, and ℓ(v) = 0 for vertices outside the cover set.

    • The total Σ_v (ℓ(v) + 1) = n + Σ_{i ≥ 2} |U_i| is the paper's Σ |V_i| up to n. Both are ≤ 3n.

    • t = O(log n) is proved as 3^t ≤ 2^t · 3n (exists_greedy_cover), and the colour list costs (t + 1)(w + 1) ≤ 100 n bits (cover_bits_le).

  2. Palettes. Alice's palette is {0, …, Δ−2}, Bob's is {Δ−1, …, 2Δ−3}, and the special colour is 2Δ−2. "The first seven colours of Bob's palette" are Δ−1, …, Δ+5. In Theorem 3 Alice's palette is {0, …, Δ−1}, Bob's is {Δ, …, 2Δ−1}, and the deferred matchings get colours Δ (Alice) and 0 (Bob).

  3. Local choices.

    • Each party's local computations are made by choice, in Lean's classical logic: the deferral, the matching (Lemma 5.3), the Fournier colouring and the greedy cover form a Plan.

    • "Find a colouring by Proposition 3.5" is likewise existential in the paper.

    • The deferral is any terminal run of the sequential procedure (exists_deferral). Every such run has the properties used.

  4. Lemma 5.4 as stated. The paper states it for Bob's vertices of degree ≤ Δ/2, with Alice learning one available colour of Bob's palette for each.

    • We prove its content: the greedy cover exists with Σ ℓ ≤ 2|U| and t = O(log n); the encoding has O(n) bits; and the cover colour is missing at v in Bob's colouring of R \ M (Plan.cover, used in cross_R'_M).

    • "Available" means not used by Bob's colouring of R_B \ M_B. These are exactly Bob's edges that use Bob's palette, which is what the correctness proof needs.

  5. ZEC game (Lemma 6.2).

    • Alice's output is a full map Fin 7 → Fin 3 (colour of {v_A, v_i} for every i); only the values on her input matter, so this is fully general.

    • A randomized private-coin strategy is a stochastic kernel per input. The joint law is the product of the inputs' uniform distributions and the two kernels.

  6. Edge colourings are maps Sym2 V → ℕ with a bound < k on edges (IsProperEdgeColoring). IsProperEdgeColoring.lineGraph_colorable bridges them to Mathlib's G.lineGraph.Colorable k.

  7. Fournier's theorem is proved in the strong form: maximum degree ≤ k and no edge joining two vertices of degree k imply k colours suffice. With k = Δ this is Prop. 3.5; with k = Δ + 1 it is Vizing (Prop. 3.4). The proof uses Vizing fans and Kempe chains, not Fournier's original argument.

  8. Lemma 5.1 scope. The paper states it for any constant Δ (cost O(n) with a constant depending on Δ). We prove it for Δ ≤ 7, the only case Theorem 2 uses: Alice sends a fixed 13-bit array per vertex (13 n bits). The general case is the same argument with (2Δ − 1) n bits.

  9. Lemma 5.3, form. lemma_5_3_matching is the paper's statement (for Δ ≥ 1): a matching M ≤ G (every vertex has ≤ 1 M-neighbour) covering every vertex of degree Δ. It follows from lemma_5_3, an injective choice f of neighbours of the degree-Δ vertices, which needs only maximum degree ≤ Δ; Algorithm 2 uses lemma_5_3 directly.

Not formalized

  • Theorem 1 and all of Section 4 (randomized (Δ+1)-vertex colouring), Lemma 3.1, Prop. 3.2 (palette sparsification, an external result), Lemma 3.3.

  • Lemma 6.1 (communication guessing, Newman's theorem), Prop. 6.3 (parallel repetition, external), Lemma 6.4, Theorem 4, Corollaries 1.1 and 1.2, Theorem 5 (ZEC-NEW, W-streaming).
