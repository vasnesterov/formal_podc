Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Byzantine Stable Matching — PODC 2025.

Paper: A. Constantinescu, M. Dufay, D. Ghinea, R. Wattenhofer, Byzantine Stable Matching, PODC 2025 (arXiv 2502.05889v2, which includes the appendix).

Errors in the paper

  1. ✏️ k = 1 breaks all "only if" directions (Theorems 2, 3, 4, 6, 7). The paper never assumes k ≥ 2. For k = 1 bSM is solvable without any communication on every network against every adversary (each party outputs the only party on the other side): bSM_solvable_k1, bSM_solvableAuth_k1, theorem2_only_if_false_k1 (Necessity.lean). E.g. Theorem 2 claims unsolvability for k = 1, tL = tR = 1 (since 1 ≥ 1/3). All our characterizations assume k ≥ 2.

  2. 🔧 k = 2 is not covered by the paper's proof of Theorem 2 (necessity). The proof applies Lemma 3 with d = 3, which needs k ≥ d = 3. The claim is nevertheless true for k = 2: our generalized Lemma 5 (lemma5) covers every k ≥ 2 (for k = 2 the cover degenerates to an 8-node double cover of K₄).

  3. 🔧 Lemma 13's reduction is invalid. "Using Corollary 2 and Lemma 3, this means that there exists a protocol Π which solves sSM on n := 6 nodes with tR = 3 and tL = 1." Lemma 3 with d = 3 only gives ⌊tR/⌈k/3⌉⌋ = ⌊k/⌈k/3⌉⌋ Byzantine parties on the right, which is < 3 for k = 4, 5, 7, 8, … (lemma3_not_enough_for_lemma13: 4 / ⌈4/3⌉ = 2), and it needs k ≥ 3; Corollary 2 is about unauthenticated bipartite networks and is irrelevant. The statement is still true: we run the split-brain argument directly for every k ≥ 2 with groups (lemma13_sSM). (A generalized Lemma 3 tolerating "any union of groups that is admissible" would also repair it; our GroupSim.Solvable.of_groupSim is such a statement.)

  4. ✏️ Proof of Theorem 7, case tR = k, tL < k/3, cites the wrong result. It invokes Theorem 4, which requires tR < k/2 (unauthenticated one-sided). The case is still solvable: the protocol ΠbSM of Lemma 9 also works on the one-sided network (it only uses L–R edges); we use it there.

  5. Typos / misreferences (harmless). Lemma 5's proof says "c1 must … decide to match party v2" (should be v1). Corollary 1 says "if tL < k/2" — it should be tR < k/2 (in a one-sided network L is disconnected and relays through R). The proof of Theorem 4 says "Corollary 2" (should be Corollary 1) and "one of the conditions tL < k/3, tR < k/2" (should be tR < k/3). The proof of Theorem 8 writes n − 2tL for k − 2tL.

Modelling choices

  • Model (CSLib Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/): synchronous lock-step rounds, deterministic history-based protocols, static Byzantine adversary (the paper's protocols are claimed secure against adaptive adversaries; we only treat static corruption). Byzantine messages are an arbitrary function of round, sender and receiver; for deterministic protocols this covers rushing/adaptive message choices. Channels are authenticated (the receiver knows the sender). Time is measured in rounds (no Δ).

  • Outputs: a party's output is its first decision (Decides); "termination" = every honest party decides at some round.

  • Parties are Fin k ⊕ Fin k; outputs are Option (Fin k) (index on the other side, none = nobody), so "outputs a party on the opposite side" holds by typing. Preference lists are Equiv.Perm (Fin k) (π 0 first); "prefers v over w" is π.symm v < π.symm w.

  • Non-competition is stated as: distinct honest parties never output the same party (partner). Symmetry is stated as an ↔ for honest u ∈ L, v ∈ R (equivalent to the paper's two implications).

  • Authenticated setting: ideal signatures as abstract tokens (Cslib.Byzantine.LockStep.Auth): honest parties emit only own or received tokens; Byzantine messages to honest parties contain only Byzantine-signed tokens or tokens honest parties already emitted (in this or earlier rounds, to anyone). I.e. a rushing, eavesdropping adversary. This is the strongest standard adversary; impossibility results hold against it a fortiori, and it is the one preserved by signature relays (relays see honest-to-honest traffic).

  • Adversary structure thresholds k tL tR: at most tL Byzantine parties in L and tR in R.

  • Impossibility proofs are done directly for all k ≥ 2 (group-level covers) instead of "small n

    • Lemma 3"; Lemma 3 itself is also formalized (Scaling.lean). Lemma 5 and Lemma 7/13 are proved for every network (resp. every network without edges inside L), which subsumes the paper's "the bipartite network is weaker than the one-sided one" remarks. Lemma 7 does not need the paper's preliminary reduction from one-sided to bipartite networks (the cover handles the R–R edges).

  • Lemma 5's cover: the paper gives the 12-node graph only as a picture; we use the voltage assignment determined by the three honest sets of Fig. 2 (ii)–(iv) (edges a–c, a–w, c–u, u–w cross).

Results taken as hypotheses (not proved)

  • Theorem 10 = [Fitzi–Maurer'98, Thm 2] (FitziMaurerBB), Dolev–Strong BB (DolevStrongBB), Theorems 8/9 (BA/BB among L with omissions, OmissionBA, OmissionBB) — explicitly allowed by the task. They are stated for finite nonempty value types. OmissionBA/BB are stated on Party k with R isolated (equivalent to the k-party statements).

Sufficiency side: construction details

  • Lemma 1 goes through interactive consistency: IC_of_BB runs one BB per party in parallel, then offlineMatch applies the offline Gale–Shapley step. Byzantine senders need no "default list"; BB consistency suffices.

  • Lemma 3 (Scaling.lean): the paper only asserts that a partition into d groups of size ≤ ⌈k/d⌉ exists. The obvious i ↦ i / ⌈k/d⌉ can give fewer than d groups (k = 4, d = 3), so we use i ↦ i % d. The lemma itself is correct.

  • Lemmas 6, 8, 10 (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/Relay.lean): one simulated round costs two real rounds (send packets, then forward). Wrappers (origin, dest, round, payload) replace the paper's (P → P′, τ, id, m): the round number plays the role of the timestamp, and packets make ids unnecessary.

    • The transfer theorems need the spec to depend only on honest decisions (Spec.HonestOnly). This holds for bSM (bSM_honestOnly). Byzantine receivers can be lied to by relays, so their simulated decisions are meaningless.

    • In the signature relay, a receiver accepts a wrapper forwarded by any designated relay.

    • Corollary 3/4 are proved as corollary34 for any network between bipartite and fully.

  • ΠbSM (PiBSM.lean, Main.lean): we verify a protocol Φ on the network "L complete + L–R edges" with omissions allowed only on L–L messages and only if all of R is Byzantine. We then compile it with the signature relay (relays = R) to the bipartite or one-sided network.

    • This factorization is the paper's Lemma 10 + Lemmas 11/12.

    • R-parties decide by strict majority of the suggestions instead of "the most common" one; the result is none if there is no strict majority (PiBSM.majority, i.e. Finset.majority? of Cslib/Foundations/Data/Finset/Quorum.lean). When some R-party is honest, the honest suggestion has ≥ k − tL > k/2 votes, so the rules coincide.

    • R-parties' lists are sent in round 0 and BA instances start afterwards, all in lock-step. The paper's Δ-timing (max(ΔBA(2Δ)+Δ, ΔBB(2Δ))) becomes the common round bound T of the instances.

  • Theorems 8/9 are hypotheses (OmissionBA, OmissionBB) stated on Party k with R isolated (lClique) and Byzantine sets thresholds k tL k. On reliable runs they require BA/BB with non-⊥ outputs (SomeSpec). This "outputs are not ⊥ without omissions" is implicit in the paper but needed: otherwise honest L-parties could output nobody while honest R-parties follow Byzantine suggestions.

  • Theorem 4 sufficiency uses Corollary 1 (with the corrected condition tR < k/2) and Theorem 2, as the paper intends.

  • Theorem 6 (ii), case tR < k/3: the paper says "symmetrical". We obtain it from the tL < k/3 case by swapping sides (Symmetry.lean).

  • Adaptive adversaries: not treated (static only). The paper claims its protocols are adaptively secure; we only prove static security.
