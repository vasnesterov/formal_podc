Byzantine Stable Matching — PODC 2025

Andrei Constantinescu, Marc Dufay, Diana Ghinea, Roger Wattenhofer. Byzantine Stable Matching. PODC 2025. DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733525. Formalized version: arXiv 2502.05889 v2 (full version with appendix; numbering follows it). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The solvability characterizations (Theorems 2–7) are proved for every k ≥ 2, in both directions; for the degenerate k = 1 all their "only if" directions are false. The proof of Theorem 2's necessity does not cover k = 2 and the reduction in Lemma 13 is invalid, but both statements hold, by short arguments on groups that keep the paper's cover and split-brain ideas. The sufficiency directions were checked only relative to Theorems 8 and 9 and the cited broadcast protocols, which are hypotheses here.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorems 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, "only if" directions for k = 1. For k = 1 every "only if" direction is false: with one party per side, bSM is solvable with no communication on every network and against every adversary, since each party outputs the only party on the other side (theorem2_only_if_false_k1, bSM_solvable_k1, bSM_solvableAuth_k1). The paper never assumes k ≥ 2, and all characterizations hold for every k ≥ 2, in both directions. For example, Theorem 2's claim that k = 1, tL = tR = 1 is unsolvable (since 1 ≥ k/3) is false. Theorems 6 and 7 are worded as "if" only, but the introduction and §5.2 claim and argue necessity too; Lemma 13 and Corollary 5 fail for k = 1 in the same way.

  • Corollary 1 says "if tL < k/2"; it should be tR < k/2, since in a one-sided network L relays through R.

  • The proof of Theorem 4 says "one of the conditions tL < k/3, tR < k/2"; the second should be tR < k/3.

  • Lemma 5's proof: "c1 must decide to match party v2" should be v1.

  • The proof of Theorem 8 writes n − 2tL for k − 2tL.

  • The proof of Theorem 7, case tR = k, tL < k/3, invokes Theorem 4, which needs tR < k/2. The statement is true: the protocol ΠbSM of Lemma 9 only uses L–R edges, so it also works on the one-sided network and settles the case.

  • The implicit requirement that BA/BB outputs are non-⊥ on runs without omissions (Theorems 8/9) is needed and is made explicit.

Typos

  • The proof of Theorem 4 cites "Corollary 2"; it should be Corollary 1.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 13 · Theorem 2, necessity for k = 2

🔧 Lemma 13 (invalid reduction)

The proof uses Corollary 2 and Lemma 3 to obtain an sSM protocol on 6 nodes with tR = 3, tL = 1, but Lemma 3 with d = 3 only yields ⌊tR/⌈k/3⌉⌋ = ⌊k/⌈k/3⌉⌋ Byzantine right parties, which is less than 3 for k = 4, 5, 7, 8, … (lemma3_not_enough_for_lemma13).

Impact. The statement stands for k ≥ 2, with a repaired proof (lemma13_sSM).

Details

For k = 4: 4/⌈4/3⌉ = 2. Lemma 3 also needs k ≥ 3, and Corollary 2 (unauthenticated bipartite) is irrelevant here.

Repair

We run the split-brain argument directly with groups, for every k ≥ 2.

🔧 Theorem 2, necessity for k = 2

The proof applies Lemma 3 with d = 3, which needs k ≥ 3, so k = 2 is not covered.

Impact. The statement stands; our version of Lemma 5's 12-node cover argument works directly for every k ≥ 2 (lemma5).

Details

For k = 2 the cover degenerates to an 8-node double cover of K₄.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (CSLib, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/).

  • 2k parties Fin k ⊕ Fin k (L, R) run deterministic protocols in synchronous lock-step rounds over a fixed network (fully, oneSided, bipartite) with authenticated point-to-point channels. There is no randomness.

  • Static Byzantine adversary with at most tL corruptions in L and tR in R. Byzantine messages are arbitrary functions of round, sender and receiver, which covers rushing. The paper claims adaptive security for its protocols; we prove only static security.

  • Authenticated setting: ideal unforgeable signatures as abstract tokens, with a rushing, eavesdropping adversary (the strongest standard one, so the impossibilities hold a fortiori).

  • A party's output is its first decision. bSM/sSM are specs on inputs, the honest set and honest decisions.

  • Impossibilities are proved directly for all k ≥ 2 by group-level FLM covers, for every network (Lemma 7/13: every network without L–L edges). This subsumes the paper's "small n + scaling" route.

  • Shared library: lock-step helpers (deliver_honest, firstSome_iff_of_ne, firstSome_map_iff, status_comapInput) from Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/Parallel.lean; the R-parties' vote is Finset.majority? from Cslib/Foundations/Data/Finset/Quorum.lean (PiBSM.majority_eq: "a suggestion of more than half of L").

  • Shared code (CSLib): the generic group simulation of Lemma 3 (GroupSim.*, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/GroupSim.lean), double covers (CoverSystem.doubleCover, …/LockStep/Cover.lean) and labellings into Fin m (Fintype.exists_fin_labelling_le, Cslib/Foundations/Combinatorics/Labelling.lean).

  • Cited protocols are hypotheses: Fitzi–Maurer general-adversary BB (Theorem 10), Dolev–Strong, and Theorems 8/9 (BA/BB with omissions). Only the sufficiency directions use them.
