Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Minimalist Leader Election — PODC 2025.

Paper: R. Vacus, I. Ziccardi, Minimalist Leader Election Under Weak Communication, PODC 2025, DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733559; formalized version arXiv 2502.12697v2 (numbering follows it).

Modelling choices

  1. Randomness as a coin field. The only random transition of BFW is W• → B•. The execution exec G init c is a deterministic function of a coin field c : ℕ → V → Bool (c t v is used only if v ∈ W• hears nothing in round t). All results of Section 3 are proved for every coin field, i.e. for every rule deciding when W• goes to B•, as the paper remarks. For the probabilistic results the coins are i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) indexed by ℕ × V (coinMeasure p = Measure.infinitePi …). Faithful.

  2. Initial configuration. Section 3 and all theorems assume Eq. (2) (GoodInit: every node is waiting and some node is a leader), which is more general than the paper's "all nodes in W•" (initAll, goodInit_initAll).

  3. Graph and diameter. G is a finite connected SimpleGraph. Wherever the paper uses the diameter D, the theorems take any D with dist(a, b) ≤ D for all a, b (so D = diam G is a special case). For Theorem 3 this covers p = 1/(D'+1) for any upper bound D' ≥ diam (part of the paper's remark on constant-factor approximations; approximations from below are not covered).

  4. Paths (Definition 4) are SimpleGraph.Walks; the flow along a path (Definition 5) is walkFlow. N_t^beep(v) counts beeps in rounds 0..t, as in the paper.

  5. "With high probability" is instantiated as probability ≥ 1 − 1/n², with explicit constants; the paper writes 1 − n^{−Ω(1)}.

Changed constants / proofs

  1. Theorem 2, time bound — different proof, explicit constants. The paper derives the O(D² log n) bound from an anti-concentration estimate (Theorem 13 via a Berry–Esseen theorem for mixing sequences, Lemmas 14, 15, 17). We do not formalize those lemmas. Instead, for two leaders u, v the triple (phase u, phase v, N(u) − N(v)) follows a pair chain. For that chain we use M = Δ + h(x_u) − h(x_v), which is a martingale, plus a bounded corrector. Together they give a Lyapunov function with drift exactly −1 (lyap_drift). Hence the chain leaves the strip |Δ| ≤ D within T₂ = ⌈(D+2)²(1+2p)³/(p(1−p)) + 4⌉ steps with probability ≥ 1/2 (alive_half). Result: P(no single leader from round k·T₂ on) ≤ n²·2^{−k} (theorem2_time). With k = ⌈6 ln n⌉ this is ≤ 1/n² (theorem2_whp). Same order O(D² log n) as the paper.

  2. Theorem 2, almost-sure part — separate argument. Proved via windows of 2D+3 rounds with an explicit good event of probability Q = (1−(1−p)^{D+1})(1−p)^{2D+3} > 0 (theorem2_tail, theorem2_ae). See also item 11.

  3. Theorem 3 — window 2D+3 instead of 2D+1, explicit constants. With p = 1/(D+1), D ≥ 1: P(no single leader from round k(2D+3) on) ≤ n²(127/128)^k (theorem3). The leader is unique from round (2D+3)·⌈512 ln n⌉ on with probability ≥ 1 − 1/n² (theorem3_whp). The longer window repairs the gap in item 10.

  4. Lemma 7 is proved for one edge and summed along the walk (flow_succ, lemma7). The paper's regular-expression case analysis is not needed. Lemma 9 is proved in the paper's strong form (lemma9_strong: some leader has the maximal beep count), Lemma 12 in a stronger form (item 10).

Issues in the paper

  1. Lemma 12 is stated too weakly for Lemma 19, and Lemma 19's case analysis has gaps (proof gap; Lemma 19 as stated fails for D ≤ 2; Theorem 3 is true). Lemma 12 says only "v beeps in some round s ≤ t + dis(u,v)". Lemma 19 needs t < s, which the proof of Lemma 12 does give. We prove t < s ≤ t + dis(u,v) (lemma12). In Lemma 19:

    • P(A) = (1−p)^{2(D+1)} ignores the state of v's chain in round t: if v ∈ B•_t, then P(A) = 0.

    • In the case N_t(u) = N_t(v), a beep of u "between t and t+D+1" may be the beep in round t itself. That beep is already counted in N_t(u), so N_s(u) > N_s(v) does not follow.

    • Typo: "N_s^beep(u) ≥ 1" should read N_s(u) > N_s(v).

    • The window 2D+1 is too short for small D when the lemma is read per configuration in round t (as its proof does). On K₂ (D = 1), with both nodes in B• in round t = 1 (both coins true in round 0), both stay leaders up to round t + 3 = t + 2D + 2 for every coin field, so the probability of an elimination before round t + 2D + 1 is 0. On the path u–w–v (D = 2, w eliminated in round 2), the earliest elimination is in round t + 2D + 1, not before it. Both are formalized for every coin field (lemma19_fails_K2: both nodes are leaders up to round 4; lemma19_fails_P3: both endpoints are leaders up to round 5). The averaged form P(A_{t+2D+1} | A_t) ≤ c used in the proof of Theorem 3 is not refuted by this, but it is not proved either.

    Our repair takes the coins of u in rounds t+2..t+D+2 and those of v in rounds t..t+2D+2, with window 2D+3 (elim_window).

  2. Theorem 2's "almost surely" is not proved in the paper (proof gap; the statement is true). The proof shows only P(two leaders in round AD² log n) ≤ n^{−2}, and a w.h.p. bound for each n does not give probability 1. One needs, e.g., Lemma 17 for every A, plus the monotonicity of the leader set. We prove the a.s. statement (theorem2_ae).

  3. Lemma 14 is stated for the wrong chain (imprecise statement). It is stated for N_t^{(u)}, i.e. the chain started in W. The proof uses E N_t = π_B t and Theorem 13, which both need the stationary start. The lemma is applied (in Lemma 15) only to the stationary copy Ñ.

  4. Proof of Lemma 14: a false intermediate inequality (proof gap; the conclusion is true). It claims P(Z ≥ E(Z) + k(2p+1)) ≥ 1/2 for Z binomial and 1 ≤ k ≤ δ√t. A binomial's median is within 1 of its mean, so this probability is < 1/2. Numerically, for p = 1/2 and k = 1 it is 0.333 at t = 100 and 0.447 at t = 1000. The argument works with any constant c ∈ (0, 1/2) in place of 1/2.

  5. Lemma 17's use of Lemma 15 does not match (proof gap; the statement is true).

    • Conditioning on the past fixes δ = N^u − N^v ∈ [−D, D]. The event E_k then needs a bound on P(|Δ + δ| ≤ D) for a shifted interval, but Lemma 15 bounds only P(|Δ| < d).

    • The chain rule needs conditioning on E_1 ∩ … ∩ E_{k−1}, not on E_{k−1} alone.

    • Lemma 15 holds only for "d large enough", while D may be small (D = 1 on cliques).

    All three are routine to fix: use Theorem 13 with a constant c, condition on the whole past, and use windows of length max(D, d₀)². We avoid these lemmas entirely (item 6).

  6. Lemma 21 is off by one (typo). It states P(Σ_{i≤n} W_i ≥ k) = P(Bin(k,p) ≤ n). For geometric W_i counting trials, the right side should be P(Bin(k−1,p) ≤ n−1). Example: n = 1, k = 2, p = 1/2 gives 1/2 vs 3/4. The proof's sentence ("in k trials, fewer than n successes") is also off by one (it describes ΣW_i > k). The form used in Lemma 14 is correct (P(ΣW_i ≤ x) = P(Bin(x,p) ≥ m)).

  7. Claim 16 (the coupled copies differ by at most one beep before they meet): checked by exhaustive search over the pair states; no issue. Not needed for our proof.

16a. Typo in the Discussion (§5). It speaks of "the upper bound in Theorem 1". There is no Theorem 1; Theorem 2 is meant.

Remarks of the paper made precise

  1. "No deterministic algorithm can break symmetry on certain graphs" (§1.3). We prove it for every graph with two distinct nodes and no isolated node (deterministic_impossible): all nodes stay in the same state forever.

  2. The assumption V ⊆ W_0 in Eq. (2) is necessary for Lemma 9 (§5 Discussion; it is not stated as a lemma in the paper). On a triangle where all three nodes are leaders, one in B• and one in F•, every leader is eliminated by round 3 for every coin field (triangle_no_leader).

Not formalized

  1. Theorem 13, Theorem 20 (Berry–Esseen for mixing sequences), Lemmas 14, 15, 17 and 21. Our proof of the Theorem 2 time bound does not need them (item 6).

  2. Table 1 and the related-work comparisons, and the Discussion's conjecture that the O(D² log n) bound is tight up to log n.
