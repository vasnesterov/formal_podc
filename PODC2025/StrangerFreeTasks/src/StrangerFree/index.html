A task is solved by an `Impl` of `taskObj T.In T.Out`, each process invoking it at most once (`OneShot`). **Full information.** `Impl.fullInfo I` is the full-information version of `I`: registers store the writer's *view* (identifier, input, all values read), processes store their view (`Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/FullInfo.lean`). "`q` appears in `p`'s view" is `View.Mentions`. **Stranger-freedom (paper, §2).** A task is stranger-free when, for every full-information algorithm that solves it wait-free using read/write registers only, in every execution, any two processes that have both output are not complete strangers: one of them appears in the view of the other. We quantify over the full-information versions of all read/write algorithms solving the task; every full-information algorithm is (behaviourally) its own full-information version. The progress condition is a parameter (`Progress.waitFree` is the paper's definition; `Progress.obstructionFree` gives the stronger hypothesis needed for the obstruction-free variant of Theorem 2.1 mentioned after its proof). ## Main results * `covering_of_strangerFree`: the covering construction — in some one-shot execution of `I`, the `n` given processes cover `n` distinct registers. * `theorem_2_1`: for `n ≥ 2` processes, every algorithm solving a stranger-free task (wait-free, or obstruction-free for the OF variant) uses at least `n` registers. The proof follows the paper: induction on the number `j` of covered registers, the invariant being a one-shot execution in which only `p₁, …, p_j` participate, they cover `j` distinct registers, and `p₁` appears in no register and no other view (`FIClean p₁`).
