Stranger-Free Tasks — PODC 2025

Eli Gafni, Giuliano Losa, Michel Raynal, Gadi Taubenfeld. Brief Announcement: Stranger-Free Tasks. PODC 2025. DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733545. Formalized version: the brief announcement (no full version used). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Theorem 2.1 (a stranger-free task for n processes needs n registers) and the lower bound of Theorem 3.1 (k-bounded lattice agreement needs k + 1 registers) are proved. The upper bound of Theorem 3.1 is not established: Algorithm 1 is not wait-free for any k ≥ 2, already with two processes, though it is safe and obstruction-free for every k, and wait-free for k = 1. So the claimed wait-free hierarchy is established only in the obstruction-free sense (and for k = 1).

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 3.1, upper bound (Algorithm 1, wait-freedom for k ≥ 2)

Algorithm 1 is not wait-free for any k ≥ 2: with two processes whose double collects keep invalidating each other, both take infinitely many steps in a one-shot execution and never output (exists_livelock, theorem_3_1_alg1_not_waitFree).

Impact. The wait-free upper bound of Theorem 3.1 (k + 1 registers, unboundedly many processes) and the wait-free "infinite hierarchy" of the abstract are not established for k ≥ 2; whether they hold remains open. Algorithm 1 is safe and obstruction-free for every k, and wait-free for k = 1.

Details

§3.1 argues that "processes that keep interfering have seen sets that converge to the same value or eventually grow in cardinality past k". Counterexample with two processes a, b and k = 2, so |seen| = 2 ≤ k never triggers the early exit at line 23. Once both have seen = {a, b}, repeat forever: a does its first collect; b writes; a's second collect fails because b's triple changed; b does its first collect; a writes; b's second collect fails. Converging seen sets do not help: without helping, the double collects keep invalidating each other, as in the double-collect scan of Afek et al., which is only obstruction-free. The livelock does not depend on how line 21 is read. Replacing > k by ≥ k in line 23 would only repair k = 2.

Repair

For k = 1 the claim is true, but the paper's sketch does not apply. Our argument: a process other than p can write A[2] (the second register; A[1] in the 0-based Lean code) only as its second write, and only if it read ⊥ there before anybody wrote it. So only finitely many interfering writes on A[2] exist, while every two failed iterations of p need one (theorem_3_1_k1).

✏️ Slips

  • Remark after the proof of Theorem 2.1: the obstruction-free variant needs stranger-freedom with respect to obstruction-free algorithms, a stronger hypothesis that the paper does not state (theorem_2_1_obstructionFree). It holds for k-bounded lattice agreement, the paper's application.

Typos

  • Line 21 of Algorithm 1 (seen ∪ collect[j][3]) is a no-op as written, probably a typo for A[j][3].

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Safety argument of §3.1

🛠️ Safety argument of §3.1 (more than k + 1 participants)

The argument ("unless at least k + 1 processes participate, the set will always be a subset of at least one register … k processes are not enough to obliterate all k + 1 registers") covers only runs with few participants.

Impact. Statement true; repaired by a counting argument (card_union_of_obliterate, alg1_solves).

Details

With more than k + 1 participants, k + 1 processes poised on distinct registers can overwrite everything after a successful snapshot, and the paper does not say why containment then still holds.

Repair

It does hold, by a counting argument over "clean" writes. A clean write by a process that has completed a collect since all registers became dirty carries a seen-set of size ≥ k + 1, and each process makes at most one write without such a collect. So if all registers become clean, the union of their seen-sets is too large for a later output of size ≤ k (card_union_of_obliterate, alg1_solves).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (CSLib Cslib.SharedMemory).

  • Asynchronous shared memory, deterministic processes, arbitrary interleavings. Atomic multi-writer multi-reader registers (any value type), one register access per step. No crashes: a process may simply stop taking steps. Wait-freedom and obstruction-freedom as usual, on infinite executions and solo runs.

  • Process type: finite with |P| = n for Theorem 2.1 and the lower bound; arbitrary (possibly infinite) for Algorithm 1.

  • One-shot tasks: the input/output relation must hold on every finite one-shot execution (participating = has invoked). Every process may invoke with every input.

  • Full-information versions store (value, writer's view) in registers. The paper's remark that an algorithm and its full-information version solve the same tasks with the same registers is proved. Stranger-freedom quantifies over the full-information versions of all algorithms solving the task.

Remark (no error). The claim of §3.2 that k-bounded lattice agreement for k + 1 processes is stranger-free needs "j in i's output ⇒ j appears in i's view", a step the paper omits as routine. In Lean this takes a causal-erasure argument: erase j's causal future to obtain an execution without j in which i outputs the same set (boundedLA_strangerFree).
