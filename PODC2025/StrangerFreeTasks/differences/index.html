Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Stranger-Free Tasks — PODC 2025.

Paper: Eli Gafni, Giuliano Losa, Michel Raynal, Gadi Taubenfeld. Brief Announcement: Stranger-Free Tasks. PODC 2025, pp. 203–206, DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733545.

Errors in the paper

  1. Algorithm 1 is not wait-free for any k ≥ 2 (Theorem 3.1, §3.1, "Wait-freedom is ensured, even with infinitely many processes, because processes that keep interfering … have seen sets that converge to the same value or eventually grow in cardinality past k"). Counterexample, already with two processes a, b (so |seen| = 2 ≤ k never triggers line 23): once both have seen = {a, b}, schedule a: first collect; b: write; a: second collect (fails, b's new triple differs); b: first collect; a: write; b: second collect (fails); repeat. Both stay pending forever and take infinitely many steps; the execution is one-shot. Converging seen sets do not help: the double collects keep invalidating each other (the double-collect scan of Afek et al. is only obstruction-free without helping). Lean: exists_livelock, alg1_not_waitFree, theorem_3_1_alg1_not_waitFree. Found by an explicit-state model checker (k = 2, n = 2). Line 21 is modelled literally and is irrelevant to the livelock: the variant seen ∪ A[j][3] livelocks as well (all seen-sets stay inside {a, b}); replacing > k by ≥ k in line 23 would only repair k = 2. Consequently the upper bound of Theorem 3.1 (wait-free, k + 1 registers, infinitely many processes) is not established for k ≥ 2, and neither is the "infinite hierarchy" of the abstract in the wait-free sense. What we prove instead (all k ≥ 1, every set of processes, possibly infinite): Algorithm 1 is safe (alg1_solves) and obstruction-free (alg1_obstructionFree); with the lower bound this gives the obstruction-free hierarchy (theorem_3_1_obstructionFree). Whether k-bounded lattice agreement is wait-free solvable with k + 1 registers for unboundedly many processes when k ≥ 2 remains open here. For k = 1 the paper's claim is true (alg1_waitFree_one, theorem_3_1_k1, any — also infinite — set of processes), but with a different argument than the sketch: a process x ≠ p can write A[1] only as its second write and only if it read ⊥ from A[1] before anybody wrote it, so only finitely many interfering writes on A[1] exist, while every two failed iterations of p need one.

  2. Gap in the safety argument of §3.1 (the claim is true). "k processes are not enough to obliterate all k + 1 registers before they have to read the whole array" — with more than k + 1 participants, k + 1 processes poised on distinct registers can obliterate all registers after a successful snapshot. The claim (containment) is nevertheless true; our proof (Alg1SafetyObliterate.lean, card_union_of_obliterate): after a configuration in which every register is S-dirty (S ⊆ seen), a later write is "type 0" if its writer has not completed a collect since, else "type 1"; a clean type-1 write carries a seen-set of size ≥ k + 1, each process makes at most one type-0 write, so if all k + 1 registers become clean the union of their seen-sets has ≥ k + 1 elements — impossible for a later snapshot output of size ≤ k. The double-collect ⇒ atomic snapshot step needs freshness of (id, seq) (Alg1SafetySnapshot.lean).

  3. §3.2, "and thus k-bounded lattice agreement for k + 1 processes is stranger-free": the routine step from "j belongs to i's output" to "j appears in i's view" (validity plus indistinguishability) is omitted. Formalizing it needs a causal-erasure argument: if j did not appear in i's view, erase the causal future of j to get an execution in which j does not participate but i outputs the same set (Impl.fullInfo_exists_erase, mentions_of_mem_output). The claim holds (boundedLA_strangerFree), for every progress condition.

Modelling choices / changed definitions

  • Model. CSLib Cslib.SharedMemory: deterministic processes, atomic MWMR registers (RegFamily ι V v₀, any value type), one shared-memory access per step, returning an output is a separate step. A task is an Impl of the dummy object taskObj In Out; inputs/outputs are the first invocation/response (inputs, outputs); the task relation Δ is required on every finite one-shot execution (Impl.Solves), i.e. on partial executions (participating = has invoked). Every result assumes the relevant processes are allowed to invoke (Impl.allowed).

  • Full-information version. Impl.fullInfo I stores in registers the pair (value of I, view of the writer) and in processes the pair (state of I, view). The paper recomputes I's state from the view; since I is deterministic the cached state is a function of the view, so the behaviour is the same. Views are View.mk owner hist with hist a list of inputs and read values (none = initial value ⊥); "q appears in the view" is View.Mentions (identifiers occurring inside inputs do not count). The paper's remark "an algorithm and its full-information version solve the same tasks and use the same number of registers" is proved (fullInfo_solves, fullInfo_waitFree, fullInfo_obstructionFree, fullInfo_oneShotOF, same register index type ι, fullInfo_covers).

  • Stranger-freedom (Task.StrangerFree T prog) quantifies over the full-information versions of all read/write algorithms solving T with progress prog (instead of "all full-information algorithms"; every full-information algorithm is behaviourally its own full-information version), that accept every input at every process (∀ p x, I.allowed p x; otherwise the hypothesis would be stronger than the paper's for input-dependent tasks), over one-shot executions, and compares the views at the end of the execution (which, in one-shot executions, are the views at output time). prog = .waitFree is the paper's definition. The obstruction-free variant of Theorem 2.1 (remark after its proof) needs the stronger hypothesis T.StrangerFree .obstructionFree (quantifying over obstruction-free algorithms); the paper does not say this. For k-bounded lattice agreement both hold.

  • Theorem 2.1 is stated for a finite process type P with |P| = n ≥ 2, a finite register index type ι, a nonempty input type, and an algorithm accepting every input at every process: n ≤ |ι|; the covering form (covering_of_strangerFree, any n ≥ 2 distinct processes, any P, ι) gives a one-shot execution where the n processes cover n distinct registers. Only obstruction-freedom on one-shot executions (OneShotOF) is used.

  • Proof of Theorem 2.1. Invariant (3) "p₁ has not been written into any register" is formalized as FIClean p₁: p₁ appears in no register and in no view other than its own (needed: the paper's "p_{j+1} cannot know that p₁ is active" also requires that no other process carries information about p₁). Instead of "let all processes run until they terminate" after the block write we only run p₁ solo (obstruction-freedom suffices). "δ₂ would be indistinguishable from the run without p_{j+1}" is realized by comparing the configurations before p_{j+1} started and after its solo run: they agree on p₁ … p_j and outside R, hence agree completely after the block write (Impl.blockWrite_agreeOn), which transports FIClean p_{j+1}.

  • k-bounded lattice agreement: outputs are Finset P; containment is required for outputs of cardinality ≤ k that have been produced so far.

  • Algorithm 1 (Alg1.lean): registers indexed by Fin (k + 1) (0-based); initial triple ⟨none, 0, ∅⟩; each register access is an atomic step, local computation is folded into the preceding access; the decision (lines 23–27) happens in the step of the last read of the second collect; line 21 is taken literally (seen ∪ collect[j][3], a no-op — probably a typo for A[j][3]); a process that has output and is invoked again returns the same output immediately.

  • Lower bound of Theorem 3.1 is stated for obstruction-free algorithms (hence also wait-free) for k ≥ 1 and |P| = k + 1, every process allowed to participate.

Not formalized

  • The anonymous-memory / anonymous-process remarks at the end of §3.1 (Guerraoui–Ruppert collect k(n − 1) + 2 times).

  • The introduction's claims about Delporte-Gallet et al., splitters, adopt-commit, and the Moir–Anderson renaming discussion / open question.
