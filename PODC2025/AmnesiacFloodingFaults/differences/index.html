Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Amnesiac Flooding: Easy to Break, Difficult to Escape — PODC 2025.

Paper: Austin, Gadouleau, Mertzios, Trehan, Amnesiac Flooding: Easy to break, hard to escape, arXiv 2502.06001v2 (full version of the PODC 2025 brief announcement). Numbering follows the arXiv version.

Model and definitions

  1. Graphs. The paper assumes finite connected graphs with at least two nodes. We assume Fintype V where termination is concerned, and G.Connected only in Theorem 5.6 (broadcast part), where it is needed. Everything else holds for arbitrary (finite) graphs.

  2. Configurations (Definition 3.1) are Set (V × V). Results about termination assume IsConfig G S (messages travel along edges), as the paper does.

  3. Definition 5.4 (balance). "For all subgraphs H that are cycles or FECs" is formalized via closed walks:

    • every c : G.Walk u u with c.IsCycle (each cycle subgraph is the support of such walks, with any base point and orientation);

    • every F : FEC G with F.walk = c₁ ++ p ++ c₂ ++ p⁻¹ (any junction and orientation).

    The FEC structure requires:

    • the two odd cycles share only the junction vertex when p is trivial, and are vertex-disjoint otherwise;

    • the path p meets the cycles only at its ends.

    Quantifying over all base points and orientations is harmless:

    • balance along a closed walk is invariant under rotation and reversal;

    • for FECs, the alternating-occupancy part does not depend on the orientation of the cycles, and the flow part follows from the odd-cycle conditions (Balanced ↔ BalancedAll, balancedAll_iff_balanced).

  4. Balance on one walk (BalancedOn). For every position k₀, the clockwise minus anticlockwise messages whose heads (senders) are at positions k₀ + 2i cancel.

    • Odd length: this is exactly "as many messages each way" (balancedOn_iff_of_odd).

    • Even length: it is the paper's "for any message m, equally many each way among messages whose heads are at even distance from m's". We also require it for parity classes with no message, where it holds trivially.

    • The paper's S^{2,H} on H² (Definition 5.3) is exactly the pull-back of S along F.walk. The paper's case-by-case list (messages at a₀/a₋₁, duplicated path messages) is what the pull-back produces.

  5. Theorem 5.1 is stated as (∃ k, A_∅^k(S) = ∅) ↔ Balanced G S for configurations on a finite graph. Corollary 5.1.1 is ∃ k ≤ 2|E|, A_∅^k(S) = ∅, with |E| = #G.edgeFinset.

  6. Theorem 5.5 (2) is ∃ Is : List (Set V), runFrom G Is ∅ = S (apply A_{I_1} first). k = 0 is allowed and gives S = ∅.

  7. Lemma 5.2.

    • The printed T (the sinks of S) is replaced by the proof's T: the sinks of S̄, i.e. the nodes sending to all their neighbours in S (sources). The printed version is refuted (lemma_5_2_as_stated_false).

    • Isolated nodes belong to sources vacuously; this is harmless.

  8. Lemma 5.4 and Lemma A.1 are proved for Balanced (and for each closed walk). Lemma A.1 is for every initiator set I.

Proofs

  1. Appendix A is not followed. The invariance direction (Lemma A.1) is as in the paper, for arbitrary closed walks: they become cycles with external activations, ind_cw_step_sub. The hard direction uses instead:

    • the front lemma not_balancedOn_of_front;

    • ancestor chains and pigeonhole on darts (iterate_eq_empty_of_balancedAll), which gives the 2|E| bound directly;

    • the spanning theorems walkFlow_eq_zero_of_isCycle and walkSig_eq_zero_of_isCycle_of_fec.

    Lemmas A.2–A.15 are not formalized individually.

  2. Theorem 5.5. The paper proves (2) ⇒ (1) via the literature's Theorem 2.1, and uses balance for (1) ⇒ (2). We derive Theorem 2.1 itself (theorem_2_1, lemma_3_1) from the dichotomy.

  3. Theorem 2.1 / Lemma 3.1 are cited from the literature in the paper. Here they are proved (multicast with arbitrary sequences of initiator sets).

Fault models (Section 5.2.2)

  1. Indexing. The paper writes S_{k+1} = A(S_k) \ T with k ≥ 1 (message dropping), and ∀ i ≥ 1, S_{i+1} = A(S_i) \ X (link failures). This lets the first-round messages (and, read literally, the second-round ones) escape the fault. With the literal reading, the second claim of Theorem 5.7 fails on a single edge: the initiator's message crosses the failed link. We let faults act from the first round on:

    • dropRun drops m ∈ srcRun G s k for any k ≥ 0;

    • uniRun removes X in every round, including the initiators' round.

  2. Message dropping. "The message (u, v)" is a message actually sent ((u,v) ∈ srcRun G s k).

    • "Not a bridge" is Mathlib's ¬ G.IsBridge s(u,v).

    • "uv lies on a path between odd cycles" is ∃ F : FEC G, s(u,v) ∈ F.p.edges, the path of a faux-even cycle.

    • "First message sent along uv" means that no message used uv in either direction in an earlier round.

    • "Fails to broadcast" means that some node other than s never receives a message.

    • "The side of the cut containing u does not contain an odd cycle" is ¬ SideHasOddCycle: no odd cycle of G all of whose vertices are reachable from u in G − uv.

  3. Uni-directional failures. X is a set of directed links failing in every round.

    • The second claim of Theorem 5.7 needs, and we assume, that X is nonempty, consists of directed edges, and that no edge fails in both directions. If an edge on a cycle fails in both directions, it simply disappears, and flooding can succeed.

    • The first claim is proved for one initiator (theorem_5_7_singleton; the failed link is at the initiator) and refuted for I = {0, 2} on a path (theorem_5_7_multi_false).

    • "Fail to terminate" is ∀ t, uniRun t ≠ ∅. "Fail to broadcast" is: some non-initiator never receives a message.

  4. Weak-Byzantine nodes. byzRun G I J k B:

    • before round k, the honest messages are those of A, and the J-sent messages are an arbitrary B t restricted to edges; from round k on, everybody follows the protocol;

    • k ≥ k₀ is chosen by the adversary (the paper fixes k₀ = twice the diameter; our results hold for every k₀);

    • Byzantine nodes may also send in the first round.

    The statements:

    • Non-termination is "not eventually quiet" (¬ EventuallyQuiet). "Lies on a cycle or a path between odd cycles" is OnCycleOrOddPath: on the support of a cycle, or of the path of an FEC.

    • Theorem 5.8 (non-termination) is proved as stated, as an iff, assuming I ∩ J = ∅.

    • Theorem 5.8 (broadcast) is false; see the README. The corrected statement (theorem_5_8_broadcast) counts failures among honest non-initiators, and reads "some honest node is unreachable from every initiator in G − J" instead of "J contains a cut vertex set". It needs no disjointness of I and J: initiators in J are treated as Byzantine.

    • If failure is counted over all nodes (Byzantine ones included), the paper's "only if" direction fails instead, even for one initiator, in a degenerate way: on the path s – u – b with J = {b} (a leaf, no cut set), b sends to u in round 1, so u never forwards to b and b is never informed. (Not formalized; noted by the reviewer.)

  5. Theorem 5.6 is proved in two parts: non-termination ("if", as stated) and broadcast ("iff", on a connected graph). Brute force suggests the non-termination part is an "iff"; we did not formalize the converse.

Not formalized

  1. Section 4.

    • Theorem 4.1 (uniqueness): Definition 4.1 is under-specified (what labels and graphs range over, how b is used at non-initiators), and the proof relies on Ramsey numbers, many gadget graphs and an "exhaustive search".

    • Theorem 4.2 / Appendix C.

    Only one item is formalized: the Random Flooding window argument of Proposition C.2 is refuted on the 5-cycle (random_flooding_window_false; Random Flooding as rfStep, a node with bit 1 forwarding to all neighbours). Parrot Flooding (Lemma C.3) was checked by brute force only. Neighbourhood-2 Flooding (Proposition C.1) is elementary and was not formalized.

  2. PODC-version statements.

    • The bound 2|E| − 3 is refuted on the path with two edges (podc_bound_false); we prove the arXiv bound 2|E|.

    • The PODC "consequences" (1)–(3) are not formalized separately: (1) and (2) follow from Theorem 5.6; (3) is refuted by the same path example as Theorem 5.8 (described in words).
