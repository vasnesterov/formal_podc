Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Energy-Efficient Distributed Agreement — PODC 2025.

Paper: Mirault–Robinson, Brief Announcement: Towards Energy-Efficient Distributed Agreement, PODC 2025 (BA). Proofs omitted in the BA are taken from its full version arXiv:2506.12282v1 ("full"), which the BA cites as [12]. Numbering: BA Algorithm 1 = full Algorithm 2; BA Lemmas 1–4 = full Lemmas 2–5; BA Theorem 2 = full Theorem 6; JoinCommittees = full Algorithm 1; binary algorithm = full Algorithm 3.

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 2 (binary consensus) — the algorithm is incorrect; the claim is unestablished. ❌

    • What fails. The full version's Algorithm 3 with its JoinCommittees(f, ⌈√n⌉) and T₀ = ⌈(f+1)/√n⌉ violates agreement for n = 5, f = 3, n = 7, f = 4 and n = 9, f = 7 (Lean: algorithm3_violates_agreement, _7_4, _9_7).

    • Not degenerate. n = 5, f = 3 leaves two non-faulty players, and n = 7 leaves three. A simulation (scripts/family.py) gives a disagreeing execution for every n = s², f = n − 2, 3 ≤ s ≤ 12.

    • The BA sketch fails too. It leaves the committees unspecified ("analogously to the multi value consensus algorithm"). For n = 5, f = 3 the sketch fails for all 1000 assignments of 3 committees of size 3 (scripts/allcomm.py ba). For the full version's exact rules, 970 of the 1000 assignments fail (scripts/allcomm.py v1; the 30 exceptions have C₁ = C₂).

    • Why the Lean executions also cover the sketch. The sketch differs from Algorithm 3 in two places. Input-1 players send to C_1..C_T (Algorithm 3: C_1..C_{T+1}), and the sketch never re-activates them. The Lean executions use neither difference, since the only input-1 player crashes in round 1.

    • Not refuted. Theorem 2 is phrased existentially ("There is a deterministic algorithm …"), and the abstract says "we show that O(⌈f/√n⌉) rounds are possible". The paper's algorithm is wrong, but the existential claim is not refuted, only unestablished. The authors' later OPODIS 2025 paper (arXiv v2, with Meir and Peleg) describes this attack and gives a different algorithm for the same bounds. We did not check it, so the claim stays unestablished here. The BA does present the binary algorithm itself ("our binary consensus algorithm operates as follows").

    • Full-version lemmas. Lemma 11 ("either |P_{f+1}| ≥ f + 1 or the algorithm reaches agreement on 0") is false: in the n = 5 execution P₄ = {p₂, p₃, p₄}, and the decisions differ. Its proof assumes that a 1-valued message is sent and delivered to a new player in every round from t on. In the execution, the round-3 message goes to C₃, whose other members have crashed, and the crashed senders of rounds 1–2 deliver to only one player each. Lemma 9 ("no player receives 1 in two distinct rounds while |P| ≤ f") also fails if a relay's message to its own committee counts: p₄ receives 1 in rounds 2 and 3. Section 4.1 of the full version assumes that a member of C_i is in none of C_{i+1}, …, C_{i+⌈√n⌉+1}. Algorithm 1 (JoinCommittees) does not give this; for n = 9, C₄ = C₁. No assignment can give it once f > ⌈√n⌉, or f = ⌈√n⌉ with n not a perfect square (e.g. n = 5, f = 3): the property makes min(f, ⌈√n⌉ + 2) consecutive committees of size ⌈√n⌉ pairwise disjoint, which needs more than n players since ⌈√n⌉² ≥ n.

  2. Lemma 2 is false as stated — 🔧. The hypothesis "p is alive in round i" means, by the paper's own definition, not crashed in rounds 1, …, i − 1. So p may crash in round i and its message to C_i may be lost (lemma_2_as_stated_false: n = 2, f = 1). The proof uses "p successfully sends its message to all destinations", so the intended hypothesis is recoverable and the fix is local: p does not crash in round i. Lemma 3's proof applies Lemma 2 only in this form. Proved as lemma_2, in the stronger form "every decider decides ≥ v".

  3. Lemma 3 carries a hypothesis m ≥ δ that is not needed. lemma_3 is proved without it. Here m is the maximum over the members of C_i that have not crashed by the end of round i (the strongest reading). The proof is direct propagation along non-faulty committee members (mv_le_correct) and does not go through Lemma 2.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. Lemma 4 (agreement) — proof gap 🔧.

    • The case where the first clean round is t = f + 1 is missing. The proof covers only t = 1 and 1 < t < f + 1. Repair: every decider receives all alive C_f members, and Lemma 3 bounds its own value.

    • "Since p does not crash" is unjustified. p is an arbitrary alive member of C_t holding δ and may crash later. Repair: after a clean round t ≤ f, all alive members of C_t hold the same value (mv_clean_le, via Lemma 3 at round t − 1), so the non-faulty member of C_t holds it and carries it to round f + 1.

    • Case t = 1 ("it follows from Lemma 2 that no player has an input greater than δ") is fine for the corrected Lemma 2 because nobody crashes in round 1.

    • Our proof (lemma_4) does not use δ. It shows directly that every decider decides the same value: the common value after the clean round, or the maximum over C_f.

  2. Energy bound of Theorem 1. The BA gives only the one-line argument. We prove the explicit bound 2 + 2·#{i ≤ f : p ∈ C_i} ≤ 2 + 2⌈f(f+1)/n⌉ (mv_awakeCount_le, card_filter_mem_joinCommittees_le).

  3. Message bound of Theorem 1. We prove (f+1)·k² + 2nk for committees of size ≤ k, which gives (f+1)³ + 2n(f+1) for k = f + 1 (mv_msgCount_le). The paper's count is n(f+1) + (f−1)(f+1)² + (f+1)n; ours bounds rounds 1 and f + 1 by (f+1)² more each. Both are O(f³ + nf).

Modelling choices

  1. Sleeping model.

    • Awake/asleep is a function of the round number and the state at the start of the round, i.e. an alarm set at the last awake round. An asleep player does not change state.

    • A message is delivered iff its sender has not crashed before the round, is awake, and (in its crashing round) the recipient is in the adversary's reach set, and the recipient is awake. This is exactly the BA's "only an arbitrary subset of messages sent by p are guaranteed to be delivered".

    • Self-messages are allowed. For Algorithm 1 they make no difference. In Algorithm 3 a relay's message to its own committee reaches itself, which changes nothing because Y is already 1.

  2. Deciders. Every player that has not crashed by the end of round f + 1 decides its Y. Agreement and validity are proved for all of them, which is slightly stronger than for "non-faulty" players. Termination at round f + 1 holds by construction.

  3. Values. Multi-value inputs come from an arbitrary linear order (the paper uses integers, "polynomial in n"). This is a generalization. Message sizes (O(log |Δ|) bits, constant for binary) are not formalized: each message carries a single value or a single bit.

  4. f ≥ 1 is assumed for Algorithm 1 (✏️: with f = 0 the pseudo-code refers to C_0). f < n is needed for the committees of size f + 1 (from JoinCommittees(f, f+1), Algorithm 1 of the full version). The correctness lemmas (lemma_2, lemma_3, lemma_4) hold for any committees with more than f members.

  5. JoinCommittees is formalized as in the full version. Slot i ∈ [1, ab] goes to player i mod n and committee ⌈i/b⌉. The BA only requires "at most ⌈f(f+1)/n⌉ committees per player", which we prove.

  6. Ceilings. In algorithm3, ⌈√n⌉ and ⌈(f+1)/√n⌉ are real ceilings (Nat.ceil), evaluated with ceil_sqrt_eq and ceil_div_sqrt_eq.

  7. Algorithm 3 details. In round h ∈ [2, f] an active player decrements T (line 12) and an activation in the same round resets it to T₀ (line 17). This follows the order of the pseudo-code. In round 1 a player with X = 1 sets T ← T₀ (line 5) even if it is not activated. In round f + 1 a player with X = 1 decides 1 (line 21). The counterexamples do not depend on these choices: the input-1 player crashes in round 1, and every relay is activated exactly once.
