import Papers.PODC2025.HidingColoringCertification.Forgetful /-! # Theorem 4.2 is false Theorem 4.2 of the brief announcement (= Theorem 1.2 of arXiv:2502.13854v1): *Let `r ≥ 1` and let `H` be a class of graphs containing at least one `r`-forgetful (connected) graph that is neither an even cycle nor has minimum degree 1. Then there is no strong and hiding `r`-round LCP for `2-col_H`, no matter the certificate size; this holds even if the LCP is not anonymous.* We state its `r = 1` instance in our one-round model (`BATheorem42`; with the hypotheses on the special graph *strengthened*: strict forgetfulness, connected, not 2-regular) and refute it (`baTheorem42_false`) with the counter LCP (`ModCounter.lean`) on `H = ` all bipartite graphs, which contain `θ(5,5,5)`: the counter LCP is complete, sound, strongly sound on every graph, uses `O(log n)`-bit certificates, is anonymous, and is hiding even against identifier-aware extraction algorithms. The authors' corrected version (arXiv v3, Theorems 2(1), 3(1), 4: classes of `r`-forgetful bipartite graphs avoiding minimum degree 1 and even cycles; anonymous or order-invariant LCPs; any certificate size) is refuted as well (`v3Theorem4_false`), with the class `{θ(5,5,5)}`. The gap in the proof: Lemma 5.3 (v3: Lemma 21) needs the odd closed walk to have at most `√(N/Δ^r)` views, but the odd cycles of `V(D,n)` can be much longer than `N` — for the counter LCP every odd
