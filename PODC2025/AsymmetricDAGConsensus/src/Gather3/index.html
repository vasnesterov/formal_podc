Algorithm 3 in the asynchronous interleaving model `Cslib.Byzantine.AsyncBroadcast` (Byzantine faults, adaptive corruption, authenticated channels). Each handler of the pseudocode is a disjunct of the send guard: * line 43: arb-broadcast the input (`GMsg.val`); * line 46: once the arb-delivered pairs `S` cover a quorum, send `DISTRIBUTES S` (once); * line 48: on `DISTRIBUTES S` from `j` with `S ⊆ Sᵢ` and before sending `DISTRIBUTET`, acknowledge (`GMsg.ack j S`, addressed to `j`; `Tᵢ` is the union of the acknowledged sets); * line 51: `READY` after acknowledgements of the own `DISTRIBUTES` from a quorum; * lines 53/55: `CONFIRM` after `READY` from a quorum or `CONFIRM` from a kernel; * line 57: `DISTRIBUTET Tᵢ` (once) after `CONFIRM` from a quorum, `Tᵢ` = union of the acknowledged sets; afterwards no more acknowledgements (`sentT`); * lines 60/62: ag-deliver `U` once `DISTRIBUTET` messages `T_q` with `T_q ⊆ Sᵢ` were received from all `q` of a quorum; `U` contains these `T_q` and only received `DISTRIBUTET` sets with `T ⊆ Sᵢ`. The asymmetric reliable broadcast of the inputs is the `val` message; its guarantees for the guild that are not automatic in the model (consistency and totality for Byzantine senders) are hypotheses on the execution (`RBC`), exactly the properties of the primitive of Alpos et al. This file: the protocol, invariants of reachable states, and the safety lemmas (Lemma 3.4, Lemma 3.5, Lemma 3.7 = common core, Lemma 3.8 validity and agreement) for any guild `G` of the execution.
