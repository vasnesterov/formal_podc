Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Auditing without Leaks Despite Curiosity — PODC 2025.

Paper: Attiya, Fernández Anta, Milani, Rapetti, Travers, Auditing without Leaks Despite Curiosity, PODC 2025 (arXiv 2505.00665 v1).

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 38 ("no writeMax has input w" in some indistinguishable execution) is false for nonces in ℕ, at w = w₀. ✏️

    • Lean: MaxReg.lemma38_false (arbitrary nonces) and MaxReg.lemma38_false_fresh (nonces pairwise distinct and ≠ N₀). Both refute the Lean statement Lemma38 of the paper's lemma.

    • The paper never fixes the nonce domain ("a fresh random nonce", "a random number"). The proof silently needs a domain with no least element (no input u < w₀ exists for w = w₀) and, for bounded nonces, no greatest element (N' > N).

    • The max register is presented as an extension ("We extend this implementation…"), not as the primary contribution.

    • Scope of the failure:

      • ℕ is our choice; the paper's model does not force it. The paper's proof (N' > N) implies nonces unbounded above. Among such domains the lemma fails exactly when the domain has a least element (ℕ, ℕ \ {N₀}, …). It seems to hold when the domain has none (ℤ, ℚ); this is not formalized. With bounded nonces (k-bit strings, the usual reading of "a random number") it fails for every w, but the proof's N' > N rules that reading out.

      • With ℕ nonces the failure is confined to w = w₀. A reader learns a pair only by fetching it (then its read is effective), the initial pair excepted, and ℕ has no largest nonce. So the leaked operation is writeMax(w₀), which does not change the abstract max register. Algorithm 3, the paper's only use, writes version numbers ≥ 1 and never invokes it. Still, the lemma explicitly quantifies "for every value w", writeMax(w₀) is an ordinary invocation with a real leak under Definition 3, and the proof covers w₀ ("the largest input smaller than w") without noticing that no such input exists. With w = w₀ the proof breaks in every nonce domain; only the statement is rescued in ℤ, by a different construction.

    • Theorem 40's clause vs Lemma 38. The Lean counterexample has two writers and refutes Lemma 38's "no writeMax has input w" in any indistinguishable execution. Under the per-instance Definition 3 (UncompromisedFam, item 4), the two writers can swap inputs, and the specific instance writeMax(0) of A is uncompromised. With a single writer the swap is impossible: M is monotone, so the writer's first operation must have input w₀. That variant would refute the per-instance reading of Theorem 40 too; it is not formalized.

  2. Claim 4, Lemma 3, Lemma 5 need at least one writer and two values. ✏️ These are degenerate cases, and "multi-writer" implies a writer exists.

    • With W = ∅, a just-invoked read is v₀-effective but not reported by a later audit.

    • Lean hypotheses: [Nonempty W], ∃ v1 v2, v1 ≠ v2 (claim4, effective_read_iff, lemma5).

    • Lemma 6 similarly needs a value v' ≠ v (lemma6).

  3. Lemma 5's quantification. "An audit invoked and returns in an extension α' of α": we read this as invoked after α (¬ Invoked α (au a, n)). An audit already running when the read becomes effective can miss it (it read R before the fetch&xor). The proof's "audit linearized after the read is effective" shows the intent. This is an ambiguity, not counted as an error.

  4. Definition 3 for writes. "op is not invoked in β" is formalized as "the instance x is not invoked with the same operation (same input)". This is what Lemma 6's proof uses: it replaces the input.

    • If the instance itself (process plus index) had to be absent, Theorem 8's write clause would fail trivially. With a single writer, a reader that fetches sequence number 2 knows the writer invoked at least two writes.

    • Theorem 8's write clause is formalized as Lemma 6.

Proof-level issues

  1. Lemma 7's proof is incomplete. 🛠️ The lemma is true; we prove a stronger version (lemma7, read_uncompromised). The two gaps:

    • Removing p_k's direct read also removes its SN.compare&swap (line 5). That step can be the one advancing SN, which p_j observes. Repair: the writer poised at line 15 for that sequence number takes that step instead; it exists by DInv. Its own later line-15 step is dropped.

    • Writers' R.compare&swap may fail only because of p_k's fetch&xor. Without p_k it would succeed and the executions diverge. Repair: drop every writer loop iteration whose compare&swap fails in α (or is unfinished). Such an iteration is a loop in the writer's local state and only writes V/B.

    • Also dropped are the writers' B[s][k] writes. The new pads are flipped at bit k exactly where p_j observes p_k's bit (padsB).

  2. Linearization. The paper's L(α) (rules R1–R3) is only sketched; Lemmas 21–24 are given with proofs that rely on it. We use a different, forward-simulation linearization (Lin.lean):

    • the abstract value is the value of sequence number SN;

    • all writes with sequence number x+1 (silent ones first, then the visible one) are linearized at σ_{x+1};

    • fetch&xor / audit reads of R during D_{x+1} are linearized there too, in access order (ghost list Dl);

    • other operations are linearized as in R1 (silent reads at their SN read, even during D).

    This avoids the retroactive insertions of R2/R3 and yields strong linearizability (not claimed by the paper). Lemma 3 refers to the paper's L(α); we prove its content for our linearization (effective_of_mem_lin, effective_read_iff, audit_sound).

  3. Lemma 2 / §3.2 prose. 🔧 "The repeat loop terminates after at most m+1 iterations": with the exiting read of R counted as an iteration, m + 2 iterations are possible (m failures caused by readers, one by a concurrent visible write, then the exit). Not formalized as a bound. Wait-freedom is proved with a potential that allows m + 2 (alg1_waitFree).

Typos

  1. Algorithm 1:

    • line 22: lsa ← sn should be lsa ← rsn;

    • line 21: bits[j] should be rbits[j].

  2. Lemma 19 "For x > 1" should be x ≥ 1. Claim 4's proof "x > prev_val" should be prev_sn. Lemma 7's proof "only changes the kth tracking bit of R unchanged to, say, b".

  3. Appendix B (the first two are ✏️):

    • q_x "reads x from SN" should be x − 1;

    • "V[x] and the B[x] are set to their final value by q_x when applying A_x" should refer to V[x−1], B[x−1];

    • Lemma 25 lists the σ_ℓ lines inconsistently.

  4. Algorithm 2, line 30 (✏️): continue must re-enter the loop at line 26. The agent model reads it so; otherwise mval at line 34 is undefined.

Modelling choices

  1. Shared-memory model. CSLib Impl: each step applies exactly one primitive. The for each loops (lines 13, 18–20) are unfolded into one step per register access. The writer writes only the B[s][j] with deciphered bit set (no dummy steps); the auditor reads all B[s][j], j < m.

  2. Primitives.

    • fetch&xor on R flips the one tracking bit of the caller; this is fetch&xor(2^j) on the encoded triple.

    • compare&swap returns a success flag.

    • SN.compare&swap(-1, 0) (paper) becomes cas(0, 0) on ℕ. It is harmless: it never changes SN.

  3. Processes. Readers, writers and auditors are disjoint roles (Proc.rd/wr/au). The paper's writers and auditors share the pads; here the pads are a parameter of the implementation. The reader's identity is an argument of read (AOp.read j), and only reader j may invoke it (allowed).

  4. Randomness. One-time pads rnd : ℕ → Fin m → Bool, and for Algorithm 2 the nonces nc : W → ℕ → ℕ. These are the parameter θ of a family of deterministic implementations. Indistinguishable executions may use other pads and nonces (Impl.IndistFam).

  5. Views. The view of p is its initial local state and its observations: invocations, primitive responses, returned values. Lemmas 6 and 7 are therefore proved for the finest notion of indistinguishability.

  6. Audit sets are Set (Fin m × V) in Algorithm 1 and Finset in Algorithm 2 (for decide).

  7. Algorithm 2 instance. Values and nonces in ℕ, lexicographic pairs, initial pair (0,0). The counterexample instance has one reader, two writers and no auditors. The model itself is generic in m, W, Au.

Not formalized

  1. Linearizability and wait-freedom of Algorithm 2 (Lemmas 28–37, rest of Theorem 40), Section 5 (Theorem 12, auditable snapshot; Theorem 13, versioned types), the consensus-number discussion.
