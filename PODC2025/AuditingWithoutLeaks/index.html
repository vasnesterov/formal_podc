Auditing without Leaks Despite Curiosity — PODC 2025

Hagit Attiya, Antonio Fernández Anta, Alessia Milani, Alexandre Rapetti, Corentin Travers. Auditing without Leaks Despite Curiosity. PODC 2025. DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733516. Formalized version: arXiv 2505.00665 v1; numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Theorem 8, the primary result, holds in full: Algorithm 1 is a linearizable, wait-free auditable MWMR register whose audits report exactly the effective reads, and whose writes and reads are uncompromised by curious readers; we prove it, and the register is even strongly linearizable. The proof of Lemma 7 misses two effects of the removed reader and takes a new simulation, while Lemma 38 and the other statement mistakes are slips. Linearizability and wait-freedom of the max register and the snapshot extensions were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Section 3.2, Termination (the bound m + 1)

The claim that the repeat loop (lines 9–14) terminates after at most m + 1 iterations is off by one: m failures caused by readers, one caused by a concurrent visible write, then the exiting iteration give m + 2.

Impact. One constant changes and writes stay wait-free: alg1_waitFree is proved with a potential that allows m + 2. The bound itself is not formalized.

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 38 (max-register extension, at the initial value w₀). With natural-number nonces, even fresh ones, a reader with no w₀-effective read can still learn that writeMax(w₀) occurred, so Lemma 38's "no writeMax has input w" in some indistinguishable execution fails for w = w₀ (lemma38_false, all nonces; lemma38_false_fresh, fresh nonces). The paper never fixes the nonce domain.

    • Impact: Theorem 8 is unaffected. With nonces in ℕ the failure is confined to w = w₀: any other pair the reader knows, it has fetched (which makes its read effective), and ℕ has no largest nonce. writeMax(w₀) does not change the abstract max register, and the paper's own use (Algorithm 3, version numbers ≥ 1) never invokes it.

    • The claim: in Algorithm 2, for every value w and reader p_j, either p_j has a w-effective read, or some execution indistinguishable to p_j has no writeMax(w). The proof replaces (w, N) by (u, N'), where u is "the largest input smaller than w" and N' > N. It fails because no input smaller than w = w₀ exists.

    • Counterexample (values and nonces in ℕ, initial pair (0, 0), one reader, two writers): writer A runs writeMax(0) with nonce 2, so R becomes (1, (0,2), ·); writer B runs writeMax(1) with nonce 1, so R becomes (2, (1,1), ·); the reader runs one read, fetching (2, (1,1), ·) and returning 1.

    • The reader has no 0-effective read. Yet in every execution it cannot distinguish from this one, it fetches the same triple. R.seq grows by one per change and R.val strictly increases, so the pair stored at sequence number 1 lies strictly between (0,0) and (1,1). That pair is (0, N) with N ≥ 1, or (1, 0). (1, 0) reuses the initial nonce and is excluded by freshness. So a writeMax(0) occurs in every such execution. The reader learns that 0 was written without reading it.

  • Claim 4, Lemmas 3, 5 and 6 (no writer, one value). These results fail without writers or with a single value: a read is then v₀-effective as soon as it is invoked, and an audit invoked right after the invocation does not report it, so Lemma 5 fails; Lemma 6's proof needs a value v' ≠ v. We assume a writer and two distinct values, and "multi-writer" implies the first (claim4, lemma5, lemma6). Lemma 5 also needs the audit to be invoked after α. An audit already running when the read becomes effective may miss it. The proof ("linearized after the read is effective") shows this is the intended reading.

  • Appendix B: q_x reads x − 1, not x, from SN.

  • Appendix B: V[x−1] and B[x−1] are finalized by A_x, not V[x] and B[x].

  • Algorithm 2, line 30: continue must restart at line 26, since mval is undefined otherwise.

Typos

  • Algorithm 1, line 22: lsa ← sn should be lsa ← rsn.

  • Line 21 uses bits for the audit's rbits.

  • Lemma 19 has "for x > 1" where it means x ≥ 1.

  • Claim 4's proof has "x > prev_val" where it means prev_sn.

  • Lemma 7's proof has the garbled "only changes the kth tracking bit of R unchanged to, say, b".

  • Lemma 25 lists the σ_ℓ lines inconsistently.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 7

🛠️ Lemma 7 (removing the reads of p_k)

The proof removes the reads of p_k and flips bit k of the pads, but misses two effects of p_k: its SN step and the writers' failed compare&swaps it causes.

Impact. The statement holds, even in a stronger form: in the indistinguishable execution p_k invokes nothing at all (lemma7, read_uncompromised, core remove_reader). The paper's construction does not give it; it takes a new simulation.

Details

  • A direct read also runs SN.compare&swap(sn−1, sn) (line 5). If that step is the one that moves SN, removing it changes the values of SN that p_j reads later.

  • A writer's R.compare&swap may fail only because p_k inserted itself into the reader set. Without p_k it would succeed earlier, and the executions diverge.

Repair

The writer poised at line 15 takes over p_k's successful SN step. Writer loop iterations whose compare&swap fails in α are dropped. These iterations start and end in the same local state and only write V/B, which readers never read.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory in CSLib (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl) with deterministic processes. Crashes are modelled by processes that stop taking steps; this matches the paper's honest-but-curious attacker.

  • Processes: m readers, any type W of writers and any type Au of auditors.

  • Base objects: R, holding a triple with read, fetch&xor on one tracking bit, and compare&swap returning a success flag; SN, a compare&swap register; registers V[s]; Boolean registers B[s][j].

  • Each step applies one primitive, so the for each loops are unfolded.

  • Randomness (one-time pads; nonces for Algorithm 2) is a parameter of a family of implementations.

  • The implemented object is an auditable register. read j records (j, value) and audit returns the set of recorded pairs; the reader's identity is an argument of read.

  • Views and indistinguishability (Podc/.../SharedMemory/View.lean): p's view is its initial local state plus its sequence of invocations, primitive responses and returned values. This is the finest reasonable reading of α|p, so "indistinguishable" results are as strong as possible.

  • Definition 2 is formalized literally, quantifying over all indistinguishable executions (any pads) and all extensions (Impl.EffectiveFam).

  • Definition 3 is Impl.UncompromisedFam: some indistinguishable execution does not invoke the same operation, with the same arguments, as that instance. For writes this is the paper's "no write with input v" (Lemma 6).

  • Algorithm 2: values and nonces in ℕ, lexicographic pairs, initial pair (0, 0).

Not formalized.

  • Linearizability and wait-freedom of Algorithm 2 (Lemmas 28–37).

  • Section 5: auditable snapshots (Theorem 12) and versioned types (Theorem 13).

  • Lemma 3 as an "iff" for a single linearization. Our L omits the pending reads of the current D phase, which are linearized at the next σ. We prove that every read in L is effective and characterize effective reads by fetches (effective_read_iff).

  • The paper's linearization L(α) (rules R1–R3, Lemmas 21–24). It is described only in outline: rules R1–R3 push silent reads and silent writes back before the visible write. We found no concrete flaw in it, but did not check this route. We use a different, prefix-closed linearization, which even gives strong linearizability, not claimed by the paper (alg1_stronglyLinearizable). The writes with sequence number x+1 are linearized at the step that moves SN to x+1. Fetch&xor and audit reads of R in the window between are linearized there too, in access order.

  • Lemma 38 in other nonce domains. With bounded nonces (k-bit strings) the leak the nonces were meant to prevent comes back: reading (v, N_max) and later (v+2, N_min) two sequence numbers apart reveals a writeMax(v+1). The lemma seems to need nonces from a domain with no least and no greatest element, such as ℤ or ℚ. That case is not formalized, and neither is the bounded-nonce leak.

  • Theorem 40's clause. Our Lean counterexample uses two writers. Under a per-instance reading of Definition 3, the writers could swap inputs, so it refutes Lemma 38's "no writeMax has input w", not that reading of Theorem 40's clause. With a single writer the swap is impossible, but that variant is argued, not formalized.
