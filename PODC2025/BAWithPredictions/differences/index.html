Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Byzantine Agreement with Predictions — PODC 2025.

Paper: Ben-David, Dzulfikar, Ellen, Gilbert, Byzantine Agreement with Predictions, PODC 2025 (arXiv:2505.01793v1; numbering follows the arXiv version, whose Theorems 13/14 are the PODC paper's lower bounds).

Summary. No main result is false.

  • The message lower bound (Theorem 14) and the round lower bound (Theorem 13, unauthenticated algorithms) are proved as stated. Theorem 13's proof cites a crash-fault bound that is valid for halting times (the paper's literal measure) but not for decision times. We prove the stronger decision-time version by reducing to the Byzantine model and proving the cited early-deciding bound there.

  • The conditional algorithms (Theorems 5 and 6) and the classification lemmas (Lemmas 1–6) are correct. Lemma 23 has a fixable proof gap. Lemmas 5 and 6 hold under weaker hypotheses.

  • The wrapper (Algorithm 1, Lemmas 28–33) is verified against the specifications of the cited sub-protocols, which are not themselves formalized.

  • The rest are under-specified details, typos and loose asymptotic statements.

1. Lower bounds (Section 10)

  1. Theorem 13: the cited bound covers halting, not decision times (⚠️, statement true).

    • What the proof does. It simulates the predicted algorithm A by an algorithm S' without predictions on n' = n − x processes "at most t' of which may fail by crashing". Then it invokes [21] (Dolev–Reischuk–Strong): every algorithm has an execution with f' faults taking min{f'+2, t'+1} rounds.

    • When this is correct. For crash faults, min{f+2, t+1} is the DRS bound on stopping (all correct processes halt). It is also the bound on deciding for uniform consensus (Charron-Bost–Schiper, Keidar–Rajsbaum). The paper says A "terminates within T(f) rounds" and that rounds "are performed", and [21] is about early stopping. Read as halting time, the proof is correct.

    • When it is not. If the measure is the round in which the last honest process decides, the crash reduction does not suffice. S' inherits only agreement among correct processes, and non-uniform crash consensus can decide in f + 1 rounds. Example with n = 3, t = 1: every process relays all inputs it knows for t + 1 rounds. A process that received all n inputs in round 1 decides their minimum at once; the others decide the minimum they know at round t + 1. This is correct against one crash. Every failure-free run decides at round 1 = f + 1 < min{f+2, t+1} = 2. It is consistent with the classical t + 1 bound, which concerns runs with f = t.

    • What we prove. We formalize the decision-time version, which is stronger: an honest process cannot halt before deciding. The simulation works verbatim for Byzantine S': Byzantine q_i sends the messages of Byzantine p_i (simProtocol, solves_simProtocol, roundLowerBoundWith).

    • The cited bound is proved, not assumed. We prove the Byzantine early-deciding bound min{f'+2, t'+1} for n' ≥ t' + 2 and at least two values (earlyDecidingLB_strong, drsBound). The classical chain argument over send-omission runs gives f' + 1 (earlyDecidingLB_weak). The extra round comes from one more process, which equivocates in the last round (sameVal_hybrid); this is possible because f' < t'.

    • Result. Theorem 13 holds as stated, for decision times, for unauthenticated algorithms (theorem13).

    • Scope. theorem13 uses CSLib's unauthenticated LockStep.Solves. Algorithms with signatures are not covered. These matter for n/3 ≤ t < n/2, the regime of Theorems 6 and 12. For n ≤ 3t the hypothesis of theorem13 is unsatisfiable.

    • We did not formalize the crash-model example above; it is a remark about the citation, not about the theorem.

  2. Theorem 13: "an execution with f faults" needs padding (imprecise proof). The cited bound produces an execution with at most f' faults. To get exactly f faulty processes we declare extra processes Byzantine that behave honestly (padScenario). This needs a spare process other than the slow one, which f ≤ t < n − 1 provides. We also note:

    • Theorem 13 implicitly needs at least two values: with one value, deciding at round 0 is correct.

    • "At least R rounds are performed" is formalized as "some honest process has no output after R − 1 rounds".

  3. Theorem 14: proof gap in the "without loss of generality |S₀| ≥ ⌈(n−1)/2⌉" step (statement true).

    • The gap. S₀/S₁ are defined as the processes that decide 0/1 for some prediction "when they receive no messages". A process need not decide at all when it receives nothing, since such a run need not be an admissible execution. So the sets need not cover the non-senders.

    • Fix. We fix one pretended input and split the processes by whether their isolated run ever decides v₁. Processes that do pretend to have the honest value v₀; processes that do not pretend v₁. Both cases give a contradiction in E_bad (t_sq_om).

  4. Theorem 14 is proved directly for agreement and for broadcast.

    • The paper proves it for Byzantine broadcast and says "this implies the same bounds for Byzantine agreement". The O(n)-message reduction only transfers the t² part; the Ω(n) part needs its own argument.

    • We prove both problems directly: agreement (message_lower_bound, message_lower_bound_classification) and broadcast (message_lower_bound_bb).

    • The explicit bound is n + t² ≤ 8·#messages + 1.

    • The faulty processes of E_good ignore all messages, not only the first ⌊t/2⌋. This is equivalent, because messages sent are counted, not received ones.

    • The bound holds for any prediction type in which honest processes can receive correct predictions, with or without signatures, and for any delay bound δ.

  5. The abstract and introduction of arXiv v1 state Theorem 14 loosely (imprecise claim).

    • They state an Ω(n²) lower bound, as does the first paragraph of §10.2. The theorem proves Ω(n + t²), which is weaker when t = o(n).

    • Ω(n + t²) = Ω(n²) when t = Θ(n), the usual convention for Dolev–Reischuk (which the paper itself quotes as Ω(n²)). Read literally, Ω(n²) is false for small t. For t = 0, "send your value to p₁; p₁ broadcasts the minimum" uses 2(n−1) messages.

    • The PODC abstract states Ω(n + t²).

    • Not formalized beyond Theorem 14 itself.

2. Classification and orderings (Section 6)

  1. Lemma 1 is proved in exact form: k_A · (⌈n/2⌉ − f) ≤ B (card_mis_mul_le). The O(B/n) statement follows for f ≤ (1/2 − ε)n. Observations 1 and 2 are merged into obs12.

  2. The position formula has a typo. For c[i] = 0 the paper writes position n − f + i − Σ_{j≤i} c[j]. It should be Σ_j c[j] + i − Σ_{j≤i} c[j]; n − f is only right for c = ĉ. The second form i + Σ_{j>i} c[j] is correct. Our pos is defined directly.

  3. Lemma 5 is proved under weaker hypotheses. We only need 1 ≤ ℓ and r ≤ n − |F|, with the same size r − ℓ + 1 − k_A (lemma5). The paper's proof is index-heavy and hard to check; ours uses rank U j ≤ pos(c_i, j) ≤ rank W j (pos_mem_Icc), where U are the properly classified honest processes and W = U ∪ mis.

    • Brute force over all instances with n ≤ 8 found no counterexample to the paper's version.

    • ℓ ≥ 1 is needed: positions are 1-indexed.

    • Lemma 6's hypothesis r ≤ n − t − k_H is not needed either.

  4. Lemma 6 is stated ambiguously. "The number of honest processes p_i such that i ∈ {π(c_i)[j] | j ≤ r}" is used in two ways:

    • Theorem 5 uses it for a process in its own ordering;

    • Lemma 24 uses the union over all honest orderings.

    We prove the union version (lemma6), which implies both.

  5. Lemmas 2–4 and Corollary 1 are pos_mem_Icc, pos_gt_of_false, lemma4/lemma4' and corollary1. Lemmas 15 and 19 (a phase with only honest leaders) are combined into exists_clean_block, a general pigeonhole statement about blocks of positions.

3. The unauthenticated conditional algorithm (Section 7)

  1. Algorithm 4 line 4 has a typo. It reads y ∈ L_y; it should be y ∈ T_i. Also m_i[z] is undefined for z ∉ T_i, but only z ∈ T_i ∩ L_i is used.

  2. Under-specified details; we fix choices, and the results hold for every choice.

    • Algorithm 3 line 14 may have several values with ≥ k+1 copies; any choice works.

    • Lines 4–5 have a unique value with ≥ 2k+1 copies only when |L_i| ≤ 4k+1.

    • Conciliation paths include the trivial path (z reaches itself).

    • When T_i ∩ L_i = ∅, conciliation returns the process's own value; this cannot happen under the core-set assumption.

    • Ties in plurality votes are broken arbitrarily.

  3. We model no early return. In Algorithm 5 a process returns one phase after deciding. In our model processes keep running to round 5(2k+1). The output is set when the process decides (line 12), or at the end (line 14), and never changes (dec_mono). This only adds honest participation, and the outputs are the paper's return values. The message bound is proved for this version and matches the paper's: each honest process broadcasts only in the phase where it is its own listener.

  4. Algorithm 3 is proved under weaker assumptions. The paper's precondition |L_i| = 3k + 1 is replaced by |L_i| ≤ 3k + 1 (CoreSet).

  5. Lemma 16, k = 0 (imprecise). It says the processes return "at the end of phase 2", but for k = 0 there is only one phase. Lemma 17's "no honest process returns before phase φ+1" should be "before the end of phase φ+1".

  6. An informal remark of §7 is false (not used anywhere). The introduction of Section 7 says "if the number of misclassified processes is larger than k … if any honest process decides, then they all decide".

    • Counterexample. Take k = 0, so there is one phase with singleton listening sets. Suppose honest p_i ranks the faulty q first and honest p_j ranks the faulty q' ≠ q first. q sends v in all rounds, so p_i gets grade 1 twice and decides v at line 12. q' stays silent, so p_j never gets grade 1 and only returns its own value at line 14.

    • This is outside the theorem's hypotheses. Algorithm 1 only uses that the conditional algorithm terminates within its time limit. Not formalized.

  7. The Section 7 introduction states the hypothesis as (2k+1)(3k+1) + k ≤ n − t, Theorem 5 as (2k+1)(3k+1) ≤ n − t − k. They are equivalent.

4. The authenticated conditional algorithm (Section 8)

  1. Lemma 23 has a proof gap (statement true).

    • The proof says that if p_i received a valid chain for x during the first k rounds, then p_i broadcast a chain for x. But p_i relays only values it adds: not when x ∈ X_i already, nor when |X_i| = 2.

    • Fix. Argue from the round in which x first entered X_i: p_i relayed it then, unless |X_i| = 2 already, in which case p_j also ends with two values (lemma23_core, spread).

  2. "Faulty p_j has a committee certificate" is under-specified. Lemma 24 needs: a process can present a certificate only if some honest process voted for it. We use the set Csup of processes with at least one honest vote (mem_Csup). It contains every process that can produce a certificate, and Lemma 24's bounds are proved for it (lemma24_faulty, lemma24_card).

  3. n − t ≥ t + 1 is really needed for |C ∩ H| ≥ k + 1 (lemma24_honest). A core-set process gets votes from all n − t honest processes and needs t + 1 of them. This is the t < n/2 hypothesis of Theorem 6, so the theorem is fine.

  4. Under-specified defaults. Line 10 is undefined if all broadcast outputs are ⊥, and line 13 if V_i is empty. Neither happens under the hypotheses; we default to the process's own input. Chains carrying the receiver's own signature are not discussed; we filter them out, which is equivalent under unforgeability.

  5. Typos.

    • Theorem 6 says "Algorithm 5" for Algorithm 7.

    • Algorithm 7 line 8 passes v_i for x_i.

    • Algorithm 6 line 13 writes x_m.

    • The §8.3 prose says 2k + 1 < n − t − k; Lemma 24 and Theorem 6 use ≤.

  6. Message bounds.

    • Theorem 6's per-process bound 2k + 1 + n + 2n|C| < 2n(|C| + 1) fails for n = 1 (degenerate case).

    • We prove ≤ 2k + 1 + n + 2n² per honest process with no hypotheses (theorem6_messages_per_process).

    • We prove ≤ n(2k+1) + (3k+1)·n(6k+3) = O(nk²) in total under the hypotheses (theorem6_messages). A message is a bundle of chains sent to one receiver in one round.

  7. Signature modelling.

    • Signatures are CSLib's abstract unforgeable tokens.

    • A certificate carries all received votes (not just t + 1).

    • A chain link signs (value, sender) but not the whole prefix. This gives the adversary more freedom, so the correctness results are, if anything, stronger.

5. The wrapper and the main theorems (Sections 5 and 9)

  1. Algorithm 1 is verified at the level of sub-protocol specifications.

    • Graded consensus is cited ([14], [37]) and so is early-stopping agreement ([32]; Theorem 10 is "not formally proved" in the paper). We do not implement them.

    • We verify the logic of Algorithm 1 (Lemmas 28–32) on abstract phase traces, in which every sub-protocol output is arbitrary subject to its specification (Wrapper.Trace.Valid).

    • The conditional algorithms enter through their specifications (Agreement and Strong Unanimity when the precondition holds). These are exactly what Theorems 5 and 6 provide. The formal composition of round algorithms with time limits is not done.

    • Lemma 33's round count is exact: roundsUpTo, rounds_le.

    • The final O(min{B/n, f}) claims of Theorems 11 and 12 are assembled in prose, from Lemma 1, theorem11_rounds and exists_pow_two_between.

  2. In the abstract trace all honest processes run all phases. In Algorithm 1 a process stops one phase after deciding. Our proofs only inspect phases up to one phase after the first decision, in which nobody has stopped yet.

  3. Asymptotic statements are loose.

    • Theorem 2 says O(min{B/n + 1, f}) rounds, Theorem 11 O(min{B/n, f}), Theorem 3 O(min{B/n, f}). The +1 is needed when B < n.

    • "O(n² log(min{B/n, f})) messages" should read log(min{B/n, f} + 2), since the log is 0 or undefined for small arguments.

    • The phase count ⌈log₂ t⌉ + 1 is undefined for t = 0.

6. Modelling choices

  • Lower bounds.

    • Theorem 14 is proved in CSLib's synchronous Byzantine model (PartialSync runs with gst = 0), via the omission model OmExec.

    • Theorem 13 is proved in CSLib's lock-step model (LockStep, complete graph, reliable channels).

    • Both have deterministic processes and a static Byzantine set.

  • Upper bounds.

    • The algorithms are CSLib round algorithms (RoundAlgorithm) verified against synchronous abstract round executions (RoundRun with SyncFrom 0). Signatures use CSLib's token model (RoundAlgorithm.Unforging, RoundRun.AuthValid).

    • Inputs are (value, classification vector). Algorithm 2 (voting) is analysed as a round on its own (classify, card_mis_mul_le). The conditional algorithms take the resulting vectors c_i as inputs.

  • Predictions. Classification predictions are P → Bool per process (true = predicted honest). B counts only bits given to honest processes (wrongBits), as in Section 3.
