import Papers.PODC2025.BAWithPredictions.RoundModel import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.Byzantine.PartialSync.RoundAuth import Cslib.Foundations.Data.Finset.Quorum /-! # Authenticated Byzantine Agreement with classification (Section 8) — the algorithm Ben-David, Dzulfikar, Ellen, Gilbert, *Byzantine Agreement with Predictions*, PODC 2025 (arXiv:2505.01793), Section 8: Algorithm 6 (Byzantine broadcast with an implicit committee) run in `n` parallel instances inside Algorithm 7 (authenticated Byzantine agreement with classification). ## Signatures Signatures are the abstract tokens of CSLib's `Auth` (`RoundAuth.lean`): * `Tok.vote j i`: `sign_j(⟨committee, p_i⟩)`; * `Tok.link j s x`: `p_j`'s signature on a link of a message chain for value `x` started by `p_s` (the token abstracts the signature on the whole prefix of the chain; a coarser token only gives the adversary more power). A **committee certificate** for `p_i` (Definition 1) is a set `cc` of at least `t + 1` processes, carried in a message together with the tokens `Tok.vote j i`, `j ∈ cc`. A **message chain** (Definition 2) is `⟨s, x, links⟩` where `links` is the list of pairs (signer, committee certificate of the signer), in signing order; it carries the tokens `Tok.link j s x` of its signers and the vote tokens of its certificates. It is valid in round `r` (`ChainOK t r`) if it has length `r`, starts with `s`, its signers are distinct and all its certificates have at least `t + 1` voters. ## Rounds of `alg k t` (Algorithm 7) * round `0` (lines 1–6): `p` sends `Tok.vote p j` to every `j` among the first `2k+1` positions of `π(c_p)`; at the end of the round `p` has a certificate iff it received `≥ t + 1` votes (we keep all received votes rather than the `t + 1` smallest); * rounds `1, …, k+1` (lines 7–8, Algorithm 6 for all senders `s` in parallel): round `r` of the broadcast instances; the chains of all instances sent to one receiver are bundled into one message (`Msg.chains`); at the end of round `r`, `p` adds to `X_p[s]` (as long as `|X_p[s]| < 2`, smallest values first) the values of valid round-`r` chains for instance `s` that `p` has not signed (whoever delivered them: the paper only listens to senders with a certificate, but every signer of a valid chain has one), and, if it has a certificate, relays one such chain per newly added value (extended by its own link) in round `r + 1`; * round `k+2` (lines 9–11): every process with a certificate broadcasts its plurality value together with its certificate; at the end of the round it decides (lines 12–14). The decision is exhibited at the beginning of round `k + 3`, i.e. after `k + 3` rounds.
