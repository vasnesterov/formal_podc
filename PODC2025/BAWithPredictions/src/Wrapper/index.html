The sub-protocols are cited (graded consensus [14]/[37], early stopping [32]) or proved elsewhere (Theorems 5 and 6, files `Unauth*.lean`, `Auth*.lean`). Here we verify the **logic of the wrapper** (Lemmas 28–32) on *abstract phase traces*: the outputs of every sub-protocol instance are arbitrary, subject only to the sub-protocols' specifications: * graded consensus: Strong Unanimity and Coherence (`GCSpec`), always; * ES and CL: Agreement and Strong Unanimity (`BASpec`) in the phases where their preconditions hold (`PreEs φ`, `PreCl φ`; e.g. `T_φ` is at least the early-stopping time for the actual number of faults, resp. `2^φ` bounds the number of misclassified processes and `n - t` is large enough). As in the paper, a process *decides* in the first phase in which GC₃ returns grade `1` and returns one phase later (or at the last phase). **Modelling simplification.** In the abstract trace all honest processes take part in all phases; in Algorithm 1 a process stops after returning.
