Byzantine Agreement with Predictions — PODC 2025

Naama Ben-David, Muhammad Ayaz Dzulfikar, Faith Ellen, Seth Gilbert. Byzantine Agreement with Predictions. PODC 2025. DOI 10.1145/3732772.3733518. Formalized version: arXiv 2505.01793 v1; numbering follows it (Theorems 13/14 are the lower bounds). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The Ω(n + t²) message bound even with fully correct predictions (Theorem 14), the conditional algorithms (Theorems 5 and 6) and the classification lemmas hold, and the wrapper is verified against the specifications of the cited sub-protocols. The proof of the round bound min{f+2, t+1, ⌊B/(n−f)⌋+2, ⌊B/(n−t)⌋+1} (Theorem 13) cites a crash-fault bound that holds for halting times but not for decision times; we prove the decision-time version by switching to Byzantine faults and proving the cited bound there, but only for unauthenticated algorithms. So the tightness of the time bound with signatures stands only in the halting-time reading of the paper's proof.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Introduction of Section 7 (remark on too-small k)

The remark that, when k is too small, "if any honest process decides, then they all decide" is false: with k = 0, two honest processes whose first-ranked process is a different faulty one can disagree on whether to decide (not formalized).

Impact. Never used: Algorithm 1 only needs termination, so nothing depends on this.

Details

Take k = 0 and two honest processes whose first-ranked process is a different faulty one; one leader sends v everywhere, the other stays silent. The first process decides v at line 12, the second never decides.

⚠️ Theorem 13 (the cited crash-fault bound covers halting, not decision times)

The proof cites Dolev–Reischuk–Strong [21] for crash faults, whose bound min{f'+2, t'+1} holds for halting times (and uniform consensus) but not for the round in which the last honest process decides, which is how the paper defines time complexity.

Impact. For unauthenticated algorithms the statement is proved as stated, for decision times, for at least two values and t < n − 1 (theorem13, earlyDecidingLB_strong; see the model section). For algorithms with signatures, the setting of Theorem 6 when n/3 ≤ t < n/2, only the halting-time reading stands.

Details

  • The step. The proof turns the predicted algorithm into an algorithm S' without predictions on fewer processes "that may fail by crashing". It then cites Dolev–Reischuk–Strong [21]: some execution with f' faults takes min{f'+2, t'+1} rounds.

  • Fine for halting. For crash faults, min{f+2, t+1} bounds the round in which all correct processes stop (DRS). It also bounds the decision round of uniform consensus. The paper says that A "terminates within T(f) rounds" and counts rounds "performed". Read literally as halting time, the proof is correct.

  • Not enough for decisions. We prove the stronger statement about the round in which the last honest process decides. S' inherits only non-uniform agreement, and non-uniform crash consensus can decide in f+1 rounds. Example: n = 3, t = 1. Everyone relays all known inputs for t+1 rounds. A process that heard all n inputs in round 1 decides their minimum at once; the others decide the minimum they know at round t+1. Failure-free runs decide at round 1 < min{f+2, t+1} = 2. This does not contradict the classical t+1 bound, which concerns runs with f = t.

Repair

The same simulation works with Byzantine S'. We prove the Byzantine early-deciding bound min{f'+2, t'+1} ourselves. The usual chain argument gives f'+1; the extra round comes from one more process that equivocates in the last round, which f' < t' allows. Reaching exactly f faults needs padding with Byzantine processes that behave honestly (padScenario).

✏️ Slips

  • The abstract and the first paragraph of Section 10.2 of arXiv v1 claim an Ω(n²) message lower bound, but the theorem gives Ω(n + t²), and read literally Ω(n²) is false for small t: for t = 0, "everyone sends to p₁, p₁ broadcasts the minimum" uses 2(n−1) messages. It is loose shorthand: Ω(n + t²) is Ω(n²) when t = Θ(n) (the usual convention for Dolev–Reischuk), and the PODC abstract states Ω(n + t²). Theorem 14 itself holds.

  • The per-process message bound in the proof of Theorem 6 fails for n = 1; the round bound and the O(nk²) total hold.

  • Algorithm 4, line 4 has y ∈ L_y where it should be y ∈ T_i; Algorithms 3–4 and Theorem 5 hold with the intended reading.

  • Theorem 6 says "Algorithm 5" for Algorithm 7.

  • The position formula for c[i] = 0 in Section 6 has a typo: n − f + i − Σ_{j≤i} c[j] should be Σ_j c[j] + i − Σ_{j≤i} c[j].

  • Asymptotic statements omit +1s: O(min{B/n, f}) should be O(min{B/n, f} + 1), and log(·) should be log(· + 2); the phase count ⌈log₂ t⌉ + 1 is undefined for t = 0.

  • The proof of Lemma 16 says "at the end of phase 2", but for k = 0 there is only one phase.

Typos

  • The proof of Lemma 17 says "before phase φ+1" for "before the end of phase φ+1".

  • Lemma 6 is stated ambiguously; we prove the union version used by Lemma 24.

  • Algorithm 7 line 8 has v_i for x_i.

  • Algorithm 6 line 13 writes x_m for the value of the chain m.

  • The prose of Section 8.3 says 2k + 1 < n − t − k where Lemma 24 and Theorem 6 use ≤.

  • Under-specified tie-breaking and defaults (Algorithm 3 line 14, empty T_i ∩ L_i, all-⊥ outputs).

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Theorem 14 · Theorem 14 · Lemma 23

🔧 Theorem 14 (choice of the faulty group)

The proof assumes w.l.o.g. |S₀| ≥ ⌈(n−1)/2⌉ for the processes S₀ that "decide 0 for some prediction when they receive no messages", but a process receiving nothing need not decide at all, so S₀ ∪ S₁ need not cover the processes.

Impact. Repaired (t_sq_om).

Details

The proof picks the faulty group B ⊆ S₀. A process receiving nothing need not decide because such a run need not be admissible.

Repair

Split the processes by whether their isolated run ever decides v₁, and give the honest processes the other value. Both cases contradict Agreement in E_bad (t_sq_om).

🔧 Theorem 14 (from broadcast to agreement)

The paper proves Theorem 14 for broadcast and says it "implies the same bounds" for agreement, but the reduction only transfers the t² term.

Impact. Proved directly for both agreement and broadcast (message_lower_bound, message_lower_bound_bb).

Repair

We prove both problems directly, with n + t² ≤ 8·#messages + 1.

🔧 Lemma 23 (committee agreement)

The step "if p_i received a chain for x in the first k rounds, it broadcast one" ignores that p_i relays only values it adds, at most two.

Impact. Repaired by arguing from the round in which x entered X_i (lemma23).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous, deterministic, static Byzantine faults (at most t), complete network. Signatures, where used, are CSLib's unforgeable tokens.

  • Predictions. Inputs are pairs (value, prediction). A classification prediction is a vector P → Bool (true = predicted honest). B counts the wrong bits given to honest processes (wrongBits). Algorithms must be correct for all predictions; validity is strong unanimity on values.

  • Theorem 14. CSLib's synchronous Byzantine model (PartialSync runs with gst = 0, any delay bound), proved through omission executions. It holds for every prediction type, with or without signatures.

  • Theorem 13. CSLib's lock-step model (LockStep, reliable channels, Solves: unauthenticated, Byzantine processes may send anything). "At least R rounds" means that some honest process has no output after R − 1 rounds (decision time, which implies the halting-time reading). Algorithms with signatures, the setting of Theorem 6 when n/3 ≤ t < n/2, are not covered. For n ≤ 3t the hypothesis is unsatisfiable, since unauthenticated Byzantine agreement is then impossible.

  • Algorithms 2–7. CSLib round algorithms, verified against arbitrary synchronous round executions in which Byzantine processes send anything (RoundRun, SyncFrom 0; AuthValid with signatures). These compile to the time-based model.

    • The conditional algorithms take the classification vectors as input. Algorithm 2 (voting) is analysed as a round on its own.

    • In Algorithm 5, processes do not stop early. Outputs are the paper's return values and never change.

  • Algorithm 1 (wrapper). Verified on abstract phase traces. The outputs of graded consensus, early-stopping agreement and the conditional algorithm are arbitrary, subject to their specifications. The cited sub-protocols ([14], [37], [32]) are not implemented.

Not formalized.

  • The cited graded-consensus and early-stopping protocols, and the formal composition of the sub-protocols into one round algorithm. Instead, Lemmas 28–33 are verified against the sub-protocols' specifications.

  • The final asymptotic statements of Theorems 11/12, which are only assembled in prose.

  • The crash-model example above (decision in f+1 rounds); it concerns the citation, not the theorem.

  • Theorem 13 for authenticated algorithms.

Trust.

  • No sorry. All listed theorems use only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

  • Non-vacuity:

    • BA with predictions is solvable for n > 3t without signatures and for n > 2t with them (strongUnanimity_solvableSync, strongUnanimity_solvableAuthSync), so Theorem 14's hypothesis is satisfiable.

    • Synchronous executions exist and the hypotheses of Theorems 5 and 6 are satisfiable (Unauth.theorem5_hyps_of_correct, AuthBA.exists_run, AuthBA.hypotheses_satisfiable).

  • Models come from CSLib: Byzantine/PartialSync (incl. Omission, Rounds, RoundAuth) and Byzantine/LockStep.

  • Shared library: roundMsgs/procMsgs and syncRun are RoundRun.msgsUpTo/procMsgs and RoundRun.sync (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/RoundAuth.lean); the isolated and fault-free omission executions of Theorem 14 are OmExec.solo/OmExec.ff (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/Omission.lean); the non-vacuity reduction uses the containment condition of Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/Containment.lean; cnt/plur/thr are Finset.countEq/plurality/threshold?
