Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Tight Bounds on Channel Reliability via Generalized Quorum Systems — PODC 2025.

Paper: A. Naser-Pastoriza, G. Chockler, A. Gotsman, F. Ryabinin, Tight Bounds on Channel Reliability via Generalized Quorum Systems, PODC 2025 (arXiv:2505.02646v1, identical to the proceedings version plus appendices).

Model (Podc/Computability/Distributed/MessagePassing/ChannelFailures/Model.lean)

  1. FIFO channels. The paper's channels are unordered. Our channels are FIFO (deliver q p delivers the head). This only restricts the adversary, so the lower bounds (Lemma 2, Theorems 2 and 6) are stronger in our model: an algorithm only has to be safe and live on FIFO executions. (For the protocol of Figure 3, whose safety we check in QuorumAccess*.lean, FIFO channels make the safety claim slightly weaker than over unordered channels.)

  2. Failures are not events. A crash is "the process takes finitely many steps", a disconnection is "a suffix of the messages on the channel is never delivered". A failure pattern f = (procs, chans) only enters fairness (Fair f): every process that is not crashed (= not in f.procs, or in f.procs but taking infinitely many steps) takes infinitely many spontaneous steps, and every channel not in f.chans between non-crashed processes eventually delivers each message. With FIFO channels this is exactly the paper's f-compliant fair execution ("from some point on the channel drops all messages" = the undelivered messages form a suffix). Finite executions are hence failure free; they are exactly the finite prefixes of executions with failures.

  3. Algorithms are deterministic, event driven, with total transitions handle p i l for an invocation, a spontaneous step tick c (with a clock reading c, ignored by asynchronous algorithms — the adversary chooses it in the asynchronous model) or a reception. Fairness requires infinitely many spontaneous steps of each correct process (the paper: "takes infinitely many steps"; our condition is stronger, so the set of fair executions is smaller and the lower bounds stronger). Blocking waits are expressed by keeping information in the local state. Determinism is no loss of generality for the lower bounds: the paper's liveness notions require termination in every fair execution (no probabilistic termination is considered), so a nondeterministic algorithm can be replaced by any deterministic refinement (its fair executions are fair executions of the original). Randomized algorithms with probability-1 termination are outside the scope of both the paper and the formalization. In the asynchronous model the clock reading c of a tick is chosen by the adversary; the paper's (clockless) algorithms correspond to algorithms ignoring it. Safety (Satisfies) is required for all finite executions, including non-monotone clock readings; under partial synchrony an algorithm can clamp readings to the maximum seen so far, so this does not strengthen the hypothesis either.

  4. Well-formed clients are built into the model: an invocation at a process with a pending operation is ignored, and a response is recorded only for a pending operation.

  5. Failure patterns are arbitrary pairs of sets; the paper's requirement that C only contains channels between correct processes is not needed. Termination mappings must satisfy τ(f) ⊆ P \ f.procs — in the paper this is part of the definition of a termination mapping; we state it as a hypothesis (hτ).

  6. Obstruction-freedom (ObstructionFree f T): for every fair f-execution, every p ∈ T and every effective invocation of p at time t₀ that eventually executes solo (EventuallySolo: from some time T₁ > t₀ on, all effective invocations are by crashed processes, and every other process with an operation pending at T₁ crashes), the operation returns (Returns: a response label of p at some time ≥ t₀). The lower-bound proofs use it only for executions where the operation is the only one ever invoked.

  7. Safety (Satisfies Spec): every finite execution's history satisfies Spec (the paper: all executions are safe).

  8. Partial synchrony (PartiallySynchronous f ev time, §7): a timed execution with discrete, monotone, unbounded time; clock readings are monotone; there are GST and Δ such that after GST clocks do not drift (reading = time + per-process offset), every correct process takes a spontaneous step at every time unit, and every message in transit at a time ≥ GST on a reliable channel between correct processes is delivered within Δ. The paper allows arbitrary delays for messages sent before GST; we deliver them by GST + Δ (DLS style), i.e. a smaller class of executions, which only strengthens Theorem 6. Real-valued time and timers are replaced by discrete time and a spontaneous step per time unit.

  9. Processes are a finite type (Fintype P); messages and local states are arbitrary types.

Definitions (Defs.lean)

  1. "Strongly connected in G \ f" (f-availability, Proposition 1, U_f) is read as mutual reachability in G \ f (paths may leave the set), as in the proof of Proposition 1 and the definition of U_f as "the strongly connected component containing U". Under the induced subgraph reading Proposition 1 is false (proposition_1_false_induced, Examples.lean).

  2. U_f is defined as {p | ∃ u ∈ U, u ⇝ p ∧ p ⇝ u}; we prove it is strongly connected, non-empty for f ∈ F and maximal (Uf_stronglyConnected, Uf_nonempty, mem_Uf_of_reach). U_f depends on (R, W), not only on F (Uf_not_unique, Counterexamples.lean); the remark after Theorem 2 that "U_f is the largest set of processes for which termination can be guaranteed" has to be read for the quorum system built in the proof.

  3. Definition 1 vs Definition 2: we prove their equivalence when no failure pattern of F has failed channels (isGQS_iff_isQS), which is the paper's hypothesis "C = ∅".

Objects (Objects.lean)

  1. Lattice agreement over any partial order V (the paper: a join-semilattice). Upward validity y ≤ ⨆ X is stated as "y is below every upper bound of the proposed values", which is equivalent in a join-semilattice and needs no ⊥. The specification only constrains single-shot histories (no process proposes twice); Downward validity is "x ≤ y for the proposal x of the deciding process".

  2. Consensus: Agreement and Validity, for single-shot histories.

  3. MWMR registers: linearizability w.r.t. CSLib's register V v₀ (a write returns the written value as a dummy acknowledgement).

  4. SWMR snapshots: our own snapObj P V v₀ (one segment per process, update i v, scan); linearizability is required for histories in which every process only updates its own segment; update returns the constant view fun _ => v₀ as a dummy acknowledgement (it must carry no information: an update returning the previous view would be a strictly stronger object, an "update-and-scan", and the theorem would only cover algorithms for it).

  5. Non-trivial value domains (missing hypothesis). The paper's proofs pick two incomparable lattice elements; our theorems assume two incomparable elements (lattice agreement) or two distinct values (registers, snapshots, consensus). The hypothesis is necessary: lattice agreement over a chain is solved by returning one's own proposal, with no communication, under a fail-prone system that has no generalized quorum system (theorem_2_false_of_chain).

Proofs

  1. Lemma 2 and Theorem 2 are proved for every object with "conflicting operations" (Conflict: two solo histories whose sequential composition is unsafe), not only lattice agreement. Registers, snapshots and consensus are obtained directly (conflict_reg, conflict_snap, conflict_cons), not via the constructions of [2], [11] (snapshots from registers, lattice agreement from snapshots) or "consensus implements an MWMR register", which the paper uses without detail (with obstruction-freedom restricted to τ(f), and, for consensus, needing infinitely many single-shot instances). Those reductions are not formalized.

  2. The liveness assumption is used only through SoloTerm (after any finite prefix without invocations, a solo operation of p followed by a canonical round-robin continuation returns). Both asynchronous and partially synchronous obstruction-freedom imply it (soloTerm_of_obstructionFree, soloTerm_of_obstructionFreePS); so Theorem 6 is proved directly (the paper: "the lower bound for lattice agreement remains valid since σ is finite"; one also needs the executions α₁, β₁ to be partially synchronous, which our canonical continuation is, ps_extend_canon).

  3. The executions α₁, β₁ of the paper "fail the processes and channels of f at the beginning"; ours never schedule f.procs and never deliver on f.chans (canonical continuation).

  4. Gluing without reordering. The paper builds σ = α₃|_{R_f∖W_f} · α₃|_{W_f} · δ|_{R_g} (Theorem 2) and σ = α₃|_{R_u} · δ · α₃|_{S_u} (Lemma 2). We use σ = α|_{X₁} · δ|_{X₂} with X₁ = R_f, X₂ = R_g (Theorem 2) and X₁ = R_u ∪ S_u, X₂ = R_u ∪ R_v ∪ S_v (Lemma 2), where β = α|_{A₁} · δ with A₁ = R_f ∖ W_f resp. R_u; the key condition is X₂ ∩ X₁ ⊆ A₁ (Claims 2–3). In Lemma 2 our σ runs u's operation first and then v's (the paper: v's first). Claims 1–3 are the closedness facts closed_reachTo, closed_reachTo_not and the inclusion X₂ ∩ X₁ ⊆ A₁ in lemma_2_of_soloTerm / theorem_2_of_soloTerm.

  5. In Theorem 2 we choose one representative t_f ∈ τ(f) and set W_f = SCC of t_f, R_f = processes reaching t_f; by Lemma 2 this is the paper's "SCC containing τ(f)".

Not formalized

  1. Theorem 1 (upper bound for registers, snapshots, lattice agreement), Theorem 4 (liveness of Figure 3, which also needs message forwarding, assumed w.l.o.g. in §5), the register protocol of Figure 4 and its linearizability (Appendix B, Theorem 7 from Adya's thesis), Theorem 5 and the consensus protocol of Figure 6. Proposition 2 (no proof in the paper) is proved in an abstract real-time form, not tied to the event model: after GST process p is in view v during [a_p + C·v(v−1)/2, a_p + C·v(v+1)/2) (proposition_2). (Example 9's claim that F' admits no generalized quorum system is proved: example_9. In Figure 1 we read f_i as: process i+3 may crash, and among the others exactly the channels (i+2, i), (i, i+1), (i+1, i) are reliable, as stated for f₁ in Example 1 and rotated.)

  2. For Figure 3 we formalize the protocol (without the message forwarding needed only for liveness) and prove its safety: Validity (QA.validity) and Real-time ordering, which includes Lemma 1 (QA.realtime_ordering), in QuorumAccess.lean / QuorumAccessProof.lean, using only Consistency of the quorum families. Encoding choices: the opaque state is the set of identifiers of the applied updates (an update is identified by its quorum_set invocation, (p, k+1) for the k-th operation of p); quorum_get returns the list of states of the chosen read quorum; each process keeps, per sender, the GET_RESP with the highest clock (the wait at lines 8/20 is for a read quorum whose latest clocks are large enough); waits are re-evaluated after every step; Validity is stated for a history ending with the quorum_get response.
