Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The necessity half of the paper's characterization stands after local changes: Lemma 2, Theorem 2 (lattice agreement, MWMR registers, SWMR snapshots, and also consensus), Theorem 6 (partial synchrony) and Proposition 1 are proved for arbitrary deterministic algorithms, once the implied non-trivial value domain is named, the remark on U_f is reworded and one proof step is closed. The sufficiency half was not checked: the upper bounds (Theorems 1, 4, 5) are not formalized, and for the quorum access functions of Figure 3 we formalize the protocol and its safety only (see the table).

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Remark after Theorem 2 (U_f is not determined by F)

The remark calls U_f "the largest set of processes for which termination can be guaranteed", but U_f depends on the quorum system, not only on F (Uf_not_unique).

Impact. The theorems themselves are unaffected; the remark has to be read for the quorum system built in the proof.

Details

With two processes and only the channel 0 → 1 reliable, both ({0},{0}) and ({0,1},{1}) are generalized quorum systems, with U_f = {0} and U_f = {1}. Termination can be guaranteed at {0} or at {1}, but not at both (Lemma 2).

Repair

Every terminating set lies in U_f of the quorum system built in the proof: τ(f) ⊆ U_f (theorem_2_generic).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 2 (also Lemma 2 and Theorem 6) needs a non-trivial object. It is false over a chain (e.g. ℕ): returning one's own proposal immediately is a safe, wait-free lattice agreement algorithm, yet under "one of two processes may crash" (τ(f) = the correct process) no generalized quorum system exists (theorem_2_false_of_chain). The same algorithm shows that registers, snapshots and consensus need at least two values. The proof picks two incomparable elements x₁, x₂, but the theorem is stated for any semi-lattice. With two incomparable elements (lattice agreement) or two distinct values (registers, snapshots, consensus) as hypotheses, everything holds (theorem_2_lattice_agreement, theorem_2_register, theorem_2_snapshot, theorem_6).

Typos

  • Proposition 1, ambiguous "strongly connected in G \ f". If it meant that the induced subgraph is strongly connected, Proposition 1 would be false (proposition_1_false_induced). Take processes 0, 1, 2, with only the channels 0 ↔ 1 failing, and write quorums {0}, {1} reachable from the read quorum {0, 1}. Then U = {0, 1}, but 0 and 1 reach each other only through 2. Not an error under the natural reading: the proof of Proposition 1 and the definition of U_f as a strongly connected component use mutual reachability in G \ f; with that reading everything is correct, and this is the reading we formalize (proposition_1).

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 6, lower bound

🔧 Theorem 6, lower bound (finiteness of σ is not enough)

For Theorem 6 the paper only argues that σ is finite, but liveness is also applied to the executions α₁, β₁, which must themselves be partially synchronous.

Impact. Statement true; proved directly (theorem_6).

Repair

One gluing argument proves the bound for every object with two conflicting operations, and it uses liveness only through one canonical continuation, which is both fair and partially synchronous.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (staged shared library Podc/Computability/Distributed/MessagePassing/ChannelFailures/, namespace Cslib.ChannelFailures).

  • Asynchronous message passing among finitely many processes, deterministic event-driven algorithms (invocations, spontaneous steps reading a local clock, receptions), FIFO channels (a restriction of the adversary, which only strengthens lower bounds). Determinism is without loss of generality: the paper's liveness is "returns in every fair execution", so a nondeterministic algorithm can be replaced by a deterministic refinement; probabilistic termination is not considered by the paper. Histories are CSLib Labels; registers and snapshots use CSLib linearizability.

  • A failure pattern (procs, chans) enters only fairness: crashed processes stop taking steps, disconnected channels stop delivering (a suffix of their messages is lost); all other processes take infinitely many steps and all other channels between them deliver every message. This is exactly the paper's f-compliant fair execution. Finite executions are failure free; safety is required for all of them.

  • (f, T)-obstruction-freedom and wait-freedom as in §2. Partial synchrony (§7): discrete time with GST and Δ, non-drifting clocks after GST, and a step of every correct process per time unit.

  • The lower bounds quantify over all algorithms, message types and local states.

Not formalized. The upper bounds: Theorem 1, the liveness of Figure 3 (Theorem 4), the register protocol of Figure 4 with Appendix B, and Theorem 5 with the protocol of Figure 6. Also not formalized: the reductions [2, 11], which our direct proofs make unnecessary. The paper derives Theorem 2 for registers and snapshots from the constructions of [2, 11], and Theorem 6 from "consensus implements an MWMR register". We did not check whether these reductions work in this failure model: they would have to work with obstruction-freedom at τ(f) only, and a single-shot consensus object needs infinitely many instances. The statements themselves are proved directly (theorem_2_register, theorem_2_snapshot, theorem_6).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: fair and partially synchronous executions exist from every finite prefix (fair_extend_canon, ps_extend_canon). echoAlg is a safe, wait-free algorithm that meets the hypotheses of Theorem 2 except non-triviality. The model is staged in Podc/ as a CSLib candidate. It reuses CSLib's Digraph.ReachableAvoiding, Label, IsLinearization and register.
