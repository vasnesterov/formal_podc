State Complexity of Population Protocols — PODC 2021

Philipp Czerner, Javier Esparza (arXiv v3 adds Jérôme Leroux). Lower Bounds on the State Complexity of Population Protocols. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467912. Formalized version: arXiv 2102.11619 v3 (July 2022); numbering follows it. v3 has the double-exponential bound for leaderless protocols (Ω(log log η)). The PODC version (= arXiv v2) claims only a triple-exponential bound and proves it differently (rejection certificates). That proof is not formalized.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Both lower bounds stand: Theorem 5.9 gives η ≤ 2^((2n+2)!) and BB(n) ≤ 2^((2n+2)!) for leaderless protocols, and Theorem 4.5 gives η < F_{ℓ,ϑ}(n) for protocols with ℓ leaders. Local changes save them: BB_L must fix the number of leaders, Lemma 5.8's hypothesis must be strict, and the proofs of Theorem 4.5, §5.3 and Corollary 5.7 need repairs that leave the final bounds unchanged. That the bound with leaders is non-elementary was not checked: it rests on the cited Lemma 4.4 at level F_ω, and we prove only that some admissible F exists.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Definition 1 (BB_L does not fix the number of leaders)

Read literally, BB_L(4) does not exist: a 4-state protocol with η leaders decides x ≥ η for every η (not_bddAbove_BBLSet, majority4_computesGe).

Impact. Evident intent: a fixed number ℓ of leaders, as in Theorem 4.5, whose bound F_{ℓ,ϑ}(n) depends on ℓ. With that reading the claims about protocols with leaders hold and follow from Theorem 4.5 (theorem_4_5_ctrlChainBound).

Details

Definition 1 defines BB_L(n) as the largest η such that some protocol with at most n states and any leader multiset computes x ≥ η. Take states A, B, a, b with L = η·A and input state B. The transitions are AB ↦ ab, Ab ↦ Aa, Ba ↦ Bb, ab ↦ bb, and identity on the other pairs; B, b output 1. This protocol decides #B ≥ #A, i.e. x ≥ η. So with 4 states every threshold is computable, and literally read, "a bound on BB_L(n)" (§4), "the inverse of a non-elementary function for protocols with leaders" (abstract) and "Ω(α(η)) for protocols with leaders" (§6) are false. The intended reading (a fixed number of leaders, or leaders counted in the size) is also the one of the cited O(log log η) construction of Blondin et al. and of the follow-up by Leroux (PODC 2022, "with a fixed number of leaders").

🔧 Lemma 5.8 (concentration hypothesis must be strict)

Lemma 5.8's proof needs ξ·C(Q\S) < i, but its hypothesis "C is 1/ξ-concentrated in S" only gives ξ·C(Q\S) ≤ i, so the lemma as stated is unestablished, though not refuted (lemma_5_8 proves the strict version).

Impact. Theorem 5.9 is unaffected: there the configuration is in fact 1/(ξ3^n)-concentrated, which gives the strict inequality.

Details

With equality, the pigeonhole step "|J| > C(S)" fails.

✏️ Slips

  • The proof of Lemma 5.8 swaps S and Q \ S: it writes C(S) where it means C(Q\S). The case "if x ∉ S take θ empty" should read "if x ∈ S, take j = 1, θ = 0, C' = x": an empty θ gives j = 0, which is useless for Lemma 5.2.

  • P'_k in Example 2.1 has k+2 states, not k+1.

  • Lemma 4.4 writes v_{g(n)} for v_{i_{g(n)}}.

Typos

  • Lemma 4.2's proof says "for every 2 ≤ i ≤ k".

  • Theorem 4.5 states η < F but its proof ends with η ≤ i_k ≤ F; strictness is immediate from k < ℓ.

  • The outline in §1 still says that Section 5 gives a triple exponential upper bound, a leftover of the PODC version; v3 proves a double exponential one.

Proof gaps in true statements (4)Theorem 4.5 · Theorem 4.5 · §5.3 · Corollary 5.7

🔧 Theorem 4.5 (condition 2 of Lemma 4.1 never established)

The proof applies Lemma 4.1 with D_b := C_ℓ − C_k, but never shows its condition 2, b·x →* D_b.

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a variant of Lemma 4.1 (lemma_4_1', theorem_4_5).

Details

The two configurations C_k ≤ C_ℓ lie in the same basis element B + ℕ^S. Lemma 4.2 only gives C_k + b·x →* C_ℓ, which is not the same thing: b input agents alone, without the leaders, need not reach D_b.

Repair

A variant of Lemma 4.1 with condition 2 replaced by B + D_a + b·x →* B + D_a + D_b. Pumping still works, because C_k + λ b·x →* C_k + λ D_b by induction. (lemma_4_1', theorem_4_5)

🔧 Theorem 4.5 (indices when applying Lemma 4.4)

The sequence of Lemma 4.2 starts at C_2 while Lemma 4.4 starts at v_0, so Lemma 4.4 cannot be applied with (ℓ, ϑ) as printed.

Impact. One constant changes and the shape of the bound is untouched (theorem_4_5).

Repair

Apply Lemma 4.4 with either δ = ℓ + 2 or a chain two elements longer; we use g = ϑ + 2.

🔧 §5.3 (unstated assumption that every state is coverable)

The text says "Recall our assumption that for every state q there is an input i_q with IC(i_q) →* C_q ∋ q", but this assumption is never stated, and it is not WLOG for Lemma 5.1(ii) as used.

Impact. Theorem 5.9 holds without the assumption (lemma_5_1_ii, lemma_5_3, lemma_5_4).

Details

π may contain transitions whose pre-states are not coverable.

Repair

We work with the set of coverable states (Lemmas 5.3 and 5.4) and strengthen Lemma 5.1(ii) to require saturation only on the pre-states of transitions in the support of π. In Theorem 5.9 these pre-states are coverable, because π is the Parikh vector of a real execution. (lemma_5_1_ii, lemma_5_3, lemma_5_4)

🔧 Corollary 5.7 (the wrong linear system for Lemma 5.8)

Corollary 5.7 sets up "|Q|−1 equations on |T| variables", but Lemma 5.8 needs to decompose i and C together with π.

Impact. The final bound of Theorem 5.9 is unchanged; we use the right system (with our own Pottier-type constant, see the Model).

Details

The decomposition requires the |Q| inequalities Σ_t π(t)Δ_t(q) + [q = x]·i ≥ 0 on |T|+1 variables, which is also what the exponent of ξ = 2(2|T|+1)^{|Q|} suggests. We use that system.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Population protocols in the multiset (Petri-net) style of the Esparza group (Podc multiset model, Cslib.PopulationProtocol.Transition, MStep, MReach, FiresSeq, FlowEq). A Protocol Q has a transition relation T ⊆ Q²×Q² that is total on pairs, a leader multiset L, one input state x, and an output map O. IC(i) = L + i·x, and inputs satisfy i ≥ 2.

  • The scheduler is the paper's fairness: if a configuration is reachable infinitely often, it is visited infinitely often. This is CSLib's Run.GloballyFair; no randomness.

  • ComputesGe η says that every fair execution from IC(i), i ≥ 2, eventually has consensus output [η ≤ i]. computesGe_iff proves the paper's reachability characterization ("every reachable configuration can reach SC_{[η≤i]}"), and all later proofs use it.

  • Cited results. Rackoff's bound is proved: g(n) ≤ 2^{2(2n+1)!} for covering sequences. Pottier's theorem is replaced by a proved, weaker Hilbert-basis bound (2M)^(2^e−1). This changes the intermediate constant ξ but not the final bound 2^((2n+2)!). Lemma 4.4 (Figueira et al.) is used as a hypothesis property CtrlChainProp. We prove that admissible functions exist (by compactness and Dickson's lemma), but not that they lie at level F_ω.

  • Lemmas 4.1 and 5.2 get the extra hypothesis b ≥ 1. In the paper it is implicit: D_b is called a configuration, and configurations have at least two agents, so condition 2 already forces b ≥ 2. We work with arbitrary multisets, and then b = 0 must be excluded: with b = 0, D_b = 0, π = 0, all hypotheses hold for a = 2 and any stable configuration reachable from IC(2), viewed as the basis element (C, ∅), while η ≤ 2 fails for any protocol deciding x ≥ 3. Both applications in the paper have b ≥ 1. (lemma_4_1_b_zero_false, lemma_5_2_b_zero_false: counterexamples to the relaxed reading)

Not formalized. The two remaining cited results are not formalized: the fast-growing-hierarchy part of Lemma 4.4, on which the "non-elementary" shape of the bound rests, and Pottier's sharper constant. So Corollary 5.7 with the paper's exact ξ, and the middle inequality η ≤ ξnβ3^n of Theorem 5.9 with it, are proved only with our larger ξ. Also not formalized: the length bound |σ| ≤ 3^n in Lemma 5.4 (unused), Section 4.1 (discussion), Theorem 2.2 (cited lower bounds; we only derive 2^k ≤ BB(k+2)), and the triple-exponential proof of the PODC version.

Trust. No sorry. All listed theorems use only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: Example 2.1's leaderless protocols compute x ≥ 2^k for every k, so the hypotheses of Theorem 5.9 are satisfiable for unboundedly large η. The 4-state protocol with leaders does the same for Theorem 4.5. Functions with the Lemma 4.4 property exist (ctrlChainProp_ctrlChainBound).
