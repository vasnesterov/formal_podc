Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Restorable Shortest Path Tiebreaking — PODC 2021.

Bodwin–Parter, Restorable Shortest Path Tiebreaking for Edge-Faulty Graphs, PODC 2021, arXiv:2102.10174v4 (numbering follows the arXiv version).

Errors and imprecisions in the paper

  1. ✏️ Definition 17 has no connectivity hypothesis. Definition 17 requires a replacement path for all s, t and all nonempty F with |F| ≤ f. If F disconnects s from t there is no replacement path. So no scheme is restorable on any graph in which some fault set with |F| ≤ f separates two vertices: any graph with a bridge when f ≥ 1, and any disconnected graph with at least one edge. As a consequence Theorem 19, read literally, is false (e.g. on K₂). Theorem 2 has the right hypothesis ("an s ⇝ t path remains in G \ {e}"), and we add it to Definition 17 (IsRestorable). Lean: IsRestorableLiteral, not_isRestorableLiteral, theorem19_literal_false.

  2. 🔧 Corollary 22: the union bound miscounts the fault sets. A false intermediate claim; the corollary holds with a larger constant. The proof bounds the number of choices of s, t, F by n^{f+2}. There are n² · N_f choices, where N_f = #{F ⊆ E : |F| ≤ f} can be as large as about n^{2f}. For K₄ and f = 1 there are 16 · 7 = 112 > 4³ choices with ordered pairs (s, t) (still 12 · 7 = 84 > 64 when s ≠ t is required; with unordered pairs 6 · 7 = 42, but the count grows like n^{2f+2} anyway). So with W = n^{f+4+c} the paper's union bound only gives about n^{f-c}, not n^{-c}. Taking W = n^{2f+4+c}, or c > f, restores the claim, and the bit complexity is still O(f log n). Lean: paper_count_false. corollary22 proves the correct bound: the failure probability is at most n² N_f |E| / (2W+1). The final arithmetic (N_f ≤ n^{2f}, choice of W, O(f log n) bits) is not stated in Lean.

  3. 🔧 Theorem 23's proof picks the wrong minimum. Proof gap; the statement is true.

    • The main gap: it takes "the edge in π₁ \ π₂ with smallest ID". It should be the smallest ID in the whole symmetric difference; otherwise an edge of π₂ \ π₁ with smaller ID can dominate.

    • Minor: it speaks of "distinct (not-necessarily-shortest) paths" and concludes that π₂ is shorter, which ignores the lengths. Edges used by both paths in opposite directions contribute ±2r(e); this is harmless for C large enough.

    The argument is clean for shortest paths of G \ F, which have equal length and traverse common edges in the same direction (IsShortestIn.dart_eq_of_edge_eq). The first bullet of the proof already reduces to that case. We prove it this way (coeff_of_ne, theorem23), with C = 2. 3a. ✏️ Theorem 23's proof, first bullet. "the initial length of any non-shortest path is at least 1 less than the length of a shortest path" should say at least 1 more, as in Theorem 20's proof. Lean: theorem23.

  4. Theorem 20: the proof is fine (clarification only). For e ∈ q₁ \ q₂ the coefficient of r(e) in the weight difference is ±1 simply because e ∉ q₂, so "at most one value of r(e) makes q₁, q₂ tie" is correct as written. Our Lean proof goes through the coefficient vector of the whole difference instead (coeff_of_ne, volume_degenerate, theorem20). 4a. 🔧 Corollary 22 applies the isolation lemma to signed weights without comment. Proof gap; the statement is true. The weight of a path in G* is ∑ ±r(e), with the sign given by the direction in which the path crosses e. Applying Theorem 21 to the family of shortest s ⇝ t paths needs two facts the proof does not state:

    • all shortest s ⇝ t paths of G \ F cross each edge in the same direction, so each edge has one sign τ(e) for the whole family;

    • the weight distribution is symmetric under negation, so the signed weights τ(e)·i(e) are again uniform on {−W,…,W}.

    Lean: cnt_eq_tau and tau (common sign), and the sign flip i ↦ τ·i in corollary22. 4b. ✏️ Corollary 22's proof, reweighting. "since path lengths change by < 1/(2n) over the reweighting" is the per-edge change; a path changes by < 1/2, as Theorem 20's proof says. Lean: isATW_of_coeff.

  5. ✏️ Theorem 21 (isolation lemma) needs distinct edge sets. Under-specified. It is stated for "a set of paths Π". The paths must have pairwise distinct edge sets (a path and its reverse always tie). In the application all paths go from s to t, and distinct shortest s ⇝ t paths have distinct edge sets (IsShortestIn.exists_mem_edges_not_mem). Our isolation lemma (Cslib.Isolation.card_not_uniqueMin_le) is stated for a family of sets.

  6. ✏️ Theorem 30's proof uses the wrong f. Typo/slip. It takes "a consistent stable restorable f-RPTS". For f + 1 faults it needs an (f+1)-restorable (f+1)-RPTS whose f-fault overlays are stored, as in Theorem 31. Lean: theorem30_correct.

  7. ✏️ Lemma 32's correctness proof misses a case. Proof gap; the statement is true. It takes "the first and last clustered vertex" of the replacement path but does not treat paths without clustered vertices. Then every edge of the path is in H. Lean: lemma32_correct.

  8. Theorem 19's proof is terse but correct. Two steps are left implicit, and we prove both:

    • The strict inequality "the u ⇝ y path through v is shorter than the one through x" needs π(u,x) ∘ (x,y) ≠ π(u,y). Otherwise π(s,x) would contain (u,v).

    • (v,u) ∈ π(t,x) forces π(v,x) = (v,u) ∘ π(u,x), by consistency and the orientation of e along shortest paths from t.

    Lean: restore_one.

  9. Appendix B (Theorem 27): slips and one open point. Not formalized.

    • ✏️ W : E → (1, 1 + 1/n²), but the bipartite weights are set to (λ − j)/n⁴. They should be 1 + (λ − j)/n⁴.

    • The vertex v* is never defined.

    • ✏️ Label(z) is undefined for the leaves of the last copy G'_d (the edge (u_d, u_{d+1}) does not exist), at every level. Only about a ∏ₖ(1 − d^{-1/2^k}) ≥ 2^{-f} fraction of the bipartite edges is forced. This only changes constants.

    • ✏️ Observation 1(c) states N(f,d) = 2^f d² where ≤ is meant.

    • Open point: W does not break all ties: z_j – x₁ – z_k and z_j – x₂ – z_k have equal weight. So the scheme needs a further generic perturbation before it is a full consistent stable symmetric RPTS.

    A numerical check of the key claim, for f ≤ 3 and small d, confirms that the corrected construction forces the expected bipartite edges (script below).

  10. Theorem 26 (cited from [9]) is not formalized; it has only a proof sketch. It is the size bound behind Theorems 30, 31 and 33. theorem31_of_theorem26 makes this dependence explicit. The case f = 0 (a union of |S| trees) is proved unconditionally: card_overlay_zero_le, theorem31_f0, theorem30.

Modelling choices and differences in the formalization

  • Graphs, faults, distances. Graphs are finite SimpleGraph V. Fault sets are arbitrary Finset (Sym2 V); non-edges are allowed and harmless, so the positive results are slightly stronger. Distances are edist (ℕ∞). A "shortest path of G \ F" is a walk of G that avoids F and has length d_{G\F}. Such walks are paths (IsShortestIn.isPath).

  • RPTS. An RPTS is a function F ↦ s ↦ t ↦ Option (G.Walk s t); none stands for "∅, no path". Definition 12 is required only for pairs connected in G \ F.

  • Consistency. "u precedes v on π(s,t)" is formalized as a decomposition π(s,t) = p₁ ++ q ++ p₂ with q : u ⇝ v. Then π(u,v) = q.

  • Definition 18 has two readings, and both are covered. With possibly negative r, "unique shortest path of G* \ F" can mean the lightest simple path (IsATWPath) or the lightest walk (IsATW). We prove the readings are equivalent (isATW_iff_isATWPath). The simple-path reading already forces every closed walk of G* to weigh at least 2. So Theorem 19 holds for both (theorem19, theorem19_path).

  • Weights are indexed by unordered pairs. We write r(u,v) = sgn(u,v) · ρ({u,v}) with ρ indexed by all unordered pairs Sym2 V, not only by edges. In Theorem 20 the extra coordinates are independent and unused, so the distribution on the edge coordinates is the paper's. In Corollary 22 the isolation bound still only involves |E|.

  • Theorem 20 is stated as an almost-sure statement. ρ is drawn from the uniform probability measure on [-ε, ε]^{Sym2 V} (volume[|box V ε], a probability measure by isProbabilityMeasure_box). The hypothesis on ε is weakened: (2n−1)ε < 1 suffices in hasMinWalks_of_generic.

  • Theorem 23 is stated for existence only. It uses C = 2, with IDs i(e) = N − id(e) ∈ {1,…,N} for any numbering id of Sym2 V. The bit complexity and running-time claims are not formalized.

  • Corollary 22 is stated in counting form. It counts bad draws i ∈ {−W,…,W}^{Sym2 V}. The running time is not formalized.

  • Theorem 2 is proved for all f simultaneously. One scheme works for every f (theorem2). theorem2_single is the paper's one-fault phrasing.

  • Theorem 37 is proved in a stronger form. Symmetry is assumed only for the non-faulty paths, and only the non-faulty paths need to be shortest.

  • Applications: correctness only, apart from the f = 0 sizes.

    • We formalize the correctness of Theorems 29, 30 and 31 and of Lemma 32.

    • Sizes are proved for f = 0: ≤ |S|(n−1) edges and ≤ n−1 edges per label.

    • For larger f, sizes are conditional on Theorem 26.

    • Theorem 33 is reduced to its exponent arithmetic (theorem33_exponent). Its spanner size bound needs Theorem 26 and the expectation/Chernoff bound of Lemma 32, neither of which is formalized.

  • Not formalized.

    • All running-time and bit-complexity statements: Theorems 3, 23, 28, 29 (runtime) and the label lengths in bits.

    • The CONGEST-model results: Theorem 8, Corollary 9, Lemmas 34–36, Theorem 35 (cited).

    • Theorem 25 (cited) and Theorem 27 (lower bound, Appendix B).

    • The Afek et al. restoration lemmas (Theorems 1 and 11; they are not used).
