Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Hedging Against Sore Loser Attacks — PODC 2021.

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 2 is false for v = Lᵢ when the leader withholds its secret (🔧).

    • Counterexample: TwoCycle.lemma_2_false (digraph A ⇄ B, leader A, Bob deviating, p = 1). Alice's net redemption-premium profit is −1. She escrowed one asset, and her key never appears on her leaving arc.

    • True versions: lemma_2 for v ≠ Lᵢ, and lemma_2_leader_released.

    • The proof of Lemma 3 explicitly pays the leader's own redemption premiums R(Lᵢ) out of the escrow premium E(u, v) = R(Lᵢ), and only then applies Lemma 2 to the leader. So the paper's own accounting excludes the leader's own deposits from "net redemption premium profit" in this case, and the fix is local. The hedging result (Lemma 6) is unaffected.

  2. Lemma 3, in its split form, is false for leaders (same reason, 🔧).

    • Counterexample: TwoCycle.lemma_3_split_false. The net escrow profit is 2, the net redemption profit −1, against the claimed ≥ 1. The total is 1.

    • We prove the total form: escrow profit ≥ 0 and total premium profit ≥ p per escrowed asset (lemma_3). When an entering asset is missing, every asset v escrowed stays unredeemed, which is why "escrowed" suffices here.

  3. Equation (1). For v ∥ q that is neither a path nor a cycle (v strictly inside q), the paper's recursion does not terminate. Such premiums do occur with ≥ 2 leaders. We set R(q, v) = p there. This is the only value the protocol needs, since a conforming v does not extend q (🔧). The paper's well-definedness remark shows it intends recursion over paths only.

  4. Lemma 1 says "if a swap completes successfully, then each v has all its premiums refunded". We prove it for executions where everybody conforms and every asset is escrowed (lemma_1, lemma_1_net).

    • With deviators it can fail for a conforming v, in a harmless way. Suppose a deviating w deposits a premium on (v, w) with a short path, and later presents a valid key with a longer path, after the premium's deadline. Then v forwards too late for its own premium's deadline and pays it, but it collects w's premium on (v, w), which covers it plus p.

    • We read Lemma 1 as a statement about the normal case where everybody conforms (the paper states it just before "What can go wrong?", mirroring the liveness property of §5.2), so this is not counted as an error. The scenario also depends on our choice of premium deadlines, which the paper does not fix. It is not formalized. (The paper's sentence "In the following lemmas, v is a compliant party" precedes Lemma 1.)

  5. Lemma 6's proof (✏️) says "a profit of at least p for that asset" for every escrowed asset. That holds only for assets that are not redeemed (Definition 1). We prove p · |unredeemed escrowed assets| ≤ net (lemma_6), and net ≥ 0 always (net_nonneg).

  6. Lemma 7 needs at least one conforming bidder, which the paper leaves implicit. Without one it fails (Auction.lemma7_needs_compliant_bidder). ✏️: Lemma 8 only uses it for a conforming bidder.

Protocol details we had to fix (under-specification)

  • Timeouts (§7.1). The paper says only "each step takes time at most Δ … each following step's timeout increases by Δ". We use the family Swap:

    • escrow-premium deadlines dl1 v ≤ t1;

    • redemption premiums with path q by t1 + |q| (|q| = number of vertices);

    • activation at t2 ≥ t1 + |V|;

    • asset deadlines dl3 with dl3 u + 1 ≤ dl3 v along arcs into followers, and t2 + 1 ≤ dl3 v ≤ B;

    • keys and redemption premiums with path q expire at B + |q|.

    The hashkey timeout (diam(G) + |q|)Δ of the base protocol is replaced by B + |q|, because the base protocol now starts after two premium phases. The constraints are used in the proofs: the follower slack is what lets a follower pass escrow premiums through, and B ≥ dl3 is what lets a leader's key meet its deadline.

  • Followers escrow only on activated arcs. The paper states this only for leaders. A follower that escrowed on a non-activated arc could lose an asset without compensation.

  • Leader's release rule. A leader that escrowed nothing (no activated leaving arc) releases its secret immediately (proof of Lemma 4). Otherwise it releases it iff all its entering arcs carry assets (proof of Lemma 3).

  • A leader deposits its redemption premiums only if all its entering escrow premiums arrived (proof of Lemma 5).

  • Followers whose phase 1 failed still take part in phase 2, as the text of §7.1 says. This does not hurt them.

  • Path checks. Redemption-premium paths must be simple paths from the depositor to the leader. Hashkey paths are paths in the sense of [Herlihy 2018]: all vertices but the last distinct.

    • The §7 definition of "path" admits cycles. If contracts accepted a cycle (i, x, i) as a premium path, a deviating leader i could put a premium on (v, i) that activates it. The conforming v cannot extend that path, so it deposits nothing on its own entering arcs, and they stay inactive. If an entering asset of v is then missing, v pays the escrow premium E(v, i) and collects nothing. (Argued here, not formalized.) This would falsify the escrow part of Lemma 3 for a v that escrowed nothing; Lemma 6 is unaffected, since Definition 1 only protects escrowed assets. We do not count it as an error of the paper: Equation (1) does not terminate on such a path (v ∥ (i, x, i) is neither a path nor a cycle), so the contract could not even compute the premium, and the paper's own definition requires distinct vertices.

    • For keys, the analogous problem with simple-paths-and-cycles was found by the model checker of the companion Papers/PODC2018/AtomicCrossChainSwaps.

  • Arc orientation. §7 defines a path by arcs (uᵢ₊₁, uᵢ). Figure 3b and all later uses need (uᵢ, uᵢ₊₁). We follow the figure (typo, ✏️).

  • One redemption premium per (key, arc). The contract keeps the first one. The contract computes premium amounts from the path, so a deviator cannot under-deposit.

  • Hashkeys may be presented on an arc whose asset was never escrowed, so that redemption premiums can be refunded.

  • §5.2 swap phase (🔧). Compliant Alice redeems Bob's principal whenever he escrows it, even if her own escrow did not happen. Compliant Bob escrows only after both premiums are in and Alice escrowed. Under the alternative reading Alice can lose pa + pb (TwoParty.alt_reading_alice_loses).

  • §9 timing (✏️). A key with path q is accepted until 1 + |q|, counted from the start of the declaration phase. Counted from time 0, Alice's key (A) would expire at the end of the bidding phase. The winner rule is a parameter (ties are unspecified). If there is no winner, a conforming Alice forfeits her 2p (Auction.no_winner_alice_pays).

  • §6 indexing. The closed form (rA + B)/Pʳ, A/Pʳ holds with r counting the main swap's premium round (B⁽¹⁾ = (A+B)/P). The "$4, 3 rounds, $1,000,000" example matches A = B = 10⁶, P = 100.

Modelling choices

  • Lock-step synchrony: every conforming reaction lands exactly one Δ after its trigger. The paper says "at most Δ".

  • Executions are records of first-occurrence times plus axioms, not a step-by-step state machine. Contract rules and unforgeability constrain all parties; conforming parties satisfy equations that define their actions. Deviators are otherwise unconstrained, so the safety theorems quantify over a superset of the real executions. Front-running is allowed: a signature can be used at the time it is submitted.

  • Premiums are natural-number multiples of a single p, the paper's "assume for simplicity that each asset has the same premium p". Payoffs are in ℤ.

  • Digraphs have no self-loops.

  • Base-protocol safety (a conforming party that loses an outgoing asset receives all incoming ones) is not re-verified. The paper cites [Herlihy 2018], and the companion formalization shows that Theorem 4.9 there needs a repair (a deadline guard on secret release). In our model the leader's release time is always within its key's deadline (rel_le).

Proof gaps in the paper

  • The proof of Lemma 2 writes q = (v, w, …, Lᵢ) for q = (w, …, Lᵢ) (typo).

  • The proofs of Lemmas 2–6 rely on unstated facts, which we prove.

    • The first premium (not_mem_fp) and the first key (ky_fwd) a conforming party observes do not contain it. This is the unforgeability argument.

    • An activated leaving arc forces all entering arcs to be activated (act_in). The leader case needs unforgeability of the leader's own premium.

    • The forwarding deadlines line up: B + |v ∥ q| = B + |q| + 1.

  • Lemma 5's leader case ("its outgoing escrow premiums are refunded eventually") needs unforgeability: nobody can deposit the leader's redemption premium on its leaving arcs.
