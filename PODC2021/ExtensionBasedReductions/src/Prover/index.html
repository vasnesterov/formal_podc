import Papers.PODC2021.ExtensionBasedReductions.Projection import Podc.Computability.Distributed.NIS.OutputQueries /-! # The prover of Theorem 1 Brusse–Ellen, PODC 2021, Section 7. Given an augmented extension-based prover `𝒫_T` for `T`, the prover `𝒫` for `S` simulates `𝒫_T` against the composed protocol `h ∘ A ∘ A₁`, where `A` is the protocol it plays against. It answers the moves of `𝒫_T` as follows (`toA C` is the `A`-part of `C`): * a query `(C, q)` where `q` takes a step of `A`: the query `(toA C, q)`; * a query `(C, q)` where `q` takes a step of `A₁`: a chain of queries re-reaching `toA (C q)` (whose inputs may differ from those of `toA C`) from the start of the phase; * an assignment query `(C, P, f)`: for every `P`-only schedule `μ₁` of `A₁` from the `A₁`-part of `C` after which all of `P` has finished `A₁`, and every assignment `g` of outputs of `A` with `h ∘ g = f`, it re-reaches `toA (C μ₁)` and asks `(toA (C μ₁), P, g)`; the first positive answer `μ'` gives the answer `μ₁ μ'`; * ending a phase at `C`: ends its own phase at `toA C` if it reached `toA C` by a query in its current phase, and does nothing otherwise. Every handler run starts with a harmless move (the assignment query `(base, ∅, ∅)`) except the single query of the first case, so that chains of `𝒫_T` made of steps of `A` become chains of `𝒫`. -/
