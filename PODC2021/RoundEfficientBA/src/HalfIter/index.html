import Papers.PODC2021.RoundEfficientBA.Half /-! # One iteration for `t < n/2` with the coin in parallel to the last round (Corollary 3.8) In the proof of Corollary 3.8 (`t < n/2`) one iteration `Π^5_iter` takes 3 rounds: the coin flip is run *in parallel to the third round* of `Prox_5`, justified by "after round 2 of `Prox_5`, the slot-pair where the honest parties lie is already fixed"; the iteration is claimed to fail with probability `1/4`. The coin of round `r` is revealed during round `r` (its threshold-signature shares are sent in that round), so a rushing adversary may choose its round-`r` messages — and, being adaptive and strongly rushing, its round-`r` corruptions — depending on the coin. We model an adversary strategy as a family `σ c` of adversaries indexed by the coin, which agree before round `r` (`PrefixAgree`). * `coin_parallel_static`: if the corruption times do not depend on the coin (in particular for a static adversary), the failure probability is at most `1/(2r-2)` (`1/4` for `Prox_5`), and validity holds for every coin. This is the paper's claim, for non-adaptive corruption. * `coin_parallel_adaptive_attack`: for `n = 5`, `t = 2`, `Prox_5` and the uniform coin in `[1,4]`, an adversary that corrupts one more party in round 3 *after seeing the coin* makes the honest parties disagree with probability `1/2`.
