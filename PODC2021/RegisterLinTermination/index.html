Register Linearizability and Termination — PODC 2021

Vassos Hadzilacos, Xing Hu, Sam Toueg. On Register Linearizability and Termination. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467925. Formalized version: arXiv 2102.13242 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Every theorem we formalized holds: Theorem 14 (linearizable SWMR ⇒ write strongly-linearizable), Theorems 10/12/13 and Corollary 11 (Algorithm 2 is write strongly-linearizable but not strongly linearizable; Algorithm 4 is linearizable but not write strongly-linearizable), Theorem 6 (with merely linearizable registers a strong adversary keeps Algorithm 1 running forever) and Theorem 7 (if R1 is write strongly-linearizable, Algorithm 1 terminates with probability 1 — in fact at most one coin sequence defeats any given strong adversary). The proof of Theorem 14 (Lemma 67) is wrong; we prove the theorem with a different construction. The gaps in the proofs of Theorems 7, 10 and 12 are closed by local changes.

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Theorem 14 · Theorem 7 · Theorems 10 and 12

🛠️ Theorem 14 (Lemma 67, Appendix E)

Claim 67.1 wrongly asserts that an incomplete write that is not last in f(H) must be read by some read of H, so the construction f* of Lemma 67 fails (lemma67_construction_fails).

Impact. Theorem 14 is true; we prove it with a different construction (swmr_writeStrongLin).

Details

From an arbitrary linearization function f the paper builds f* by deleting the last operation of f(H) when it is an incomplete write. But the next operation may be a pending read, linearized with an added response (Definition 2 allows it). Counterexample, on the atomic SWMR register: in G the writer completes write 1 and invokes write 2, and a reader invokes a read; f(G) = write 1, write 2, read. In the extension H the read returns 1 and f(H) = write 1, read. Then f*(G) = f(G), f*(H) = f(H), and the writes write 1, write 2 of f*(G) are not a prefix of the writes write 1 of f*(H) (lemma67_construction_fails). The claim also tacitly assumes distinct written values.

Repair

Take, for each history, a linearization with the fewest operations of the writer; restricting a linearization of an extension to the operations of the prefix shows this number is monotone, so the write sequences are prefixes of each other (swmr_writeStrongLin, for any set of SWMR histories — shared memory, message passing or hybrid).

🔧 Theorem 7 (Lemma 19 is too weak)

Lemma 19 ("for each round j, with probability ≥ 1/2 nobody enters round j + 1") only bounds the probability of an infinite game by 1/2, not by 0.

Impact. Theorem 7 is true, in a stronger form: at most one coin sequence defeats a given strong adversary (Thm7.forever_subsingleton, theorem7).

Details

The proof does give the needed conditional statement (the adversary's order of the two round-j writes is fixed before the round-j coin is flipped).

Repair

Against a fixed (deterministic) strong adversary, the round-j coin that lets the game go on is determined by the history before it is flipped, so at most one coin sequence keeps the game alive (Thm7.forever_subsingleton), which has probability 0 (theorem7).

🔧 Theorems 10 and 12 (Lemma 42, Case (b))

Lemma 42 Case (b) uses "r starts after w completes" where only "w writes Val[-] before r starts" is available.

Impact. Statements true; the gap is filled (Alg2.alg2_writeStronglyLinearizable).

Repair

Fixed via Lemma 40.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Shared memory (Sections 4–6): CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl): n processes (Fin n), atomic base registers Val[i] (single-writer by the code), deterministic programs, one base-object access per step; wait-free, no failures needed. Linearization functions map executions (label lists with internal steps), as the paper's proofs use them; Theorem 14 is also proved for arbitrary sets of histories.

  • Write strong-linearizability is CSLib's strong linearizability restricted to a set of operations (Impl.IsStrongLinFunFor RWOp.isWrite): only the write subsequence must be prefix-monotone.

  • Section 3: Algorithm 1 over abstract registers whose responses are chosen by the adversary, constrained only by linearizability of every register's history (Game.LinRegs); a strong adversary is a deterministic function of the whole execution (σ : List Ev → Ev), and p₀'s coin flips take their values from a coin sequence ω (genRun σ ω). Crashes are processes that are no longer scheduled; returned processes may take noop steps.

Routine details the paper skips.

  • Lemma 18's step "(*) implies that the round of the write is ≥ j" needs an induction over rounds that the paper leaves implicit.

  • Appendix D asserts without proof that the timestamps in each Val[-] never decrease (needs an invariant: p_k reads its own register).

  • Definition 55 lists constraints on f(H) without showing that such a sequence exists.
