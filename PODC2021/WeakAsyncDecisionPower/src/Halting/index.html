import Podc.Computability.Distributed.DistributedAutomata.Fairness /-! # Halting acceptance cannot distinguish cyclic graphs (Lemma 3.1) Czerner, Guttenberg, Helfrich, Esparza, *Decision Power of Weak Asynchronous Models of Distributed Computing*, PODC 2021 (arXiv:2102.11630v2), Lemma 3.1. Given graphs `G`, `H` with edges `{u_G, v_G}`, `{u_H, v_H}` on cycles, the paper glues `2g + 1` copies of `G` and `2h + 1` copies of `H` into a chain `GH`: in every copy the cycle edge is removed and replaced by an edge to the next copy, and the last copy of `G` is joined to the first copy of `H`. We use `n = 2T + 1` copies of both graphs (`T` the length of the simulated prefix) and describe the chain as a "voltage graph" over the disjoint union `X = G ⊕ H`: node `(x, i)` is adjacent to `(y, i + volt x y)` for every edge `{x, y}` of `X`, where the voltage is `±1` on the cycle edges and `0` elsewhere, plus the junction edge `(v_G, n - 1) — (u_H, 0)`. The projection `GH → X` is a local isomorphism away from four special nodes, and the copy index changes by at most one along an edge, so nodes of the middle copies (index `T`) see, during `T` steps, exactly what their projections see in `X` (`sim`). The reference run on `X` simulates an accepting run of `G` and a rejecting run of `H` (interleaved for liberal and exclusive selection, in lock-step for synchronous selection). With halting acceptance, middle-copy nodes of `G` and of `H` then stay accepting resp. rejecting forever, so the run is neither accepting nor rejecting, contradicting the consistency condition. -/
