Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Decision Power of Weak Asynchronous Models — PODC 2021.

Paper: Czerner, Guttenberg, Helfrich, Esparza, Decision Power of Weak Asynchronous Models of Distributed Computing, PODC 2021, arXiv 2102.11630v2. Scope: Section 3 (Lemmas 3.1–3.5, Corollaries 3.3, 3.6).

Model

  1. Transition function. The paper's δ : Q × [β]^Q → Q becomes δ : Q → (Q → ℕ) → Q. It is only ever applied to counts cut off at β (view), so the two are equivalent: values on other inputs are irrelevant. Machine.Halting asks δ q P = q for all P : Q → ℕ, not only P ∈ [β]^Q; a paper machine is represented by δ' q P := δ q ⌈P⌉_β, which has the same runs and is halting in this sense iff the paper's machine is halting.

  2. States. Q is an arbitrary type. Finiteness ([Finite Q], with Q : Type) is assumed only in Lemma 3.5 and Corollary 3.6, the only places where the proofs use it. Allowing infinite Q elsewhere enlarges the class of automata, so those impossibility results become stronger. Likewise, the label alphabet Λ is an arbitrary type (the paper: a finite set).

  3. Graphs. Graphs are bundled (LGraph Λ with V : Type, Finite V). The convention "connected, at least 3 nodes" is the predicate Admissible k, together with an optional degree bound k : ℕ∞. The consistency condition (Consistent k) and the labelling property (IsLabelling k) are only required on admissible graphs, as in the paper.

  4. Schedules.

    • A schedule is ℕ → Set V of permitted selections, and every node must be selected infinitely often (as in the paper's definition of a schedule).

    • Pseudo-stochastic fairness requires every finite list of permitted selections to occur infinitely often as a contiguous block, exactly as in §2.

    • Liberal selection permits ∅, as the paper's 2^V does.

  5. Classes. The classes xyz are not separate types. They are hypotheses: M.β = 1 (d), M.Halting (a), A.fair = .adversarial (f). Every theorem is proved for all three selection constraints, so the paper's appeal to [16] (decision power is independent of the selection) is neither needed nor formalized.

  6. Acceptance. "A accepts G" means that some fair run is accepting, as in the paper. Under the consistency condition this is equivalent to every fair run accepting (Automaton.accepts_iff).

  7. Majority. Labels are Bool: true is a, false is b. Majority is #b < #a.

Statements

  1. Lemma 3.1.

    • The paper states it for DaF. We prove it for every halting automaton, with any counting bound, fairness and selection. This covers Daf and DaF.

    • "Contains a cycle" is formalized as ¬ G.graph.IsAcyclic.

    • Only the class of all graphs (k = ⊤) is covered. The bounded-degree remark (Appendix C, "the graph constructed in the proof of Lemma 3.1 is k-degree-bounded") is not formalized.

  2. Lemma 3.2.

    • Stated for automata with adversarial fairness and any selection constraint; the paper's proof handles only synchronous selection.

    • IsCovering H G f is a surjective, label-preserving map that is a bijection from N_H(v) onto N_G(f v), as in the paper. Both graphs must be admissible.

  3. Corollary 3.3.

    • Stated for every degree bound k ≥ 2, including k = ⊤. This covers both the main statement and "this also holds when restricting to k-degree-bounded graphs".

    • The scaling factor is l > 0. H.count = l • G.count replaces L_H = λ L_G.

  4. Lemma 3.4. Stated with the cutoff β + 1 for every selection constraint. For exclusive selection this is new: the paper's clique proof needs the synchronous schedule.

  5. Lemma 3.5.

    • Proved for every non-counting automaton (β = 1) with any fairness and any selection; the paper states dAF and uses exclusive selection.

    • The cutoff is existential, as in the paper. Our K differs from the paper's explicit m(|Q|−1)+2, which the argument does not justify (see below).

  6. Corollary 3.6. "No DAf or dAF automaton decides majority" becomes A.fair = .adversarial ∨ A.M.β = 1 → ¬ A.Decides ⊤ majority, with finite Q and any selection.

Proofs

  1. Lemma 3.1. We follow the cut-and-paste construction, with these changes.

    • Both graphs get 2T + 1 copies, where T is the length of the simulated prefix. The paper uses 2g + 1 copies of G and 2h + 1 copies of H.

    • The chain is a voltage graph over G ⊕ H, with the same shape as the paper's GH.

    • The distance argument uses a position function that changes by at most 1 along an edge.

    • The reference run on G ⊕ H interleaves G-steps and H-steps (liberal and exclusive selection), or runs both synchronously (synchronous selection). The paper selects the copies of both selections at once. That is not an exclusive selection: the paper relies on [16] here. A sequentialisation within one step would also work, because it changes states only at the special junction nodes.

  2. Lemma 3.2. For exclusive selection, the fibre f⁻¹(v) of the selected node is executed one node at a time. The fibre is independent because f is a covering. CSLib's ωSequence.flatten turns this into an exclusive fair schedule with the same eventual behaviour (exists_fair_of_liftable).

  3. Corollary 3.3. The labelled cycles are graphs on ZMod m. The λ-fold covering is the projection ZMod (λn) → ZMod n. Its fibres have size λ (ncard_preimage_castHom). The labelling property moves between G, H and the two cycles.

  4. Lemma 3.4. We use stars instead of cliques.

    • The schedule is "all leaves, then the centre" (one node at a time for exclusive selection), or the synchronous schedule.

    • Leaves with equal labels always share a state (hinv).

    • The centre sees the leaf counts per label cut off at β, and these agree for the two graphs (min_ncard_preimage_eq).

    • The paper's clique argument with synchronous runs does not apply to exclusive selection.

  5. Lemma 3.5. We follow the paper. The parts are:

    • Stars are represented by their abstraction (centre state, leaf counts), with the order StarLE (same centre, pointwise ≤, same support).

    • Claim (1) is reach_lift: new leaves copy old ones, along any surjection of leaves; for exclusive selection a fibre is executed one node at a time.

    • Claim (2): Dickson's lemma (starLE_pwo) and finiteness of antichains give a uniform bound on the minimal elements, hence the cutoff m (exists_cutoff_of_upward_closed).

    • The pigeonhole and copying step is reach_stable_extend.

    • "Rejects iff a stably rejecting configuration is reachable" holds for both fairness notions (accepts_iff_reach_stablyIn, using the construction exists_fair_frequently).

    Points where we differ:

    • The final cutoff is K = max(m_Y, m_N)·|Q| + 4. The paper's K := m(|Q|−1)+2 is smaller than the bound (m−1)|Q|+2 that its own argument needs whenever m > |Q|.

    • Acceptance needs its own m (for configurations that can escape Y). The paper only says "an analogous argument".

    • From cutoff-equal label counts to equal acceptance, we remove nodes whose label occurs more than K times, one at a time, using stars and the labelling property (hremove, hred).

  6. Corollary 3.6. The witnesses are two stars with label counts (a, b) = (K+2, K+1) and (K+2, K+2). They agree after cutting off at K but differ on majority.

Typos and slips in the paper (no effect on results)

  • Lemma 3.4 proof: "⌈Q_{G(t+1)}⌉_β = ⌈Q_{H(t+1)}⌉_β" should use β + 1.

  • Lemma 3.2 proof: "C_t(v) = C_t(f(v))" should be C'_t(v) = C_t(f(v)) (two different runs).

  • Lemma 3.5 proof: "MinPre*(C) is upward-closed" should be Pre*(C). States and labels are conflated (L_G(q) for a state q).

  • Covering maps: the paper does not say that H must satisfy the graph convention. We assume both graphs are admissible.

Not formalized

  • Sections 4–6: extensions, simulations, the characterizations of the decision power, and bounded degree.

  • The selection-independence theorem of [16]. It is not needed, because every lemma is proved for all three selection constraints.
