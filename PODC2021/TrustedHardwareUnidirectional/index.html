Classifying Trusted Hardware via Unidirectional Communication — PODC 2021

Naama Ben-David, Kartik Nayak. Brief Announcement: Classifying Trusted Hardware via Unidirectional Communication. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467948. Formalized version: arXiv 2105.10566 v1 (full version, 11 pp.); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The paper set out to split trusted hardware into two power classes, but the Claim of §4.1 is false: a simple echo protocol implements unidirectional rounds over sequenced reliable broadcast (SRB) for every n > 2f, so the separation "shared memory ⊋ unidirectionality ⊋ SRB" (Fig. 1, abstract) collapses, and with the Claim of §4.2 unidirectionality and SRB are equivalent for n > 2f. The positive claims hold: Theorem 1 in an eventual form, the §3.2 claim (SWMR objects give unidirectionality) as stated, and the Appendix A claim. The §4.2 construction is safe with the conflict check described in the text; Algorithm 3's pseudocode omits that check and then violates agreement.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Claim of §4.1 (SRB cannot implement unidirectional rounds for n > 2f, f > 1)

The claim is false: a simple echo protocol over SRB implements unidirectional rounds for every n > 2f; the formal claim is refuted for n = 5, f = 2 (claim_4_1_false, srb_implements_unidirectional).

Impact. The paper's separation "shared memory ⊋ unidirectionality ⊋ SRB" (Fig. 1, abstract) collapses: with the Claim of §4.2, unidirectionality and SRB are equivalent for n > 2f. The appendix "corner case" f = 1 is not special. The classification in the abstract and §1 does not follow from the paper.

Details

Counterexample protocol (Echo.echoProtocol). In round r, each process SRB-broadcasts its signed message. Whenever it delivers some process's round-r message, it SRB-broadcasts an echo of it, i.e. relays the signed message. It ends round r once it has delivered echoes of its own message from n − f distinct senders. It has received q's message if it delivered q's message or any echo of it.

  • Why it works. Take two correct processes p, q. Their echo-sender sets have n − f elements each, so they share a sender s (possibly Byzantine), because n > 2f. SRB delivers s's messages to everybody as prefixes of a single sequence. Whichever of echo(p), echo(q) comes first in that sequence has been delivered by both p and q before they ended the round.

  • Liveness. The ≥ n − f correct processes echo everything.

  • Within the paper's model. The protocol uses only what the paper itself assumes and uses: transferable signatures (§2), relayed signed messages counting as "received", and waiting for messages from n − f processes — exactly the ingredients of the paper's own Appendix A protocol for f = 1. Definition 2 puts no other constraint (e.g. wait-freedom) on how rounds end.

  • What breaks in the paper's proof. Scenario 1 asserts that C₂ "must eventually start the next round since they are receiving all messages sent by correct processes". This is unjustified: a round may wait until its own messages have been echoed by n − f processes. In Scenarios 1–3, C₁ and C₂ do not end the round until their delayed messages reach Q, and then the scenarios are no longer indistinguishable.

  • Lean. We prove that the echo protocol satisfies Definition 2, plus integrity and liveness in every fair execution, against any SRB adversary with unforgeable signatures, for every n > 2f (Echo.echoProtocol_implements).

✏️ Slips

  • Algorithm 3 (pseudocode of the L1 rule, agreement). As printed, it violates agreement (property 2 of SRB): with n = 3, t = 1 and a Byzantine sender, two correct processes deliver different messages with the same sequence number (Alg3.pseudo_disagreement). The text of §4.2 says a process builds an L1 proof only if it sees t+1 copies of its own value "and no other value", and the Lemma 3 Case 1 argument uses this check; the pseudocode (lines 29–39, validateValue) merely ignores conflicting copies. With the text's rule, agreement (Lemma 3 Case 1) and Lemma 4 are proved (Alg3.agreement), so the construction itself is not affected.

    • Counterexample (n = 3, t = 1). The sender 0 is Byzantine; it sends m to 1 and m' to 2. In the forwarding round, 2 hears 1 (so unidirectionality holds), but 1 does not hear 2. Each of 1 and 2 has two copies of its own value: its own and 0's. Under the printed rule both build L1 proofs.

    • Result. Process 0 builds L1 proofs for both values. So {1, 0} is an L2 proof of m and {2, 0} is an L2 proof of m', and the correct processes 1 and 2 deliver different messages with the same sequence number.

  • Claim of §4.2 (which unidirectional round Algorithm 3 uses). Definition 2 only relates messages sent in the same round, but Algorithm 3 uses "the unidirectional round" for every (sender, sequence number) instance without saying which round. Processes handle instances at different paces, and may skip one after seeing an L2 proof, so a single round sequence cannot be aligned across processes. A separate round instance per (sender, sequence number), which tagging provides, settles it; our Alg3 model assumes this.

  • Theorem 1 (SRB implements TrInc: exact vs eventual interface). Theorem 1 is stated for the exact TrInc interface, where CheckAttestation returns true for every attestation previously output, but over SRB this holds only once the attestation has been delivered. The result holds in the eventual form, which is exactly what the paper's proof shows (property (1)). We prove consistency between checkers, non-equivocation (one valid attestation per counter), soundness (property (2)) and completeness for increasing counters (property (1)).

  • Proof of Theorem 1 (implementation of Attest). Attest never returns null for a non-increasing counter; the returned "attestation" is simply never valid (TrInc.attest_nonincreasing_invalid). k is never incremented. The attestation omits prev, which is determined anyway (TrInc.accepted_prev).

Typos

  • validateL1Prf is defined twice; the second definition should be validateL2Prf.

  • maybeDeliver(val) takes no parameter.

  • key == k refers to an undefined key.

  • Lemmas 2 and 3 speak of last[p]; the code has next_p.

  • "line ??" in Lemma 2.

  • checkL2Proof is called without a proof argument.

  • Lemma 2 needs a process to count its own copy, otherwise n = 2t + 1 correct processes only produce t copies from others.

  • §3.1 says "but using" for "by using".

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Claim of Appendix A

🔧 Claim of Appendix A (reliable broadcast gives unidirectionality for f = 1)

The proof ignores a Byzantine member of Q and assumes w.l.o.g. that all of Q sees the same value.

Impact. The claim stands, with the same protocol and a different case split (AppendixRB.appendix_claim).

Details

  • "All processes in Q must receive at least one of p or p''s messages in phase 1" is false for a Byzantine member of Q.

  • "W.l.o.g. they receive p's value" ignores that different members of Q may see different values.

  • "Valid phase-2 messages must contain n − 1 values" contradicts the protocol's check m ≥ 2.

Repair

If n ≥ 4, Q contains a correct process. If n = 3, the m ≥ 2 check and the non-equivocation of reliable broadcast force the Byzantine member's phase-2 message to contain p's or p''s value.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Round layer spec (Rounds.lean, Definition 2). A system exposes, for each process, the list of completed rounds; entry r holds the round-r messages received before the round ended. Messages are chosen by an arbitrary adaptive client (round-r message = function of earlier receipts). ImplementsUnidirectional requires four properties:

    • completed rounds never change;

    • Definition 2 for all correct p ≠ q;

    • integrity: a message "received" from a correct q is q's real round-r message;

    • liveness: every correct process completes every round in every fair execution.

  • SRB systems (SRB.lean, Definition 1). Asynchronous; a set F of Byzantine processes, |F| ≤ f; one SRB instance per process.

    • SRB properties 2–4 mean the deliveries of correct processes are prefixes of one log per sender. A Byzantine sender appends arbitrary messages to its log.

    • Signatures are unforgeable and transferable. A message declares the signed messages it relays, and a Byzantine process may relay (q, m') only if q is Byzantine or m' is in q's log.

    • Protocols are arbitrary deterministic message-driven state machines with arbitrary message and state types.

    • Scheduling is arbitrary. Fairness is exactly SRB properties 1 and 2. Fair executions exist for every protocol (round robin, SRBProtocol.rrExec_fair).

  • SWMR shared memory (SWMR.lean, §3.2). Every access (append-write of the own object, read of one object) is an atomic step under an arbitrary interleaving. Byzantine processes overwrite their own objects arbitrarily; there is no bound on |F|. Fairness: correct processes take infinitely many steps. Fair executions exist.

  • Theorem 1 (TrInc.lean) is proved at the level of the SRB log of one sender. A correct checker's delivered messages form a prefix of that log; the stored attestations are a function of that prefix.

  • Claim of §4.2 (Alg3.lean) and Appendix A (AppendixRB.lean) are formalized at the level of the end-of-round data:

    • §4.2: who forwarded what, who heard whom before compiling L1 proofs, and who issued L1 proofs;

    • Appendix A: phase-1/phase-2 senders and phase-2 contents, with one common content per sender (reliable broadcast).

    These capture the safety arguments of Lemmas 3–4 and the Appendix exactly. Liveness (Lemmas 2 and 3 Case 2) is formalized only through its counting core.

  • Operational liveness of Algorithm 3 (Lemmas 2 and 3 Case 2 beyond their counting core); Lemma 1, which is immediate.

  • Fig. 1's claim that trusted logs are weaker than SRB (only "SRB implements TrInc" is claimed and proved).

  • The §3.2 claim for ACL objects other than SWMR registers (PEATS, sticky bits, all objects of [16]).

  • The introduction's "not by message passing primitives (e.g., trusted counters, trusted logs)". An open remark: the same echo idea seems to work with TrInc-style attested logs. A receiver accepts the k-th attested message of s only after all earlier ones (Algorithm 2's prev), so "trusted logs do not give unidirectionality" is doubtful as well.

Trust. No sorry. All listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

  • SRBProtocol.rrExec_fair and SWMR.rrExec_fair: fair executions exist.

  • echo_fair_exec_exists: a fair execution of the echo protocol with n = 5, f = 2, two Byzantine processes, in which all rounds complete.

  • Alg3.agreement_nonvacuous and AppendixRB.valid_nonvacuous.
