import Papers.PODC2021.TrustedHardwareUnidirectional.Rounds -- CSLib candidate: generic model (SRB systems / round-layer spec), see README. /-! # Asynchronous Byzantine systems with sequenced reliable broadcast (Definition 1) Every process `s` is the designated sender of one SRB instance. Since SRB delivers, at every correct process, the messages of `s` in sequence-number order (property 3), and a sequence number `k` delivered by one correct process is eventually delivered with the *same* message by all correct processes (property 2), the messages of `s` that correct processes deliver form prefixes of one common sequence, the *log* of `s` (`Config.log s`; the index in the log is the sequence number). For a correct `s` the log is what `s` broadcast (property 4); a Byzantine `s` appends arbitrary messages to its log, subject to unforgeability of signatures (see below). * A protocol (`SRBProtocol`) is a deterministic message-driven state machine: initial state and initial broadcasts of `p` (given `p`'s client), and a reaction to the delivery of message `m` from sender `s`, returning the new state and the messages broadcast. * `Config.dl q s` = number of messages of `s` delivered so far by `q`. * Steps (`Step`): a correct `q` delivers the next message of some `s` (`deliver`), or a Byzantine `s` appends a message to its log (`byz`). * **Signatures.** Each message `m` declares the messages of other processes it relays (`relays m`, pairs `(q, m')` = "`m` carries `q`'s signature on `m'`"). Correct processes sign exactly what they broadcast, so a Byzantine process can only relay `(q, m')` if `q` is Byzantine or `m'` is already in `q`'s log. Correct processes are not restricted (the protocol is trusted not to forge). * Fairness (`Fair`): SRB properties 1 and 2 — a correct `q` eventually delivers every message of a correct sender, and every message of a Byzantine sender delivered by some correct process. Asynchrony is the absence of any further constraint: the scheduler interleaves deliveries arbitrarily. `SRBProtocol.Implements A f` says that `A` implements unidirectional rounds whenever at most `f`
