Brokering with HTLCs is NP-hard — PODC 2021

Eric Chan, Mohsen Lesani. Brief Announcement: Brokering with Hashed Timelock Contracts is NP-Hard. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467952. Formalized version: the 4-page proceedings version (no arXiv version exists).

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The reduction from Knapsack to minimum leader sets in flower graphs is right in spirit, and two local changes make it correct (theorem_4_1_repaired): double all weights and the capacity first, and read the number of levels m with the parenthesis in its intended place. Lemma 4.2 (and the forward half of Lemma 4.4) is false for the construction as printed, while Lemma 4.3 (for V ≥ 2) and the backward half of Lemma 4.4 hold. What is checked is the combinatorial correctness of the reduction: its polynomial running time and NP-hardness as a complexity class are not formalized.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 4.2 (and the forward half of Lemma 4.4) for instances with a weight-1 item

When some item has weight 1, the "bitcoin pump" contracts (yᵢ, b) carry min_w(X) − 1 = 0 B, so for the satisfiable instance (v, w) = (1, 1), (1, 1), (1, 2), V = 2, W = 2 the flower graph has no adequate leader set for any number of levels m (lemma_4_2_false_minW_one, paper_minW_one).

Impact. The construction cannot work on such instances. They are ordinary Knapsack instances, weight-1 items are not a degenerate case, the paper reduces from Knapsack in general, and it gives no way to handle them. A new first step, doubling all weights and the capacity, repairs the reduction (theorem_4_1_repaired).

Details

The y-parties are the broker's only bitcoin source besides the supplier: each yᵢ, 2 ≤ i ≤ m, pays (min_w(X) − 1) B. With min_w(X) = 1 they pay nothing, so the flower graph is adequate only if all items fit into the knapsack, whatever m is. The instance above is satisfiable (the first two items) and meets all the paper's assumptions; even every single item fits. But Σ_w(X) = 4 > 2 = W. The intended formula for m (see the slips) divides by zero here. The amount min_w(X) − 1 cannot simply be raised: Lemma 4.3's proof needs it to be below every weight.

Repair

Double all weights and the capacity first (theorem_4_1_repaired).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 4.2, number of levels (all weights ≥ 2). The printed m = ⌈Σ_w(X)/min_w(X) − 1⌉ + 1 has a misplaced parenthesis. It equals ⌈Σ_w(X)/min_w(X)⌉, which is too small: for the satisfiable instance (v, w) = (1, 2), (1, 2), (1, 4), V = 2, W = 4 (which meets all the paper's assumptions) m = 4 and the broker gets at most 4 + 3·1 = 7 < 8, so the flower graph has no adequate leader set at all (lemma_4_2_false). The broker b starts with nothing and must pay every item contract (b, xᵢ) = wᵢ B, so it must receive Σ_w(X) bitcoins in total. Its only bitcoin income is W from the supplier s and min_w(X) − 1 from each of y₂, …, y_m. So Σ_w(X) ≤ W + (m − 1)(min_w(X) − 1) is necessary for any leader set to be adequate, whatever the schedule (budget_of_adequate). The intent is m = ⌈Σ_w(X)/(min_w(X) − 1)⌉ + 1: writing ⌈x − 1⌉ + 1 instead of ⌈x⌉ makes no sense otherwise, and the proof's "left with at least Σ_w(X) B" holds exactly for that reading. The reduction is correct for every m ≥ 1 that satisfies the budget (lemma_4_4_of_budget, theorem_4_1_of_budget), in particular for the intended m, whenever all weights are ≥ 2 (theorem_4_1_fixed).

  • Lemma 4.3's proof assumes that no single item has value ≥ V, justified by "otherwise the Knapsack problem is trivial", but an item with vᵢ ≥ V and wᵢ > W does not make the instance trivial. The lemmas are true under the stated assumption (lemma_4_3, satisfiable_of_adequate). Without it, Lemma 4.3 and the backward half of Lemma 4.4 fail even for the paper's own m: take the items (2, 20), (0, 10) with V = 2 and W = 12. The instance is unsatisfiable, yet {b, x₁} is adequate: x₁'s 2 E start the whole chain, which then releases the supplier's bitcoins. This example was checked by hand, not in Lean. The reduction first has to discard items with wᵢ > W, and then answer "yes" directly if some remaining item has vᵢ ≥ V.

  • Lemma 4.3 also needs V ≥ 2. With V = 1, the assumption vᵢ < V forces all values to be 0; then (b, y₁) = 0 E and {b, z₁} is adequate although the instance is unsatisfiable. This case is degenerate.

  • The reduction needs positive weights, which the paper never states: a weight-0 item can be funded for free and min_w(X) − 1 is meaningless. This is standard for Knapsack (theorem_4_1_repaired).

  • Lemma 4.2's proof says "after repeating this process m times", but only the m − 1 parties y₂, …, y_m send bitcoins; the + 1 in the intended m pays for this (adequate_of_satisfiable).

  • In Lemma 4.3's proof, {b, y₁} and {b, y_{m+1}} give b no bitcoins, not min_w(X) − 1; this is harmless.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • A transaction graph is a multigraph of contracts. Each contract e has a sender, a receiver, an asset and an amount, and each party has an initial endowment own p a (TxGraph, Model.lean). Contracts are created one at a time, under the HTLCP rules with brokering of §§2–3. A leader may create its outgoing contracts at any time. A follower may do so only once all its incoming contracts exist. In both cases the sender must tentatively own the amount: its endowment, plus what it received on created incoming contracts, minus what it already escrowed. This is the paper's master-contract bookkeeping.

  • Adequate L: some order of creations creates every contract ("the set L can create all edges"). The model is asynchronous, with no failures and no adversary: everybody conforms, and the broker chooses the order. Secret release and triggering are not modelled; the paper's notion of adequacy concerns only edge creation. A minimum leader set is an adequate set of minimum cardinality (IsMinimumLeaderSet).

  • A flower graph (IsFlower) has a broker b incident to every contract, with contracts to and from every other party. Only b is a broker, and every other party owns exactly what it sends. In the paper's graphs b starts with nothing (brokerOwn); the paper's case analysis assumes this throughout.

  • flower K m c is the construction of Figure 2, parameterized by the number of levels m and by the bitcoin amount c on the contracts (yᵢ, b), 2 ≤ i ≤ m. paperFlower K is the paper's choice, c = min_w(X) − 1 with the printed m. The construction is a flower graph (Flower.isFlower). Knapsack has natural-number values and weights.

Main.lean

Not formalized. Polynomial running time of the reduction, the closing remark that Knapsack stays NP-hard for polynomially bounded weight ratios, and NP-hardness as a complexity class. We verify the combinatorial correctness of the reduction: K is satisfiable iff {b, s} is a minimum leader set, iff minimum leader sets have size 2. Also not formalized: the example of Figure 1, the counterexample without the vᵢ < V assumption (hand-checked only), and the step that discards overweight items.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The hypotheses are not vacuous: lemma_4_2_false and lemma_4_2_false_minW_one exhibit instances meeting all assumptions (V ≥ 2, vᵢ < V, positive weights), and theorem_4_1_repaired holds for all such instances. The model is self-contained (Mathlib only). The two generic tools Adequate.first_hit and Reachable.batch' are possible CSLib candidates if another token-game model needs them.
