Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Brokering with HTLCs is NP-hard — PODC 2021.

Paper: Chan, Lesani, Brief Announcement: Brokering with Hashed Timelock Contracts is NP-Hard, PODC 2021 (proceedings version, 4 pages).

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. ✏️ Lemma 4.2 (and the forward direction of Lemma 4.4) is false with the printed m. The paper sets m = ⌈Σ_w(X)/min_w(X) − 1⌉ + 1, rendered from the PDF to rule out a pdftotext artifact. The broker's total bitcoin income is W + (m − 1)(min_w(X) − 1), and its outgoing bitcoin contracts total Σ_w(X). Since the broker owns nothing initially, Σ_w(X) ≤ W + (m − 1)(min_w(X) − 1) is necessary for adequacy of any leader set (budget_of_adequate). The instance K₁, with items (1,2), (1,2), (1,4), V = 2 and W = 4, satisfies every assumption of the paper and is satisfiable. Yet 8 > 4 + 3·1, so the flower graph has no adequate leader set (lemma_4_2_false). The intended formula is m = ⌈Σ_w(X)/(min_w(X) − 1)⌉ + 1, for three reasons. (a) ⌈x − 1⌉ + 1 is a pointless way to write ⌈x⌉. (b) The proof's sentence "after repeating this process m times [each time gaining (min_w(X) − 1) B], by the definition of m, the broker b is left with (at least) Σ_w(X) B" is true exactly under this reading. (c) The fix only moves a parenthesis. With it the reduction is correct for weights ≥ 2 (theorem_4_1_fixed).

  2. 🔧 min_w(X) = 1: Lemma 4.2 is false for any m. The contracts (yᵢ, b) carry (min_w(X) − 1) B = 0 B, so the broker's bitcoin income is only W, and the graph is adequate only if Σ_w(X) ≤ W (paper_minW_one, and lemma_4_2_false_minW_one for every m). The instance K₂, with items (1,1), (1,1), (1,2), V = 2 and W = 2, satisfies every assumption of the paper (and every item fits by itself), is satisfiable, and its flower graph has no adequate leader set. Under the intended formula m is undefined (division by zero). The amount cannot be raised to min_w(X): Lemma 4.3's proof ("by definition of min_w(X), the broker cannot create any contracts to any item party") needs it to be below every weight. We repair this by doubling all weights and the capacity before the construction (Knapsack.double, theorem_4_1_repaired). Knapsack instances with a weight-1 item are not trivial, and the paper reduces from general Knapsack. The doubling step is not recoverable from the paper (it never mentions this case) and adds a step to the reduction. The paper never restricts the weights, and min_w ≥ 2 is no standard convention. Theorem 4.1 is not refuted: the repaired reduction is correct.

  3. ✏️ "Otherwise the Knapsack problem is trivial." Lemma 4.3's proof assumes that no single item has value ≥ V. This is not justified as stated: an item with vᵢ ≥ V and wᵢ > W does not make the instance trivial, and it breaks Lemma 4.3. For the items (2, 20), (0, 10) with V = 2 and W = 12, the paper's own construction (m = 3, c = 9) makes {b, x₁} adequate, although the instance is unsatisfiable. This was checked by hand, not in Lean. The reduction must first remove the items with wᵢ > W. All our theorems carry the paper's assumption ∀ i, vᵢ < V (hv). The assumption is stated in the paper (in the proof of Lemma 4.3) and the false "otherwise trivial" is a claim inside a proof; the lemma is true under the stated assumption, and the reduction's missing preprocessing step is covered by the paper's remark up to the overweight items.

  4. ✏️ V ≥ 2 is needed (hypothesis hV of lemma_4_3 and of the main theorems). With V = 1, the assumption vᵢ < V makes all values 0, and (b, y₁) = 0 E. Then {b, z₁} is adequate whenever the budget allows, although the instance is unsatisfiable. This case is degenerate.

  5. ✏️ Positive weights are needed (0 < wᵢ, used through c = min_w(X) − 1 < wᵢ). A weight-0 item can be funded by the broker for free. This is standard for Knapsack.

  6. ✏️ Slips in proofs (no effect on the statements). Lemma 4.2's proof counts m bitcoin-giving rounds; there are m − 1 (y₂, …, y_m). Lemma 4.3's proof says that {b, yᵢ} gives b min_w(X) − 1 bitcoins; this is false for y₁ and y_{m+1}, which is harmless. The paper dismisses the leader set {b} in one sentence ("since b is a broker, at least one other party should be a leader"); in Lean it is covered by monotonicity (not_adequate_of_supplier_not_mem handles any L ⊆ {b, p}, s ∉ L).

Modelling choices

  • Edge creation semantics (§§2–3 are informal). We create contracts one at a time. A leader may create any outgoing contract at any time. A follower may create its outgoing contracts only after all its incoming contracts exist. The sender must own the amount at that moment: endowment + received − escrowed, the master-contract bookkeeping. Adequate L means that some order creates all contracts, matching "the set L can create all edges"; the broker's choices, such as which items to fund, are part of the existential.

  • Endowments. The paper does not state what the broker owns initially. Every argument in §4 (for example "b needs 2 D to create (b, s)" and "b only receives W B from s") requires that it owns nothing. Every other party of a flower graph owns exactly what it sends ("all other parties have the assets to create their outgoing contracts at the beginning"). This is TxGraph.brokerOwn.

  • Secrets, hashlocks, timelocks and triggering are not modelled. Adequacy in the paper concerns only edge creation.

  • Flower graph definition (TxGraph.IsFlower): the broker c is incident to every contract; there are no loops; every other party has a contract to and from c; no other party is a broker; and every other party owns what it sends.

  • Construction parameters. flower K m c takes the number of levels m and the bitcoin amount c of the contracts (yᵢ, b), 2 ≤ i ≤ m, as parameters. paperFlower K uses the paper's values. Levels are indexed 0, …, m (paper: y₁, …, y_{m+1}); the paper's coin Cᵢ is Asset.C i.

  • min_w(X) is ⨅ i, wᵢ (0 for the empty instance, for which all theorems still hold). m uses the rational ceiling Nat.ceil, as printed.

  • Knapsack is over ℕ: Satisfiable K ↔ ∃ R, V ≤ Σ_R v ∧ Σ_R w ≤ W.

  • NP-hardness is not formalized. No complexity classes and no polynomial-time bounds. We prove that the reduction is correct: K satisfiable ⇔ {b, s} is a minimum leader set ⇔ some minimum leader set has size 2. The paper's closing remark is not formalized either: m may be exponential, and Knapsack stays NP-hard for polynomially bounded weight ratios. With the repaired m ≈ Σ_w/(min_w − 1) ≤ 2n·max_w/min_w (for min_w ≥ 2, and doubling preserves ratios), that remark still makes the reduction polynomial.

  • §3, general graphs. The paper derives NP-hardness for general transaction graphs from the NP-hardness of FVS. We formalize the two facts behind this: every adequate leader set is an FVS (any endowment), and without brokering every FVS is adequate (adequate_iff_isFVS). FVS is defined as acyclicity of the follower subgraph (¬ TransGen u u).
