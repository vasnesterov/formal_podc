Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of A Thin Self-Stabilizing Asynchronous Unison Algorithm — PODC 2021.

Paper: Y. Emek, E. Keren, A Thin Self-Stabilizing Asynchronous Unison Algorithm with Applications to Fault Tolerant Biological Networks, PODC 2021. Formalized against the full version (arXiv:2102.12787v2, 18 May 2021).

Theorem 1.1 is correct (formalized in full, with explicit constants). Every gap in its analysis below has been repaired in the formalization.

Model

  1. Deterministic transition function. The paper's SA model has randomized transitions δ : Q × {0,1}^Q → 2^Q. We formalize only the deterministic case, δ : Q → Set Q → Q (Cslib.StoneAge.Algorithm). AlgAU is deterministic, so Theorem 1.1 is unaffected.

  2. Schedules.

    • A schedule is any A : ℕ → Set V. Empty activation sets are allowed, which makes the setting slightly more general.

    • Fairness is the paper's "no node starvation": every node is activated infinitely often.

    • The round operator ϱ and R(i) = ϱ^i(0) are as in the paper. For an empty node set we define ϱ(t) = t; all theorems are vacuous or trivial there.

  3. Graph class. "Diameter at most D" is G.ediam ≤ D (Mathlib): any two nodes are joined by a walk of length at most D. This implies connectivity.

  4. Levels and turns.

    • Levels are integers with 1 ≤ |ℓ| ≤ k.

    • A turn is the structure Turn k: a level, a faulty flag, and validity proofs (faulty requires |ℓ| ≥ 2).

    • The AU clock of a turn is its position on the cycle, in ZMod (2k) (clock). The output is none on faulty turns.

    • card_turn proves that there are exactly 4k − 2 states.

  5. Transition rules (Table 1).

    • AA condition. We implement "every sensed turn is able and its level is ℓ or φ(ℓ)". aaCond_iff proves this equivalent to the paper's "good ∧ Λ ⊆ {ℓ, φ(ℓ)}".

    • AF and FA are verbatim.

    • A model checker (tools/algau.py) checks the reading exhaustively on K2, K3, P3 and C4. It confirms Obs 2.1, 2.3, 2.5 and Lemma 2.10, and that no fair execution avoids good configurations.

  6. AU specification (Cslib.StoneAge.AUFrom).

    • From time T on, all nodes are in output states.

    • Safety: neighbouring clocks differ by −1, 0 or +1.

    • Clocks change only by +1.

    • Liveness: at least i output changes in [t, ϱ^{d+i}(t)) for every t ≥ T.

    • The paper uses d = diam(G) (theorem_1_1_diam). We prove the statement for every d ≤ D with G.ediam ≤ d.

Theorem 1.1

  1. Statement.

    • We state stabilization by the explicit time R(N(D)), where N(D) = ∑_{j=1}^{k-1}(k-j)(k-j-1) + (2k−3) + (D+1)·2(k−1)(2k−2) and k = 3D+2.

    • stabRounds_le proves N(D) ≤ 70 (D+1)^3, which is the paper's O(D³).

    • The proof works verbatim for every k ≥ 3D+2 (theorem_1_1_general). The paper fixes k = 3D+2.

Section 2.3 (analysis)

  1. Obs 2.7/2.9.

    • We use the magnitude form: along a walk of protected nodes |λ| grows by at most 1 per edge (natAbs_le_of_walk). This replaces the cyclic level distance.

    • Obs 2.9 needs |λ_v^s| < k − d only for t ≤ s < t', not for s = t'. With s = t' included, as in the paper's statement, the application in Lemma 2.20 at d = D would fail: there v is at level exactly 2D+2 = k − D at time t'. The paper's proof of Obs 2.9 only uses s < t'.

  2. Obs 2.8 / Lemma 2.11 (liveness).

    • Obs 2.8's proof ("apply Obs 2.7 to shortest paths") omits a step. Obs 2.7 gives pairwise cyclic level distances ≤ D. To conclude that all levels lie in a window of D+1 consecutive levels, one needs a short extremal-point argument: take the extreme levels on either side of a fixed node's level; their distance must be the short arc because 2k = 6D+4 > 3D. The statement is true. The formalization uses an equivalent integer lifting along walks from one node instead.

    • Lemma 2.11 compares levels cyclically ("ℓ_min(i+1) > ℓ_min(i)"). This is only a presentation issue (it means "advances along the window").

    • Formalization: unwrapped integer clocks U_v(t) (Good.lean: exists_offsets, uclk_inv, uclk_rho_iter) with "min U grows by ≥ 1 per round". The statement of Lemma 2.11 holds as stated.

  3. Lemma 2.12.

    • Formalized exactly, bound ϱ^{2(k−|ℓ|)+1}(t) (fa_core, lemma_2_12).

    • The step "no neighbour can move to turn ψ^{+1}(ℓ) from another turn" also needs that an able neighbour at ψ^{+1}(ℓ) cannot come from the faulty turn there. This holds because FA changes the level; it is proved explicitly as step (B).

  4. Lemma 2.13.

    • The time bound is written ϱ^{2(k−|ℓ|)+2}(t') with an undefined t'. It should be t.

    • The proof uses that v (the endpoint with λ_v = ℓ) performs an AF transition. That is impossible if |ℓ| = 1: level ±1 has no faulty turn, and a node at ±1 that is not protected is stuck.

    • Hence the lemma is only proved under 2 ≤ |ℓ|. This is the only way it is used: in Lemma 2.14 with |ℓ'| ≥ |ℓ|+2 ≥ 3, and in Lemma 2.19 with a suitable endpoint. We did not decide whether the |ℓ| = 1 case is false.

    • The paper's edge form is lemma_2_13_edge, proved for 2 ≤ |ℓ|. It is derived from a node form (lemma_2_13: u changes level or becomes protected), which is what the later proofs use.

  5. Lemma 2.14.

    • Formalized with the paper's exact bound (k−|ℓ|)(k−|ℓ|−1), via cost14 = ∑ (2(k−j)+2) (lemma_2_14, lemma_2_14_aux).

    • The paper's inner induction is on the current level of u. Formally this needs strong induction on the remaining distance. The reason is that u may start anywhere in Ψ^≫(ℓ), and the cost of each step depends on the current level.

  6. Corollary 2.15.

    • Explicit T₀ = R(∑_{j=1}^{k-1}(k−j)(k−j−1)).

    • The positive and negative halves run in parallel, both starting at time 0.

  7. Corollary 2.17. Explicit T₁ = ϱ^{2k−3}(T₀). The paper says ϱ^{O(k)}(T₀).

  8. Lemma 2.19 (constant).

    • The paper claims t' ≤ ϱ^{k(k−1)}(t) with z = ∑_{j=2}^k (2(k−j)+2). Its argument charges one Lemma 2.13 application per level j.

    • The argument does not cover three things:

      • both endpoints of an edge move, so up to two progress steps per level are needed;

      • v may have several non-protected edges, which resolve one after the other;

      • edges {k, −k} may become non-protected meanwhile.

    • We prove the bound 2(k−1)(2k−2) = 4(k−1)² in two phases (lemma_2_19_phaseA, phaseB):

      • phase A: v moves inwards until it is protected or at ±1;

      • phase B: while v is stuck at ±1, every neighbour reaches ∓1 or becomes protected.

    • It is still O(k²), and the paper's final O(D³) is unaffected. We did not decide whether k(k−1) itself is true.

    • Lemma 2.19 (and Lemma 2.22's main argument) only need the graph to be out-protected (t ≥ T₀), not justified.

  9. Lemma 2.20.

    • The claim is formalized with general ℓ, d (lemma_2_20_claim) under 1 ≤ ℓ, ℓ + 3d < k.

    • The claim itself does not need t ≥ T₁.

    • The paper's conclusion "G is protected at time t'" for an arbitrary later t' needs persistence of protection. That holds only after T₁ (Lemmas 2.18 and 2.10).

    • Our lemma_2_20 concludes that G is protected at the time v first reaches level 2D+2. It also derives the times t, t' with properties (I)–(III) from "|λ_v| = 1 at t, |λ_v| ≥ 2D+2 later". The paper only says "plugging d = D". We need: from −1 a node can only move to 1, and between 1 and k−1 levels change by ±1.

    • Existence of t_w, t'_w: the paper says "there must exist". Formally, t'_w is the first time w reaches ℓ+2d (exists_first), and t_w is the last time before it at level ≤ ℓ+1 (Nat.findGreatest).

  10. Lemma 2.21.

    • Formalized with an explicit hypothesis that protected graphs stay protected. This is supplied from T₁ on.

    • "Grounded" (Grounded) is as in the paper: v lies anywhere on a walk of length ≤ D with all nodes protected and an endpoint at ±1.

  11. Lemma 2.22.

    • The paper's potential argument picks x_t and P^t anew at every time, with tie-breaking by |λ|. It then applies Lemma 2.19 to x_{τ(i)}. But when that node becomes protected, x_t may already be a different node at the same distance, so "d decreases or v becomes grounded" does not follow literally.

    • Repair (lemma_2_22_aux): fix the walk P (from v to the first non-protected node x) at the start of a phase. Up to the first time some node of P changes protection status:

      • an interior node becoming non-protected gives a shorter walk (recursion on its length);

      • x becoming protected makes P grounded, since newly protected nodes are at ±1 (natAbs_eq_one_of_newly_prot).

    • Bound: ϱ^{(D+1)·2(k−1)(2k−2)}(T₁). No tie-breaking or G_p-distances are needed.

Corollary 1.2 and Theorems 1.3/1.4

  1. Corollary 1.2 (synchronizer).

    • Proved only for deterministic Π and static tasks: the output is eventually fixed and legal, and Π synchronously self-stabilizes on the given G within f rounds.

    • Bound: R(N(D) + D + f). The extra D rounds absorb the initial skew of the unwrapped clocks.

    • Section 4 specifies δ* only for AA steps. On other steps we keep the first two coordinates.

    • Error. For arbitrary tasks, as the paper states it, the corollary is false. Π* only guarantees that every node shows the synchronous execution at its own simulated round; rounds differ by at most 1 across edges (synchronizer_simulation).

    • Counterexample in Lean (Counterexample.lean), already for D = 1 (complete graphs). Task: "outputs equal at all times and changing infinitely often".

      • It is solvable synchronously on every complete graph by δ(b,S) = ¬(true ∈ S) (toggle_sync). That algorithm cycles on P₃, so the counterexample uses the class D = 1.

      • It is solvable by no deterministic SA algorithm on K₂ under the fair round-robin schedule (no_async_alg_roundRobin).

    • Randomization, expectation and w.h.p. bounds are not formalized. Neither is the |Q*| = |Q|²(4k−2) count.

  2. Theorems 1.3/1.4 (randomized synchronous LE / MIS, Section 3) are not formalized: they are out of scope, being randomized with w.h.p. analysis.
