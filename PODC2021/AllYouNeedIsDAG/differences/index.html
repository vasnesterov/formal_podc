Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of All You Need is DAG (DAG-Rider) — PODC 2021.

Paper: Keidar, Kokoris-Kogias, Naor, Spiegelman, All You Need is DAG, PODC 2021, arXiv:2102.08325v2.

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Lemma 1 is false as stated. The statement of Proposition 2 is true, but its proof has a gap. Section 6.1 defines "commit" as popping from the stack. This includes leaders committed indirectly in lines 39–43. Lemma 1 claims that every later leader u has strong_path(u, v) to every committed leader v. The proof only uses the commit rule of line 36, so it covers only directly committed leaders. Counterexample (n = 4, f = 1, see Lemma1Counterexample.lean; observer: correct process 3, whose DAG at wave_ready(w) is rounds 1..4w minus its own (4w, 3), a realizable view): the leader v₁ of wave 1 is not committed in wave 1. The wave-2 leader v₂ has no strong path to v₁ and is not committed either. The wave-3 leader v₃ is committed directly and reaches v₁ but not v₂. So v₁ is committed with v₃, while v₂ (in the same DAG) has no strong path to v₁. Proposition 2's proof cites Lemma 1 for all committed leaders. Repair: lemma1_direct proves Lemma 1 for direct commits. The total-order proof then shows that the output of a process is the delivery of the "full chain" of its last directly committed leader. That chain depends only on the global DAG (orun_inv, gchain_through). Classification: false statement (Lemma 1, with Section 6.1's definition of commit); proof gap with true statement (Proposition 2). Section 5 states Lemma 1 right after the commit rule; read as being about direct commits only, it is true (lemma1_direct) and only the Proposition 2 gap remains.

  2. Chain quality (Section 3) holds only for suitable delivery orders. The paper states it without proof. order_vertices delivers "in some deterministic order".

    • If that order sorts by source first, chain quality fails (chain_quality_false: n = 4, f = 1, a prefix of size 3 with 2 Byzantine blocks).

    • If the order is round-major (non-decreasing rounds, arbitrary order inside a round), the claim holds (chain_quality). Key fact: in every prefix of the output, every round below the highest round of the prefix already has 2f + 1 vertices.

    Classification: under-specified definition / unproved claim (false for some admissible orders, true for round-major ones).

  3. Lemma 2 / Claim 6: the probabilistic argument needs a stronger Lemma 2 than the one stated. This is a proof gap; the statement is true.

    • Lemma 2 gives a set V per process, depending on the process's DAG when it completes the wave. The coin is revealed once f + 1 processes call it, so after the first correct process completes the wave. Other correct processes complete it later, and by then the adversary may know the leader.

    • What is needed is a set fixed before the reveal that every later round-4 vertex (including Byzantine ones created afterwards) reaches. We prove such a set exists: core f C w E3 = the sources strongly reached by ≥ f + 1 of any 2f + 1 existing round-3 vertices E3.

      • It has ≥ 2f + 1 members (core_card, by double counting rather than the paper's informal mapping to the common-core protocol).

      • Every round-4 vertex with 2f + 1 strong edges reaches all of it (sPath_of_mem_core).

      • It is determined by the strong past of E3 (core_congr).

    • The paper's Lemma 2 follows (lemma2).

  4. Claim 2's proof ("by induction on rounds") is unsound as written. The statement is true.

    • Line 25 checks only the number of strong edges. A Byzantine vertex may have weak edges to vertices of the same or a higher round, so predecessors need not have smaller rounds.

    • The proof also silently assumes that every correct process eventually reaches the vertex's round. That is round liveness, which the paper never proves and which itself needs Claim 2.

    • We prove round liveness and Claim 2 jointly: induction on rounds, with an inner strong induction on the time at which the vertex was added (roundLive_succ, histLive_of_roundLive, claim2, round_liveness).

  5. Proposition 4 (Validity): the proof skips a step. The statement is true. Weak edges only ensure that vertices created by correct processes after time T₀ reach v. A committed leader may be Byzantine. The missing step: every vertex of round ≥ R₀ + 1 has 2f + 1 strong edges, so it has at least one strong edge to a correct vertex of round ≥ R₀, which reaches v. Here R₀ exceeds all correct rounds at T₀. (run_validity, internal lemmas A/B.)

  6. RBC authenticity is not among the listed RBC properties. The paper's Integrity says only "at most once per (r, k)". Validity also needs the standard property that a correct process delivers a message of a correct sender k only if k broadcast it. Otherwise the contents of a correct process's vertex could be forged. Section 2 says this in prose: p_k in r_deliver(m, r, p_k) "is the process that called the corresponding r_bcast_k(m, r)", and the recipient can verify the sender's identity. We model it as Run.Valid.auth. Not a mistake.

  7. Claim 6 arithmetic. With success probability 2/3 − ε per wave the expectation is 1/(2/3 − ε) = 3/2 + 9ε/(4 − 6ε) + …, not ≤ 3/2 + ε. We prove ≤ (3f+1)/(2f+1) ≤ 3/2 for a perfect coin (ε = 0). Classification: typo-level imprecision.

  8. Pseudocode slips.

    • Line 7 requires predecessors in ⋃_{k ≥ 1} DAG[k]. Read literally, round-1 vertices, whose strong edges point to the genesis DAG[0], could never be added. We use k ≥ 0.

    • Algorithm 2 signals wave_ready(0) at r = 0, and round(0,1) = −3. This is harmless (no commit) and omitted.

    • Line 25 checks only |v.strongEdges| ≥ 2f + 1. The prose (Section 4) also requires the strong edges to point to round r − 1. Without that check a Byzantine leader of wave w' > w could point to round 1 directly, bypass a directly committed leader v of wave w, and break total order. Our model encodes strong edges as sources of round r − 1 (the prose version). This attack is not formalized.

    Classification: typos / under-specified pseudocode.

  9. Minor. Claim 7's proof ("when it delivers v to itself, it adds v") ignores the buffer and round conditions of lines 6–7. They hold eventually by round liveness (claim7).

Modelling choices (formalization vs. paper)

  • Non-equivocation is built in. Vertices are identified by (round, source), and all contents come from one global Content. This is the consequence of RBC Agreement + Integrity on infinite runs that the paper uses.

    • Safety (Safety.lean, ChainQuality.lean) is proved for arbitrary closed and valid local DAG snapshots. It needs no assumption on schedules, on the relation between processes, or on the number of Byzantine processes. Only n ≤ 4f + 1 is used for total order; the paper has n = 3f + 1.

  • Ordering layer. Algorithm 3 is a deterministic function of the snapshots at wave_ready(w), w = 1, 2, … (orun). "Some deterministic order" is an arbitrary function sortV listing each finite set once (SortSpec).

  • DAG layer (DagLayer.lean), asynchronous:

    • Each step of a correct process is one iteration of the main loop, after an adversary-chosen batch of RBC deliveries. A buffered vertex is added only if its predecessors were already present before the iteration; the paper's sequential for-loop can add a chain in one pass. This only delays additions (stuttering).

    • RBC Validity, Agreement and Authenticity are hypotheses of Run.Valid. Their satisfiability is shown by a lock-step run (NonVacuity.lean).

    • Weak edges of correct vertices are specified by their effect, "a path to every DAG vertex of round ≤ r − 2", which set_weak_edges produces. Any such set is allowed.

  • Byzantine set. It is static (fixed in the run). The paper allows adaptive corruption. Safety does not depend on it. For liveness, the theorems are about processes that are never corrupted.

  • Blocks. Correct process p puts block blk p r into its round-r vertex. This is the paper's assumption that every process a_bcasts infinitely many blocks with consecutive sequence numbers. a_deliver(m, r, k) is represented by outputting the vertex id (r, k); its block is C.block (r, k).

  • Probability.

    • Agreement and Validity are proved under the deterministic hypothesis "infinitely many good waves" (Run.GoodWave).

    • Claim 6 is proved in an abstract coin game (Cslib/Probability/CoinGame.lean, specialised in Coin.lean): perfect uniform coin (ε = 0), and an adaptive deterministic adversary whose good set for wave i is a function of the earlier coins.

    • The link between the two (the good set is the robust core, fixed before the coin is revealed) is the content of goodWave_of_core + core_congr + core_card.

    • What remains informal is identifying the execution's probability space with the coin game: the adversary's choices before the reveal of coin w depend only on coins < w, which is the coin's unpredictability.

  • Expected waiting time. It is proved for every finite horizon K; the untruncated bound follows by monotone convergence (not formalized).

Not formalized

  • Communication and time complexity (Section 6.2, Table 1), and post-quantum safety. These are cryptographic or engineering claims.

  • The threshold-signature coin implementation (Section 2).

  • The total-order attack enabled by the missing round check of line 25 (item 8, third bullet), which is described only in words.
