All You Need is DAG (DAG-Rider) — PODC 2021

Idit Keidar, Eleftherios Kokoris-Kogias, Oded Naor, Alexander Spiegelman. All You Need is DAG. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467905. Formalized version: arXiv 2102.08325 v2. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Integrity, total order, agreement, validity and the expected number of waves until a commit all hold (agreement and validity given infinitely many good waves, which occur with probability 1), but the probability argument of Claim 6 needs a new object, a robust core fixed before the coin is revealed. Lemma 1 is false for indirectly committed leaders, chain quality holds only for round-major delivery orders, and the proofs of Claim 2 and Validity have gaps; all of these are closed by local changes. The complexity claims and the link from executions to the coin game were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 1 and the proof of Proposition 2 (total order)

Lemma 1 is false for indirectly committed leaders: with n = 4, f = 1, a process commits v₁ and then v₃, while the later wave-2 leader v₂ sits in its DAG with no strong path to v₁ (Lemma1Counterexample.lemma1_false).

Impact. The proof of Proposition 2 (total order) applies Lemma 1 to every committed leader, so it has a gap. Total order still holds: Lemma 1 for direct commits is enough (lemma1_direct, total_order).

Details

  • Section 6.1 says a process commits a leader when it pops it from the stack. Leaders reached in lines 39–43 count too. Lemma 1 says every later leader in anyone's DAG has a strong path to every committed leader.

  • The proof assumes the commit rule of line 36 was met, which is true only for direct commits.

  • Section 5 introduces Lemma 1 right after the commit rule, so it can be read as speaking of direct commits only. Under that reading Lemma 1 is true (lemma1_direct), and the gap moves to Proposition 2.

  • The counterexample is observed by correct process 3, whose DAG at wave_ready(w) holds all vertices of rounds 1..4w except its own (4w, 3) (a realizable view):

    • Waves 1 and 2 fail the commit rule (only one round-4/round-8 vertex reaches the leader).

    • The wave-2 leader v₂ = (5,0) points only to round-4 vertices that avoid v₁ = (1,0).

    • Wave 3 commits v₃ = (9,0) directly. v₃ reaches v₁ (via (5,1) → (4,0)) but not v₂.

Repair

The output of a process is always the delivery of the full greedy chain of its last directly committed leader, and that chain depends only on the global DAG. (lemma1_direct, orun_inv, delivered_prefix, total_order)

🔧 Section 3 (chain quality)

Chain quality, stated without proof, fails if the unspecified "deterministic order" of order_vertices lists vertices by source first: with n = 4 the prefix of size 2f + 1 = 3 has only one correct block (ChainQualityCounterexample.chain_quality_false).

Impact. An under-specified definition: for any round-major delivery order the claim is true (chain_quality).

Details

The counterexample is a lock-step run with n = 4 and a Byzantine process 0 that behaves correctly: wave 1 delivers the leader (1,1); wave 2 then delivers (1,0), (2,0), …. For a round-major order (rounds non-decreasing, any order within a round, even Byzantine first), every prefix of the output has ≥ 2f + 1 vertices in each round below its top round, and at most f Byzantine vertices per round.

✏️ Slips

  • Claim 6's "≤ 3/2 + ε" is slightly off for ε > 0: 1/(2/3 − ε) > 3/2 + ε.

  • Line 7 requires predecessors in ⋃_{k≥1} DAG[k], which excludes the genesis round, so literally no round-1 vertex is ever added.

  • Line 25 does not check that strong edges point to round r − 1. The prose requires it, and without it a Byzantine leader could skip a committed leader and break total order (described in words only).

Typos

  • Algorithm 2 calls wave_ready(0) with round(0,1) = −3 (harmless).

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 2 → Claim 6 · Claim 2 and the proof of Claim 7 · Proposition 4

🛠️ Lemma 2 → Claim 6 (probability of a commit)

The probability argument needs a stronger Lemma 2: Lemma 2 gives each process a set V from its own DAG when it completes the wave, but the coin is revealed as soon as f + 1 processes have called it, so the adversary may know the leader before slower correct processes complete the wave.

Impact. Statement true; we prove the needed robust version, and with a perfect coin the expected number of waves until a commit is ≤ (3f+1)/(2f+1) ≤ 3/2 (lemma2, Coin.dagRider_expected_waves).

Repair

Take any 2f + 1 round-3 vertices that exist; this already holds when the first correct process completes the wave, i.e. before the reveal. Let the core be the round-1 sources reached by ≥ f + 1 of them. Then:

  • the core has ≥ 2f + 1 members (double counting; this replaces the informal appeal to the common-core protocol);

  • every round-4 vertex ever created, Byzantine or not, strongly reaches the whole core;

  • the core is determined by the past of those vertices.

(core_card, sPath_of_mem_core, core_congr, lemma2, goodWave_of_core, Coin.dagRider_expected_waves)

🔧 Claim 2 and the proof of Claim 7

Claim 2's "induction on rounds" is unsound as written: line 25 checks only the strong edges, so a Byzantine vertex may have weak edges to vertices of the same or a higher round.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (claim2).

Details

The proof also assumes every correct process eventually reaches the vertex's round. That progress claim is never proved and itself needs Claim 2. Claim 7's proof likewise ignores the buffer conditions.

Repair

We prove progress and Claim 2 jointly: induction on rounds, with an inner induction on the time at which the vertex was added. (round_liveness, claim2, claim7)

🔧 Proposition 4 (Validity)

The proof skips a step: weak edges only guarantee that vertices created later by correct processes reach v, but the committed leader may be Byzantine.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (run_validity).

Details

Missing step: a vertex of a late enough round has 2f + 1 strong edges, so at least one of them goes to a correct vertex, which reaches v. The proof also needs RBC authenticity: a correct sender's message is delivered only if it was sent. The paper's list of RBC properties omits it, but Section 2 says it in prose, and we model it as Run.Valid.auth.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • n = 3f + 1 processes, at most f Byzantine (a static set chosen by the adversary). Asynchronous network.

  • Reliable broadcast is abstract. A run gives each correct process an arbitrary sequence of batches of delivered vertex ids. RBC Validity, Agreement and Authenticity are hypotheses (Run.Valid).

  • Non-equivocation is built in: vertices are identified by (round, source) and have one global content.

  • Correct processes run Algorithm 2 step by step (one main-loop iteration per step). When a process advances from round 4w, the ordering layer runs on its current DAG. Algorithm 3 is implemented literally (waveReady, orun); "some deterministic order" is an arbitrary function sortV.

  • The global perfect coin is a function coin : ℕ → P (agreement).

  • Safety theorems hold for arbitrary closed, valid local DAGs, with no assumption on schedules (only n ≤ 4f + 1 is used: two sets of 2f + 1 sources intersect. The bound on Byzantine processes enters only through the RBC abstraction, i.e. non-equivocation).

  • Agreement and Validity assume infinitely many good waves: every correct process's round-4 vertices reach the leader.

    • Such a wave occurs whenever the coin hits the robust core.

    • In an abstract adaptive-adversary coin game (perfect coin, ε = 0), k consecutive misses have probability ≤ (f/(3f+1))^k, and the expected waiting time is ≤ 3/2.

    • The identification of the execution's probability space with this game is informal (see differencies.md).

Trust.

  • No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

  • The counterexamples are checked by decide +kernel.

  • Non-vacuity: a lock-step run with n = 4, f = 1 satisfies all hypotheses of Run.Valid, and all of its waves are good, so the Agreement and Validity hypotheses are satisfiable.

  • The coin game (Cslib/Probability/CoinGame.lean, shared with VABA) and quorum intersection (Cslib/Foundations/Data/Finset/Quorum.lean) are shared infrastructure.

  • No CSLib model was reused. The Byzantine models in CSLib are round/time-based state machines, whereas DAG-Rider needs an abstract RBC.
