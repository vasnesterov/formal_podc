import Mathlib /-! # DAG-Rider: the DAG abstraction Keidar, Kokoris-Kogias, Naor, Spiegelman, *All You Need is DAG*, PODC 2021 (arXiv:2102.08325v2), Section 4 (Algorithm 1). Because vertices are disseminated by reliable broadcast, two correct processes never hold different vertices with the same round and source (non-equivocation). We therefore identify a vertex with the pair `(round, source)` (`Vtx P = ℕ × P`) and store the contents of *all* vertices that are ever delivered in one global `Content`: strong edges (as a set of sources: a strong edge of `(r, p)` to `s` points to `(r - 1, s)`), weak edges and the block. A local `DAG_i` of a process is then just a finite set of vertex identifiers. * `SEdge C v u` / `Edge C v u`: a strong edge / any edge from `v` to a vertex `u` of round `≥ 1` (the pseudocode's `path` and `strong_path` only use vertices of `⋃_{r ≥ 1} DAG[r]`). * `SPath C v u` / `Path C v u`: reflexive-transitive closures (`strong_path(v, u)`, `path(v, u)`). * `Closed C D`: `D` contains all successors of its vertices (Claim 1 of the paper). * `Valid f C D`: every vertex of `D` has at least `2f + 1` strong edges (line 25 of Algorithm 2).
