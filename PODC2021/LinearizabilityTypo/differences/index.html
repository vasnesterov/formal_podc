Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Linearizability: A Typo — PODC 2021.

Paper: G. Sela, M. Herlihy, E. Petrank, Linearizability: A Typo, PODC 2021 (brief announcement), full version arXiv:2105.06737v2. Section/definition numbers follow the arXiv version.

Overall verdict: the paper is correct. Every claim we formalized holds as stated, up to the modelling choices below. We found no errors, only implicit assumptions (items 3 and 9).

Modelling of histories (Section 2.1)

  1. Events. An event is inv p x o or res p x r (Cslib.SharedMemory.HerlihyWing.Event). It has a process p, an object x, and an operation o (with its arguments) or a response r (termination condition and results merged into one value). All objects share the types Op and Res. The per-object sequential specification restricts which ones are meaningful.

  2. Operation identities. The paper identifies operations of H, H', complete(H'), S and H|x implicitly, through equivalence (H|P = S|P). We make this explicit: an operation is OpId = (p, x, k), the k-th operation of process p on object x. Restricting to a process or an object does not change these identities, and in a well-formed history they agree with "the k-th invocation of p, paired with its next matching response" (Definition 2.1). <_H is RT H a b: both are operations (completed) of H, and the response of a comes before the invocation of b.

  3. Sequential specifications are arbitrary sets of histories, one set per object (SeqSpec = X → Set History). We do not require them to be prefix-closed.

    • The "only if" direction of locality (Theorem 5.1) needs the empty history to be legal for every object (hnil : ∀ x, [] ∈ spec x). Prefix-closure plus non-emptiness, as the paper assumes, gives this. Without it the direction fails trivially: take an object whose specification contains no empty history.

    • Deterministic objects (detSpec) contain only complete sequential histories. This makes no difference: legality is only ever checked on complete sequential histories S.

  4. Pending invocation / complete(H). We define an invocation to be pending when no later event of the same process follows it (keep, completeAux). The paper says "no matching response follows". The two coincide on well-formed histories, and all results assume well-formedness.

  5. Extension. IsExtension H H' means H' = H ++ R, R consists of responses only, and H' is well-formed. Well-formedness of H' is how we make "responses to pending invocations" precise: each appended response matches a distinct pending invocation of H.

  6. Sequential histories. IsSeq is the paper's definition: invocation/matching-response pairs, possibly followed by one pending invocation. Complete sequential histories are seqHist M for a list M of operation records. In L1, S is a legal sequential history equivalent to complete(H'), so it is necessarily complete.

  7. Total operations (Section 3.3, Theorem 5.2). Total spec p x o means: for every legal complete sequential history s of x, some response r makes s · inv p x o · res p x r legal. This is the formal reading of "defined for every object value". Every operation of a deterministic object is total (detSpec_total).

Definitions

  1. Definitions 2.2 and 4.1 (LinearizableHW, Linearizable) are stated literally: extension, L1 via complete(H') and equivalence, and L2 as <_H ⊆ <_S or <_{complete(H')} ⊆ <_S. For proofs we use list characterizations (IsLinO, IsLinA), proved equivalent in linearizableHW_iff and linearizable_iff.

  2. Definition 7.1 (linearization points) (LinearizableLP). A linearization point is a gap of H, given as a prefix length lp a: after the invocation (Invoked (H.take (lp a)) a) and, for completed operations, not after the response.

    • The paper's "distinct points" may share a gap. Their relative order is then the order of the resulting sequential history S = seqHist M, which is required to be sorted by lp. This is equivalent to choosing distinct real-valued points.

    • The set T of linearized pending invocations, and their responses, are the pending operations that occur in M together with their recorded responses.

    • "Placing invocation and response at the point" is expressed by requiring M's operations and responses to match H (Matches).

Proofs

  1. Theorem 5.1 (locality). We prove the theorem by a different route from the paper's, which repeats Herlihy–Wing's transitive-closure/minimal-cycle argument with <_H replaced by <_{complete(H')}.

    • For each object we take its linearization and give its operations the running-maximum linearization points from Claim 1, computed in H.

    • Points of different objects never coincide, because each is the invocation time of an operation of that object.

    • We then merge all operations by (point, position). The merged list respects the real-time order of H and restricts to each per-object linearization.

    We did not formalize the cycle argument itself. Its reasoning looked correct on inspection.

  2. Theorem 5.2 (nonblocking). We follow the paper's proof:

    • If the pending invocation is already linearized, the same linearization works.

    • Otherwise the operation is appended at the end of S with a response given by totality.

  3. Claim 1. We follow the paper's proof: the point of e_i is right after the later of e_i's invocation and e_{i-1}'s point, i.e. a running maximum of invocation times.

  4. Section 3 counterexamples. We use concrete instances: values 0/1 for the registers, and x = 1, y = 2 for the queue. All claims of Sections 3.1–3.3 and 5.1 are verified exactly as stated.

  5. Section 4 remark ("L2 is equivalent to <_{H'} ⊆ <_S"). This is WellFormed.precedes_complete_iff, which gives real-time order towards completed operations in complete(H) vs. H.

Section 6 (alternative interpretation)

  1. LinearizableAlt formalizes Definition 6.1 combined with the original L2. Pending operations never end, so a <_H b holds iff a completed before b was invoked, where b may be pending.

    • The stack of Figure 5 is a partial specification: pop on the empty stack has no legal response. This models the paper's "spinning" pop.

    • We use H_s = A: Push(1) completed, then B: Pop() → 1, then B: Pop() pending. The figure does not fix whether Push(1) overlaps the first pop; this does not matter for the claims.

Not formalized
