import Papers.PODC2021.WakeUpMatching.Basic /-! # Theorem 6.3: minimum NAF load = matching cover number (unless it is 1) Dani, Gupta, Hayes, Pettie, PODC 2021 (arXiv:2104.09096v2), Theorem 6.3. *Easy direction* (`exists_naf_of_cover`): `k` matchings covering `V` give a NAF of load `≤ k` (send `v` to its partner in some matching containing it). *Hard direction* (`exists_cover_of_naf`): for `L ≥ 2`, a NAF of load `≤ L` gives `L` matchings covering `V`. The paper's proof repeatedly redirects `f (f v)` to a load-zero vertex `v`; as written that process need not terminate (applied to a leaf of a star with a bidirected edge it cycles) and the claimed final shape (cycles and stars) is not exactly what one gets. We use an extremal version of the same local move instead: * `bad f = {v | f (f (f v)) ≠ f v}` (the image of `v` is not on a 2-cycle); * the move `f (f v) := v` for `v ∈ bad f` with load `< L` keeps a NAF of load `≤ L` and strictly shrinks `bad f` (`card_bad_move_lt`); * if every bad vertex has load `L ≥ 2`, then all its in-neighbours are bad, so counting in-edges inside `bad f` gives `2 |bad f| ≤ |bad f|`, i.e. `bad f = ∅` (`bad_eq_empty`). So there is a NAF `g` of load `≤ L` with `g ∘ g ∘ g = g` (every component is a "double star" around a 2-cycle). For such `g`, label the in-neighbours of each vertex injectively by `Fin L` and put the edge `{x, g x}` into matching number `label x` whenever every in-neighbour of `x` is `g x`; this gives `L`
