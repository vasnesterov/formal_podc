import Papers.PODC2021.WakeUpMatching.Basic /-! # One round of the maximal matching protocol (Algorithms 1–3) and Lemma 5.1 Dani, Gupta, Hayes, Pettie, PODC 2021 (arXiv:2104.09096v2), Sections 2.3, 4 and 5. **Radio model (no collision detection).** In each timestep every node sends, listens or sleeps; a listening node `v` receives the message of `u` iff `u` is the *unique* neighbour of `v` that sends in this timestep; otherwise (silence or collision) it receives nothing (`recv`). **One round** (three timesteps) of Algorithm 1, given the current partner variables `partner` and the roles `ρ` drawn by the nodes (`recruit` with probability `r(t)/2`, `accept` with probability `r(t)/2`, `sleep` otherwise; nodes with a partner sleep): * timestep 1: recruiters send their ID; acceptors listen (`hear₁`); * timestep 2: an acceptor that heard `x` sends `(x, myID)`; recruiters listen (`hear₂`); a recruiter that hears `(x, y)` with `x = myID` sets `partner ← y` (`matchR`); * timestep 3: a matched recruiter sends `(x, y)`; acceptors that sent in timestep 2 listen (`hear₃`); an acceptor that hears `(x, y)` with `y = myID` sets `partner ← x` (`matchA`). Node IDs are the vertices themselves (the paper assumes unique IDs). * `round_isMatchingState` — **Lemma 5.1** (one step): the partner variables encode a matching after every round, for *every* choice of roles (hence with probability one); * `round_mono` — matched nodes stay matched to the same partner (`M(t) ⊆ M(t')`); * `round_matches_of_isolated` — the sufficient condition used in the proof of
