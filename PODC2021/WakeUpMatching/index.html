Wake Up and Join Me! (energy-efficient maximal matching in radio networks) — PODC 2021

Varsha Dani, Aayush Gupta, Thomas P. Hayes, Seth Pettie. Brief Announcement: Wake Up and Join Me! An Energy-Efficient Algorithm for Maximal Matching in Radio Networks. PODC 2021. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467950. Formalized version: arXiv 2104.09096 v2 (full version); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Algorithm 1 outputs a maximal matching w.h.p. (Lemma 5.4), but the neighbour-assignment result rests on Theorem 6.3, whose normalisation does not terminate and takes a new argument. Theorem 6.5 claims maximum load at most k, while the true bound is k + 1, attained on disjoint copies of P₃ whenever the matching subroutine succeeds, and its coverage bounds hold only by a changed count. The energy bound of Theorem 1.1 for Δ ≥ 2, the paper's main cost claim, and the running time were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 6.5 (maximum load of the neighbour assignment)

Algorithm 4 with parameter k does not have maximum load at most k: on disjoint copies of the path P₃ with k = 1 the load is 2 whenever both matching calls succeed, which happens with high probability (theorem_6_5_load_false, exists_run).

Impact. The true bound is k + 1 (theorem_6_5_load), and the polylog-factor claim of the abstract survives. The proof never discusses the load, and the bound k is part of the claim.

Details

Algorithm 4 calls the maximal matching subroutine k + 1 times: once in line 1 and once in each of the k loop iterations. Each call gives a vertex at most one new partner, so it adds at most one vertex pointing to it. Hence the load is at most k + 1, and this is attained. Take a path ℓ — c — ℓ' and k = 1. The first call matches c with one leaf ℓ. In the iteration, ℓ' is the only unassigned vertex and c its only neighbour, so every maximal matching of the cross graph matches them. Then c is re-assigned to ℓ', while ℓ still points to c, so c has load 2. With c disjoint copies (n = 3c) this happens in every execution where both calls succeed. By the paper's own Theorem 1.1 that has probability 1 − O(1/n²), so the claimed property fails with high probability.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 1.1 for Δ = 1 (energy bound). For Δ = 1 the energy bound 2C (log n)(log Δ) is 0, but a node is matched only in a round in which it sends or listens, so on any graph with an edge the event of Theorem 1.1 is empty (theorem_1_1_delta_one). With the usual convention log Δ ≥ 1 this disappears. The energy part of Theorem 1.1 is not formalized otherwise.

  • In the proof of Lemma 5.4, "first bad round: Δ(t) ≥ 1/(2r(t))" should read Δ(t − 1).

  • The proof of Lemma 5.6 switches from d(v, t−1) to d(v, t) inside an expectation conditioned on M(t−1); a case split on d(v, t−1) is intended.

  • The energy computation of Theorem 1.1 has r(T) for 3r(T).

Typos

  • T = CΔ log n need not be an integer.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 6.3 · Theorem 6.5

🛠️ Theorem 6.3 (the normalisation does not terminate)

The proof redirects f(f(v)) to a load-0 vertex v, "repeated … to all leaves in turn", but at a leaf of a star with a bidirected edge r ↔ x₁ this just moves the leaf to x₁ and cycles forever.

Impact. The statement holds with a new argument (theorem_6_3, exists_cover_of_naf), and the proof of Theorem 6.5 uses it.

Details

The claimed final shape also contains leaves and misses "double stars".

Repair

Apply the move only where f(v) is not on a 2-cycle and v has load < L. Each move strictly decreases |{v : f³(v) ≠ f(v)}|. When no move applies and L ≥ 2, a counting argument gives f³ = f, so every component is a 2-cycle with leaves. Labelling in-neighbours injectively by 1..L then yields L matchings.

🔧 Theorem 6.5 (coverage: a false intermediate claim)

The proof claims that after k iterations at most (1 − 1/(2L+2))^k (1 − ε) n vertices of S ∪ R remain unassigned, which fails because the cross-graph matchings may keep picking unassigned vertices outside S ∪ R (proof_6_5_step_false).

Impact. The coverage bounds hold with a change to the bookkeeping (theorem_6_5_coverage_exp, theorem_6_5_full_coverage).

Details

Example: the star K_{1,9} with a partial NAF on 3 vertices of load 2. After 7 iterations a vertex of S is still unassigned, although the bound is (5/6)^7 · 3 < 1.

Repair

Count all assigned vertices. Each call assigns at least |S \ A|/(2L+2) new vertices, and this gives the stated coverage (1 − ε)(1 − e^{−k/(2L+2)}) and the "in particular" claim for k ≥ (2L+2) ln n.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Graph theory (Section 6). G is a finite simple graph. A NAF is f : V → V with G.Adj v (f v). A matching cover is k Mathlib matchings (Subgraph.IsMatching) whose vertex sets cover V. Theorem 6.3 assumes no isolated vertices; otherwise neither NAFs nor covers exist.

  • Radio model (Sections 2.3, 4, 5). Synchronous rounds of three timesteps, no collision detection: a listener receives iff exactly one neighbour sends. IDs are unique (they are the vertices). Algorithms 2–3 are implemented message by message (Radio.lean). In each round a node draws a role: recruit with probability r(t)/2, accept with probability r(t)/2, sleep otherwise. Matched nodes ignore their role. All draws are independent.

  • Probability. Executions of T rounds are paths Fin T → V → Role with the product law (Chain.lean). One-round statements (Lemmas 5.2, 5.6) condition on the current matching M(t−1). Lemma 5.1 holds for every role sequence, which is stronger than "with probability one".

  • Algorithm 4. The maximal matching subroutine is a black box. An execution is a sequence of maximal matchings: the first of G, the later ones of the assigned–unassigned cross graph. Theorem 6.5 is proved for every execution in which all calls succeed; the probability wrapper 1 − O(k/n²) is Theorem 1.1's. log is ln.
