Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Wake Up and Join Me! (energy-efficient maximal matching in radio networks) — PODC 2021.

Paper: V. Dani, A. Gupta, T. P. Hayes, S. Pettie, Wake Up and Join Me! An Energy-Efficient Algorithm for Maximal Matching in Radio Networks, PODC 2021 (brief announcement); full version arXiv:2104.09096v2 (numbering follows it).

Errors in the paper

  1. Theorem 6.5, "maximum load at most k", is false; the true bound is k + 1. 🔧 Algorithm 4 makes k + 1 calls of the maximal matching subroutine (line 1 and k loop iterations). In each call a vertex gets at most one new partner, so at most one new vertex points to it: the load is at most k + 1 (theorem_6_5_load). This is attained: on c disjoint copies of P₃ with k = 1, the first call matches each centre with one leaf, and the second (cross-graph) call necessarily matches the other leaf with the centre, which is re-assigned to it; the first leaf still points to the centre, so the load is 2 > k (theorem_6_5_load_false; exists_run shows such executions exist). This happens in every execution in which both calls return maximal matchings, an event of probability 1 − O(1/n²) by the paper's own Theorem 1.1 (n = 3c); so the claimed event has probability O(1/n²), not 1 − O(k/n²). The constant k is itself the claim, and the paper nowhere states or argues k + 1 (its proof is silent on the load). Theorem 6.5 is a secondary result ("We also consider …"). The counterexample is within the paper's own model: line 4 makes the subroutine a maximal matching of the assigned–unassigned cross graph, so on P₃ the second call must match the remaining leaf with the centre; the load |f⁻¹(v)| is Definition 6.4's. Also for k = 0 the claim fails on any graph with an edge (load 1). The abstract's claim (load within a polylog factor of optimal) survives with k + 1.

  2. Proof of Theorem 6.5: false intermediate claim (statement unaffected). 🔧 The proof says that after k iterations "at most (1 − 1/(2L+2))^k (1−ε) n nodes from S ∪ R will remain unassigned". The maximal matchings of the cross graph may keep picking unassigned vertices outside S ∪ R, so vertices of S ∪ R can stay unassigned: on the star K_{1,9} with the partial NAF 1 ↦ 0, 2 ↦ 0, 0 ↦ 1 (L = 2, |S ∪ R| = 3), a successful run of 7 iterations leaves 2 ∈ S unassigned although (5/6)^7 · 3 < 1 (proof_6_5_step_false). Repair: count all assigned vertices. Each call assigns at least |S \ A| / (2L+2) new vertices (pigeonhole over the L + 1 cover matchings + "maximal ≥ half of any matching" in the cross graph), so |S| − |A_j| ≤ |S| (1 − 1/(2L+2))^(j+1), which gives the stated coverage (theorem_6_5_coverage, theorem_6_5_coverage_exp, theorem_6_5_full_coverage).

  3. Proof of Theorem 6.3: the normalisation procedure is not well-founded as described. 🛠️ "Repeated application of this rule [redirect f(f(v)) to a load-zero v] to all leaves in turn eventually leads to a NAF whose components are directed cycles or stars with one bidirected edge." Applied to a leaf x₂ of a star with bidirected edge r ↔ x₁, the rule sets f(r) = x₂ and makes x₁ a leaf; applying it "to all leaves in turn" cycles forever, and the final shape it describes contains leaves. Also "double stars" (a 2-cycle with leaves at both ends) are not stars. The theorem is true. Repair (Theorem63.lean): apply the move only at a vertex v whose image is not on a 2-cycle and whose load is < L; it strictly decreases |{v : f(f(f v)) ≠ f v}| (card_bad_move_lt). When no move applies and L ≥ 2, a counting argument shows f ∘ f ∘ f = f (bad_eq_empty): every component is a 2-cycle with leaves. Odd cycles thus disappear for L ≥ 2 (they are turned into stars of load ≤ 2) instead of being covered by two matchings as in the paper. The L matchings are then built by labelling the in-neighbours of each vertex injectively (exists_cover_of_tidy).

  4. Theorem 1.1 is literally false for Δ = 1. ✏️ The energy bound 2C (log n)(log Δ) is 0 for Δ = 1 (allowed: Δ is "any upper bound on the maximum degree"), but a node can only be matched in a round in which it sends or listens. On any graph with an edge the event of Theorem 1.1 is empty for every execution (theorem_1_1_delta_one), so its probability is 0 < 1 − 1/n². This is a degenerate parameter corner; with the usual convention log Δ ≥ 1 it disappears. The energy part of Theorem 1.1 is checked only in this corner; its maximality part is Lemma 5.4, which we prove.

Minor imprecisions and typos

  • ✏️ Proof of Lemma 5.4: "Let t be the first bad round; that is, t is minimal such that Δ(t) ≥ 1/(2r(t))" — should be Δ(t − 1) (Definition 5.3). Our exists_first_bad uses Δ(t−1).

  • ✏️ Proof of Lemma 5.6: the chain of inequalities passes from d(v, t − 1) to d(v, t) inside a conditional expectation given M(t − 1) (d(v, t) is not M(t−1)-measurable). The intended argument (case split on d(v, t−1) ≥ 1/(3r(t)), since d(v,t) ≤ d(v,t−1)) is what we formalize (lemma_5_6).

  • ✏️ Proof of Theorem 1.1: 3 ∑_{t=1}^T r(t) = r(T) + 3 ∑_{t=1}^{T−1} r(t) should be 3 r(T) + … (not formalized).

  • T = C Δ log n is used as a number of rounds but need not be an integer. We take any natural T ≥ 1 (and T ≥ 1000 Δ ln n in lemma_5_4_whp).

Modelling choices and differences

  • Graphs / NAFs. V is a finite type, G : SimpleGraph V. A NAF is f : V → V with G.Adj v (f v) (Definition 6.1); load f = max_v |f⁻¹(v)|; minNAFLoad G and matchingCoverNumber G are sInf of the sets of achievable values (Definitions 6.1, 6.2), with matchings = Mathlib Subgraph.IsMatching. Theorem 6.3 is stated for graphs without isolated vertices (hG): otherwise there is no NAF and no matching cover, and both minima are undefined in the paper (in Lean both are 0 by the sInf ∅ = 0 convention). We also prove the junk-free threshold form exists_naf_iff_exists_cover (k ≥ 2).

  • Partial NAFs (Definition 6.4) are f : V → Option V; coverage |S|/|V| is stated as a bound on |S| (#(domain f)).

  • Algorithm 4 is modelled with the maximal matching subroutine as a black box: an execution is a sequence of partner functions m 0, …, m k with m 0 a maximal matching of G and m (j+1) a maximal matching of the cross graph (assigned–unassigned edges of G), the effect of "only unassigned nodes recruit, only assigned nodes accept" (as in the paper's proof). The probabilistic wrapper "with probability 1 − O(k/n²)" (all calls succeed, by Theorem 1.1) is not formalized; all our statements hold for every execution in which all calls succeed. The energy claim O(k log² n) is not formalized.

  • log in Theorem 6.5 is taken to be the natural logarithm (Real.log); with log₂ the hypothesis k ≥ (2L+2) log n is stronger, so the statement is covered. n may be any upper bound on |V|.

  • Radio model (Radio.lean): synchronous rounds of three timesteps, no collision detection, a listener receives iff exactly one neighbour sends; IDs are the vertices (the paper assumes unique IDs, generated w.h.p.). Messages per timestep are typed (ID, pair, pair) as in Algorithms 2–3. Algorithm 1's per-round coin x ∈ [0, 1] is replaced by a role in {recruit, accept, sleep} with probabilities r/2, r/2, 1 − r; matched nodes draw a role that the protocol ignores (they sleep), which does not change the law of the execution.

  • Lemma 5.1 is proved for every sequence of role choices (stronger than "with probability one").

  • Lemma 5.2 / 5.6 are proved for one round, conditioned on the state M(t−1) (fixed partner variables), with the product law over role vectors as an explicit finite sum (prob). In Lemma 5.2, Δ is any common upper bound on d(v, t−1) and d(w, t−1) (the paper uses the maximum residual degree Δ(t−1), which is such a bound). In Lemma 5.6, goodness of round t is a hypothesis (Z ≡ 0 otherwise, as the paper notes).

  • Lemma 5.4 (Lemma54.lean). The probability space is explicit: executions are paths ω : Fin T → V → Role with the product weight of the per-round role laws (Chain.lean; pathProb). We prove P(M(T) maximal) ≥ 1 − T |V| exp(−((T/(3Δ)) ln(3/2) − 1/2)/(6e)) (lemma_5_4) and, for T ≥ 1000 Δ ln n, ≥ 1 − 2T|V|/n⁶ (lemma_5_4_whp); the paper states ≥ P(A) ≥ 1 − o(1/n²) with per-event bound n^{−(4+ε)}. We bound P(M(T) maximal) directly (the failure event is contained in the union of the events E_{v,t}), which is the part of Lemma 5.4 used later; P(A) has the same bound by the same argument (not stated separately). The paper's Riemann sum / integral ∫ r = (T/(3Δ)) ln(r(t)/r(t*)) is replaced by the exact telescoping inequality r(t) ≥ (T/(3Δ)) ln(r(t)/r(t−1)) (sum_rate_ge), which gives the same bound ∑_{t'∈I} r(t') ≥ (T/(3Δ)) ln(3/2) − 1/2. ln(3/2) ≥ 1/3 is used in the whp form (exponent 6 instead of the paper's 4 + ε per event after the union bound — the paper's exponent is better; ours suffices for o(1/n²)). Δ is a natural number ≥ 1 bounding all degrees; rates after round T are frozen at r(T) (they are never used).

  • Theorem 1.1 is not formalized beyond Lemma 5.4 (maximality) and the Δ = 1 corner: the w.h.p. energy bound (Chernoff) and the running time are not formalized.

  • Not formalized: Propositions 2.3–2.4 (cited/random-graph facts), the energy claim of Theorem 6.5, the O(n³) remark after Theorem 6.3.

  • Proposition 2.2 is proved in vertex form (#N.covered ≤ 2 #M.covered, a matching with e edges covers 2e vertices).
