Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Good-case Latency of Byzantine Broadcast — PODC 2021.

Paper: Abraham, Nayak, Ren, Xiang, Good-case Latency of Byzantine Broadcast: a Complete Categorization, PODC 2021 (arXiv:2102.07240v3; numbering follows it).

Summary: every lower bound we formalized holds in its intended range. Several statements are false for very small n (n ≤ 3), one proof (Theorem 19) gives more than it claims and does not cover f = n − 1, and the lower-bound executions need small changes to respect unforgeable signatures. The 2-round BRB protocol (Theorem 5) is verified. The other upper bounds (Figures 3, 5, 6, 9, 10) are not formalized, except the timing core of Lemma 1.

1. Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Theorem 10, read literally, is false for n = 3 (f = 2, 3). (✏️ Outside the intended range.) Table 1 and §5.4 use n/3 < f < n/2, which no integer f satisfies for n = 3; the counterexample (f ≥ n/2) refutes only the literal hypothesis f > n/3. For three parties a simple protocol has good-case latency Δ + δ < Δ + 1.5δ, for every f. A non-broadcaster commits the proposal Δ after first learning it if it saw no conflicting proposal. A party that saw two proposals adopts the commit certificate of the other non-broadcaster. The latency is proved both with synchronized start (three_latency) and, as in Definition 6, with unsynchronized start, measured from the broadcaster's start (three_latency_unsync, any skew allowed by UValid). Lean: ThreeParties.theorem_10_false_three, ThreeParties.theorem_10_n4_necessary. The paper's proof needs five groups g, h, A, B ∋ s, C with A ≠ ∅, i.e. n ≥ 4; we prove Theorem 10 for n ≥ 4 (Thm10.theorem_10).

  2. Theorems 4, 8 and 9 are false for n = 2 (any f ≥ 1). (✏️ Outside the ranges of Table 1.) With one non-broadcaster, "commit the first value received from the broadcaster" is BRB and commits after one message delay (one round, resp. δ). Lean: theorem_4_false_two, theorem_8_false_two (SmallCases.lean). The proofs need two non-empty groups besides the broadcaster; our theorems assume n ≥ 3.

  3. Theorems 6 and 7 fail for n = 1 (latency 0). (✏️ Out of range: n = 1 forces f ≥ n, outside the paper's implicit f < n.) theorem_7_false_one. For n = 2 and for n = 3, f = 1 psync-BB is unsolvable, so Theorems 6 and 7 hold vacuously there (PsyncSmall.lean); the paper's own proof of Theorem 7 needs f ≥ 2 and n ≥ 3.

  4. Theorem 19: the proof gives a stronger bound than stated, and it fails for f = n − 1. (a) Not an error, the bound is just not what the proof gives. The executions of the proof give ⌊n/(n−f)⌋·Δ, one Δ more than the claimed (⌊n/(n−f)⌋ − 1)Δ, once the good-case proposal is delayed by Δ (allowed). For f = n/2 the paper's bound Δ is weaker than Theorem 9 (Δ + δ); ours is 2Δ. Lean: dishonestMajority_lower_bound, and theorem_19 for the paper's statement. (b) ⚠️ Proof gap. For f = n − 1 the groups G_i with odd i have size n − f − 1 = 0. The executions then have a single honest party, and agreement gives nothing. The hypothesis n − f ≥ 2 of our stronger bound is necessary. For n = 3, f = 2, the 3-party protocol of item 1 at δ = Δ commits by 2Δ < 3Δ; this transfer to the synchronized-start IsBRB formulation is argued informally. The paper's (n − 1)Δ holds for n = 3: it is Theorem 9 with δ = Δ (theorem_19_three). For n ≥ 4 and f = n − 1 we prove ⌊n/2⌋Δ by monotonicity in f (allButOne_lower_bound), without showing it tight. (c) Typo. The broadcaster's split ("0 to G_j for 1 ≤ j ≤ (d+1)/2, 1 to G_j for (d+1)/2 ≤ j ≤ d+1") gives both values to the middle group.

  5. Unforgeability in the lower-bound executions. (🔧 Proof gaps; the statements are unaffected.) The paper assumes ideal signatures but never checks that Byzantine parties can send what the executions make them send.

    • Theorem 10, Executions 2/3. As written they are not valid executions. In Execution 1 the Byzantine C and h behave honestly with value 0 and use the default delay δ towards the honest B. So B's messages may relay C's and h's signatures on value-0 messages. In Execution 2, B must replay these messages to g until about Δ + 0.5δ, but C is honest there and holds value 1, so it never produced those signatures. Execution 3 has the same problem for h. Fix used: in Executions 1 and 4, C → B and h → B messages (resp. A → B, g → B) are delayed beyond the replay horizon. X_authValid checks the repaired executions with a causal replay lemma (Run.tokens_replay_rel).

    • Theorems 7 and 9. Byzantine replays of other executions must be truncated in time: later messages carry signatures that are never produced in the new execution. This is harmless for the argument.

  6. Lemma 1 (proof imprecision, statement correct). The proof of part (1) says: if h' receives v' by t − Δ (t = commit time of h), the forwarded proposal "stops h from committing". Step 4a only requires no equivocation until t_prop + Δ + 0.5d, which can be earlier than t when the votes arrive late, so this step is not justified as written. Comparing with t_prop + 0.5d instead works for parts (1) and (2). We check this abstract timing core (Lemma1.part1, Lemma1.part2); the protocol itself is not formalized.

  7. Theorem 10, Execution 1, minor. The main text says the Byzantine C "pretends to start 0.5δ later"; the appendix uses honest start with modified delays instead. We follow the appendix.

  8. Implicit size assumptions (not errors). Theorem 7 needs a non-empty A and B ∪ E ≠ ∅ (n ≥ 3, f ≥ 2). Theorem 9 needs n ≥ 3. Theorem 19 needs n − f ≥ 2 (see 4b). All lower bounds need |V| ≥ 2.

2. Model

  1. Shared model. CSLib Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync. Discrete time; deterministic state machines that take one step per tick from their start; batched delivery with a delivery time chosen by the adversary; static Byzantine faults; ideal signatures as abstract tokens (Auth, Algorithm.Unforging, Run.AuthValid). Every lower bound is proved for authenticated protocols, which is the setting of the paper, and so covers the unauthenticated case. Every process has an input; only the broadcaster's input matters for the specifications. The lower bounds use executions in which the other inputs are fixed.

  2. Specifications (Basic.lean). IsBRB, IsBB, IsPsyncBB follow Definitions 2–4. Agreement covers all pairs of correct processes including p = q, so a process cannot change its decision. Commits are read off the state (decide). "Terminate" is not modelled; it is irrelevant for the properties. psync-VBB (external validity) is not used.

  3. Synchrony, time convention. A batch sent at τ and delivered at dl is visible in the state at time dl + 1; its effective delay is dl + 1 − τ. Thus the state at time t is the local history at time t, and "commits before T" is CommitsBy … (T − 1). SyncValid f d means effective delays in [1, d] between correct processes. The paper also allows delay 0; forbidding it weakens the adversary, so the lower bounds are stronger statements. The protocol must be correct for all runs with delays ≤ Δ (known bound). Good-case latency for actual bound δ quantifies over runs with delays ≤ δ (Definition 6).

  4. Partial synchrony. We use CSLib's convention verbatim (Run.Valid f D, delays dl − τ ∈ [0, D] after GST) and rounds of length D. "Commits within r rounds" means the state at time rD + 1 (PsyncGoodCaseRounds), as in the formalization of Kuznetsov–Tonkikh–Zhang. The lower bounds exhibit lock-step executions (round-r messages delivered at rD). Theorem 6 is proved in the stronger form "not committed at time 2D", i.e. before the round-2 messages are processed.

  5. Asynchrony (Theorems 4, 5). Asynchronous executions are all runs of the model (asyncRuns: any GST, any delay bound). Every such run eventually delivers all messages, but not every fair asynchronous execution is such a run, so the positive Theorem 5 is proved for this class only. Asynchronous rounds (Definitions 9–10) are not formalized. Theorem 4 is stated for lock-step executions: there a protocol with good-case latency of 1 asynchronous round would commit by time D + 1, and we show it does not commit even by 2D. Theorem 5's latency is proved as "every honest party commits by 2Δ in synchronous runs with delay bound Δ", i.e. two message delays.

  6. Unsynchronized start (Unsync.lean). The protocol is an ordinary algorithm, run through the wrapper lateStart A whose input (v, k) gives the start time k. Before its start a process is silent and drops what it receives. UValid f d σ forbids correct-to-correct messages from arriving before the receiver starts. This weakens the adversary, so the lower bound is stronger. The constraint covers all send times, including time-0 batches, so every correct process starts before time d, the run's delay bound. Theorem 10 is stated with skew σ = δ/2 in the protocol's requirements (as the paper assumes), δ = 2e even, and δ < Δ; the latter is needed because the execution uses delay Δ − δ, which must be ≥ 1 in our model. Good-case runs have synchronized start, which is a subset of the paper's good case. Unforgeability is assumed for the wrapped algorithm lateStart A.

  7. Byzantine parties that "behave honestly in another world". We model each as a correct process of an auxiliary run (with the paper's modified delays) and make it Byzantine with Run.enlarge. For Theorem 10, Executions 2 and 3 are then the same auxiliary physical execution up to the start-time shift of A and C (claim2). Delays the paper calls ∞ are replaced by a finite L beyond every relevant horizon.

  8. Theorem 7. In Execution 1 the Byzantine E also sends its round-0 messages to A. They equal those of an honest E, which A receives in Execution 2 too. This lets us reuse the executions ρ₁…ρ₅ of the Kuznetsov–Tonkikh–Zhang lower bound (library module Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/TwoStepLowerBound.lean), which are exactly Figure 4 with p = broadcaster.

  9. Theorem 19. The ring of groups is a time-dependent covering system with uniform delay Δ (DCover, Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/Cover.lean). The broadcaster has two copies (left/right), each talking only to its neighbour group after the proposal. Locality ("information travels one group per Δ") replaces the paper's informal distance argument.

  10. Theorem 5 protocol. Parties gossip all known signed tokens instead of "forwarding the n − f votes". A party votes for the first proposal token of s it knows, possibly relayed by others; simultaneous proposals are tie-broken by choice. The latency statement holds without n ≥ 3f + 1.

3. Not formalized

The upper-bound protocols of Figures 3 ((5f−1)-psync-VBB, Theorems 12–15), 5, 6, 9, 10 (Theorems 11, 16, 17, 18) and the BA primitive they use, Lemma 1 (3), Lemma 4. Asynchronous rounds (Appendix A). The O(n/(n−f))Δ upper bound.
