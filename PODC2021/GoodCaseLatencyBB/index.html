Good-case Latency of Byzantine Broadcast — PODC 2021

Ittai Abraham, Kartik Nayak, Ling Ren, Zhuolun Xiang. Good-case Latency of Byzantine Broadcast: a Complete Categorization. PODC 2021, pp. 331–341. DOI 10.1145/3465084.3467899. Formalized version: arXiv:2102.07240v3 (numbering follows it). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All the lower bounds of Table 1 hold after local repairs, and we formalized every one of them: 2 rounds (asynchrony and partial synchrony), 3 rounds for n ≤ 5f − 2, 2δ, Δ + δ, Δ + 1.5δ with unsynchronized start, and Θ(n/(n−f))Δ; for f = n − 1, n ≥ 4 only ⌊n/2⌋Δ is proved instead of (n − 1)Δ, which keeps the Θ. The Δ + 1.5δ executions are invalid with signatures until slightly modified, and several theorems need very small n excluded, mostly outside the paper's intended ranges. Of the upper bounds, on which tightness rests, we checked only the 2-round BRB protocol and the timing core of Lemma 1.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Theorem 19 for f = n − 1 (dishonest majority)

For f = n − 1 the proof of Theorem 19 does not work, so for n ≥ 4 the claimed (n − 1)Δ is unestablished: the groups of size n − f − 1 are empty and every execution has a single honest party, so agreement gives nothing.

Impact. The bound holds for n − f ≥ 2 (even one Δ stronger, dishonestMajority_lower_bound, theorem_19) and for n = 3 (theorem_19_three). For f = n − 1, n ≥ 4 we only get ⌊n/2⌋Δ (allButOne_lower_bound), which still matches the O(n/(n−f))Δ upper bound up to a constant.

Details

The hypothesis n − f ≥ 2 of our stronger bound is necessary. For n = 3, f = 2, the 3-party protocol above (at δ = Δ) commits by 2Δ < 3Δ = ⌊n/(n−f)⌋Δ; we argue this transfer informally. The paper's claim (n − 1)Δ for f = n − 1 does hold for n = 3, since it is Theorem 9 with δ = Δ (theorem_19_three). For n ≥ 4 and f = n − 1 the bound ⌊n/2⌋Δ follows by monotonicity in f; this is proved, but not shown tight (allButOne_lower_bound).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 10 is false for n = 3 if read literally: a simple 3-party BRB protocol has good-case latency Δ + δ for every f (ThreeParties.theorem_10_false_three). For n = 3 no integer f satisfies the paper's intended range n/3 < f < n/2 (Table 1, §5.4), and the counterexample has f ≥ n/2, so it refutes only the literal hypothesis f > n/3. Theorem 10 holds for all n ≥ 4 (Thm10.theorem_10).

  • Theorems 4, 8 and 9 are false for n = 2: with a single non-broadcaster, "commit the first value received from the broadcaster" is BRB and commits after one message delay (theorem_4_false_two, theorem_8_false_two). The proofs need two non-empty groups besides the broadcaster, and our theorems assume n ≥ 3 (theorem_4, theorem_8, theorem_9).

  • Theorems 6 and 7 fail for n = 1 (theorem_7_false_one). That case forces f ≥ n, outside the paper's implicit f < n.

Typos

  • In Theorem 19 the broadcaster's split assigns both values to the middle group (overlapping index ranges).

  • Execution 1 of Theorem 10 is described differently in Section 5.4 ("C pretends to start late") and in Appendix C.4 (modified delays).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 10 · Lemma 1, parts

🔧 Theorem 10 (signatures in the lower-bound executions)

The paper assumes ideal signatures but never checks unforgeability, and with it Executions 2 and 3 of Theorem 10 are not valid executions as written.

Impact. The statement stands and the indistinguishability chain is kept, with some links delayed in Executions 1 and 4 (Thm10.theorem_10, X_authValid).

Details

In Execution 1 the Byzantine C and h behave honestly with value 0 and reach the honest B within δ. In Execution 2 the Byzantine B must replay its Execution-1 messages to g until about Δ + 0.5δ. Those messages can relay C's signatures on 0-messages, which the honest C of Execution 2 (holding 1) never produces. The replays in Theorems 7 and 9 need truncation in time, which is harmless.

Repair

Delaying C → B and h → B in Execution 1 (and the mirror links in Execution 4) fixes it (X_authValid).

🔧 Lemma 1, parts (1) and (2) (the Δ + 1.5δ protocol)

The proof argues that a conflicting proposal reaching h by its commit time t "stops h from committing", but Step 4a only checks for equivocation until t_prop + Δ + 0.5d, which can be earlier than t.

Impact. The lemma stands (Lemma1.part1, Lemma1.part2).

Repair

Compare with t_prop + 0.5d instead; we check this timing core abstractly (Lemma1.part1, Lemma1.part2).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Base model. We use the shared Byzantine message-passing model of CSLib (Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync):

    • discrete time and deterministic state machines;

    • at most f static Byzantine parties;

    • the adversary chooses every delivery time;

    • ideal unforgeable signatures as abstract tokens. Every lower bound is proved for authenticated protocols and therefore also covers unauthenticated ones.

  • Synchrony. The protocol knows Δ and must be correct in all runs with delays ≤ Δ. Good-case latency for an actual bound δ quantifies over runs with delays ≤ δ. Time is counted with effective delays: a message received at time t is in the state at time t, and a reply can leave at t. So "commits before T" means "decided in the state at T − 1". Delays are ≥ 1, which only weakens the adversary. (This SyncValid f d is the shared library class Run.SyncValid f (d − 1) of Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/RunClasses.lean up to the GST, bridge syncValid_iff.)

  • Partial synchrony. We use CSLib's convention: rounds of length D, and "within r rounds" means the state at rD + 1. The lower bounds use lock-step executions.

  • Asynchrony. Asynchronous executions are all runs of the model, i.e. runs with an unknown GST and an unknown post-GST delay bound (asyncRuns f = library Run.AsyncValidBounded f, bounded asynchrony; not the unbounded class Run.AsyncValid). Asynchronous rounds (Appendix A) are not formalized: Theorem 4 is stated for lock-step executions, and Theorem 5's latency as "two message delays".

  • Unsynchronized start. The wrapper lateStart gives each party its own start time; nothing reaches a correct party before it starts. This constraint also covers time-0 batches, so every correct party starts before time d (the run's delay bound). Theorem 10 assumes skew δ/2, δ = 2e even and δ < Δ.

  • Byzantine parties "behaving honestly with other delays" are correct parties of an auxiliary run, made Byzantine with Run.enlarge.

Theorem 19's proof gives ⌊n/(n−f)⌋Δ, one Δ more than the stated (⌊n/(n−f)⌋ − 1)Δ: it suffices to let the good-case proposal take Δ instead of 0. For f = n/2 the stated bound (Δ) is even weaker than Theorem 9 (Δ + δ). The stated bound is true; it is just not tight to its proof.

Small cases. In the 3-party protocol, a non-broadcaster commits the proposal Δ after first learning it, provided it has seen no conflicting proposal. A party that saw two proposals adopts the commit certificate of the other non-broadcaster. The latency Δ + δ is measured from the broadcaster's start. We prove it both with synchronized start and with unsynchronized start, for any skew allowed by our model; that model forces correct parties to start before time δ. The paper's proof of Theorem 10 needs five groups g, h, A, B ∋ s, C with A non-empty, i.e. n ≥ 4 (ThreeParties.theorem_10_n4_necessary). Theorems 6 and 7 hold vacuously for n = 2 and for n = 3, f = 1, because psync-BB is unsolvable there (psyncBB_impossible). The paper's own construction for Theorem 7 needs f ≥ 2.

The executions of Theorem 7 are those of the Kuznetsov–Tonkikh–Zhang lower bound (same PODC, same construction), taken from the library module Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/PartialSync/TwoStepLowerBound.lean (FastByzantineConsensus still has its own copy, to be switched to the library).

Not formalized. The upper-bound protocols of Figures 3, 5, 6, 9 and 10 (Theorems 11–18) and the BA primitive they use; Lemma 1 (3); asynchronous rounds; the O(n/(n−f))Δ upper bound.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity of the hypotheses:

  • the echo protocol solves psync-BB for f = 0 (echo_psyncBB_zero);

  • the verified 2-round BRB protocol satisfies the BRB hypotheses of Theorems 4 and 8 for n ≥ 3f + 1;
