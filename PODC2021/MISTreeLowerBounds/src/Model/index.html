import Papers.PODC2021.MISTreeLowerBounds.Problem import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.ColourSystem.FreeCoxeter /-! # The port-numbering model on the `Δ`-edge-coloured `Δ`-regular tree The paper proves its lower bound in the deterministic port-numbering (PN) model on `Δ`-regular trees, with two inputs (§2.1, §2.3): * a `Δ`-edge colouring, each node knowing the colours of its incident edges, and * an *edge port numbering* (each edge has the numbers `1, 2` at its endpoints), i.e. an orientation of the edges. We work on the infinite `Δ`-regular tree, realized as the Cayley graph of the free Coxeter group `FreeCox Δ = ⟨1, …, Δ | 1², …, Δ²⟩` (CSLib): nodes are group elements, the neighbour of `v` along the edge of colour `c` is `v * gen c`. Port numbers equal colours (as in the graph family of Lemmas 12 and 15; since the colours are known, arbitrary port numbers carry no extra information). The only free input is the orientation (`Orientation`), given as a direction bit on each dart, antisymmetric along each edge. A `t`-round algorithm is a function of the radius-`t` view (as in the paper, §2.1: "a function that maps `T`-radius neighborhoods into outputs"). The radius-`t` view of the root `1` consists of the inputs on all edges having both endpoints within distance `t` (`AgreeOn t`); the view of `v` is the view of the root in the translated input `I.shift v`. In particular a 0-round algorithm sees no orientation at all: this is the convention used in the proof of Lemma 12 ("they do not even see the port numbering of the edges, that is, their orientation"). `Solves P A`: the algorithm `A` (output = one label per incident edge, indexed by colour) solves `P` on every input: at every node the configuration is in `P.node` and along every edge of colour `c` the two labels form an edge configuration. -/ -- CSLib candidate (round elimination on the oriented Δ-edge-coloured Δ-regular tree) namespace Podc.MISTreeLowerBounds open Cslib.ColourSystem FreeCox
