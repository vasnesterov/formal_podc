Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Generalized CAS and a space-efficient universal construction — PODC 2026.

Paper: Hadzilacos, Thiessen, Toueg, Generalized Compare-and-Swap and Space-Efficient Universal Constructions for the Infinite-Arrival Model, PODC 2026, arXiv 2605.19237v1. Scope: Algorithm 1, Theorems 4 and 5, Appendix B.

Statement-level

  • Theorem 4 (Algorithm 1 is a wait-free linearizable universal construction). Proved as stated: theorem4 (linearizable, waitFree). Linearizability is CSLib's Linearizable, the amended Herlihy–Wing definition; it matches Appendix A. Wait-freedom is CSLib's WaitFree: in every infinite execution, an operation that stays pending forever takes finitely many steps. This matches Appendix A's definition.

  • Theorem 4 (space linear in the number of participants). Proved with explicit constants: space C ≤ 4 + |{processes appearing in α}| (space_bound). The 4 static objects are C, A, S and the dummy cell; each participant allocates at most one cell. "Participated by time t" means "took a step in the execution so far", as in Section 2.1.

  • Theorem 5 (steps linear in the point contention). Proved with explicit constants (theorem5). Let c be the number of pending operations when p executes line 3. Then every interval of the operation contains at most 105 c + 44 memory steps of p. With the invocation and the response, the operation has at most 105 c + 46 steps. The theorem is stated on Traces, which carry ghost variables; exists_trace shows that every finite execution of alg is a trace. The "same operation" hypothesis is phrased as "p is pending at a and does not return in [min a n3, m)".

  • No statement was found false. The issues below are proof-level only.

Proof-level

  • Lemma B.2.14, inductive step (genuine gap 🔧). From O_{n+1} = O_n the proof concludes o' = o_{3n+2} = o_{3n+3} = o_{3n+4} for some o' ∈ O_n. This does not follow: the three operations may be distinct elements of O_n. Repair: by Proposition B.2.13, no operation is identified by four periods, so each operation of S(o) is identified by at most 3 periods; pigeonhole gives at most 3|S(o)| periods. The repair is one line, but the written argument is logically invalid.

  • Our counting argument differs from B.2.13/B.2.14.

    • Instead of the operation "identified" by a useful period (Definition B.2.4), we use rises. A rise of τ is a step after which τ is done and A.time ≠ τ (Trace.RE).

    • Every useful period contains a rise of its identified operation. Indeed, o' is stored in A at T2, and at T3 > max(T1, T2) it is done and not stored, so a rise happens in (T2, T3].

    • Our OpStart.useful (Lemma B.2.12) directly produces a rise of an operation of S(o) inside the time window of the period.

    • Each operation rises at most 3 times (Trace.card_RE_le). Between two rises the operation must re-enter A. Its owner writes it into A at most twice while it is done (Inv2.wd_le, a ghost counter), because the next execution of line 5 sees that it is done. This is the content of the proof of Proposition B.2.13.

    • Periods are disjoint in time, so their rises are distinct; this gives at most 3|S(o)| periods (OpStart.periods, OpStart.it_le).

  • Proof of Theorem B.2.15, arithmetic error (🔧). At most 3c disjoint periods allow up to 15c + 4 complete non-terminal iterations after the first, not "15|c|". The total bound should be 15c + 6 iterations, not 15c + 2. Linearity is unaffected. We prove at most 15|S(o)| + 5 complete iterations (OpStart.it_le). The step count of a pending operation is at most 9 + 7 · it (Inv2.ns), where it is the number of complete iterations.

  • Lemma B.2.6 and Proposition B.2.5 for Noop. "o' is not pending, hence complete" is used also when o' is Noop, which is not an operation execution. The argument still works because no process writes Noop into A. Our OpStart.old_out handles timestamp 0 separately.

  • Lemma B.2.6 and the period of Proposition B.2.8 are not stated in the paper's form. We prove OpStart.A_SO: from line 8 of a non-first iteration on, every operation in A with timestamp ≤ t(o) is in S(o), and right after line 8 the operation in A has timestamp ≤ t(o). The paper's B.2.8 then becomes re_not_SO: if A holds an operation outside S(o) after line 8, then an operation of S(o) rose in between. The paper's proof of B.2.8 contains this argument (the first successful line-15 GCAS after T8).

  • Appendix B.1 is reorganized as a state invariant. The facts of B.1 (Observations B.1.9–B.1.15, Lemmas B.1.16–B.1.25) are clauses of Inv (Invariant.lean) or consequences of it (Facts.lean). Examples: "after line 7, if S is unchanged its operation is done" (copied, Lemma B.1.21); "a process about to succeed on line 13 does so for a pending, not yet linearized operation" (l13, Lemmas B.1.16, B.1.25, Proposition B.1.24); "the response stored with an operation is its response in the linearization" (Claims B.3.1.3–B.3.1.5).

  • Theorem B.3.1 (linearizability). Same linearization (the order of the successful CAS on S), built incrementally with CSLib's IsLinearization.push / append_ret_of_respIn instead of the explicit completion H' and sequential history S of the paper.

  • Assumption B.1.1 (every participating process takes at least two steps) is not needed.

  • Prop B.2.13 / time conventions. The paper's "value at time t" is the configuration after step t. In Trace, C n is the configuration before step n. All arguments are adapted accordingly.

Modelling choices

  • Deterministic applyT. The type T is a CSLib ObjSpec, with a deterministic transition function. The paper (Section 2.1, Appendix A) allows a nondeterministic state-transition relation. The proof does not use determinism, but the formalized statement covers only deterministic types.

  • Comparator of GCAS on A. Triples (time, op, ptr) are compared lexicographically (footnote 2). This needs a linear order on operations ([LinearOrder T.Op], e.g. bit-string encodings) and on pointers (Ptr = WithBot ℕ). Noop is ⊥, and ptrNoop is the pointer ⊥.

  • Memory manager. AllocateCell returns the cells 0, 1, 2, … in order, from a base object MM. Algorithm 1 never frees, so any allocation order is this one up to renaming of cells. The initial contents of all non-dummy cells are arbitrary (cellInit); the paper leaves them unspecified. The dummy cell holds (0, ⊥).

  • Null and ⊥. Responses in cells and in S are RVal R = null | bot | val r (Assumption B.1.2).

  • Granularity. Each access to a shared object is one step, and local computation (lines 11, 12) is merged into the next access. Line 2 is a step only if the process allocates (the test ptr = Null is local). Line 16 is the response step and returns the response read on line 5, instead of reading the cell again. These choices change step counts by constants only.

  • Line 5 fallback. If line 5 reads a cell whose response is neither Null nor a response (only ⊥ is possible, in an uninitialized cell), the process re-executes line 5. The invariant shows that this never happens (Inv.own_cell).

  • Crashes, infinite arrival. There is no crash event: a crashed process simply takes no more steps, which the asynchronous CSLib model already allows. The process type P is arbitrary (possibly infinite). Invocations are unrestricted (allowed := True).

  • Ghost variables (Ghost). They record timestamps (ts), the linearization (lin), completed operations (k), iteration and step counters (it, ns), the "written while done" counter (wd), a clock and the times of the lines of the current iteration (lt). They do not influence the algorithm: arun_fst and exists_arun show that projecting them away gives exactly the executions of alg.
