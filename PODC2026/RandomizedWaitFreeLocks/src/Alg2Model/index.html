The steps of the thunk itself are irrelevant for safety and are not modelled. * Ghost state: `started`, `used` (the descriptor was passed to some `Lock()` call), `clock` (incremented by *every* step, including no-op steps), `call p`/`callIdx p` (the pending `Lock()` call of `p` and the clock value at its invocation). * Actions (`Act`): `invoke p d` (allowed iff `p` is idle and `d` is unused; the descriptor requirement of Section 2.2 — then `stat d = wait` automatically). The invocation step *is* the first shared-memory step of the call, i.e. line 11 of the first `HelpAndPromote()` of line 22 (the `L.Read()`); it emits `inv p d`. `abort p` sets `p`'s `abort_sig` (only during a call). `sched p j` performs the next shared-memory step of `p` (one line); `j` is the index sampled at line 30 (written at line 31). * Randomness is replaced by adversarial choice: the index `g` of line 30 is the action parameter `j`. This is a sound over-approximation for safety (every probabilistic execution is one of these executions). Line 16 reads `C` and stores `⌊log₂ C⌋` with `Nat.log 2 0 = 0` (the paper leaves `log 0` undefined; `C = 0` is possible in the calls of line 22), and line 17 reads `R[λ]`. All entries of `R` (an unbounded array here) initially hold the dummy. * Lines: the `while` test of line 25 is two steps (`l25a` reads `D.isDone()`, `l25b` reads `D.stat`); the local `abort_sig` test of line 26 is evaluated in the step that performs the next shared-memory operation (line 27 or line 31; the sampling of line 30 is local); line 33 (`C.FAD()`) and line 34 (`D.isDone()`, the response, event `resp p D (done D)`) are two steps. Within `HelpAndPromote()`: lines 11, 12, 13, 14 (two steps), 15, 16, 17, 18 (reads `d.stat` and computes `w'` by lines 18–20), 21. * Invalid actions (e.g. scheduling an idle process): configuration unchanged except `clock + 1`, event `tau`.
