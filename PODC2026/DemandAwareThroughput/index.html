Demand-Aware vs. Demand-Oblivious Throughput — PODC 2026

Matthias Bentert, Chen Avin, Stefan Schmid. A Separation Between Optimal Demand-Oblivious and Demand-Aware Network Throughput. PODC 2026. DOI 10.1145/3796701.3815941. Formalized version: arXiv 2605.04699 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All the main results hold, and we prove them without assumptions: the separation theorem (Theorem 5: throughput 5/8 − ε is achievable for large n), the two results from the literature it uses, dependent rounding (Theorem 3) and the Chernoff bound under negative correlation (Theorem 4), and also Propositions 1–4, Lemma 1, Observations 1–4 and Theorem 6. The proofs of Theorem 5 and Proposition 1 need local repairs, and the problem box of Demand-Aware Weak Direct Throughput and Proposition 3 have typos with evident intent. The running times (Propositions 5, 6) and the NP-hardness result (Theorem 7) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • The problem box of Demand-Aware Weak Direct Throughput asks whether "the direct throughput of G … is at least κ"; it should say "weak direct throughput" (defined in §1.1). Read literally, the problem coincides with Demand-Aware Direct Throughput, and Theorem 6 would then contradict Proposition 3 for n ≥ 2. The intent is clear from the name, §1.1, Observation 4 and the proof of Theorem 6.

  • Proposition 3 constructs M_κ but says "(M, κ) is a no-instance"; it should be (M_κ, κ).

  • Proof of Theorem 5: "Let ε = κ − 5/8 > 0" should be ε = 5/8 − κ (since κ < 5/8). "Hence b_ij < (2n−1)κ a_ij" fails when a_ij = 0 (then they are equal; this is harmless). "b_ij < min(a_ij, d_ij)" should read b_ij < d_ij.

  • Numerical remark after Theorem 5: by the proof's own two conditions, κ = 0.6 needs n ≥ 26068, not 26000. The other three numbers (≈ 700, 2250, ≈ 27 million) check out.

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Theorem 5 · Proposition 1 · Proposition 1

🔧 Theorem 5 (non-integer number of variables in Theorem 4)

For each row the proof applies Theorem 4 "with t = n + Y_i" variables (the n rounding variables plus Y_i = ∑_j (1 − b'_ij) constant ones of weight 1), but Y_i is not an integer in general.

Impact. Statement true; local fix (line_bad_prob_le).

Repair

Add n constant variables of weight 1 − b'_ij ∈ [0, 1] instead. The mean, δ and the bound e^{-2nε²/3} stay the same. (line_bad_prob_le)

🔧 Proposition 1 (arithmetic)

In the displayed computation 5/6 − 5ε/12 − ε²/2 is set equal to 5/6 − 5ε/6 + ε − ε − ε²/2, which is false.

Impact. Statement true; the final inequality < κ(1 − ε) still holds, because ε²/2 < 7ε/12 (proposition_1).

🔧 Proposition 1 (graphs with at most one self-loop)

The graphs with ≤ 1 self-loop are dismissed with "cannot host (5/6)M_κ since 1 − ε > 2/3", which is not an argument.

Impact. Statement true; we repair the case analysis (two_node_bounds_rel, proposition_1).

Repair

We replace the graph-by-graph reasoning with two cut inequalities that hold for every flow on two nodes: D_ab ≤ A_ab and D_ab + D_aa + D_bb ≤ A_ab + A_aa + A_bb. Proposition 1, Proposition 4 (first part) and the M₂ example follow by arithmetic. (two_node_bounds_rel, proposition_1)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • A network on Fin n is a directed multigraph given by an ℕ-adjacency matrix A. It is (2n−1)-regular if all row and column sums are 2n − 1. Arcs have capacity 1/(2n−1); equivalently, as in the paper, capacity 1 with demand (2n−1)·θ·M.

  • A flow is a finite family of non-empty paths (walks with at least one arc, so self-demand uses a self-loop or a closed walk) with non-negative amounts. It must serve exactly the demand of every ordered pair.

  • "Throughput of G for M is at least κ" is rendered as "G hosts θM for some θ ≥ κ". The four problems are DAThroughput, DADirectThroughput, DAWeakThroughput and DAWeakDirectThroughput.

  • Capacity semantics. The paper charges a path once per multigraph arc it uses.

    • Constructions (yes-instances) use the stricter rule CanRoute: a path is charged once per traversal of the pair (u, v).

    • No-instances are proved for the weaker rule CanRouteRel: a path through (u, v) is charged once.

    • The paper's semantics lies between the two, so both kinds of results transfer to it. Direct routing is the same under all three rules.

  • Randomness is used only in proofs. It lives on finite sample spaces: a distribution is a non-negative weight function with total 1 (FinDist). This is not a distributed-computing model: there are no processes, failures or adversaries.

Some of our proofs differ from the paper's:

  • Proposition 2: we pad ⌈n·M⌉ to a regular matrix (CSLib Matrix.exists_regular_above) instead of analysing the greedy algorithm.

  • Observation 3: we derive it from Proposition 2 instead of the external Theorem 1 (Keslassy et al.).

  • Theorem 3 and Lemma 1: we obtain the cycle direction by linear algebra (exists_cycle_direction, exists_circulation) rather than by walking along a cycle.

Not formalized.

  • Theorem 1 (Keslassy et al.; not needed).

  • Propositions 5 and 6 (running times O(n³) and O(n⁴ log n)), and the paper's greedy proof of Proposition 2. Randomized tests (n ≤ 4) found the greedy optimal.

  • Theorem 7 (NP-hardness).

Routine details the paper skips. n_κ is "the smallest positive integer such that n_κ ≥ 6/(16ε) + 1 and e^{-2ε²n_κ/3} < 1/(2n_κ)", but the proof of Theorem 5 then uses both conditions for every n ≥ n_κ, and the second is not monotone in general. It does hold for all n ≥ n_κ, because 2n·e^{-cn} is decreasing beyond its maximum and is > 1 up to it. We take n_κ from "eventually" statements instead (eventually_large).

Trust.

  • No sorry. All listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.

  • Theorems 3 and 4 are proved, not assumed, so Theorem 5 is unconditional.

  • The two-node and §2 examples show that the definitions are not vacuous: there are both yes-instances and no-instances.
