As in the paper we equivalently give every arc capacity `1` and scale the demand by `r`: `G` can host `M` iff it can route the multicommodity flow `r • M` (`CanHost`). * A flow is a finite collection of pairs `(P_k, d_k)` of non-empty paths and non-negative amounts. It must serve exactly the demand of every ordered pair `(u, v)` (including `u = v`; a path has at least one arc, so demand from a node to itself uses a self-loop or a closed walk), and the total amount crossing the arcs from `u` to `v` must not exceed the number `A u v` of such arcs. A path that traverses the pair `(u, v)` several times is charged several times (see `differencies.md`: this is the most restrictive reading, used for constructions; the upper bounds in this development only use that a path through `(u,v)` is charged at least once). * `CanDirectlyHost`: the same with single-arc paths only. * The four decision problems of the paper (`DAThroughput`, `DAWeakThroughput`, `DADirectThroughput`, `DAWeakDirectThroughput`), with `N = 2n - 1` arcs per node. "The throughput of `G` w.r.t. `M` is at least `κ`" is rendered as "`G` can host `θ • M` for some `θ ≥ κ`" (the paper's throughput is the largest such `θ`). -/ namespace Podc.DemandAwareThroughput open
