Uniform Content-Oblivious Leader Election on Eulerian Graphs — PODC 2026

Fabian Frei, Ran Gelles, Ahmed Ghazy, Alexandre Nolin. Brief Announcement: Toward Uniform Content-Oblivious Leader Election on General Graphs. PODC 2026. DOI 10.1145/3796701.3815949. Formalized version: the 4-page brief announcement (no arXiv version). Numbering follows it.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The combinatorial core is correct: any port pairing of a graph with all degrees even induces a unique circuit decomposition (Lemma 2), re-pairing at a node merges distinct circuits into one (Lemma 4), the merging phases of Lemma 5 end, after at most log₂ n phases, in a single Eulerian circuit with a single leader, and the pulse arithmetic of Theorem 1 gives O(|E|·IDmax). Two steps of the Lemma 5 sketch each need one added rule: in a mutually selecting pair of circuits both merges must not be executed, and the OR protocol, which we verify on an asynchronous content-oblivious ring, must say how the leader's own input is used. The election as a pulse-level algorithm was not checked: Lemma 3 with its ring-election black box, the max-ID protocol and the synchronisation of merges are not formalized.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • The intro says the "maximum-ID node on the merged circuit becomes its new leader". It is the maximum among the merged leaders (the root of the merge tree; id_le_id_top_of_top_eq). This need not be the maximum-ID node of the circuit: in Lemma 3 a node may only relay passively on a circuit and then does not take part in its election.

  • "Eulerian" is defined as all degrees even, but a disconnected graph cannot end with a single circuit, so Lemma 5 and Theorem 1 need connectivity, which we assume (lemma5).

  • The max-ID step of Lemma 5 (bitwise ORs over "prefix-free" encodings) needs an order-preserving prefix-free code. Otherwise it returns the lexicographically largest code, not the largest ID. Not formalized.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 5, merging phases · Lemma 5, OR protocol

🔧 Lemma 5, merging phases (mutually selecting circuits)

The sketch lets every circuit merge into its adjacent circuit with the largest leader ID, so two circuits that select each other are merged twice, and the second re-pairing can split the merged circuit again (DoubleMerge.double_merge_splits).

Impact. Statement true; repaired by letting the larger circuit of a mutually selecting pair not merge (phaseStep_spec, lemma5).

Details

In the sketch a designated node of C re-pairs one pair of C with one pair of the selected circuit C'. Two circuits often select each other; for example, if only two circuits remain, each one's only neighbour is the other. The second re-pairing then acts on two pairs that by then lie on one circuit. Take K_{2,4} (x, y adjacent to 2, 3, 4, 5) with circuits C₁ = x2y3x and C₂ = x4y5x. Merging at x (the node designated by C₂) gives one Eulerian circuit. Merging at y as well (the node designated by C₁, re-pairing as in Lemma 4) gives two circuits again, x2y5x and x3y4x. Whether this happens depends on how the pairs are labelled, which the sketch leaves open ("w.l.o.g."). So the sketch does not show that the number of circuits halves, or even decreases.

Repair

The circuit with the larger ID in a mutually selecting pair does not merge. Then the merge links form a forest, every link joins two different circuits, and each merged tree contains at least two old circuits and is led by its root, the largest ID in it. We prove that the number of circuits at least halves per phase, so there are at most log₂ n phases. (The protocol must then also tell C whether C' selected C; we do not formalize that.) (DoubleMerge.double_merge_splits; repaired: phaseStep_spec, lemma5)

🔧 Lemma 5, OR protocol (the leader's own input)

The sketch does not say how the leader's own input enters the OR protocol, and if "the first node with input 1" excludes the initiating leader, the protocol outputs 0 when only the leader borders another circuit (OrProtocol.literal_wrong).

Impact. Under-specified; the evident fix (the leader reflects if its own input is 1) is verified on every schedule (OrProtocol.orProc_final, orProc_totalSent_le).

Details

The leader ℓ_C starts a clockwise pulse that "the first node with input 1 reflects"; if the pulse completes the cycle, the leader forwards it once more and every node outputs 0. Under the literal reading, for every ring size, all executions give output 0 when only the leader has input 1, and the algorithm would stop with several circuits.

Repair

If its pulse completes the cycle and its own input is 1, the leader acts as the reflector. We verify the fixed protocol in the asynchronous content-oblivious ring model for every schedule: it terminates quiescently, every node outputs the OR, and at most 2|C| pulses are sent. Also, nodes with input 1 that come after the first reflector receive a counter-clockwise pulse first and must behave like nodes with input 0, which the paper's sentence "receive a counterclockwise pulse without a clockwise one" covers. (OrProtocol.literal_wrong; fixed: OrProtocol.orProc_final, orProc_totalSent_le)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • A network is a finite simple graph G (Mathlib SimpleGraph), connected for Lemma 5 and Theorem 1. "Eulerian" means every degree is even, as in the paper. The ports of v are the darts (v, w), and a port numbering is a bijection with Fin (deg v).

  • A port pairing (Pairing G) is a fixed-point-free, node-preserving involution on darts. Its circuits are the classes of the relation generated by "paired" and "reverse" (Pairing.Circ). Each class is realised by a Mathlib circuit circuitWalk d that follows the pairing.

  • Lemmas 4 and 5 and Theorem 1 are formalized at the circuit level. A state is a pairing with a leader map; two darts lie on one circuit iff their circuits have the same leader, and the leader is a node of its circuit (LeaderInv).

    • Lemma 3, the per-circuit ring elections, is a black box: an arbitrary map elect, constant on circuits, that picks a node of each circuit.

    • A merging phase (PhaseStep) quantifies over every order of the merges and every choice of the common nodes where they happen.

    • Asynchrony, pulses and IDs inside the protocols are not modelled at this level. Theorem 1's pulse count is an arithmetic consequence of the black-box costs.

  • The OR protocol is modelled in CSLib's content-oblivious model (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/ContentOblivious/): an oriented asynchronous ring of n ≥ 1 nodes, pulses without content, an adversarial scheduler, no failures, deterministic nodes and a single leader.
