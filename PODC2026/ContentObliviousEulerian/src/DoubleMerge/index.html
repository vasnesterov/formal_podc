import Papers.PODC2026.ContentObliviousEulerian.Merging /-! # Merging a mutually pointing pair twice splits the circuit again Frei, Gelles, Ghazy, Nolin, PODC 2026, §2.2, proof sketch of Lemma 5: "every circuit `C` selects one neighboring circuit `C'` and merges into it", the merge being done by "a unique designated node in `C` that borders the maximal `C'` … by locally chaining its port pairing". With only two circuits `C₁, C₂` they select each other. If both merges are executed (each by the node designated by its circuit, re-pairing as in Lemma 4), the second re-pairing acts on two pairs that by then lie on the *same* circuit, and it can split that circuit again. Example: `K_{2,4}` with `x = 0, y = 1` adjacent to `2, 3, 4, 5`. The pairing that pairs `2 ↔ 3`, `4 ↔ 5` at `x` and `y` (and `x ↔ y` elsewhere) induces `C₁ = x 2 y 3 x` and `C₂ = x 4 y 5 x`. Merging at `x` gives one circuit (`merge_at_x`); re-pairing at `y` the pair of `C₁` with the pair of `C₂` (the merge requested by the other circuit) yields again two circuits, `x 2 y 5 x` and `x 3 y 4 x` (`double_merge_splits`).
