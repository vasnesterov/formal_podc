import Papers.PODC2026.ContentObliviousEulerian.Repair import Papers.PODC2026.ContentObliviousEulerian.PointerForest /-! # Lemma 5: merging the circuits into one Eulerian circuit (combinatorial core) Frei, Gelles, Ghazy, Nolin, PODC 2026, §2.2, Lemma 5. We formalize the circuit-level effect of the merging phases (not the pulse-level protocol that implements them). A *state* is a pairing `π` together with a leader map `lead : G.Dart → V` such that two darts are on the same circuit iff their circuits have the same leader, and the leader of a circuit is a node of it (`LeaderInv`). Circuits are identified with their leaders (`Ldr lead`); two circuits are adjacent if they share a node (`CircAdj`). **One phase** (`PhaseStep`). Every circuit `C` determines the adjacent circuit `ptr C` whose leader has the largest ID. Every circuit `C` merges into `ptr C`, *except* the larger circuit of a mutually pointing pair (`Pointer.IsRoot`). Each merge is done by a node common to `C` and `ptr C` that re-pairs one of its pairs on (the current circuit containing) `C` with one on `ptr C`, as in Lemma 4 with two pairs (`Pairing.join`); the merges are executed one after the other, in any order and at any common nodes. The new leader of a merged circuit is its root (the largest ID). The exception for mutual pairs is our repair of the paper's sketch, which lets *every* circuit merge into its neighbour: in a mutual pair both merges would be executed, the second one inside a single circuit, which can split it again (`DoubleMerge.lean`). Results: a phase keeps the invariant and at least halves the number of circuits (`phaseStep_spec`); a phase is possible as long as there are at least two circuits (`exists_phaseStep`, using connectivity); with a single circuit no phase is possible and that circuit is an Eulerian circuit (`single_circuit_eulerian`).
