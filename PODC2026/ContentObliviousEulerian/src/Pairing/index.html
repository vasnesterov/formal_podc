import Mathlib -- CSLib candidate: Cslib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/PortPairing.lean (port pairings = Kotzig transition systems) /-! # Port pairings and the circuits they induce Frei, Gelles, Ghazy, Nolin, *Brief Announcement: Toward Uniform Content-Oblivious Leader Election on General Graphs*, PODC 2026, §2.1. A network is a finite simple graph `G`. The ports of a node `v` are the darts `d : G.Dart` with `d.fst = v` (the dart `d = (v, w)` is the port of `v` leading to `w`). A *port pairing* (Kotzig's "transition system") partitions the ports of every node into pairs; we represent it by a fixed-point-free involution `pair` on darts that preserves the node. A pairing induces a successor map on darts, `next d = pair d.symm`: a walk entering `v` along `d` leaves `v` through the port paired with the one it entered by. `next` is a permutation, and conjugating it by `Dart.symm` inverts it. Two darts lie on the same *circuit* (`Pairing.Circ`) if they lie on the same `next`-cycle up to reversal. This is the smallest equivalence relation containing `d ~ pair d` and `d ~ d.symm` (`Circ.pair`, `Circ.symm_dart`, `Circ.closed`). The key fact (`not_sameCycle_symm`): a dart and its reversal never lie on the same `next`-cycle, so the two orientations of a circuit are two different `next`-cycles. -/ namespace
