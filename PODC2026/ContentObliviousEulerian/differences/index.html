Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Uniform Content-Oblivious Leader Election on Eulerian Graphs — PODC 2026.

Paper: F. Frei, R. Gelles, A. Ghazy, A. Nolin, Brief Announcement: Toward Uniform Content-Oblivious Leader Election on General Graphs, PODC 2026 (4 pages, proof sketches only).

Gaps in the paper's proofs (statements true)

  1. Lemma 5: mutually selecting circuits. The sketch says "every circuit C selects one neighboring circuit C' and merges into it", by a designated node of C that "locally chains its port pairing". When C and C' select each other (always the case with two circuits left), two re-pairings are applied to the same pair of circuits. The second one re-pairs two pairs of a single circuit, which can split it (DoubleMerge.double_merge_splits on K_{2,4}: merging x2y3x and x4y5x at x and then again at y gives x2y5x and x3y4x). The sketch therefore does not establish that the number of circuits halves. Repair: the larger circuit of a mutually selecting pair (Pointer.IsRoot) does not merge. Then the links form a forest (Pointer.not_linkRel_of_nodup) and every class has at least two circuits (Pointer.two_mul_card_image_top_le). Each class is led by its root, the maximum ID (Pointer.id_le_id_top_of_top_eq). Implementing the rule requires C to learn whether C' selected C (one more broadcast per phase); this is not formalized. The description is part of the proof sketch of an existential lemma ("There is an algorithm …"), and a repaired algorithm exists. Read as a correctness claim about a specific algorithm, the §2.2 prose is under-specified rather than wrong: whether the double merge splits depends on the labelling of the pairs, which the paper leaves "w.l.o.g." → 🔧

  2. Lemma 5: OR protocol, the leader's input is not specified. As described, the leader initiates and forwards its pulse; if the pulse completes the cycle, everyone outputs 0. Read literally (the leader is never "the first node with input 1"), this is wrong when only the leader has input 1 (OrProtocol.literal_wrong, every ring size). Counting the leader as a possible reflector is the evident intent; the gap is an under-specification of the sketch. Fixed (the leader reflects its own pulse when its input is 1), the protocol is verified for all asynchronous schedules: quiescent termination, correct OR at every node, at most 2|C| pulses, and the stated observation patterns (OrProtocol.orProc_final, orProc_totalSent_le, orProc_observations). A claim inside a proof, close to a routine detail. → 🔧

  3. Lemma 3 (not formalized; unverified assumptions on the black box). The sketch needs the ring election of Chalopin et al. to satisfy properties that the paper does not state:

    • passive relays can recognise that an election on their circuit has finished ("observed the pulse pattern of a complete ring leader-election algorithm");

    • an election on a circuit sends pulses over every edge of it (otherwise a later-activated virtual node could start a second election on an already finished circuit);

    • the pulse count is O(IDmax) per edge, not only O(n·IDmax) in total (relays stretch virtual links, so a bound in terms of the number of participants does not transfer to |C|);

    • a participant may start arbitrarily late, as long as it has not received a pulse.

  4. Max-ID by ORs (not formalized). "Encodes this identifier in prefix-free form … sequence of OR protocols applied to the bits": this computes the lexicographically largest codeword. It is the largest ID only for an order-preserving prefix-free code (e.g. unary length, then binary).

Imprecisions

  1. The introduction says "the maximum-ID node on the merged circuit becomes its new leader"; it is the maximum among the leaders of the merged circuits (§2.2's "last one of this chain"). These differ in general even if the ring election elects its maximum participant: in Lemma 3 a node may only relay passively on a circuit and then does not take part in its election.

  2. "Eulerian network" is defined as "all nodes have even degree"; connectivity is implicit. We assume G.Connected for Lemma 5 and Theorem 1 (needed: a disconnected graph cannot end with a single circuit).

  3. Lemma 5 claims O(log n) phases without saying why the initial number of circuits is polynomial in n. After step 3 distinct circuits have distinct leaders, so there are at most n circuits, hence at most ⌊log₂ n⌋ phases (lemma5).

Modelling choices

  1. Lemma 2. Ports are numbered 0, …, deg v − 1 and paired (0,1), (2,3), … (the paper: 1, …, deg v and (1,2), (3,4), …). A port numbering is arbitrary (PortNumbering G). "Circuit decomposition" = the partition of E into the edge sets of circuits (closed trails, Mathlib Walk.IsCircuit) that follow the pairing, including the transition at the closing node. Uniqueness = every circuit that follows the pairing has one of these edge sets (edges_of_follows). We prove "pairing exists ⇔ all degrees even" (nonempty_pairing_iff) but not the other direction of Kotzig's Lemma 1 for arbitrary decompositions ("decomposition ⇒ even degrees"), which the paper only cites.

  2. Lemma 4. Stated for any k + 1 ≥ 1 pairs at one node on pairwise distinct circuits. The pairs can be any pairs of the node on those circuits, not necessarily the ones on which the node ran the election. The merging statement is the equivalence newCirc d d' ↔ oldCirc d d' ∨ (d, d' both on C₀ ∪ … ∪ C_k), plus the edge-set union (lemma4_edges), the unchanged circuits (lemma4_other) and locality (repair_pair_of_fst_ne). "In which v is the leader" is bookkeeping (stage3_leaderInv). "Concatenation" is shown as edge-set union; that the merged circuit is a single closed trail is circuitWalk of the new pairing.

  3. Step 3 (Lemma 4 at all nodes). Nodes are processed one after the other (Stage3, in any order). The re-pairings of different nodes involve disjoint sets of circuits, so this is equivalent to doing them simultaneously.

  4. Lemma 5 at circuit level. Merges are executed one after another (execLinks), in any order and at any node common to the two circuits. Each merge re-pairs one pair of the current circuit containing C with one pair of the current circuit containing C', i.e. Lemma 4 with two pairs. Simultaneous local re-pairings at different nodes commute, so any concurrent execution corresponds to some order. Phase synchronisation, orientation agreement and the OR/max-ID protocols are abstracted away; a phase is a relation PhaseStep that quantifies over all these choices.

  5. Theorem 1. Formalized only as (a) the circuit-level composition of the four steps with Lemma 3 as a black box elect (theorem1_circuit_level): termination after ≤ log₂ n phases with a single Eulerian circuit and a single leader; and (b) the pulse arithmetic (theorem1_pulses). The latter assumes per-circuit black-box costs c₁|C|·IDmax for the elections and c₂|C|(log₂ IDmax + 1) per phase, and derives ≤ (c₁ + 4c₂)|E|(IDmax + 1), using n ≤ IDmax + 1 and (log₂ m + 1)² ≤ 4m. "Uniform" and "deterministic" are properties of the unformalized pulse-level algorithm.

  6. OR protocol. On an oriented ring (the paper first agrees on an orientation) with the leader at node 0; inputs are per virtual node. The paper's "first node with input 1 reflects it" is implemented as: a node with input 1 reflects the first clockwise pulse it receives.
