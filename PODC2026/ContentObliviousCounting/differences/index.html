Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Efficient Counting and Simulation in Content-Oblivious Rings — PODC 2026.

Paper: J. Chalopin, Y.-J. Chang, G. A. Di Luna, H. Zhou, Efficient Counting and Simulation in Content-Oblivious Rings, PODC 2026; full version arXiv 2603.28260v1 (numbering follows it).

Classification: the labels ❌ 🔧 ✏️ are those of the README · [gap] unproved step, not counted as a mistake · [under-spec] under-specified definition · [typo] typo · [model] modelling choice of the formalization.

Model

  • [model] Content-oblivious oriented ring on ZMod n for every n ≥ 1 (n = 1: self-loop, n = 2: double link), from Cslib/Computability/Distributed/ContentOblivious/Model.lean. Channels are pulse counters (pulses are indistinguishable, so this is the paper's "FIFO w.l.o.g."). The adversary delivers any pulse in transit to a node that is willing to read that port; a blocking receive on a specific port = the node reads only that port, receive on port q (any) = both ports. Nodes are deterministic; all start at time 0; inputs (leader flag, bits, messages, IDs) via Prog I.

  • [model] "Terminates / quiescently terminates" is stated finitely: a bound on the pulses of every execution (so every execution is finite, exists_final) and properties of every final configuration (no step possible): every node has returned and no pulse is in transit.

  • [model] Theorem 9 / Definition 8 (composable ending property) are replaced by a semantic condition Proc.Composable (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/ContentOblivious/Compose.lean): bounded + quiescent termination + clean (a node that has not returned and is ready to read from a neighbour that has returned has a pulse of the current procedure in transit). Definition 8 implies clean. We prove a generalization of Theorem 9 for arbitrary sequences of stages chosen by the nodes' local states (Cslib.ContentOblivious.Schedule.final, .totalSent_le, .composable), which is what Algorithms 5, 6 and 10 and the simulation of Theorem 14 need (loops of procedures).

Section 4–6 (composition, ComputeOR, bit sending, message exchange, simulation)

  • ❌ Lemma 16 / Theorem 17: CCWBitRelay with a relay leader does not have the composable ending property (the paper explicitly covers this case: Lemma 16 "If the leader is a CCWBitRelay…", Algorithm 8 lines 2–3, 6). After forwarding the termination wave the relay leader keeps receiving on any port (Algorithm 8, line 5), i.e. it waits on the wave's port d, which Definition 8 forbids. Its clockwise neighbour returns first and may already send pulses of the next procedure into that port; the relay leader consumes them as pulses of the current round. Exhaustive check (modelcheck/t_relaycomp.py, t_pairs.py): two consecutive CCW rounds (or two CW rounds) with a relay leader on n = 3 give wrong outputs; with an active leader every pair of procedures composes correctly, and alternating CW/CCW rounds (as in MsgExchange) also happen to work with a relay leader. Theorem 14 assumes an active leader and is unaffected; Theorem 22 as stated (and §9.2, whose relays may include the leader) covers a relay leader, and its proof relies on this false composability. Lean, composed outputs: BitSending.relay_leader_composition_wrong (n = 3, relay leader 0, active 1 and 2, two CCW rounds; a 16-step execution ends final and quiescent with process 2 holding [0, ⊥] instead of [⊥, 0]). Lean: BitSending.not_composable_relay_leader (¬ proc.Composable (oriented 2) xRelay: node 0 relay leader, node 1 active with ⊥; the witness configuration cRelay is reached in three steps) and BitSending.cw_not_composable_relay_leader. A single round with a relay leader is still correct (outputs, quiescent termination, ≤ 6n pulses: BitSending.final_spec, returns, totalSent_le hold for any leader position).

  • [gap] Theorem 22 with a relay leader. Theorem 14 makes the leader active, Theorem 22 does not say so. Its proof composes the rounds via Theorem 9, which needs the (false, see above) composable ending of Theorem 17 for a relay leader. Random-schedule testing of Algorithms 5–6 with a relay leader (n ≤ 6, messages ≤ 3 bits) found no error — the alternation CW/CCW/ComputeOR happens to be safe — but this case has no proof; we prove Theorem 22 with an active leader.

  • [gap] Lemma 16 (a maximal relay segment is "equivalent to a single link") is asserted without proof. The formalization does not use it: outputs, quiescent termination and the 6n bound of Theorem 17 are proved directly for arbitrary relay sets.

  • [under-spec] Algorithm 7 does not say what is returned when in ≥ 3; this never happens (in ≤ 2).

  • [typo] Algorithm 5, comments of lines 4, 5, 11, 12: "⊥ if phase > len(msg)" should be ≥.

  • [model] Algorithms 5–6: we store the received values including ⊥ and decode by dropping ⊥ (List.filterMap id); the paper's bitencode is not specified.

  • [model] Theorem 14: a synchronous ring algorithm is a SyncAlg (state → messages to the two neighbours; state × received messages → new state) run for a number of rounds T known to all processes; relays have no messages. The cost bound is explicit: round k costs at most 15·n·b_k + 3·n pulses, b_k = longest message of round k (Theorem 22's 15nL + 3n).

Section 5 (anonymous counting)

  • 🔧 Distance formula (p. 9). For processes counted in a completed phase (myPhase < i) the paper's d = myPhase(myPhase+1)/2 − k − 1 is off by one: the correct value is myPhase(myPhase+1)/2 − k. Also the original leader has myPhase undefined (it is never probed), so the formula does not apply to it (its distance is 0). Counterexample: n = 2, the non-leader gets d = 0 (the leader's distance) instead of 1. The model checker confirms the corrected formula for all n ≤ 40. Lean: AnonCounting.paperResult_off_by_one (the formula yields d − 1 for every process with myPhase < i), paperResult_wrong (for every n ≥ 2 the clockwise neighbour of the leader computes 0 instead of 1), paperResult_two (n = 2), paperResult_leader (undefined at the original leader). The algorithm itself is correct with the corrected formula corrDist (anonCount_final).

  • [model] Under-specified pseudo-code, fixed by choice: SndCount sends the bits of count least significant first and nothing for count = 0; the leader terminates after SndCount (its "return n"); myPhase is none until written. The counting result does not depend on these.

  • ✏️ Lemma 10 calls items (4)–(5) "immediate corollaries", and its premise says p₀ has counted = false (it has counted = true; p₁ … p_i are the uncounted ones). The formalization proves the full per-phase structure for every reachable configuration (reach_inv) and reads off (4)–(5) at the end (final_data; relayed is one larger there because of the completion probe).

  • [model] Theorem 13 / 4 with explicit constants: every execution sends exactly totalPulses n ≤ 6n⌊√(2n)⌋ + 8n ≤ 12n⌊√n⌋ + 14n pulses (totalPulses_le, totalPulses_le_sqrt; exact values for n = 1..9: 6, 18, 34, 48, 70, 98, 104, 134, 170, totalPulses_small).

  • [typo] Theorem 4 / §5: d ∈ [0, n] should be [0, n−1].

  • ✏️ Lemmas 11–12 index the last phase as ⌈n^0.5⌉; the number of phases is the i with i(i−1)/2 + 1 ≤ n < i(i+1)/2 + 1, i.e. about √(2n) (e.g. n = 40 has 9 phases, ⌈√40⌉ = 7). The asymptotic bound is unaffected (lastPhase_spec, lastPhase_forty).

Section 9.1 (minimum finding)

  • ✏️ Algorithm 10, line 8: the bit loop runs while phase < len(bits) with the process's own length, so processes with longer inputs would call ComputeOR more often than the others and the calls would no longer match. The text says inputs are "represented as a bit string of equal length B"; we loop while phase < B.

  • [under-spec] Algorithm 10 needs at least one participant (otherwise the length search never stops) and non-empty encodings (|0| = 1). In §9.2 the first is guaranteed by the ComputeOR(eligible) guard.

  • [model] Theorem 5 is proved with the explicit bound 6·n·|xmin| pulses, |x| = ⌊log₂ x⌋ + 1 (the paper's count 2B calls of ComputeOR, 3n pulses each).

Section 8 (aggregation, Theorem 1, Corollary 2)

  • 🔧 λ-preprocessing (proof of Theorem 1). With the input "identifier length is at most i" and "stop at the first false outcome" the loop stops at i = 1 unless some ID has one bit, and "the largest index with outcome true" then does not exist. We use ComputeOR(size(ID) ≥ i), stop at the first false outcome, and set λ = i − 1.

  • ✏️ Self-decomposability. "f(X₁ ⊎ X₂) = f(X₁) ⊕ f(X₂) for disjoint multisets", read literally, excludes counting ({1} and {1} are not disjoint); what is meant is disjoint sets of processes. We assume f(X₁ + X₂) = f(X₁) ⊕ f(X₂) for all nonempty multisets (this is what is used); neither associativity nor commutativity of ⊕ is needed.

  • [model] Lemma 26 is proved (not assumed) for a Cole–Vishkin variant: colour reduction with multi-bit colour messages for λ rounds (message widths w ↦ size(2w−1), total ≤ 5λ bits), colours < 8, then 8 MIS rounds with the leader first (no 6→3 reduction), one round to learn the neighbours' membership and one aggregation round; virtual rings of 2 processes are covered. The cost is the same O(λ) per process as the paper's λ + O(log λ) + … CONGEST[1] rounds.

  • [under-spec] Aggregation step. The paper does not say which MIS neighbour a leaf joins; a leaf joins its clockwise virtual neighbour if it is in the MIS, else its counter-clockwise one. Values are sent as true :: enc w so that empty encodings are unambiguous.

  • [model] Dissemination: neither of the paper's two options; the leader broadcasts the bits of the result with two ComputeORs per bit (3n(2L+1) pulses, O(nβ) like SndCount/RcvCount).

  • [model] The number of phases is proved ≤ ⌊log₂ n⌋ (an MIS of a cycle of m ≥ 2 nodes has ≤ m/2 nodes); explicit bound for Theorem 1: 3n(λ + 2⌊log₂ n⌋ + 2β + 3) + ⌊log₂ n⌋·(78nλ + 15nβ + 180n) pulses; Corollary 2 with IDs of at most C(⌊log₂ n⌋+1) bits: (81C + 216)·n·(⌊log₂ n⌋+1)² pulses.

  • [under-spec] §9.2 runs the counting algorithm "on the virtual ring induced by the active set", which need not contain the leader, while Theorem 14 requires an active leader; the complexity paragraph (sum of indicator bits over the whole ring) avoids this. Theorem 6 is not formalized.

Section 10 (lower bound)

  • [model] Theorem 3, "w.l.o.g. the arc is clockwise". Not a real gap: Lemma 28 is direction-agnostic. The formalization avoids the mirror case anyway by choosing the sparse left edge among the ⌊n/2⌋ edges just clockwise after the leader and using clockwise intervals only. Constants: n·⌊log₂ n⌋ ≤ 14·M + 4·n (and n·⌊log₂ n⌋ ≤ 28·M for n ≥ 2^10).

  • 🔧 Lemma 28's proof. "Replay O verbatim" cannot be literal: O contains events of I₂ \ I₁ (absent from R'), and I₁'s own events must be rescheduled relative to the outside. Replaced by a rigorous crossing-sequence merge (Cslib.ContentOblivious.splice).

  • [under-spec] Lemma 28, "same local history". We prove equal final local states and pulse counters for all nodes of R' (also those of I₁). The hypothesis "leader ∉ I₂" is not needed for the lemma, only in Theorem 3.

  • [model] Theorem 3 is proved per execution: every execution in which the leader has an output sends Ω(n log n) pulses (only safety of counting on all rings is assumed, no termination). The paper's form ("has an execution") follows when the leader outputs in every final configuration (theorem3).

  • [typo] I_t = (v, …, v+t) has t+1 processes; the formalization indexes by the number of processes.
