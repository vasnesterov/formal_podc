A synchronous algorithm `A` on the virtual ring of the active processes sends, in every round, a bit string to each of its two neighbours and updates its state from the two strings it receives. The content-oblivious simulation runs one message exchange (Algorithms 5–6) per simulated round: a staged program whose stages are themselves staged programs (nested composition). We show that after `T` rounds every active process holds exactly the state of the synchronous execution, that the execution is quiescently terminating and composable, and that simulating a round in which all messages have at most `b` bits costs at most `15·n·b + 3·n` pulses, i.e. `O(b)` pulses per process.
