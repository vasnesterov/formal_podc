It does not matter how you define LCLs — PODC 2026

Antonio Cruciani, Avinandan Das, Alesya Raevskaya, Jukka Suomela. Brief Announcement: It Does Not Matter How You Define Locally Checkable Labelings. PODC 2026. DOI 10.1145/3796701.3815923. Formalized version: the full version arXiv:2602.18188v1 (40 pages).

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. Theorem 5.1, the graph-theoretic key of the reduction from LCLs to PN-checkable problems, is false as stated, and the version we prove needs edge-consistency for all pairs of vertices, which a PN-view verifier cannot enforce. So the main Theorem 7.1, which relies on it through Lemmas 5.4 and 5.8, is unestablished, though not refuted: a local repair with a larger r_D is plausible but not proved. Section 6 (PN-checkable → RE formalism, Lemmas 6.2–6.6) is correct up to slips.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 5.1 (graph-theoretic key of the reduction to PN-checkable problems; also Lemma 5.13 and Corollaries 5.11, 5.14, 5.16; used by Lemma 5.4, hence Lemma 5.8 and Theorem 7.1)

The hypothesis compares colours only with the centre's colour inside an r-gball, but the conclusion claims all of gball_r(v) is injectively coloured, and two vertices of the gball at gadgeted distance up to 2r can share a colour: in the gadgeted K_{3,3} with r = 1, two original vertices of the same side may (theorem_5_1_false).

Impact. Lemma 5.4 (soundness of the lift B → D) invokes the false theorem, so Lemma 5.8 and the main Theorem 7.1 inherit the gap and are unestablished, though not refuted. Theorem 5.1 becomes true once edge-consistency is required for all pairs of vertices; we prove that repaired version in full, with the paper's proof strategy (theorem_5_1_repaired). But in the application (Lemma 5.4) consistency is only checked inside PN-views of radius r_D. We see no reason to doubt that these results hold with the repaired theorem and a larger r_D, but this part is not formalized.

Details

Condition 2 ("edge-consistent in every r-gball") requires: for every edge {v,u} and every v' ∈ gball_r(v) with χ(v') = χ(v), v' has a neighbour of colour χ(u). The hypothesis never compares a pair at gadgeted distance up to 2r.

Counterexample: base graph K_{3,3}, edges expanded into 1, …, 9 A-gadgets (an injective, hence distance-2r, edge colouring), r = 1. Take an injective colouring and give the two original vertices 0 and 1 the same colour. They are at distance 2 in K_{3,3}, so at gadgeted distance 2 > r and graph distance > 2. Hence conditions 1 and 2 hold. Both lie in the 1-gball of the original vertex 3. (theorem_5_1_false)

The paper's proof establishes only that the colour of the centre v and of its outer neighbours is unique in gball_r(v) (Corollary 5.14, Lemma 5.13). Corollary 5.16 then concludes uniqueness for every pair, which does not follow. Even those partial claims fail at the boundary of the gball:

  • Lemma 5.13 is false as stated: in the gadgeted K_{3,3} with r = 1, the original vertex 1 may get the colour of the outer vertex v of the expanded edge {0,3} next to 3 (lemma_5_13_false). The idea is the same: 1 ∈ gball_1(3), but 1 and v are at gadgeted distance 3/2 > 1, so the hypothesis never compares them. The lemma is a step of the paper's proof of Theorem 5.1, whose repaired version we prove separately.

  • Corollary 5.14 fails too, by a SAT experiment that is not Lean-checked: in the gadgeted K_4 with lengths 1, …, 6 and r = 1, a SAT search found a colouring satisfying conditions 1 and 2 in which two adjacent original vertices get the same colour. The proof of 5.14 applies Lemma 5.13 to a vertex u' that may lie outside gball_r(v). Nothing here rests on this experiment.

Repair

If edge-consistency holds for all pairs of vertices, then every gball_r(v) is injectively coloured. This uses x as a distance-2r edge colouring with positive lengths; simplicity and 3-regularity are not needed (theorem_5_1_repaired). The proof follows the paper's ideas, made precise:

  • An original vertex never shares a colour with a vertex in a triangle (Lemma 5.2/5.9; the Figure 2 case analysis).

  • Colours propagate rigidly along expanded edges, so matched edges have equal length (Lemmas 5.12–5.13).

  • The distance-2r edge colouring then forces two equally coloured vertices of a gball onto the same expanded edge, read in opposite directions.

  • That is impossible: at the middle of the edge, two vertices at distance ≤ 2 would get the same colour.

This does not save the local form of the theorem: Problem D would need a view radius large enough to cover every pair the argument compares; see differencies.md.

✏️ Slips

  • Gadgeting (Section 5.1): x : E(G) → ℕ allows length 0, which the gadget construction cannot handle; we require positive lengths.

  • Lemma 6.2 silently needs every view of C_D to have a root with 3 children. This holds for PN-views of 3-regular graphs.

  • Proof of Lemma 6.4: (T(v→u)) ≅ (T(v∖u)) should read (T(u∖v)).

Typos

  • The remark "only original vertices are non-triangle vertices" is attributed to Lemma 5.2, but it holds by construction.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • No distributed model is formalized. The checked results are about problem definitions, graphs and colourings. Round complexities, symmetry breaking ♢(M) and the simulations of Section 3 are not modelled.

  • Section 5: a base graph is given by an edge type with endpoint maps (Base), so multigraphs are allowed and edges have an orientation that is used only to lay out the chain. len is the paper's edge colouring x. gadget(G, x) is Base.graph on original vertices and chain positions gad e k, k < 6·len e.

    • The paper's A-gadget vertices 1, 2, 3, 5, 4, 6 become positions 0, …, 5, so the chain is a path plus chords and reading it backwards is k ↦ 6L−1−k.

    • Gadgeted distance uses doubled 0/1 weights.

    • "Distance-k edge colouring" means line-graph distance ≤ k ⇒ different colours (the paper does not define it).

    • Conditions 1 and 2 of Theorem 5.1 are IsDist2Coloring and IsEdgeConsistentIn r, taken literally. Our repaired hypothesis is IsEdgeConsistent.

  • Section 6: PN-views up to isomorphism are canonical forms View L k (label plus multiset of child views). pnView G σ r v is the tree of non-backtracking walks. An RE problem is a pair of multiset constraints on the half-edge labels around a node and along an edge, on a 3-regular simple graph. Outcomes are PMFs over labellings.
