Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of It does not matter how you define LCLs — PODC 2026.

Paper: Cruciani, Das, Raevskaya, Suomela, It does not matter how you define locally checkable labelings, arXiv:2602.18188v1 (full version of the PODC 2026 brief announcement).

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 5.1 is false as stated (❌) (theorem_5_1_false). Gadgeted K_{3,3}, lengths 1..9, r = 1, colouring injective except χ(0) = χ(1) (two original vertices on the same side). The hypotheses of the paper hold literally: K_{3,3} is 3-regular and simple, and the injective colouring is a distance-2r edge colouring under any definition. Condition 1 holds since the vertices are at graph distance ≥ 3. Condition 2 holds since they are at gadgeted distance 2 > r. Yet 0, 1 ∈ gball_1(3).

    • r = 1, K_{3,3} is not degenerate. The fix changes a hypothesis of the theorem, its proof reasons with the stated version, and the proof also misses the pairs not involving the centre (Corollary 5.16 does not follow from 5.13–5.15). The abstract and title are about Theorem 7.1, not Theorem 5.1, but Theorem 7.1 depends on it (item 4).

    • Reading checked on the rendered page (p. 26): condition 2 is spelled out as "for every vertex v' ∈ gball_r(v) such that χ(v') = χ(v) …". An alternative reading of the name "edge-consistent in every r-gball" is "for any two vertices of a common r-gball (of any vertex)". Under it, SAT checks on small instances found no counterexample: K_4 and K_{3,3} at r = 1 are UNSAT; Q_3 at r = 1 is UNSAT; Prism_6 and Petersen were inconclusive. We judge the explicit definition.

    • The colours are χ : Vert → C for an arbitrary type C (the paper: χ : V(G) → ℕ). The counterexample uses C = Vert, a finite type, so it transfers to ℕ by any injection.

  2. Lemma 5.13 is false as stated (lemma_5_13_false). Same instance; the original vertex 1 gets the colour of the outer vertex of the expanded edge {0,3} at 3. Part of the ❌ of item 1.

  3. Corollary 5.14 fails (SAT experiment only, not Lean-checked). Gadgeted K_4, lengths 1..6, r = 1: a colouring satisfying conditions 1 and 2 with two adjacent original vertices of the same colour exists. Its proof uses Lemma 5.13 for a vertex u' outside gball_r(v). Lemmas 5.2 and 5.9 and Corollaries 5.10–5.11 were also tested under the literal hypotheses:

    • Corollary 5.11 has SAT counterexamples (K_4, K_{3,3}).

    • Lemma 5.9 is UNSAT on K_4.

    • Lemma 5.2 and Lemma 5.12 were inconclusive or UNSAT.

    These are experiments only; no finding here rests on them.

  4. Theorem 7.1 (the main theorem) is unestablished, not refuted. Its proof goes A → B → D → E. The step B → D (Lemmas 5.4, 5.8) uses Theorem 5.1. With our repaired Theorem 5.1 the step plausibly goes through, if Problem D uses a large enough view radius (item 8), but this is not formalized. It is covered by the ❌ of item 1.

Repaired Theorem 5.1 (Base.theorem_5_1_repaired)

  1. Hypothesis strengthened. Condition 2 is required for all pairs of vertices (IsEdgeConsistent), not only for v' ∈ gball_r(v).

    • The proof compares only vertices on expanded edges that meet gball_r(v), together with their far endpoints and neighbours. So a version with consistency up to gadgeted distance about 2r + 2 should suffice. This local version is not formalized.

    • SAT checks with consistency up to gadgeted distance 2r were UNSAT: K_4 and K_{3,3} at r = 1, Q_3 at r = 2.

  2. Hypotheses weakened.

    • The base graph may have loops and parallel edges and need not be 3-regular.

    • Lengths must be positive. The paper's x : E → ℕ allows 0, for which "a sequence of 0 gadgets" is meaningless.

    • "Distance-k edge colouring" (undefined in the paper) is taken as: distinct edges at line-graph distance ≤ k (endpoints at base distance ≤ k − 1) get distinct colours.

  3. Proof. It follows the paper's architecture, but each step is proved where the paper argues with figures or "by symmetry".

    • Local core (Lemma 5.2 / Figure 2, Local.lean): the vertex before a gadget never shares its colour with the gadget's paper-vertices 4/5.

    • Lemma 5.9 / Corollary 5.10: originals never share colours with gadget vertices.

    • Lemmas 5.12/5.13: rigid propagation along expanded edges, gadget by gadget, up to the 2 ↔ 3, 4 ↔ 5 symmetry of the A-gadget. This gives equal lengths and equally coloured far ends.

    • Conclusion via the distance-2r edge colouring: two equally coloured vertices of a gball lie on the same expanded edge, matched with itself, either identically or backwards. Backwards fails at the middle gadget: for odd length, paper-vertex 2 gets the colour of 4 or 5; for even length, two adjacent vertices 6 — 1 get the same colour. In the paper this case is covered by Lemma 5.12 (a gadget sequence is injectively coloured), whose proof by minimal counterexample is sketchy; the final sentence of Lemma 5.13 ("no 2 sequences have the same length in the r-gneighborhood") covers different edges.

Not formalized / informal parts of the paper (reviewed, not checked in Lean)

  1. Problem D.

    • Reconstructibility ("for every two vertices labelled the same, the labels of their neighbours are also the same") is checked inside a PN-view, a tree whose leaves have only one neighbour in the view. Read literally, it fails whenever a leaf shares a colour with an internal node. The intended meaning (neighbours in G of vertices whose neighbourhood is visible) has to be assumed.

    • With the repaired Theorem 5.1, the view radius r_D = (4k+1) r_B + 1 must also cover all pairs compared by the argument (item 5). This is a change of constants in the problem definition.

    • k (the colour bound of x) must bound the lengths used; this is implicit.

  2. Section 4.

    • The decoded instance of an arbitrary 3-regular graph is a multigraph, possibly with vertices of degree 3 > d, while A is an LCL on simple graphs of maximum degree d (Lemma 4.3 runs A on it). The introduction says an LCL can declare such nodes unconstrained, but always-solvability of that extension is not argued.

    • An isolated vertex of G becomes a "chordless cycle" of length 2 (a double edge).

    • "WLOG every element of C_B has radius exactly r_B".

  3. Lemmas 4.6, 5.8, 6.7, Theorem 7.1. These are complexity statements over unspecified models M with Θ/Ω notation. Not modelled.

  4. Lemma 5.3 / 4.1 (uniqueness of encoding). Not formalized (decode is not defined for Section 5).

Section 6 (formalized; correct up to the following)

  1. Typo. Proof of Lemma 6.4: (T(v→u)) ≅ (T(v∖u)) should be (T(u∖v)) (the paper's own Remark).

  2. Hidden assumption. Lemma 6.2 needs every view in C_D to have exactly three root children; we add it to C_V. Such views are the only ones that occur on 3-regular graphs, so nothing is lost.

  3. Modelling.

    • PN-views up to isomorphism are canonical forms (View).

    • ζ (neighbour enumeration of a view) is a fixed Multiset.toList order; γ is chosen by choice.

    • Σ_E = View × Fin 3 instead of C_D × [3]; only labels with first component in C_D are accepted.

    • Radius written r + 1 ≥ 1.

    • The second conjunct of I_fit is redundant, since C_E is a set of unordered pairs.

  4. Lemmas 6.5/6.6 are proved for PMF outcomes. Locality of outcomes is not modelled. Lemmas 4.4, 4.5, 5.6, 5.7 are the same argument (SolvesWithProb.map). Their soundness premises (Lemmas 4.2, 4.3, 5.4, 5.5) are not formalized.
