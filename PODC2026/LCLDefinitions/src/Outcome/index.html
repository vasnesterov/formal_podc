import Papers.PODC2026.LCLDefinitions.REFormalism /-! # Outcomes and the transfer of success probability (Lemmas 4.4, 4.5, 5.6, 5.7, 6.5, 6.6) Formalization support for *"It does not matter how you define locally checkable labelings"* (Cruciani, Das, Raevskaya, Suomela; PODC 2026, arXiv 2602.18188). An *outcome* on a fixed instance is a probability distribution over output labellings (`{(σ^{out,i}, p_i)}_{i ∈ I}` in the paper); we model it as a `PMF` on the type of labellings (the pushforward of the paper's indexed family). It *solves* a problem with probability at least `q` if the probability of the set of legal labellings is at least `q`. Lemmas 4.4, 4.5, 5.6, 5.7, 6.5 and 6.6 of the paper all have the same proof: if a map (the "lift") sends legal labellings for one problem to legal labellings for the other, then the pushforward of an outcome solving the first problem with probability `≥ q` solves the second with probability `≥ q`. This is `SolvesWithProb.map`. We instantiate it for Lemmas 6.5 and 6.6 with the lifts of Lemmas 6.3 and 6.4. The locality (non-signaling / dependency radius) of the outcomes is not modelled here. -/ namespace Podc.LCLDefinitions -- CSLib candidate (PN-views / outcome transfer are reusable beyond this paper) SolvesWithProb {α : Type*} (O : PMF α) (legal : Set α) (q : ℝ≥0∞) : Prop := q ≤ O.toOuterMeasure legal /-- **Probability transfer** (the common argument of Lemmas 4.4, 4.5, 5.6, 5.7, 6.5, 6.6): if the lift `f` maps legal labellings for `A` to legal labellings for `B` ("the lift is sound"), and the outcome `O` solves `A` with probability at least `q`, then the outcome obtained by sampling from `O` and applying the lift solves `B` with probability at least `q`. -/ theorem SolvesWithProb.map {α β : Type*} {O : PMF α} {A : Set α} {B : Set β} {q : ℝ≥0∞} (hO : SolvesWithProb O A q) (f : α → β) (hf : ∀ x ∈ A, f x ∈ B) : SolvesWithProb (O.map f) B q := by unfold SolvesWithProb at * rw [PMF.toOuterMeasure_map_apply] exact hO.trans (O.toOuterMeasure.mono fun x hx => hf x hx) section Section6 variable
