import Mathlib /-! # PN-views (Section 2.1 of the paper) Formalization support for *"It does not matter how you define locally checkable labelings"* (Cruciani, Das, Raevskaya, Suomela; PODC 2026, arXiv 2602.18188). The radius-`r` PN-view `T^r_G(v)` of a node `v` is the tree of non-backtracking walks of length `≤ r` starting at `v`, labelled by the label of the last node of the walk; the paper considers it up to isomorphism of labelled rooted trees. ## Modelling choice We represent a PN-view directly by its **canonical isomorphism type**: a labelled rooted tree of depth `≤ k` with unordered children is * a label, if `k = 0`; * a root label together with the *multiset* of the (depth `≤ k - 1`) subtrees of its children, if `k > 0`. This is the type `View L k`. Two labelled rooted trees (of depth `≤ k`) are isomorphic iff their canonical forms are equal, so the paper's `≅` between PN-views becomes `=` here. Shallow leaves are represented by empty child multisets. ## Main definitions * `View L k`, `View.label`, `View.trunc` (drop the deepest level), `View.prune a c` (the paper's `(T, v \ w)`: remove one child branch `c` of the root and truncate). * `branch σ k p u`: the PN-view of depth `k` of the non-backtracking walks that enter `u` from `p` (the paper's `T^r_G(p → u)` for `k = r - 1`). * `pnView σ r v`: the radius-`r` PN-view `T^r_G(v)`. * `PNValid C σ`: validity of a solution of the PN-checkable problem given by `C`. ## Main results * `trunc_branch`, `trunc_pnView`: truncation commutes with taking views. * `branch_eq_prune_pnView`: the Remark of Section 6, `T(v → u) ≅ T(u \ v)`. * `branch_unique`: views of branches are the unique solution of their defining recursion (the core of the induction in the proof of Lemma 6.2). -/ namespace Podc.LCLDefinitions -- CSLib candidate (PN-views / outcome transfer are reusable beyond this paper) universe u w /-- Canonical forms of labelled rooted trees of depth `≤ k` with unordered children: a depth-`0` tree is its root label, a depth-`(k+1)` tree is a root label together with the multiset of its children's depth-`k` subtrees.
