Forget-IT — differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Forget-IT — PODC 2026.

Paper: proceedings version, PODC 2026 (Algorithms 1–3, §3 definitions, §4.2 proof sketch). The full version [3] was not available.

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 3, robust good-case latency 3δ — false (❌). Definition (§3): F_rgc is the set of f-bounded adversaries "for which GST coincides with a correct leader entering its view"; T_rgc = sup DL, with DL = T − GST. Counterexample RobustLatency.robust_latency_counterexample: n = 4, f = 1, leaders leader(V) = V − 1 mod 4, process 0 Byzantine (leader of view 1), process 1 correct (leader of view 2), Δ = 2 ticks, GST = 21. Process 1 enters view 2 in the step at GST, but no correct process decides before GST + 14. The decision comes at GST + 14, in view 3. In paper time one message delay after GST is one tick (δ = 1 ≤ Δ). Cause: processes 2 and 3 entered view 2 before GST and executed line 65 (vc[2] ← true), so line 37 forbids them to vote for the leader's proposal.

    • Every hypothesis of the paper's model holds in the run. All processes start at time 0 (by GST). Clocks do not drift. There are no crashes. Messages before GST are delayed (asynchrony) and none is lost. After GST every message is delivered in one tick. Process 0 sends only echo-1/2/3(1, SAFE) to processes 2 and 3. Process 1's entry is an active entry (line 80) and it broadcasts its prepare.

    • Why this is not a local fix: the fix changes the family of executions over which the metric is taken, i.e. the content of the claim. The paper also motivates robust latency precisely by "brief periods of asynchrony", which is the situation here. The proof sketch's "first view V after GST … each correct process enters view V (line 85)" suggests the intended reading "the whole view starts after GST". That version is not proved here, and it also needs δ < Δ (or tie-breaking in the protocol's favour): when δ = Δ, the 3Δ timer of the first process to enter the view (line 45) may fire exactly when the vote-1 quorum arrives, and that process then never locks.

    • Scope: the title and the abstract claim good-case latency 3 rounds. That is the non-robust notion, the one Theorem 1 states, and it is true (good_case_latency). The robust variant comes in the introduction as "in fact, our main result obtains an even stronger notion". It is the paper's distinguishing claim over TetraBFT (Table 1).

    • About δ: the responsiveness paragraph defines δ(E) as the maximum delay over all messages of the execution. Under that reading the pre-GST delays to process 1 (up to 21 ticks) would make 3δ(E) large. That reading contradicts the partial-synchrony paragraph ("δ ≤ Δ the actual message delay after GST", bounds relative to max(τ, GST)), which we follow. With clock drift before GST, which the paper allows, the same scenario can be made with small delays for every message (not formalized).

    • Reviewer note (informal, not formalized): the claim also fails under the δ(E) reading, through crash–recovery, which the paper allows before GST. Take the same setting. Processes 2 and 3 enter view 2. They crash and recover at once, and line 53 sets vc[2] = true. Process 1 enters view 2 one message delay later, and that time is GST. All message delays are at most some small d, so δ(E) ≤ d. Let process 0 be silent from then on. Line 37 again blocks every vote-1 of 2 and 3 in view 2. The view change of view 2 needs a third vote-2 for view 2, and only process 1 can send it, at its 3Δ timer (line 45). So no correct process decides before GST + 3Δ, and 3Δ / δ(E) is unbounded. Also, the formalized run decides at GST + 14 ticks with Δ = 2 ticks. That is more than 3Δ = 6, so even the weaker bound 3Δ fails.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. 🔧 Agreement sketch. The sketch says "at least 2f + 1 correct processes broadcast ⟨vote-2, V_dec, v_dec⟩"; only f + 1 correct ones are guaranteed. That suffices: |S| + |L| = (2f + 1) + (f + 1) > n (base). The sketch also argues as if all correct processes had entered view V_dec + 1 before anything happens there. In asynchronous runs, the last locks in V_dec and the echoes of later views interleave arbitrarily. We prove an invariant parameterised by the eventual set of lockers. If at least f + 1 correct processes send their vote-2 for V_dec (if at all) with lock v_dec, then in every reachable state: guards of views > V_dec are v_dec or unset; echo slots of views ≥ V_dec hold v_dec and no second (SAFE) echo is sent; vote-1s and vote-2 guards of views > V_dec are v_dec (Concl, concl_reach). It also needs two facts the sketch leaves implicit: the guard of the current view is never ⊥, so line 50 cannot fire spuriously, and correct vote-2s are unique per view.

  2. Non-triviality is not argued in the sketch beyond one sentence. We derive it from good-case latency plus agreement (non_triviality).

Modelling choices (abstract model, Defs.lean, Spec.lean)

  1. Views are ℕ. Entering view 1 touches views −1 and 0 (lines 32–33); both are the dummy view 0 here. Correct processes run the view change of this dummy view at start-up, which is harmless (checked by the latency proof).

  2. Persistent memory: per-view unbounded arrays instead of a window of three views (line 18). This is equivalent: every rule reads only views in {view−2, view−1, view} or sets the guard of the view it enters, and views never decrease.

  3. Timing conditions (lines 45, 65) and line 45's "non-passively entered" are dropped in the abstract model, so timers may fire at any time. The recovery procedure may run at any time. A crash is the loss of volatile state (vol step) followed by recover. All of these only add behaviours.

  4. The network: a correct process may receive any message ever broadcast by a correct process, at any time and repeatedly, and any message from a Byzantine process. Abandoned broadcasts (line 19) and lossy links only remove behaviours. Received and accepted messages may be forgotten at any time.

  5. "Upon … wait until …" (lines 35–36) is a single guarded rule; line 40's inner if vote2 = ⊥ is part of the guard. A rule may fire again while enabled (the functions send_echo_* and the = ⊥ tests make repetitions harmless).

  6. Lines 58, 61, 63 ("existence of a set S of 2f + 1 accepted messages such that …") are stated with the largest admissible set (lockSet, notPotSet), which is equivalent. Line 58 is split into two rule instances, alertSet V (some ℓ) and alertSet V none.

  7. divergent_guards (line 123) is stated as "for every guard G, num − #G ≥ f + 1". This is equivalent to the test with the most frequent guard and independent of tie-breaking.

  8. ⟨vote-2, V, lock, guard⟩ carries Option (Value ∪ {SAFE}) as guard. The guard of a correct process is never ⊥ (invariant guardV).

  9. potential_decision has type Value (the pseudocode declares Value ∪ {SAFE} but only ever assigns a lock value).

  10. Decisions: every execution of line 44 is recorded as a decision event, even a repeated one. Agreement is proved for all events. The paper's decided flag is not persistent memory.

  11. Static Byzantine set (the paper: adaptive, equivalent for deterministic protocols, footnote 2).

Modelling choices (timed model, Protocol.lean)

  1. CSLib's partially synchronous model: correct processes start at time 0, take a step every tick, and have drift-free clocks; there are no crashes, and delivery between correct processes is reliable by max(τ, GST) + δ. This is a subset of the paper's executions, so the counterexample (item 1) is valid in the paper's model. For good-case latency, GST = 0 makes the paper's model coincide with this one, except for the discrete time and the step semantics below.

  2. Local computation takes one tick. In a step a process processes the messages delivered now and runs K ≥ 1 passes over candidate rule instances. The messages it broadcasts are sent at the next tick. Hence one message delay is δ + 1 ticks, and the latency bound is 3(δ + 1). The proofs work for every K ≥ 1; rules still enabled after K passes fire in the next tick. The counterexample uses K = 1 (with K = 4 the run has the same timing but its kernel check needs too much memory). That view 2 decides nothing does not depend on K: after their timeouts processes 2 and 3 never vote in view 2 (line 37), so no correct process can lock there (line 39). The exact decision time GST + 14 does depend on the step semantics.

  3. "for some v …" (lines 50, 73–88): candidates are the values occurring in received messages plus SAFE, tried in that order (SAFE first). A broadcast reaches every process, including the sender.

  4. Timers: line 45 fires once clk − entered ≥ 3Δ and the view was entered actively; line 65 once clk − entered ≥ 4Δ. entered is reset on every view entry.

  5. The leader broadcasts prepare only if value_to_propose[V] ≠ ⊥ (never ⊥ when a leader actively enters, by lines 27, 82–83).

Not formalized

  1. Termination in general (view synchronization after GST, line 88 passive entry, constant memory), view length 7Δ, O(n²) messages per view, O(1) persistent memory, optimistic responsiveness, the external-validity and atomic-broadcast extensions.
