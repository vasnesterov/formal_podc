Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Order statistics in population protocols — PODC 2026.

Paper: D'Archivio, AlMahmoud, Natale, Mallmann-Trenn, Order Statistics in Population Protocols via Simple Dynamics, PODC 2026 (proceedings numbering; full version HAL hal-05548829 numbers Section 4 as Section 3).

A. Modelling choices (faithful, but made explicit)

  1. Opinions range over a finite linear order α instead of ℝ. All dynamics only compare and copy opinions, so a real initial configuration evolves inside its finite value set; the matrix formulation needs a finite state space.

  2. Fixation probability is formalized as lim_{t→∞} P[X_t = (x, …, x)] (finite Markov chain T, (T^t) c s); since consensus configurations are absorbing this equals the probability of eventually being in consensus on x forever. No trajectory measure is built. Existence of the limit is proved, not assumed.

  3. Ties. The paper proves its laws for distinct initial opinions and says the general case "trivially follows by summing". We prove the general statement (sum of π_k over the ranks of x, #{u | c u < x} < k ≤ #{u | c u ≤ x}) and derive the distinct-opinion statements.

  4. Weighted model (Sec. 4.2): weights w ≥ 0 with W = ∑ w > 0 (needed for the activation probabilities w/W to make sense), self-loops allowed, p ∈ (0, 1). "Connected" is connectivity of the undirected support graph {u, v} with w u v > 0 or w v u > 0 (supportGraph); for circulations this coincides with strong connectivity.

  5. Clique sampling: "sample u, v u.a.r." = independent uniform u, v, v = u allowed (as in Lemma 4.1's 1/n²).

  6. Biased-Median sampling (ambiguity). Sec. 4.5 says u "samples uniformly at random an unordered pair of agents (v, s) (v and s may be equal)". Lemma 4.8's counts 2(n-j)+1, 2j-1 (out of n²) require two independent uniform samples (ordered pairs); we formalize that reading. Under the literal reading (uniform among the n(n+1)/2 multisets) the law is different: for n = 3, p = 1/2 (Median) the fixation law is (2/7, 3/7, 2/7) instead of (1/4, 1/2, 1/4) (exact computation, script not kept in the repo; not formalized).

  7. Self-Excluding Median and 3-Majority: three independent uniform samples (Lemma 4.9's count out of n³), possibly including the activated agent. 2-Choices (Cor. 3): u samples two independent uniform agents (possibly itself) and adopts their opinion if they agree. Binary opinions are Bool with false < true (0/1).

  8. Theorem 2 range: requires p ∈ (0, 1/2] (so p/(1-p) ≤ 1 is a probability). The abstract's "binomial protocol … K - 1 ~ Bin(n-1, 1-p)" therefore only covers 1 - p ≥ 1/2; stated with this hypothesis.

  9. Converse of Proposition 6. The hypothesis is read as "the geometric law holds from every configuration with pairwise distinct opinions"; values range over a linear order with at least n elements (otherwise there are no such configurations).

B. Errors / gaps in the paper

  1. Proposition 6 (⇐), proof gap. After "relabel at every round the nodes so that node i holds X^t_(i)", the matrices P^(t) depend on the (random) configuration at time t. Equation (6), P[X^t_(i) = X^0_(j)] = (∏_s P^(s))_{j,i}, has a random right-hand side and is not justified by "similarly to Lemmas 4.3 and 4.4" (the expectation of a product of dependent random matrices is not the product of expectations); Lemma 4.7 is about a deterministic sequence. The statement is true: we prove it with the harmonic function c ↦ ∑_{k ≤ #{u : c u ≤ y}} π_k (a bounded martingale), whose harmonicity only needs that π is stationary for every possible P^(t), i.e. equal in/out flows across every cut (Lemma 4.6) — copyOrMaxW_fixation.

  2. Proposition 6 (⇒), invalid proof. The proof claims that geometric fixation probabilities imply "there exists N such that for all t ≥ N, P[X^t_(i) = X^0_(j)] = π_j" — a limit need not be attained at a finite time (already for the clique with n = 2, P[X^t_(1) = x_(1)] = π_1 + (1-π_1) r^t ≠ π_1 for every t), and again treats P^(N) as deterministic. The statement is true: copyOrMaxW_geomFix_iff. New proof: (i) threshold projection y ≤ · turns Copy-or-Max into binary Copy-or-Max (lumping), so the binary fixation probabilities are determined by the hypothesis; (ii) the binary fixation probability is harmonic, and harmonicity at the configuration with a single 0 at agent v forces in-flow(v) = out-flow(v); (iii) if the support graph were disconnected, a configuration with distinct opinions could never reach consensus.

  3. (Minor) D vs D⁻¹ slip in the proof of Proposition 6, Item iv). Lemma 4.7 itself is fine: its item iv) is an existential hypothesis ("there exists a non-singular D …"), satisfied by D = diag(1/√π_k). Only the instantiation in the proof of Proposition 6 is wrong: with D = diag(√π_k), D⁻¹ P^(t)ᵀ D is not symmetric (detailed balance π_a K_ab = π_b K_ba makes D Pᵀ D⁻¹ symmetric instead). Trivially fixable. Lean check for n = 2, p = 1/2 (Counterexample.prop6_itemIv_sqrt_slip, which also checks the corrected choice). Lemma 4.7 is not formalized (our proofs do not need it).

  4. Definition 4.2 is under-specified (Theorem 7). Definition 4.2 only constrains configurations with pairwise distinct values, but from the first opinion change on every configuration has ties, so the proof of Theorem 7 ("we can follow the proof of Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4") needs the rank structure on tied configurations too. The paper's own gloss ("P[S_ij] is a function of the ranks i and j only") shows the intended class, which is our fix below. Clarification — under the literal reading of Definition 4.2 Theorem 7 would fail: Counterexample.tieMax equals Copy-or-Max (p = 1/2) on every configuration with distinct opinions (tieMax_eq_copyOrMax; so it is Order-Based with Lemma 4.1's q_j(i) and satisfies Theorem 7's positivity hypotheses) and runs the Max dynamics on tied configurations; it converges to consensus a.s., but from (0, 1, 2) it fixes on 2 with probability 7/9 (tieMax_fixation) instead of π_3 = 4/7 (tieMax_not_geometric). Fix (intended reading): require the rank-event structure on all configurations, formalized in threshold form (OrderBased: for every lower set D holding m agents, P[an agent outside D adopts a value in D] = f_{m+1}, P[an agent in D adopts a value outside D] = h_m; this is implied by the rank-event condition with ties broken arbitrarily, and is all the proof needs). Then Theorem 7 holds (orderBased_fixation); no laziness / aperiodicity assumption is needed. Copy-or-Max, Biased-Median and Self-Excluding Median satisfy this definition with exactly the paper's f_k, h_k (Instances.lean).

  5. Theorem 1, convergence time "O(log n) parallel rounds w.h.p." (proof only in the full version, Theorem 9): not formalized. The proof is a sketch with gaps: the holding times W_s (geometric, unbounded, state-dependent) are "upper-bounded by i.i.d. Z_s" with a bounded, state-independent distribution — no such pointwise or stochastic domination holds; the "spread time evenly over ranks" step is heuristic; the restart rule "if [the new top count] is larger, consensus is only faster" is asserted without a monotonicity argument. The bound also implicitly needs p to be a constant (for p → 1 the dynamics approaches the voter model, which needs Θ(n) parallel rounds). The statement is plausible but unverified here.

  6. Fact 4.1 (examples of Order-Based dynamics) is stated without proof and not formalized; note that as written ("adopts Y_j with probability depending only on j") it covers neither Copy-or-Max nor Biased-Median (their adoption probabilities depend on the position of the agent's own value). Full-version Fact 3.2 (median on the cycle) is informal; Corollary 10 (Top-k) is an application with its own concentration analysis — both not formalized.

C. Minor issues

  1. Lemma 4.2, footnote 3: the boundary convention should be X_(k) = 0 for k = 0 or k = n + 1 (not k = n); irrelevant since f_1 = h_n = 0. Our Lemma 4.2 (orderStat_step) is the stronger distributional form and holds for all configurations (ties included), as the paper claims.

  2. Lemma 4.4 is proved in the paper for distinct initial values only; orderStat_ancestry gives the version with ties (P[X^t_(i) = x] = ∑_j (Pᵗ)_{j,i} 1[X^0_(j) = x]), for every Order-Based dynamics. The order statistic is formalized by the predicate IsOrderStat (unique, by isOrderStat_existsUnique); Lemma 4.3 is formalized for an arbitrary real valuation of the opinions (orderStat_expectation).

  3. Lemma 4.5: we formalize detailed balance (lemma45_detailed_balance) and the diagonal bound g_k ≥ (n²+1)/(2n²) (lemma45_diag_bound, valid for every p ≤ 1); irreducibility, aperiodicity and uniqueness (Perron–Frobenius) are not needed by our proof and not formalized.

  4. Full version only: Lemma 3.9 and the proof of Theorem 8 divide by n³; the correct normalisation is n⁴ (as in the proceedings). Ratios, hence the law, are unaffected.

  5. Theorem 8's law is formalized literally with Real.Gamma (sxLaw); its normalisation (sum_sxLaw) is proved via the rising-factorial Chu–Vandermonde identity; Corollary 4's Beta-Binomial pmf is betaBinomial N a b x = C(N,x) B(x+a, N-x+b)/B(a,b) with B(a,b) = Γ(a)Γ(b)/Γ(a+b).

  6. Proof route: instead of the paper's rank-ancestry + Perron–Frobenius argument (Lemmas 4.3–4.5, 4.7) the fixation laws are proved with harmonic functions of threshold counts and a finite-chain absorption lemma; the rank-ancestry lemmas are verified separately (RankAncestry.lean). The paper's final step "as there exists a sequence of events with non-zero probability leading to consensus, the probability … is π_k" is made rigorous by Cslib.Probability.Markov.tendsto_sum_compl_of_absorbing.
