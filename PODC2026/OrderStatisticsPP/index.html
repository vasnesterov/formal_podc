Order statistics in population protocols — PODC 2026

Niccolò D'Archivio, Hind AlMahmoud, Emanuele Natale, Frederik Mallmann-Trenn. Order Statistics in Population Protocols via Simple Dynamics. PODC 2026. DOI 10.1145/3796701.3815922. Formalized version: the proceedings (numbering follows it; the full version, HAL hal-05548829, numbers Section 4 as Section 3). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. All laws of the consensus value are proved, also for initial configurations with ties: Copy-or-Max (Theorem 1, Proposition 6), Biased-Median (Theorem 2, Corollary 3), Self-Excluding Median (Theorem 8, Corollary 4), and Order-Based dynamics (Theorem 7, with a repaired Definition 4.2). Both directions of Proposition 6 needed new proofs. The O(log n) convergence time stated in Theorem 1 rests on an invalid proof, and nothing we proved saves it.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 1 (the O(log n) parallel-rounds claim; full-version Theorem 9)

The deferred proof of "convergence occurs in O(log n) parallel rounds with high probability" dominates the geometric, state-dependent holding times by bounded i.i.d. variables, which is invalid.

Impact. The claim is plausible, but it is neither proved nor refuted (not formalized). The fixation laws, the paper's main content, are unaffected.

Details

The geometric, state-dependent holding times are "upper-bounded by i.i.d. Z_s" with a bounded, state-independent distribution, but no such domination holds. The restart step ("consensus is only faster") lacks a monotonicity argument. The bound implicitly needs p constant: as p → 1 the dynamics approaches the voter model, which needs Θ(n) parallel rounds.

🔧 Definition 4.2 and Theorem 7 (Order-Based dynamics)

Definition 4.2 only constrains configurations with pairwise distinct opinions, and under this literal reading Theorem 7 is false: a dynamics that is Copy-or-Max (p = 1/2) on distinct configurations but Max on tied ones fixes on 2 from (0, 1, 2) with probability 7/9 instead of π_3 = 4/7 (Counterexample.tieMax_not_geometric).

Impact. The paper's gloss ("a function of the ranks only") shows the intended class, and under it Theorem 7 holds (orderBased_fixation). All three concrete dynamics are covered.

Details

After the first copy every configuration has ties, so the definition says nothing about the states the dynamics actually visits. The instance above is Order-Based in the literal sense.

Repair

We require the rank structure on all configurations, in threshold form. Then Theorem 7 holds; Copy-or-Max, Biased-Median and Self-Excluding Median satisfy it with exactly the paper's f_k, h_k (orderBased_fixation).

✏️ Slips

  • §4.5 (Biased-Median): "an unordered pair (v, s), may be equal" must mean two independent uniform samples, as Lemma 4.8's counts require, and Theorem 2 holds under that reading (biasedMedian_fixation). With a uniform unordered pair the Median law for n = 3 is (2/7, 3/7, 2/7), not binomial (an exact computation, not formalized).

  • Proposition 6, item iv) instantiates Lemma 4.7 with D = diag(√π) where diag(1/√π) is needed (Counterexample.prop6_itemIv_sqrt_slip); Lemma 4.7 itself is fine.

  • Lemma 4.2's footnote boundary should be k = n + 1, not k = n.

Typos

  • The full version divides by n³ where n⁴ is meant (ratios unaffected).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Proposition 6 · Proposition 6

🛠️ Proposition 6 (⇐) (weighted Copy-or-Max has the geometric law)

The proof relabels the nodes by rank at every round, so the matrices P^(t) depend on the random configuration and eq. (6), P[X^t_(i) = X^0_(j)] = (∏_s P^(s))_{j,i}, has a random right-hand side.

Impact. Statement true; new proof (copyOrMaxW_fixation).

Details

Eq. (6) is not justified by "similarly to Lemmas 4.3 and 4.4", since the expectation of a product of dependent random matrices is not the product of expectations. Lemma 4.7 concerns deterministic sequences.

Repair

A bounded harmonic function of threshold counts, which only needs π to be stationary for every possible P^(t), i.e. balanced flows across every cut (copyOrMaxW_fixation).

🛠️ Proposition 6 (⇒) (the geometric law forces a connected circulation)

The proof claims that geometric fixation probabilities give an N with P[X^t_(i) = X^0_(j)] = π_j for all t ≥ N, but a limit need not be attained in finite time.

Impact. Statement true; new proof (copyOrMaxW_geomFix_iff).

Details

Already on the clique with n = 2, P[X^t_(1) = x_(1)] = π_1 + (1−π_1)r^t ≠ π_1 for every t. The proof also again treats P^(N) as deterministic.

Repair

Projecting onto a threshold reduces to binary Copy-or-Max. Harmonicity of the binary fixation probability at the configuration with a single 0 at v forces in-flow(v) = out-flow(v). Disconnection would make consensus unreachable (copyOrMaxW_geomFix_iff).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • n ≥ 1 agents, opinions in a finite linear order (a real initial configuration evolves inside its finite value set). Random sequential scheduler: one agent updates per step. Each dynamics is a finite Markov chain on configurations, given as a row-stochastic matrix built from a random map. No failures, no adversary.

  • Sampling follows the paper's counts: clique pairs (u, v) are independent and uniform (v = u allowed); weighted Copy-or-Max picks edge (u, v) with probability w u v / W; the median dynamics draw independent uniform samples; 2-Choices and 3-Majority are binary.

  • The fixation probability of x is lim_{t→∞} P[X_t = (x,…,x)]. Consensus is absorbing, so this is the probability of eventual consensus on x. The limit's existence is proved, not assumed. With ties, the probability of x is the sum of π_k over its ranks. The binomial law of Theorem 2 is Mathlib's ProbabilityTheory.binomial (binomLaw_eq_binomial: π_k = P[Bin(n-1, 1-p) = k-1]). Theorem 2 needs p ≤ 1/2, so the abstract's K − 1 ~ Bin(n−1, 1−p) covers only 1 − p ≥ 1/2.

  • Proof route differs from the paper: harmonic functions of threshold counts plus a finite-chain absorption lemma, instead of rank ancestry plus Perron–Frobenius. The paper's rank-ancestry lemmas are verified separately.
