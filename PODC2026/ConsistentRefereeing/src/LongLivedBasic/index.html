import Papers.PODC2026.ConsistentRefereeing.Defs import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.SharedMemory.Covering /-! # Long-lived contest: setting, schedules, and the decide entry (Section 5 of [ACE26]) Infrastructure for the impossibility results of Section 5 of Attiya, Castañeda, Enea, *Impossibility Results for Strong Linearizability: The Difficulty of Consistent Refereeing* (PODC 2026, arXiv 2506.18401), cited as [ACE26]. We fix (`LLCtx`) an implementation `I` of the long-lived contest object from arbitrary base objects, the referee `ref`, and a prefix-closed linearization function `h` (w.r.t. the nondeterministic specification `llSpec`) on the admissible executions (`LLAdm ref`: competitors only invoke `compete`, the referee invokes `decide` at most once), together with wait-freedom on admissible executions. All of Section 5 happens inside this context. ## Schedules In an admissible execution every process has at most one possible next label: * a competitor `q` that is idle can only invoke `compete q`; a pending process can only take its (deterministic) next step `I.nextLabel C q`; * the referee invokes `decide` if it has not invoked yet, takes its next step if pending, and has nothing to do once `decide` returned. Hence (admissible) extensions of an execution `α` are exactly the label lists obtained from *schedules* (`List P`, or `ℕ → P` for infinite extensions): `X.ext α σ` lets the processes of `σ` take their next label in turn (`X.nl`). The paper's "`αq`" is `X.ext α [q]`, a "competitors-only extension" is `X.ext α σ` with `X.Comp σ` (no referee in `σ`). ## Main definitions * `LLCtx` — the context; `X.Good α` — `α` is a valid admissible execution. * `X.nl`, `X.ext1`, `X.extL`, `X.ext` — next label and schedule extensions. * `X.Dec α x` — `decide()` appears in `h α` with output `x`; `X.DecIn α` — it appears. * `X.not_dec_of_resps` — if `decide` does not appear in `h α` and some competitor has completed more than `x` operations in `α`, no extension has `decide` with output `x` in its linearization (the counting argument used throughout Section 5). * `X.transfer` — schedules of processes of `Q` produce the same labels from executions whose final configurations agree on `Q`. -/ set_option
