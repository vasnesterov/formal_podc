Differences between the formalization and [AFW26]

Rendered from differencies.md of Canonical-round algorithms and optimal Byzantine resilience — PODC 2026.

Errors / gaps in the paper

  1. Corollary 10: the proof does not cover 3f < n ≤ 4f. Corollary 10 is stated for n ≤ 5f, but it is derived from Theorem 1 via Algorithm 3 (crusader agreement from n consistent broadcasts), which is only correct for n > 4f (Theorem 9: two sets of n − 2f senders must intersect). The statement is nevertheless true for every n > f ≥ 1 by a direct argument that the paper's introduction hints at: the adversary lets every process hear n − f processes other than the correct sender in the first K rounds (or delivers the sender's messages one round late, for communication-closed algorithms); the other processes then have no information about the sender's input but validity forces them to accept it. Formalized as corollary10, corollary10_commClosed (Broadcast.lean), using only broadcast validity for a correct sender and no Byzantine process. Consequently the "sharp boundary at n = 5f" does not apply to reliable/consistent broadcast: no canonical-round broadcast algorithm has bounded rounds for any n once f ≥ 1.

  2. Theorem 14 needs gather agreement with i = j (an output contains at most one pair per process id). With agreement only for distinct correct processes, validity (ii) of crusader agreement fails: Gather.validity_fails_without_self_agreement (n = 4, f = 1, an output with two pairs of the faulty process). The paper's wording ("a set of distinct (process id, value) pairs", "for any k, if pᵢ and pⱼ are correct") is ambiguous; we require it for all correct i, j including i = j.

  3. "n ≤ 5f" literally includes trivial cases where the theorems fail: for n = 1, f = 1 the single process decides its input in round 0 (literal_theorem1_fails_n1). The paper's setting is 3f < n ≤ 5f; we prove Theorems 1/7 and Corollaries 6/8 for every 2f ≤ n ≤ 5f, f ≥ 1 (thm1_core, thm7_core), and state the paper-facing versions with 3f < n ≤ 5f.

  4. Theorem 7's proof in the paper is a sketch ("see [8]"); it is fully formalized here.

Generalizations (the formal results are stronger)

  1. General range 2f ≤ n ≤ 5f instead of n = 5f "for simplicity": groups |B| = |D| = f, |A|, |C|, |E| ≤ f (Groups, Groups.exists). |B| = |D| = f is what makes the other four groups send exactly n − f messages per round; A, C, E may even be empty.

  2. Messages: a canonical-round algorithm may send any function of its history (the paper sends the full history and the random number). Larger class of algorithms ⇒ stronger impossibility.

  3. Decisions: dec is applied to every history the process goes through (not only once per round); the decision is the first some (this models irrevocability). Safety constrains only first decisions.

  4. Theorems 1 and 7 hold tape by tape: the adversary of α₂ forces round complexity > K (Theorem 1) or non-termination (Theorem 7) for every tape; the expectation (Lemma 5) and the probability-0 statements are immediate corollaries, for every probability measure on tapes (not only i.i.d. choices).

  5. Safety is required only at finite prefixes of admissible executions (the paper: in every execution); termination bounds only for admissible adversaries in our (smaller) class. Both make the hypotheses weaker, hence the theorems stronger.

  6. Communication closure: we follow Section 3.2 (round-r messages arriving after round r are discarded; early messages are kept). The introduction says early and late messages are discarded; our class is larger. Formally: msg and dec factor through ccView.

  7. Nontrivial convergence (ncSpec) does not require x₀ ≠ x₁ or d₀ ≠ d₁.

Modelling choices (see README "Model")

  1. Processes are Fin n. The tape gives one random number per process and round (ρ p r, drawn when the round-r message is sent), matching the canonical-round message format; the paper lets every transition use a random number.

  2. The adversary is a function of the time and the current configuration (histories with all random numbers drawn so far, messages sent by Byzantine processes, delivery counters), not of the whole prefix. This is non-anticipating by construction; it is a subclass of the paper's adversaries (stronger impossibility). Byzantine processes act only through adversary events (Event.byz); they have no state.

  3. Round advancement counts round-r messages from n − f distinct senders (the paper: "n − f round r messages"), applied repeatedly in one atomic step ("cascade"). Messages received before wake-up are saved; a process may even "decide" before waking up (round 0) — harmless.

  4. Fairness: every correct process eventually wakes up and every link into a correct process is served infinitely often (a deliver on an empty link is a no-op); this implies the paper's "every message sent to a correct process is eventually received". Admissibility requires fairness for every tape.

  5. Late processes: in the paper's α₀ (resp. α₁) the processes of D (resp. B) do not wake up within K rounds; we wake them up at time 0 but deliver their messages only after round K (Theorem 1) or always one round late (Theorem 7, exactly as in the paper). This keeps one uniform schedule (schedule) and makes fairness immediate.

  6. Random choices of E: in α₂, E is Byzantine and the adversary cannot observe tape entries of Byzantine processes; the simulated copies of E use a fixed r₀, and α₀, α₁ run on the tape ρE ρ (rows of E constant). The adversary of α₂ reads the random numbers of the correct processes from their histories (tapeOf), which is exactly the non-anticipation needed in Lemma 5. The adversaries of α₀, α₁ depend on the tape of α₂ (e.g. A replays α₂, which involves future random numbers of D): this is legitimate because safety must hold for every adversary and every tape — the paper uses the same fact implicitly.

  7. Indistinguishability is proved via one cover (cov: A, B, C, D plus two copies E₀, E₁ of E) instead of Lemmas 3 and 4 separately; clean₂_eq, clean₀_eq, clean₁_eq are Lemma 3/4 and the corresponding statement for α₂.

  8. Problem specifications (Problems.lean): consensus with strong validity; approximate agreement on ℝ with validity "between two correct inputs"; approximate agreement on graphs with the geodesic convex hull defined via SimpleGraph.edist (distance 2 between the distinguished vertices); connected consensus requires R ≥ 1 (for R = 0 the spec degenerates) and includes the range D of decisions explicitly.

Positive side (Section 5) — Gather.lean

  1. Gather and approximate agreement are black boxes given by their specifications; Algorithm 4 is the local computation between the calls (Run, Phase1, Phase2, decision). Lines 2, 5, 6 are relations ("some v appears …" allows any choice); "appears k times" is read as "at least k". Termination is not modelled (it follows trivially from termination of the subroutines).

  2. |Sᵢ| ≥ n − f (used in Prop. 13) follows from the common core; Lemma 11 holds for any SC ⊆ Sᵢ. Gather agreement between different processes is never used.

  3. The paper does not show that decisions lie in D (1 ≤ g ≤ R); this follows from AA validity since the AA inputs are in {0, R} (ceil_y_mem). Theorem 18 uses |⌈a⌉ − ⌈b⌉| ≤ 1 for |a − b| ≤ 1 without proof (abs_ceil_sub_ceil_le). Lemma 17 needs neither Lemma 16 nor |F| ≤ f.

  4. Theorem 19 (binding) is formalized abstractly: the execution prefix α is represented by a fixed common core SC of the first gather; "extensions" are all runs satisfying the specs whose correct first-gather outputs contain SC; one value v* (depending only on SC) bounds all non-⊥ branches, for every R.

  5. Corollary 15 (GatherLowerBound.lean): gather is specified at every prefix (agreement, validity, and a common core of size n − f contained in all outputs returned so far); Algorithm 4 with R = 1 (caOfGather, Line 2 made deterministic with a classical choice) has literally the same executions; requires two distinct values.

Not formalized

  1. Theorem 9 / Algorithm 3 (superseded by the direct proof of Corollary 10), the real-time complexity (Section 2.1, Section 5.3), Remark 1, the Monte-Carlo footnote of Section 6.

Non-vacuity

  1. Sanity.lean: for n > 5f a deterministic, communication-closed one-round crusader agreement algorithm is safe and terminates in round 1 under every admissible adversary (exists_bounded_crusader); rrAdv_admissible exhibits admissible adversaries for every algorithm. So the hypotheses of the lower bounds are not contradictory, and the 5f threshold is sharp for crusader agreement. (Its counting: a sender counts for a value if any of its round-1 messages in the communication-closed view carries it.)
