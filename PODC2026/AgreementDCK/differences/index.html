Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of What is Agreement About if not Common Knowledge? — PODC 2026.

Paper: David–Moses, Brief Announcement: What is Agreement About if not Common Knowledge?, PODC 2026 (proceedings version; no full version available). The brief announcement states its results without proofs, so all proofs here are ours.

Errors and imprecisions in the paper

  1. Theorem 1.7, "for every run r of P_A and v ∈ V_A the nonfaulty processes decide v in r iff r ⊨ θ_v": false only in a degenerate case. This fails in runs with N(r) = ∅. There "the nonfaulty processes decide v" (the paper's all_decide(v)) holds vacuously, while θ_v need not hold. By Θ-Exclusivity, θ_v can hold for at most one value. So the failure does not depend on how P_A is built (for |V_A| ≥ 2). The same degenerate case affects the paper's motivating example before Theorem 1.7: there θ_v := "the nonfaulty processes decide v", and in a run with N(r) = ∅ both θ_0 and θ_1 hold, which violates Θ-Exclusivity and footnote 5 (θ_0 ⇔ ¬θ_1). Our converse (item 2) uses "some nonfaulty process decides v" instead. Lean counterexample: theorem_1_7_iff_false_without_nonfaulty. It uses one run with no nonfaulty process and all θ_v false; all hypotheses of Theorem 1.7 hold. Repaired statement: allDecide_newDec_iff assumes (S.nf r).Nonempty. Without that assumption the two directions still hold in this form: θ_v implies that all nonfaulty processes decide v, and a decision on v by some nonfaulty process implies θ_v (theorem_1_7).

  2. Converse remark after Theorem 1.7 is imprecise; it needs recall of decisions. The paper says "underlying the design of every AoN protocol is a set of facts Θ as described in Theorem 1.7". Read literally, the remark is trivial: Θ with all θ_v false satisfies (1)–(3). We read it as: there is Θ satisfying (1)–(3) whose θ_v holds exactly when v is decided.

    • We prove this reading when decisions are local, AoN holds for every value, Agreement and Validity hold, and nonfaulty processes remember their decisions (Stable). The proof takes θ_v = "some nonfaulty process decides v" and 𝛙(v) = 𝛅(v) (exists_theta_of_AoN).

    • Without memory the reading is false: no_theta_for_forgetful_protocol gives a one-process, failure-free protocol that decides at time 1 and then forgets. The paper's standing assumption ("a process about to decide remains in such a state until it performs the action") does not rule this out, because it constrains states only before the decision.

    Classification: under-specified informal claim.

Proofs supplied (no proof in the paper)

  1. Theorem 1.2 is cited from [29]; we prove it directly (System.kop, theorem_1_2). It needs only that decideᵢ(v) is i-local. The "conscious action" assumption is not needed.

  2. Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.6. We prove one general lemma, CN_of_precondition: under AoN(v), every precondition φ of deciding v satisfies ⊨ all_decide(v) → C^N@𝛅(v)(φ). The proof uses the induction rule CN_of_EN_valid. KoP then gives KN_CN_of_precondition. Theorem 1.5 takes φ = all_decide(v). Corollary 1.6 takes φ = valid(v) and φ = no_dec(w). This also proves the sentence before Corollary 1.6 ("for every precondition φ_v …").

  3. Theorem 1.7. The paper does not say what P_A is. Our construction runs P unchanged and adds the decision rule newDecᵢ(v) := ψᵢ(v) ∧ K_i(i ∈ N → θ_v). We prove that it is local, AoN for every value, satisfies Agreement and Validity, satisfies Decision when ⊨ ⋁ θ_v, and that its decisions are stable for nonfaulty processes (newDec_stable).

    • The memory hypothesis is used only for stability ("conscious decisions"). All other conclusions hold without it.

    • Only ⊨ θ_v → E^N@𝛙(v)(θ_v) is used. By the induction rule this is equivalent to the paper's C^N hypothesis (sufficient_of_EN, CN_self_of_EN_self).

    • The hypothesis "P solves A" is not used.

Modelling choices

  1. Runs and time. The system is an arbitrary type R of runs, standing for all runs of P in the model. Time is ℕ. The local state of i is loc i r t : L i. Knowledge quantifies over all points of all runs in R. N(r) is an arbitrary set nf r, as in the paper ("faulty" left abstract). Facts are R → ℕ → Prop. Run facts are R → Prop, lifted with RunFact.lift when they are nested.

  2. Decisions are facts dec i v : R → ℕ → Prop ("i decides v at (r, t)"). The standing assumption that decisions depend on the local state is the hypothesis DecLocal. A process is not required to decide only once. Every property in the paper (AoN, Agreement, Validity, all_decide) speaks only of whether a decision happens in the run. In P_A a decision "happens" whenever the decision condition holds; the actual action can be taken the first time it holds. A faulty process may satisfy the condition for several values under our rule. This does not matter, because Agreement and Validity constrain only nonfaulty processes, as in the paper.

  3. "Extends P by adding new decision actions". The new decisions are modelled as extra local facts on the same system. They are pure outputs: they do not change local states or messages. If a model records the decision in the local state, the runs, and hence the knowledge, of P_A would differ from those of P. We do not cover that variant.

  4. Definition 1.3. DK i ψ φ is defined for every fact ψ. The paper requires ψ to be i-local, and we assume locality (IsLocalProfile) wherever it matters (Theorem 1.7, for the locality of newDec).

  5. Definition 1.4. EIter ψ φ m is E^{m+1} (index shifted by one). CN ψ φ r := ∀ m, EIter ψ φ m r is exactly "E^m for all m ≥ 1".

  6. Validity condition of a problem: a run fact valid v with ⊨ i ∈ N ∧ decideᵢ(v) → valid(v) (Validity). Agreement and Decision are the usual properties for nonfaulty processes.

  7. Corollary 1.6 assumes AoN(v) for all v ∈ V. We assume it only for the decided value v, which is a stronger statement.

  8. Classical common knowledge (System.E, System.C, used only in Example.lean) is among all processes. In the example every process is nonfaulty, so this is the classical notion that the paper contrasts with.

Additional results (not in the paper as statements)

  • CN_iff_EN: the fixed-point characterization C^N@𝛙 φ ↔ E^N@𝛙 (φ ∧ C^N@𝛙 φ).

  • CN_of_EN_valid: the induction rule.

  • CN.KN_CN, CN.eventually_KN: the remark after Definition 1.4.

  • Example.lean: an asynchronous two-process AoN protocol in which dynamic common knowledge of the decision holds in every run, while classical common knowledge (even E²) of the decided bit holds at no point.
