Sinkless orientation made trivial — PODC 2026

Alexandre Nolin. Brief Announcement: Sinkless Orientation Made Trivial. PODC 2026. DOI 10.1145/3796701.3815953. Formalized version: the 4-page brief announcement (HAL hal-05678283); no full version was found. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. Only slips were found: filled in, the paper's proof sketches hold, covering the min-ID orientation (Theorem 2.1), Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3, and the deterministic O(log_δ n) sinkless orientation of Theorem 1.1, counted in LOCAL rounds. The CONGEST implementation, the paper's main point, was checked only for Theorem 2.1, so the CONGEST bound of Theorem 1.1 is not verified. The randomized part relies on external reductions and was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 2.2, "in particular" clause: the ball of radius ⌈log_{δ−1} n⌉ is said to contain "a node of degree δ or a cycle", but it should read "degree < δ" (lemma_2_2_clog). As printed it is false, e.g. on a path with δ = 3. "Degree < δ" is what the main statement (item (i)) gives and what Algorithm 2 uses.

  • The definition of k-sinksparse asks for sinks at distance ≥ k, but the proof of Theorem 2.1 gives > k, and only that makes "the BFS trees of different nodes do not overlap" true: two sinks at distance exactly k (k even) share a midpoint. We use the stronger > k (lemma_2_3).

  • Section 3: "every node v" with at least max(ε deg(v), 1) outgoing and incoming edges is meant for minimum degree δ ≥ 3. Read literally for arbitrary graphs, it is impossible at degree-1 nodes: a single edge has no such orientation (no_orientation_in_and_out_single_edge).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Deterministic, synchronous, fault-free LOCAL with unique identifiers; every node knows n. No randomness.

  • Round complexity is locality. An orientation algorithm A : ∀ G, Orientation G is R-local if the direction of each edge uv is the same in any two graphs that agree on all edges incident to nodes at distance < R from u, i.e. on what u learns in R rounds. Since A sees the node set V, a rule may depend on the global identifier set, slightly more than standard LOCAL. This is harmless for the explicit algorithms and makes the lower bound stronger. The agreement relation GAgree is the shared one of Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Local/OnlineLocalLocality.lean, and isLocal_iff identifies IsLocal A R with the shared Cslib.Local.IsLocal (the out-neighbourhoods u ↦ {v | out u v} are an R-local function).

  • Theorem 2.1 is also implemented as an explicit flooding algorithm in CSLib's synchronous SET-LOCAL model, with O(log n)-bit states. CONGEST message sizes are otherwise not modelled.

  • Graphs: Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 2.3 hold for any (even infinite) simple graph; Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 1.1 assume a finite node set.

  • Tie-breaking in Algorithm 2 uses a fixed choice function. The proofs use only the specifications of the choices, so ID-based tie-breaking is covered as well.

Main.lean

The tightness proof is the classical reflection argument. A T-local algorithm with 2T + 2 ≤ k (given 2T + 2 + 3k identifiers) puts two sinks on some (k+1)-node path. So k-sinksparse orientations need about k/2 rounds, while k + 1 suffice.

Remarks.

  • The hypothesis r ≥ 1 of Lemma 2.2 is redundant: for r = 0 the bound is 1.

  • The paper's proofs are sketches. It gives no proof of Lemma 2.2 ("the argument is standard").

  • Constants: Algorithm 1's k = 4⌈log_{δ−1} n⌉ is twice what is needed; we keep the paper's value. The tightness of Theorem 2.1 is only asserted ("obviously tight, even in LOCAL").

Not formalized. The randomized part of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 (they rely on the shattering and reduction results of Ghaffari–Su and Ghaffari et al.), the CONGEST implementation of Algorithm 2 and Lemma 2.3 (only the LOCAL round complexity is formalized, so the CONGEST bound of Theorem 1.1 is not checked), and the cited Ω(log_δ n) / Ω(log_δ log n) lower bounds.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The SET-LOCAL round model of theorem_2_1 is CSLib's Cslib.LocallyIterative.
