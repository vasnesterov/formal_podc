import Papers.PODC2026.ReachingUnivalency.EpsBA /-! # Reaching Univalency — §4: one level of (Probabilistic) ε-RPK The protocol of Fig. 2/Fig. 3 at one level of the recursion, with the two recursive calls on the committees treated as black boxes (as in the paper's analysis: "it does not matter exactly how the committees carry out ε-BA, so long as they do so correctly"). **Model.** Processors `Fin n`, a static set `F` of Byzantine processors, two committees `C 0`, `C 1` (the paper's `C₁`, `C₂`). A *sampling choice* fixes, for each round `r ∈ {0,1}`, each processor `p` and each of the three steps, the `k` processors (with repetitions) whose values `p` learns. A sampled correct processor reports its true value; a sampled Byzantine processor `q` reports to `p` an arbitrary `Option Bool` chosen by the adversary (`none` = `⊥`/silence). The committee ε-BA instance of round `r` is an arbitrary function `comm r` from the input vector to the output vector (chosen by the adversary), constrained only by `CommOK`: if the committee has fewer than `|C r|(1/3 - ε)` faulty members, its outcome satisfies ε-BA. Majority ties in step 3 are broken by the adversary (`tie`). Step 1 uses a threshold `T1` (the paper: `T1 = 2/3 - ε/2`; our corrected protocol in `Corrected.lean` uses `2/3 - ε/4`); steps 2 and 3 are as in the paper. The paper's notion of a *bad* sampling choice (§4.2) is `NotBad`; the per-processor deviation of a sample from the population is `dev`. `Lemma6Claim` is Lemma 6 as stated. -/ namespace
