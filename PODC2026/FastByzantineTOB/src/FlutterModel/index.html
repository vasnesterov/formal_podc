Byzantine nodes have no state: their messages are arbitrary and can be delivered at any time. * Authenticated FIFO links: for a correct sender `a` and any receiver `b`, `sent a b` is the list of all messages `a` sent to `b`, and `dlv a b` how many of them `b` has delivered; a delivery from a correct sender delivers the next message, `(sent a b)[dlv a b]`. Messages from Byzantine senders are arbitrary (but carry the true sender identity). * Time: each correct node has a local clock in a linear order `T`; `tick` moves it forward (local clocks are non-decreasing, as the paper's proof of Lemma 15 assumes). Timing bounds (`Δ`, `Ψ`) are irrelevant for safety and not modelled. * `beat()` may be executed at any time (the paper executes it "regularly" and at scheduled times; allowing more behaviours only strengthens safety results). * The instances `con[x]` of Representative Binary Consensus are black boxes: `con[x].Decide v` reaches a correct server as an unconstrained environment action (`Action.decide`).
