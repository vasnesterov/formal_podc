Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Fast Byzantine Total Order Broadcast — PODC 2026.

Paper: Monti, Camaioni, Roman, Fast Byzantine Total Order Broadcast, PODC 2026, arXiv:2412.14061v2.

Statement-level issues

  1. "At least 5f + 1 servers" (abstract; introduction) vs. hard-coded thresholds. §2.1 fixes n = 5f + 1, and all proofs use it. Blink's 4f + 1/2f + 1 and Flutter's lock quorum 4f + 1 are wrong for n = 5f + 2. Lean counterexamples: blink_agreement_fails_5f2 (Blink with a dep satisfying Validity, Agreement and Integrity) and flutter_totalOrder_fails_5f2 / flutter_not_prefix_5f2 (Flutter with a con satisfying Representative Validity and Agreement). Repaired: thresholds n − f (blink_safe_general, theorem8_totalOrder_general). In both counterexamples every black-box decision comes after the correct proposals that justify it, so they also hold under a temporal reading of the specs. This is a 🔧 mistake. (a) The paper's model section states n = 5f + 1 explicitly, and the counterexamples violate that model. (b) Writing thresholds for n = 5f + 1 and saying "at least 5f + 1" is a common convention in BFT papers, meaning the resilience bound f < n/5. (c) The repair (4f + 1 ↦ n − f) is local and evident. But the paper does explicitly claim more generality, and the claim is about its specific algorithm Flutter, the title result. The same threshold pattern occurs in Papers/PODC2019/VABA and Papers/PODC2021/RoundEfficientBA, with one difference: there the model section and the numbered theorems themselves claim every f < n/3, and the proofs reason in that generality. Here the model (§2.1, with a citation) fixes n = 5f + 1, every numbered result is stated and proved in that model and is true there; only the abstract/introduction phrase "at least 5f + 1 servers" overreaches.

  2. Lemma 5 as stated does not say whether the sequence may be finite. Read as an ℕ-indexed strictly increasing sequence of elements of X, it is false for finite nonempty X (lemma5_literal_false); "sequence" may however be read as finite or infinite, so this is an imprecision rather than an error. Intended: a finite or infinite enumeration, as the proof shows (φ is a bijection with {n ≤ |X|}). Proved as lemma5_finite (sorted list) and lemma5_infinite (z : ℕ → α, range z = X). Our infinite case assumes X infinite instead of splitting on |X|. The proof uses the intended version.

  3. ✏️ Lemma 6 as stated does not say that z, z' take values in X; if they may leave X it is false (lemma6_literal_false: X = {0}, z = id, z' = 2·). Read as sequences in X it is true. Proved with range z = range z' (= X) (lemma6_infinite, via Mathlib's StrictMono.range_inj_of_wellFoundedLT; lemma6_finite for sorted lists). The proof uses z_n, z'_n ∈ X, and the hypothesis holds at its uses (Corollary 2 by construction; Corollary 3 by Lemma 18).

Proof-level differences (statements unaffected)

  • Theorems 2 and 4 (Blink): the paper's proofs go through Lemma 1, i.e. the Termination of dep. Our proofs use only the Validity and Agreement of dep, so they also cover runs where dep never decides.

  • Lemma 4: the paper uses the Integrity of dep; we use its Agreement (with p = q). We also state Integrity (DepIntegrity); the safety theorems do not need it.

  • Lemmas 2–4 and Theorems 2/4 are proved for general thresholds. The hypotheses are n + |F| < Q + M and M ≤ Q for Agreement, and M, Q ≥ 2f + 1 for Representative Validity. The paper's argument "3f + 1 and f + 1 correct servers must meet" is the instance Q = 4f + 1, M = 2f + 1, n = 5f + 1. Note that Agreement needs n ≤ 5f + 1 with these thresholds, which item 1 makes precise.

  • Lemma 16 is proved in a safety form: "if f + 1 correct servers propose True for y (at some time) and p's lock time is ≥ y.bet at time k, then y ∈ candidates_p at time k". The paper's statement F ⊆ C[π] also covers servers whose lock time never reaches b, and that part uses the liveness Lemma 10. The general hypothesis is n < (f + 1) + q, with lock quorum q.

  • Lemmas 17–20 and Corollary 4 are proved as invariants at every time (orders_gapFree, corollary4):

    • order calls are strictly increasing and decided True;

    • every tag decided True below an order call is itself an order call;

    • the order-call sequences of two correct servers are prefix-comparable.

    The paper instead identifies the complete (possibly infinite) sequence of order calls with the enumeration of F (Corollaries 2–3, Lemma 21). That needs liveness and is not formalized.

  • Theorem 8 is formalized in its safety part: at any two times the tob.Deliver sequences of two correct servers are prefixes of one another (theorem8_totalOrder). "Eventually deliver the same messages" is not formalized.

  • Lemma 15 is a local invariant of each server's sequence of messages sent to all servers (LInv.lemma15). FIFO enters only in Lemma 16.

  • Integrity (Theorem 7): we conclude "θ broadcast m at some time of the run". The black-box Representative Validity is over whole runs, so the supporting proposal may lie in the future.

Modelling choices

  • Asynchronous interleaving model, safety only. No Δ, Ψ, GST or real time. Any finite asynchronous prefix is consistent with the paper's partial synchrony (choose Δ, Ψ large), so the counterexamples are valid in the paper's model. The positive results hold in every asynchronous run, which is stronger than needed.

  • Clocks: one local clock per correct node, moved forward by tick (non-decreasing; the paper uses this in Lemma 15). Initial clocks are arbitrary.

  • beat() may run at any time. The paper runs it "regularly" (§4.2) and at the bets of observed messages (line 27). The superset of behaviours makes the safety results stronger. In the Flutter counterexample every beat is a scheduled one: at time 20, by a server that observed the message with bet 20.

  • Line 16 of Blink ("the value suggested at least 2f + 1 times") is a nondeterministic guard. With the paper's W = 4f + 1 there is exactly one such value. The wait threshold W plays no role in safety.

  • Links. Blink: a delivery of [Suggest, v] from a correct σ is allowed iff σ proposed v. Repeated deliveries are allowed, which only generalizes. Flutter: per-link FIFO queues (sent, dlv) for correct senders; Byzantine senders' messages are arbitrary. In Flutter all server-to-server sends go to every server, so the queue from ρ to any server equals ρ's history obox (GInv.srv). Likewise for clients (cbox).

  • Servers and clients are disjoint node sets (P ⊕ C). The paper allows overlap (footnote 1). Overlap only merges identities and is not needed for the results here.

  • Atomic handlers: each upon handler (e.g. lines 31–36) is one atomic step, as in the pseudocode.

  • History variables are added to the states (props, decs, procd, orders, dels, obox, bcast, cbox, depProp, depDecs, decs, input). They do not influence the algorithm.

  • Black-box specs are hypotheses on whole runs. dep: Validity and Agreement (Integrity optional). con[x]: Representative Validity and Agreement. Termination is never assumed.

  • Order on tags is Assumption 1: bet, then client, then message (Tag, via toLex). (⊥, ⊥, −∞) is ⊥ in WithBot Tag; −∞ in remote_times is ⊥ : WithBot T.

  • lock_time() is the largest value t with at least q entries ≥ t, characterized in le_lockTime_iff. The quorum q is a parameter (paper: 4f + 1).

  • Time type: any linear order T. Clients need + and • on T to compute bets ([AddCommMonoid T]).
