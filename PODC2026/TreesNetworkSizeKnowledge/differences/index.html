Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of LCLs on Trees Without Knowledge of n — PODC 2026.

Paper: Balliu, Brandt, Kuhn, Olivetti, Picavet, Schmid, The Distributed Complexity Landscape on Trees Depends on the Knowledge About the Network Size, PODC 2026 (arXiv 2605.12787 v1). Formalized: Theorem 1 (Section 3) with its proof; non-vacuity of its hypotheses.

Model and definitions

  1. LOCAL model, extensional. Algorithms are pairs (time, out) with a locality condition (Alg.IsLocal) instead of message-passing state machines. Every full-information LOCAL algorithm with per-node termination satisfies it. The view at round t contains all edges incident to nodes at distance ≤ t (GAgree … (t+1)), one hop more than standard LOCAL. This only enlarges the class of algorithms, so the lower bound is stronger. No input besides the IDs; n is not available.

  2. IDs. Nodes are natural numbers and their names are the IDs (unbounded, arbitrary). The paper says IDs are "arbitrary natural numbers"; same.

  3. Graph class. The algorithm must be correct on all trees of maximum degree ≤ Δ for a fixed Δ ≥ 3 (the paper assumes a constant Δ). Our hard instances have maximum degree 3, so the result holds for every constant Δ ≥ 3. (For k = 1 the instances are paths, so Δ = 2 would also do; we do not state that case separately.)

  4. k-hierarchical 2½-colouring is the node-labelled description of Section 2.3 (constraints 1–4), not the half-edge LCL encoding of Section 2.2. Constraint 3's "same level" is read as: both nodes lie in the same level i ≤ k (remainder nodes output D by constraint 1 anyway).

  5. Ω(n) is Knuth's (the paper says Section 3 uses Knuth's definition): ∃ ε > 0, ∃ n₀, ∀ n > n₀, some n-node tree has a node with time ≥ εn. The runtime of an algorithm on a graph is the largest termination time of a node. The proof's contradiction hypothesis is the exact negation ("< εn on all n-node trees"), where the paper writes "at most εn". This is immaterial.

Lower bound graph (Definition 16, Observation 17)

  1. Definition 16 is encoded with explicit identifiers (comp; path node t = Nat.pair 0 t, node y of the b-th attached component of path node t = Nat.pair (2t+b+1) y). The paper allows an arbitrary ID assignment, but its proof needs only one, so fixing it is harmless.

  2. Observation 17: only the upper bound is used and proved, with c₂ = 3^j ≤ 3^k (card_comp_le). The lower bound c₁ L_i is not needed.

  3. That the levels of the 2½-colouring problem on the lower bound graph are the path levels is used implicitly in the paper. We prove it via a general criterion (levelSet_eq) for every host.

Proof of Theorem 1

  1. Gap (repaired, 🛠️). Property 4 is proved with the modified graph Ĝ. The conclusion "the produced 2-colouring cannot be proper" needs the u–v path in Ĝ to be properly 2-coloured. That needs all lower-level nodes adjacent to it to output D in Ĝ, so that no path node may output E. The induction hypothesis is stated only for G ("by running A on G"), and Ĝ is not a k-hierarchical lower bound graph. Repair: we prove the induction claim (claim) for every host, i.e. every rooted tree with a level labelling satisfying local degree conditions (HostOK), and every level-j component embedded in it by an ID renaming (Emb). Hosts are closed under the surgery (hostOK_hat), and the attached components stay embedded (emb_hat_child). The statement is not affected. One could argue that "the induction hypothesis also holds in Ĝ" is routine, but this is a genuine repair for two reasons. The paper explicitly fixes the induction to runs on G. And applying it in Ĝ needs the induction itself to be re-run on surgered graphs, whose own property-4 steps do further surgery. So the claim has to be generalised to a class closed under surgery.

  2. Induction claim. We prove "every node of the component outputs D" directly, instead of the paper's properties 1–4 for two nodes u, v followed by "this implies that the whole graph is labelled D". Property 3 is proved for every path node at position ≥ T + 1 (time_le_of_pad). The padding is attached at position 0, so the upper end needs no condition.

  3. T_i = ⌊ℓ_i/10⌋ instead of ℓ_i/10, which need not be an integer. Positions: u at T+1, z at 2T+3, v at 3T+5 (all < ℓ_i since ℓ_i ≥ 100), instead of u, v at distance T_i + 1 from the two ends. z is at distance ≥ T + 2 from u and v; this is needed with our generous views (the neighbours of z change in Ĝ). The paper's "distance at least 2T_i + 1" (property 2) alone would not give a node at distance > T_i from both; its actual choice of u, v does.

  4. Padding (G''). The paper extends "an arbitrary path P₁ at distance > T_i from v". We attach a pendant path of fresh IDs to a level-1 node at position 0 of the component (leafCode). This keeps the maximum degree at 3 and covers i = 1, where G' is the path P₁ itself.

  5. Parameters. As in the paper, N_{i+1} = f(1/(20 c₂ L_i), 100 c₂ L_i) and ℓ_{i+1} = ⌊N_{i+1}/(c₂ L_i)⌋ (NOf, ellOf, Lseq), with c₂ = 3^k. From time < εN we get time ≤ ℓ_{i+1}/20 ≤ T_{i+1}.

  6. The final contradiction uses constraint 2 at the first node of the level-k path. The paper goes through Observation 18; this is equivalent.

Not formalized

  1. Theorem 2 (k-rake-and-compress). Lemma 19 and Observation 20 are not formalized either. The proof of property 1 is a sketch. The parity surgery of Theorem 1 does not transfer directly, because a single-sink orientation of a path can be locally consistent (e.g. with increasing IDs, every node points to its larger-ID neighbour). We neither verified nor refuted it.

  2. Theorems 3–7, Lemma 21 onwards, and the randomized results of Section 7.
