Differences between the formalization and [Hirvonen–Suomela, PODC'12]

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed Maximal Matching: Greedy is Optimal — PODC 2012.

Paper: Juho Hirvonen, Jukka Suomela, Distributed Maximal Matching: Greedy is Optimal, PODC 2012, DOI 10.1145/2332432.2332464. Formalized version: arXiv 1110.0367 v1 (the only arXiv version); numbering follows it.

Summary. No error affects the results. Lemma 1, Theorem 2, Corollary 3, Lemma 5, Theorem 6 and all intermediate lemmas (4, 7, 8, 10–16) are proved. The only gap is the passage from the paper's formal model (possibly infinite trees) to finite graphs. We close it.

Gaps and imprecisions in the paper

  1. Finite graphs (🔧, proof gap with true statement). Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 speak of "anonymous k-edge-coloured graphs", but the formal model of §2.3 only covers colour systems, i.e. edge-coloured trees, which may be infinite. All lower-bound instances are infinite: each realisation, and also the final U and V, is a d-regular tree with d ≥ 2. Read with the usual meaning of "graph" (finite), the lower bound needs one more step. Every finite ball of a d-regular colour system must occur as the view of a node in a finite, simple, d-regular, properly k-edge-coloured graph. We construct such a graph (MPAlgorithm.completion): 2^k copies of the ball; a boundary node lacking its colour-c edge is joined to its twin in the copy with coordinate c flipped. With it the lower bound transfers to finite graphs (MPAlgorithm.findsMaximalMatching_of_finite). So theorem2_lower_messagePassing, theorem6_messagePassing and corollary3_messagePassing hold for finite simple (and, for Theorem 6 / Corollary 3, regular) graphs.

  2. Operational model vs. view model (under-specified, harmless). §1.2 describes synchronous message passing; §2.3 replaces it by "the output is a function of (v̄V)[r+1]" and justifies this in one sentence. We formalize both models and prove both directions. (a) An r-round message-passing algorithm on any edge-coloured graph depends only on the radius-(r+1) view (MPAlgorithm.state_eq_of_view), so it is a §2.3 algorithm with running time r (hasRunningTime_toAlgorithm). (b) Conversely, a full-information protocol computes any §2.3 algorithm in r rounds on every graph (out_ofView). (c) Outputs on the universal cover give a maximal matching on every graph, including graphs with cycles (isMaximalMatching_view).

  3. Typo. In the proof of Lemma 8, "by symmetry, w ∈ ȳY implies w ∈ x̄X" should read w ∈ ȳX.

Modelling choices and changed definitions

  1. The group G_k is implemented as reduced words over Fin k, stored in reverse (FreeCox k), with a hand-built group structure. Colours are 0, …, k-1 instead of 1, …, k; the greedy algorithm processes them in increasing order. |x| is len x. head(x) = c is startsWith c x (|c x| < |x|). pred and tail are expressed through len (x * gen c) < len x.

  2. Colour systems (IsColourSystem): e ∈ V and closure under moving to the parent. This is the paper's prefix-closedness. C(V, v) is defined as {c | v c ∈ V} (colours), which the paper notes equals the edge-based definition. Lemma 4 is IsColourSystem.inv_smul plus colours_inv_smul (the colour-preserving isomorphism).

  3. Algorithms are total functions Set (FreeCox k) → FreeCox k → Option (Fin k) (⊥ = none). The running-time condition and (M1)–(M3) are imposed only for colour systems and nodes in them, as in the paper. Theorem 6 assumes that A has some running time r; this is part of the paper's definition of a distributed algorithm.

  4. Templates (Template): the forbidden colour τ is a total function on G_k. Values outside T are irrelevant. (C2) σ[h-1] = τ[h-1] is agreement on S ∩ {|s| ≤ h-1} (Compatible).

  5. Realisations and A(T, τ, t). The paper builds the realisation (V, p) = real(T, τ) and sets A(T, τ, t) = A(V, v) for any v ∈ p⁻¹(t) (well defined by Corollary 10). We define real T t as the view from t of the graph "tree edges of T + half-loops of all colours ≠ τ(t)", i.e. the v̄-translate of the paper's realisation. We set out A T t = A (real T t) e. Corollary 10 becomes Good.apply_real: every node of the realisation that maps to b outputs out A T b.

  6. Extensions are built through Remark 1 (unfolding half-loops / universal covers), not through the recursive relation ⇝ of §3.3. Only 1-colour pickers are needed (Template.extend). The realisation is the (d-h)-picker case, handled separately.

    • Lemma 7 is isTemplate_extend (for b = 1) and isRegular_real (for the realisation).

    • Lemma 8 is proved for arbitrary edge-coloured graphs (ColouredGraph.view_inv_smul, Template.extend_symm).

    • Lemma 9 (commutation of extensions) is not formalized. It is only used to derive Corollary 11, which we prove directly with a covering-map argument (Template.real_extend).

  7. Lemma 15 is proved with the same distant/near split as the paper: K₃ and L₃ are the matched pairs whose two endpoints are both at distance > r+1 from e. The parity count is organized differently. N = X \ (K₃ ∪ L₃) is finite. It carries three fixed-point-free involutions: M(X, ξ) on N (under the contradiction hypothesis), M(K, κ) on N ∩ K₁, and M(L, λ) on N ∩ L₁ - χ. This gives |N| both even and odd. We only conclude y ∈ X; the paper's extra y ∈ K₂ ∪ L₂ is not needed.

  8. Observation (c) of §3.9 (compatibility of the χ̄-translates) is not used and is not formalized. Observations (a), (d) and (e) are isTemplate_glue, compatible_glue_left and compatible_glue_right. Observations (a)–(h) before them are bundled in StepHyp and proved in stepHyp_of_critical.

  9. Corollary 3 is formalized as the paper derives it: with k = Δ + 1 colours, an algorithm correct on Δ-regular instances (in particular, on graphs of maximum degree Δ) needs Δ rounds. The O(Δ + log* k) upper bound and Linial's Ω(log* k) lower bound (§1.3) are cited results and are not formalized.

  10. Message-passing model details. A node outputs after exactly r rounds instead of "stops after some round". This is equivalent: a stopped node can idle. Edge colours serve as port numbers, and the incoming messages are indexed by colour. A ColouredGraph may contain half-loops and parallel edges of different colours. Lower bounds assume only correctness on simple graphs (no half-loops, at most one edge between two nodes), which is the stronger statement; the upper bound (greedy) holds on all of them.

  11. Lemma 1, implementation. In the message-passing model greedy runs as a full-information protocol (ofView greedy). The paper's remark that greedy needs only small messages and little memory is not formalized.

  12. Not formalized. The informal remarks of §1.3–1.4: the case d = k is trivial, and the result also covers the port-numbering and broadcast models.
