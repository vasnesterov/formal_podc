import Papers.PODC2012.CloserLookFT.ElectionCore /-! # Safety of the election algorithm (Theorem 2.4): at most one leader The paper proves Theorem 2.4 with an informal "accounting system of credits". We make it precise with ghost state (`Ghost`): * `age p`: the order in which processes wrote `turn` (`0` = not yet); since every process writes `turn` exactly once, `turn` always holds the youngest started process; * `cr p`: the credits of `p`; register `V[j]` holds `2^j` credits while it is `0`; * `own j`: the process holding the `2^j` credits taken from `V[j]` (while `V[j] ≠ 0`). Credits move as in the paper: writing `p` over `0` into `V[j]` moves `2^j` credits from the register to `p`; writing `0` over a non-zero value moves `2^j` credits from the writer to the register; when `p` overwrites a non-zero `V[j]` and is *younger* than `own j`, the owner hands its `2^j` credits to `p` (the paper's Rule 3/4 transfer "from `q` to `p`", made state-based by comparing ages instead of referring to maximal groups of processes that "noticed together"). The invariant `Inv` says that credits are conserved (`n + 2^L - 1` in total), every process holds at least its requirement `req` (`2^k` at level `k`, `2^L` once elected, otherwise what it may still have to pay back), plus the structural facts `c1`, `c2` that replace the paper's Lemma 2.3. An elected process holds `2^L` credits, so two of them would need `2^(L+1) > n + 2^L - 1` credits when `n ≤ 2^L`. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace Podc.CloserLookFT open Finset
