Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Waiting in Dynamic Networks — PODC 2012.

Paper: Casteigts, Flocchini, Godard, Santoro, Yamashita, Brief Announcement: Waiting in Dynamic Networks, PODC 2012; full version Expressivity of Time-Varying Graphs and the Power of Waiting in Dynamic Networks, arXiv:1205.1975v1 (numbering below follows the arXiv version).

Model and definitions

  • Time domain ℕ only (the paper allows ℕ or ℝ⁺). The regularity proof (Theorem 4.1) does not use discreteness; Theorems 3.1 and 5.1 are constructions in ℕ.

  • Edges are triples (u, a, v) (the paper writes (u, v, a)). The footprint E is implicit: we take all of V × Σ × V, an edge outside the paper's E being never present. Equivalent since V and Σ are finite. The lifetime 𝒯 is absorbed into ρ.

  • Presence and latency. The paper requires ρ, ζ to be "arbitrary computable functions". The main theorems (theorem_3_1, theorem_4_1, theorem_5_1) are for arbitrary ρ, ζ. The computable versions (nodes Fin n, alphabet Fin q, TVG.IsComputable: presence a computable predicate, latency a computable function) are in Computable.lean: theorem_3_1_computable, theorem_4_1_computable, theorem_5_1_computable, and the converse LnowaitComp_eq_computable (not claimed as a theorem in the paper, only as "as powerful as a Turing machine"). The decoder φ⁻¹ is shown computable via a primitive-recursive base-b decoder (phiInv_eq_phiDec), for bases of the form c + 2.

  • Journeys are an inductive relation Journey P u t w v t' with a pause rule P instead of explicit sequences of transitions (sᵢ, tᵢ →aᵢ sᵢ₊₁, tᵢ₊₁). Same content.

  • Start date. The paper's acceptance condition does not say when journeys start; Section 3 implicitly uses date 0 (φ(ε) = 0), the example G₁ date 1. We put a start date into the automaton; the families quantify over it. It matters: if any start date were allowed, L_nowait(G₂) ≠ L (G2_other_start_date). ✏️ (implied assumption).

  • Waiting at the start node. In the paper start(J) is the departure date of the first transition, and J*x = J*_{0,x}. In our model a journey is at the initial node at the start date and the first departure obeys the pause rule, so with waiting it may leave later. For L_wait and L_nowait this changes nothing (no waiting: same; waiting: the regularity proof covers any start configuration, and the strict variant is a finite union of a · L_wait(from (v, ζ(e,0))), also regular — not formalized). For bounded waiting the first pause is also ≤ d. Under the literal definition, Lemma 4.2 is false: with one node, b present only at 0 with latency 1 and a present only at 1, every journey of ba from date 0 contains the walk a (so a ≺ ba), but no journey of a starts at date 0. Not formalized (✏️, see README).

  • Bounded waiting waitAtMost d: each pause (including before the first edge) lasts at most d.

  • Families 𝓛_nowait, 𝓛_wait, 𝓛_wait[d]: sets of languages over a fixed alphabet α : Type accepted by TVG-automata with a finite node type V : Type.

Section 2 (example G₁)

  • Formalized as stated (ExampleG1.lean), start date 1, distinct primes p, q. The latencies of e₃, e₄ ("any") are parameters. The presence condition of e₂, "t ≠ pⁱqⁱ⁻¹, i > 1", is read as "for all i > 1". Correct; determinism also proved (G1_deterministic).

Section 3 (Theorem 3.1)

  • Base of the encoding. The paper uses base q = |Σ|. For q = 1 the encoding φ(w) = 1.w is not injective (every non-empty word gets 1), so φ⁻¹ and G₂ are undefined (✏️ slip, degenerate alphabet). We encode in any base b ≥ max 2 q; theorem_3_1 is the paper's statement for b = q ≥ 2, and Lnowait_eq_univ covers every q with b = q + 2.

  • φ(ε) = 0 "by convention" while the formula gives q⁰ = 1: harmless (typo-level); we follow the convention.

  • φ⁻¹ is a classical inverse on the image of φ (junk value ε elsewhere, irrelevant since edges are absent there).

  • Strength. Theorem 3.1 is stated for computable L, but the construction needs nothing about L: with arbitrary ρ every language is in 𝓛_nowait (Lnowait_eq_univ). With the paper's computability requirement on ρ, ζ, decidable L gives a computable G₂ (see Computable.lean).

Section 4 (Theorem 4.1)

  • Easy inclusion (regular ⊆ 𝓛_wait): as in the paper, a DFA is a static TVG (ofDFA). The paper says determinism is needed; our ofDFA_lang works for any pause rule allowing immediate departure. (Remark, not a mistake.)

  • Hard inclusion: different proof. The paper's route (the quasi-order ≺ on words, Proposition 4.4, Theorem 4.12 via Lemma 4.13 and Lemma 4.10, then Harju–Ilie, Theorem 4.7) is not formalized. We prove L_wait(G) regular via walks: schedulable edge sequences are subword-closed (Sched.sublist, the corrected Lemma 4.2), hence regular (Haines' theorem, from Higman's lemma); intersect with the regular set of walks from I to F, then project edges to labels. So Theorem 4.7, Corollary 4.6, Propositions 4.3, 4.4, 4.8, 4.9, Theorem 4.12, Lemma 4.13 are neither used nor checked in Lean; Lemma 4.10 is refuted (lemma_4_10_false).

  • Lemma 4.10 (wqo Q ⇒ (𝒫(Q), ≤_P) wqo) is false for arbitrary subsets (Rado's example, lemma_4_10_false). ❌.

  • Proof of Theorem 4.12 / Lemma 4.13 (examined, not formalized): Lemma 4.13 needs x ∈ Down≺(X) \ X whenever ax ∈ X, i.e. x ≺ ax, which fails in general because ≺ requires equal endpoints (one edge u →a v: ε ⊀ a). The proof of Theorem 4.12 identifies x ≺ y with x ⊆ y ("ie x ⊆ y", contradicting Remark 4.14), asserts without argument that the y's can be chosen distinct, and applies Lemma 4.10 to infinite sets. Part of the 🛠️ item for Theorem 4.1 in the README.

  • Proposition 4.8 (monotonicity), left part: ≺ quantifies over journeys starting at date 0, but the y-part of a journey of zy starts later. Works under our reading (waiting at the start node), fails under the literal one together with Lemma 4.2. Covered by the ✏️ start-date item.

  • Corollary 4.6 is printed as "Down≺(L)L" (missing relation symbol). Typo.

  • Section 2, journeys in G: presence is required only "for 1 ≤ i < k" (the last edge would be unconstrained); should be 1 ≤ i ≤ k. Typo; the TVG-automaton journey definition and our Journey require every edge present.

Section 5 (Theorem 5.1)

  • Typos in G_{2,d}: presence tests "⌊t/(d+1)⌋ ∈ φ_d(Σ*)" should be ∈ φ(Σ*), and the latency of the edges into v₂ uses "ψ_d⁻¹(t)" for ψ_d(t). "time-expanded by a factor d" should be d + 1. Fixed in G2d.

  • Proof gap: the proof shows only that every (computable) L is L_wait[d](G_{2,d}) (G2d_LwaitAtMost), i.e. computable languages ⊆ 𝓛_wait[d]. The equality of families needs 𝓛_wait[d] ⊆ 𝓛_nowait (never argued) and 𝓛_nowait ⊆ 𝓛_wait[d] (would need 𝓛_nowait ⊆ computable languages, not proved in the paper). We prove both by direct simulations, for arbitrary TVGs: dilate (time dilation, dilate_LwaitAtMost) and withPause (nodes V × Fin (d+1) encode the next pause, withPause_Lnowait). Both preserve computability (Computable.lean).

  • The final remark "|φ_d(w) − φ_d(w')| > d, minimal when the last letters are i, i+1" is imprecise (words of different lengths), but the claim follows from injectivity of φ; our proof does not use it.

  • The paper writes both "d > 0" and "for any fixed d ≥ 0"; we allow all d ≥ 0 (d = 0 is L_nowait, LwaitAtMost_zero).

Not formalized

  • Theorem 4.7 (Harju–Ilie), cited; not needed.

  • Whether (Σ*, ≺) is a well quasi-order (Proposition 4.4). Probably true via better-quasi-order theory (the Smyth-type order over walks, which form a bqo), but neither proved nor refuted here.

  • Remark about timed automata (Section 2).
