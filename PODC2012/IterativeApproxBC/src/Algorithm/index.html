3 and 4 Algorithm 1: node `i` drops the `f` smallest and the `f` largest received values (ties broken arbitrarily) and sets its state to the average of its own state and the remaining `|N_i⁻| - 2f` values, all with weight `a_i = 1 / (|N_i⁻| + 1 - 2f)`. * `alg1Step G f` — Algorithm 1 as a *step relation*: any valid trimming may be used, in every iteration anew (so tie-breaking may be arbitrary, time-dependent and even adversarial). * `TieBreak G f` — a deterministic tie-breaking rule; `TieBreak.alg` is then an `IterAlg` (a transition function `Z_i` of the paper), whose runs are runs of `alg1Step` (`TieBreak.isRun`). `TieBreak.nonempty`: such a rule exists if every `|N_i⁻| ≥ 2f`. * `alg1_lower_step`, `alg1_upper_step` — Lemmas 3 and 4 (with arbitrary lower/upper bounds `ψ`, `Ψ` of the fault-free states), plus the "`f + 1` in-neighbours in `P`" step used in Claim 1. * `alg1_validStep` — **Theorem 2** (validity (1)). -/ open Finset Cslib.ApproxConsensus namespace
