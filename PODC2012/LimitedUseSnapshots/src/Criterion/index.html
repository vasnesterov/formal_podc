import Papers.PODC2012.LimitedUseSnapshots.Spec import Papers.PODC2012.LimitedUseSnapshots.CriterionAux import Mathlib.Data.Finset.Max /-! # A linearizability criterion for 2-component max arrays History-level sufficient condition for an execution of a max-array implementation to be linearizable, in the style of the paper's proof of Theorem 3.4 (and of its erratum): the results of the scans are pairwise comparable (H1); every scan sees the contribution of every operation that precedes it (H2); and every non-zero component `w` of a scan result has a *witness* update with value `w` that is invoked before the scan returns and whose real-time predecessors are all seen by the scan (H3). The linearization orders the completed scans by (sum of the result, response position) and inserts every invoked update immediately before the first scan that may see it. -/ namespace Podc.LimitedUseSnapshots open Cslib.SharedMemory variable {P : Type} [DecidableEq P] /-! ### Proof of the criterion Scans are ordered by `sk` = (sum of the result, response position).
