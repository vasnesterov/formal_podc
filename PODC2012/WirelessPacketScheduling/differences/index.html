Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Dynamic Packet Scheduling in Wireless Networks — PODC 2012.

Paper: Kesselheim, Dynamic Packet Scheduling in Wireless Networks, PODC 2012, arXiv 1203.1226v1. Formalized: Theorem 1 (Section 3) and Theorem 20 (Section 8).

Theorem 1 (Algorithm 1)

  1. Theorem 1 is false (statement-level, ❌). theorem1_false, theorem1_false_ln.

    • Instance. W = I on 200 links, 5100 packets per link (n = 1 020 000, I = 5100), φ = 1. The static algorithm is all-or-nothing: if ‖W R_S‖∞ ≤ I', it delivers S in I' steps (f ≡ 1); otherwise it delivers nothing.

    • Result. Algorithm 1 fails with probability ≥ 1 − 60·(24/25)^200 > 0.98 > 1/n.

    • Hypotheses. All of the paper's hypotheses hold. 𝒜(I', n') succeeds with probability 1 whenever ‖W R_S‖∞ ≤ I'; the hypothesis says nothing about inputs beyond that bound (an algorithm that delivers most of an overloaded input would not give a counterexample; the falsity uses exactly this freedom, which the proof's Y_j also assumes away). φ = 1 is a constant. The single-hop instance is exactly the setting of Section 3, with m = max(|E|, D) = |E| = 200.

    • Why not excused. It is not degenerate, since n is large and φ = 1 is the standard choice. The repair changes the algorithm (ψ doubled) or the leading constant (2 f(mχ) → 4 f(mχ)), and that constant is part of the claim (it is the throughput in Section 4). No implied assumption rescues it, because the paper's own proof treats overloaded classes as total failures.

    • Scope. Theorem 1 is about the paper's specific Algorithm 1. The title and abstract are about the dynamic stable protocol built from static algorithms with their throughput. Section 4 is stated for algorithms with f(m) I + g(m,n) schedules and is not refuted. The applications that go through Algorithm 1 (Cor. 13, 14; Sec. 7.2) are only unestablished, and probably hold with a constant factor 2 worse.

    • Which log. The paper writes "log" without a base. The counterexample holds for base 2 and base e; the claim is parametrized by the base b (Theorem1Claim b).

    • Our reading of "success". "Algorithm 1 uses at most X steps" requires all packets to be delivered when Algorithm 1 ends. Algorithm 1 has a fixed schedule, so its length is deterministic (treated separately in Time.lean). The probabilistic content is that every packet is delivered, and that is what fails.

  2. Root cause (proof, Claim 2).

    • The error. E[(W R_j^{(i-1)})(e)] ≤ ‖W R^{(i-1)}‖∞/ψ ≤ χ/2 is false. With ψ = ⌈2^{-i+1} I/χ⌉ and ‖W R^{(i-1)}‖ ≤ 2^{-(i-1)} I, one only gets ≤ χ.

    • The repair. ψ = ⌈2^{-i+2} I/χ⌉ would fix this step and change the time bound to 4 f(mχ) I + …. We did not formalize the repaired theorem; it needs multiplicative Chernoff bounds for weighted sums and negative association of balls-into-bins loads.

  3. Further gaps in the proof of Claim 2 (not formalized, documented only).

    • ψ ≥ 4(ξ − i + 2φ log n) is false for i close to ξ. The definition of ξ gives only 2^{-i+1} I/χ > 2^{ξ-i+1} φ log n, i.e. ψ > 2φ log n at i = ξ. Fix: define ξ with a larger constant (e.g. 16φχ log n) and run the clean-up with that parameter.

    • ‖W R^{(i)}‖ ≤ χ(∑Y_j + ∑Z_j) < χψ/2 only gives ≤ 2^{-i} I + χ/2, because of the ceiling.

    • E[∏ e^{4Y_j}] ≤ ∏ E[e^{4Y_j}]: the FKG sketch is not a proof. Negative association (Dubhashi–Ranjan) gives it.

    • E[Z_j] ≤ 1/(8e) uses that 𝒜(χ, mχ) fails with probability ≤ 1/(mχ) and that mχ ≥ 8e. That is fine, since χ ≥ 54.

    • The Chernoff estimate for Z needs the form (eμ/R)^R. The R ≥ 6μ form does not apply (ψ/4 < 6ψ/(8e)). Fine.

  4. Step bound. alg1Time_le proves the displayed estimate exactly, for I ≥ 0, f(mχ) ≥ 0 and m ≥ 1. The paper's = 2 f(mχ) I + O(log n · f(mχ) + …) drops a factor χ = Θ(log m) in the middle term. The correct form O(log n · log m · f(mχ)) matches the introduction's O(I log m + log n log² m + log² n log m). This is a typo (✏️) and is not formalized as an asymptotic statement.

  5. Typos.

    • 2^{-ψ/4} ≤ 2^{ξ-i+2φ log n} (sign).

    • 2^ξ/2^i should be 2^i/2^ξ in Claim 2 and in the sum after it.

    • "one of the last φ log n executions" vs. ⌈φ⌉ + 1 in Algorithm 1.

    • "(W · R_j^{(i+1)})(e)" should be R_j^{(i-1)}.

    • "for each constant φ ≥ 0": φ = 0 makes ξ undefined. We require φ > 0.

  6. Modelling choices.

    • Algorithm 1. We use max ψ 1 delay values so that the delay type is never empty; this is irrelevant whenever the loop runs.

    • Black box. 𝒜 is any PMF-valued function returning a subset of its input.

    • Delays. All packets get delays, but only those of remaining packets matter. This is equivalent to drawing delays for the remaining packets only.

Theorem 20 (local clocks)

  1. The paper's chain at λ = ln m/m is wrong (proof). It claims (1 − (1−1/m)λ)^{m−1} < 1/e^{ln m} ≤ λ.

    • The first inequality is false for every m: the left side is ≈ m^{-(1-1/m)²} > 1/m.

    • For m = 4 the left side even exceeds λ (paper_step_fails_four). For m ≥ 5 it is < λ, which is all the proof needs.

    • Repair. Our proof removes the factor 1 − 1/m: the window length k can be taken arbitrarily large. The criterion becomes (1 − λ)^{m−1} < λ, which holds at λ = ln m/m for all m ≥ 4 (key_ineq).

  2. Strictness (proof gap). "γ-competitive" with γ = m/(2 ln m) and OPT rate 1/2 asks for stability at all λ < ln m/m. The paper shows instability only for λ ≥ ln m/m, which is not a contradiction. We repair this by continuity: for m ≥ 4 there is λ < ln m/m with (1−λ)^{m−1} < λ (exists_rate_below, theorem_20).

  3. m = 2, 3 (claim inside the proof false; statement unestablished). The proof claims that for λ ≥ ln m/m no acknowledgement-based local-clock protocol is stable on the network of Figure 1. This is false for m = 2, 3. The protocol "send each packet in its arrival slot" needs no clock, and makes the long link's opportunities i.i.d. with probability (1−λ)^{m−1}. That is > λ at λ = ln m/m (rate_lt_opp_two/three), so the long link's queue is stable (immediate_stable_two/three). Short links have empty queues.

    • Theorem 20 quantifies over all networks of size m. So for m ≤ 3 it is not refuted, only unestablished by this proof.

    • Our theorem_20 covers m ≥ 4.

    • m ≤ 3 (two or three links, competitive ratio ≈ 1.37–1.44) is a corner of the asymptotic statement (🔧: the condition m ≥ 4 saves the intent).

  4. Model of "acknowledgement-based with only a local clock" (modelling choice). The paper argues informally. We model the consequence of this restriction on the network of Figure 1:

    • Short links. Each runs as an independent process in its own local time, because its feedback is always "success". We take an arbitrary LinkExec on its own probability space, combined by Measure.pi.

    • Clocks. The clocks differ by adversarial switch-on times d_i. Before switch-on a link is silent, and its arrivals start at switch-on. This is the paper's "delaying the start of the protocol at a link does not change this behavior".

    • Long link. It is arbitrary: any process on a probability space mapping measure-preservingly to the short links' randomness. Successful transmissions are only allowed in silent slots. This is more general than the paper (which needs only ack-based).

    • Not formalized. We do not formalize general protocols as functions of histories, nor the reduction from them to this model.

  5. Stability ⇒ window throughput. The paper asserts that stability yields k with at least (1−1/m)λk expected transmissions in every window of length k (with a typo: (1−1/m)λ). We prove λk − B for expected queue length ≤ B (window_tx), via queue conservation. This needs a uniform bound on the expected queue, which is the stability notion of Section 4.1 ("expected queue lengths bounded at any time").

  6. Randomized offsets → deterministic offsets. The paper picks random delays. We average over all offset vectors in [k]^{m−1} and conclude that some deterministic vector makes every long-link execution unstable (contradiction with a uniform bound over the finitely many vectors).

  7. Positive side (global clock, immediate protocol). These are modelled directly as the long link's queue Markov chain (Queue.slot; TDMA frame = a slot without plus a slot with an opportunity), not derived from the general model:

    • Slot order. Arrival first, then a possible transmission.

    • TDMA. Queue.tdma_stable covers the long link. A short link under TDMA is the same queue with the slots swapped (not separately formalized).

  8. Not formalized.

    • The competitive-ratio definition itself; the statement is phrased via rates.

    • The SINR realization of Figure 1 (short links never interfere with each other or get interfered with; the long link is blocked by any short transmission). We take these interference rules as the network's definition.

Not formalized at all

  • Section 4 (Theorem 3, Lemma 4–7, Theorem 8).

    • Lemma 7 has visible typos: Pr[Δ = −1] = 1 should be q, and Pr[Δ = iH+1] = a/(1−b)·b^i does not give a probability distribution. These were not checked further.

  • Theorem 11; Section 6 corollaries; Section 7 (Lemma 15, 17, Theorem 19).
