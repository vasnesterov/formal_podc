Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The generic simulation of Figure 3 does not work for Σ, contrary to Section 4.4 and Theorem 4: with two simulators it produces runs whose simulated failure detector is not in Σ*, and no repaired simulation is proved. The simulations for Ω_k (Theorem 2) and ◇P (Theorem 3) hold, and so does the Ω*-based consensus algorithm (Theorem 5), whose agreement proof needed a stronger invariant. The converse simulation (Theorem 1) and the other classes of Section 4.4 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Section 4.4 and Theorem 4 (Figure 3 simulates IIS_n[Σ*] in ARW_n[Σ])

With two simulators, a legal Σ history and a fair failure-free schedule, Figure 3 with PROP_{◇P} makes q₁ weakly correct while the simulated quorums contain q₁ forever, even at the strongly correct q₀; so the simulated detector is not in Σ* (Fig3.sigma_simulation_false).

Impact. The claim that one predicate covers P, Σ, S, ◇S, S_x, ◇S_x fails for Σ; Theorem 4 ("for the failure detector classes studied in this paper", here t = n − 1) is false for Σ. The Ω_k and ◇P simulations (Theorems 2, 3, Corollary 1) are not affected.

Details

p₀ always executes its whole round before p₁ starts. Then p₀ never sees p₁, so SC = {q₀} and q₁ is weakly correct. The paper allows this: Section 4.4 itself notes that with Σ not every correct simulator yields a strongly correct process. In the rounds p₀ owns, its quorum is {p₀}, so it does not wait. In the other rounds (line 4, no wait) its quorum is {p₀, p₁}. That is a legal Σ history: every quorum contains p₀. p₁'s quorum is always {p₀, p₁} and, as owner, it waits for p₀, which has already written. C*.read() returns FD_VAL[i], the last value read, so the simulated Σ* output of q₀ is {q₀, q₁} in every odd round. Σ* requires quorums to eventually contain only strongly correct processes. The proof in Section 4.4 ("can be easily translated") only covers the values read in owned rounds, where the owner waited for its quorum.

Repair

Not formalized. Returning only values used in owned rounds would fix the outputs of strongly correct processes, but the literal Σ* also constrains weakly correct processes, whose quorums (here {q₀, q₁} at q₁) need not be strongly correct.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 3 / Claim C1

🛠️ Lemma 3 / Claim C1 (agreement of the consensus algorithm of Figure 5)

The induction for Claim C1 ("all val_ld_seen = true tuples of a round carry the same estimate") ignores that line 13 may adopt the leader's own estimate (first disjunct of line 12) rather than a voted one, and both may be visible at once.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Exec.agreement, theorem5), for every resolution of the choices at lines 12 and 15 and for arbitrary leader outputs.

Details

The leader's estimate can change after it was voted for: line 15 lets it adopt a value when its view is mixed, and it may have followed another leader meanwhile. The proof needs to know that the leader named by the voted tuples writes the voted value. The final step of Lemma 3 ("in both cases they adopt v") has the same gap.

Repair

A stronger invariant Inv: (C1) the true, undecided tuples of a round share estimate and leader ℓ, and (L) p_ℓ's own tuple (if undecided) carries the same estimate (Exec.inv). It is preserved by a case analysis on containment and immediacy (Exec.inv_succ): a process seeing p_ℓ in a view where all processes name ℓ as leader forces p_ℓ's own view to be of that kind too.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • IIS (Model.lean): an infinite schedule of one-shot immediate snapshot rounds (views satisfy self-inclusion, containment, immediacy), crash failures (non-increasing participation), any number of crashes, asynchrony (all view patterns allowed). Strongly correct processes SC = ⋃ SC_k literally as in Section 2.6 (with "i writes" added to SC₀, which only matters if nobody is correct).

  • Figure 5 (Consensus.lean): the failure-detector output read at line 4 is arbitrary; Ω* (some strongly correct ℓ is eventually output to everybody) is assumed only for termination. The "∃ v" choices at lines 12 and 15 are nondeterministic (Upd allows every witness).

  • Figure 3 (Fig3.lean, Fig3Live.lean): ARW simulators with atomic collect and arw_write_snapshot steps (a sequential run of these objects is a legal run), any fair schedule (correct = infinitely many steps, crash = no more steps), failure-detector histories H i t arbitrary up to the class properties (Ω_k: eventually the same set containing a correct process; ◇P: eventually exactly the correct ones). The simulated IIS schedule is read off the run (simSchedule). Positive results are thus for runs whose base objects behave atomically.
