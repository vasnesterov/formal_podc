Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Failure Detectors for the IIS Model — PODC 2012.

Paper: M. Raynal, J. Stainer, Increasing the Power of the Iterated Immediate Snapshot Model with Failure Detectors, PODC 2012 (brief announcement). Formalized from the full version: IRISA research report PI 1991, February 2012 (hal-00670154v1), whose numbering we follow.

Modelling choices

  • IIS schedules (Model.lean, Schedule). A round is the set of participants of IS[r] and their views, with self-inclusion, containment and immediacy as fields (ISRound). Participation is non-increasing (a process that skips a round has crashed). Rounds are numbered from 0 in Schedule (the paper starts at 1); Fig3*.lean keep the paper's numbering and convert explicitly. Any number of crashes is allowed; nothing requires a correct process unless a theorem says so.

  • Strongly correct processes (Schedule.SCk, Schedule.SC). Literal Section 2.6, with one change: in SC₀ we also require that i writes in the round. The two definitions agree whenever some process is correct (the paper assumes this); without a correct process the paper's SC₀ would contain every process because "∀ j ∈ C" is vacuous. SC is the union of the chain (the paper's SC_K, K the stabilization index; the same set since the chain is increasing, SCk_mono).

  • Algorithm of Figure 5 (Consensus.lean). The tuple ⟨est, r_leader, val_ld_seen, dec⟩ is Loc, ⊥ is none. Line 5 is wr. Lines 7–18 are the relation Upd, one constructor per branch. The choices "∃ v" at lines 12 and 15 are nondeterministic: Upd admits every witness, so all safety results hold for every resolution. The leader read at line 4 is an arbitrary function ld : ℕ → P → P (the failure-detector output). Ω* is used only for termination (OmegaStar: some strongly correct ℓ is output by every participant from some round on). Crashed processes keep their last state (Exec.frozen); a decision is dec = true with value est (Exec.Decides).

  • Simulation of Figure 3 (Fig3.lean, Fig3Live.lean). An operational semantics with atomic steps: one iteration of the repeat loop of lines 2–3 (collect + read of C + test of PROP), line 4, and lines 6–7 (arw_write_snapshot). S[r].collect() and S[r].arw_write_snapshot() are atomic: a sequential execution is one legal execution of these objects (Figure 4 run sequentially returns exactly the writers so far). Positive results proved in this model (Theorems 2 and 3) are therefore proved for the runs whose base objects behave atomically. A counterexample in it (Section 4.4) is a legal run of the paper's algorithm. A failure-detector history is a function H i t of the simulator and the global time. A simulator is correct iff it is scheduled infinitely often (SCorrect); a crash is the end of its steps. The output of the simulated C* at q_i in round r (Cfg.out) is FD_VAL[i] when q_i invokes S[r].arw_write_snapshot(). With the other natural convention (value at the beginning of round r, i.e. the value of round r − 1) the Section 4.4 counterexample is unchanged, since the outputs alternate.

  • Ω_k (IsOmegaK): only "eventually all output the same L containing a correct process"; the constraint |L| = k is dropped (it is not used). ◇P (IsDiamondP): eventually every simulator trusts exactly the correct simulators. Σ (IsSigma): only the intersection property; the completeness property is void in the failure-free run used.

Statement-level issues

  • Section 4.4 and Theorem 4 false for Σ (Fig3.sigma_simulation_false). Two simulators, failure-free, a legal Σ history and a fair schedule. Figure 3 with PROP_{◇P} (the predicate Section 4.4 prescribes) yields an IIS execution in which q_1 is weakly correct while the simulated Σ* outputs contain q_1 forever, also at the strongly correct q_0. So the simulated failure detector is not in Σ* and the produced run is not an IIS_2[Σ*] run. Theorem 4 ("for the failure detector classes studied in this paper", t = n − 1 = 1, where the extra conjunct |arw_view| ≥ n − t − 1 = 0 is void) is false for Σ. Label ❌. The claim is a correctness claim about the paper's simulation and a numbered theorem. The equivalences of Theorem 2 / Corollary 1 (Ω_k) and Theorem 3 (◇P) are unaffected, and Σ, S, ◇S, S_x, ◇S_x are handled in a one-paragraph remark (Section 4.4) and in the t-resilience extension (Section 5). The abstract says the simulations work "for a large family of failure detector classes". That claim stays true for {Ω_k}_k, ◇P, P. The introduction is more specific ("This simulation is generic in the sense that it works for any of the previously cited failure detectors", which include Σ), but the equivalence of ARW_n[C] and IIS_n[C*] is existential and is not refuted for Σ (only this simulation is). Not an implied-assumption or typo case: repairing it changes the simulation (e.g. C*.read() must return the value used at the last owned round), and even then the literal Σ* also constrains weakly correct processes, whose quorums need not consist of strongly correct processes (q_1's quorum is {q_0, q_1} in our run).

    • The same history is also an S and a ◇S history (p_0 is never suspected), and the run violates the completeness of S* / ◇S* (q_1 is not permanently suspected by q_0). We did not formalize S, ◇S, S_x, ◇S_x.

  • Theorems 2 and 3 (Ω_k, ◇P): true in the atomic-step model (Fig3Live.lean): liveness, the correspondence between correct simulators and correct simulated processes, and the Ω*_k / ◇P* properties of the simulated detector. The claim "produces an execution that could have been obtained in IIS_n[C*]" is checked through these properties plus the IS properties of simSchedule. The simulated algorithm A and the task are not modelled.

  • Theorem 5 / Lemmas 1–3 (consensus in IIS_n[Ω*]): true (theorem5). Proof of Lemma 3 repaired, see below. Proofs of Lemmas 1 and 2 are fine up to routine details. Label 🛠️.

Proof gaps

  • Claim C1 (inside the proof of Lemma 3, also used by Lemma 2). The induction ("all the processes that write ⟨−, ℓ, true, false⟩ during round r + 1 have adopted their estimate at line 13, so it is the only one present") ignores that line 13 may adopt the estimate of the leader p_ℓ itself (first disjunct of line 12) instead of the estimate of a val_ld_seen = true tuple (second disjunct). Both may be present in the same view, and line 12 does not say which one to take. The leader's own estimate may have changed since it was voted for (line 15 lets it adopt a value, or it may have followed another leader meanwhile). The last step of the proof of Lemma 3 ("in both cases they adopt v") has the same gap. Repair: the stronger invariant Inv adds (L), "the tuple written by the leader named by the true tuples carries the same estimate". Its preservation (Exec.inv_succ) is a case analysis using containment and immediacy: a process that sees p_ℓ in a uniform view forces p_ℓ's view to be uniform as well. Agreement then holds for every resolution of the choices at lines 12 and 15 (Exec.agreement).

  • Lemma 2 (termination) is proved in the stronger form "every correct process decides" (Exec.termination_correct). Weakly correct processes decide too, because the decision of a strongly correct process reaches every correct process (Exec.all_decide_of_SC).

  • The agreement and validity proofs do not use Ω* at all (arbitrary leader outputs).

Other remarks

  • Not formalized: Theorem 1 (simulation of ARW_n[C] in IIS_n[C*], the Borowsky–Gafni simulation of Figure 2), Corollary 1 and the solvability equivalences (no task/solvability layer), Theorem 4 beyond the Σ counterexample and its round-level core thm4b_core.

  • Corollary 1 needs an argument the paper does not give. The paper never defines solving a task in IIS_n[C*]. Lemma 2 suggests that only strongly correct processes must decide. Then the simulation of Theorem 2 leaves correct ARW simulators whose simulated process is weakly correct without a decision. Under the other reading (every correct IIS process decides), Theorem 1 leaves weakly correct IIS processes without a decision. For colorless tasks either gap closes by letting undecided processes adopt a decision written by another (in IIS weakly correct processes see the strongly correct ones forever; in ARW a simulator can read a shared decision register). This is the standard trick and presumably why Corollary 1 is restricted to colorless tasks, but "immediate consequence" omits it. Not formalized.

  • Section 2.6's informal claims are formalized: SC₀ ≠ ∅ if some process is correct (SCk_zero_nonempty), SC ⊆ C (correct_of_mem_SC), the chain is increasing (SCk_mono), and after some round strongly correct processes see only strongly correct processes (eventually_view_SC, Section 3.2).

  • Round-level cores of the strong-correctness arguments (SimulationCores.lean): thm3_core (Theorem 3), thm2c_core (Theorem 2(c)), thm4b_core (Theorem 4(b)).
