Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Price of Equivocation in Byzantine Agreement — PODC 2012.

Jaffe, Moscibroda, Sen, On the Price of Equivocation in Byzantine Agreement, PODC 2012 (10-page proceedings version; the "full version" with the deferred proofs was not available).

Statement-level issues

  1. Theorem 13 (Uₙ(h)) is wrong for n ≢ h (mod 2). The paper states Uₙ(h) = C(n,3) − ((n−h)/2)·h² + 1 for h ≤ n/3 (also in the introduction). Claim 14 minimises abc over integers a, b, c ≥ h with a + b + c ≥ (n + 3h)/2; its proof treats only n ≡ h (mod 2) ("the second case is proved similarly"). For n ≢ h the minimum is h²·⌈(n−h)/2⌉, so the correct value is C(n,3) − h²⌈(n−h)/2⌉ + 1 (theorem_13), which is smaller than the displayed one by h²/2 (theorem_13_false). Example: U₄(1) = 3, while the formula gives 3.5 (U_four_one). For odd h the displayed value is not even an integer, although Uₙ(h) is defined as a minimum integer. 🔧. The intended version follows from the paper's own Claim 14, which is about integers with a + b + c ≥ (n+3h)/2, and the fix is a rounding symbol. The abstract only claims asymptotically tight bounds; Uₙ is "our final result" of the introduction. The paper's proof does not compute the ceiling: it explicitly restricts to n ≡ h (mod 2) and defers the other parity as "proved similarly". Carrying out that case with the paper's own argument (integer c ≥ (n+3h)/2 − 2h) gives exactly c = ⌈(n−h)/2⌉, with the same extremal configuration (two classes of size h, the third as small as allowed). The displayed value is not an integer for odd h and odd n − h, while Uₙ(h) is defined as an integer. So the statement is wrong only in the parity case the proof sets aside; the extremal structure and every asymptotic consequence are unchanged.

  2. Theorem 2 fails for n = 0. With no vertices there are no S ≠ T, so every hypergraph is f-tolerant, but no hypergraph is (−2f)-disjoint (theorem_2_card_zero). Degenerate (✏️); we assume n ≥ 1.

  3. Theorem 2 for f ≥ n/2. Then h = n − 2f ≤ 0. We use natural-number subtraction (h = 0 when 2f ≥ n). Both sides are false for every n ≥ 1 in this range, with the paper's integer h as with ours (not_fTolerant_of_le_two_mul, not_hDisjoint_zero; for negative h the colouring A = B = ∅, C = V is valid as well), so the equivalence is unaffected.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. Proposition 15, second bullet, is false (claim inside the proof of Claim 14, inside the proof of Theorem 13; 🔧). It says the minimum of g(a,b) = ab((n+3h)/2 − a − b) along the faces of the triangle P is (n+h)²h/16. Along each face g is a concave quadratic, and (n+h)²h/16 is its maximum there; the minimum along a face is attained at its endpoints and equals h²(n−h)/2. Counterexample n = 4, h = 1, (a,b) = (1,1): g = 3/2 < 25/16 (prop_15_face_false). The final answer of Claim 14 is still right because the vertex values are also compared ("Comparison"); we replace the real-variable argument by the two-line integer inequality abc ≥ h²(a+b+c−2h) for a,b,c ≥ h (mul_mul_ge, card_crossing_ge).

  2. Theorem 2: proof deferred to the full version. We supply one (theorem_2): (⇐) is the paper's Figure 1 (A = S \ Z, B = T \ Z, C = S ∩ T) and holds for every f; (⇒) shrinks a valid colouring to sizes p, q, c ≥ h with p + q + c = 2n − 3f and pads with fresh faulty vertices; this is where f ≥ n/3 is used (3h ≤ 2n − 3f ⇔ n ≤ 3f).

  3. Lemma 4: proof deferred. Supplied (lemma_4), via components and a sub-sum lemma; it holds also for odd |U| (bisection = parts ⌊|U|/2⌋, ⌈|U|/2⌉).

  4. Theorem 3 assumes 3 ∣ n silently (uses |C| = n/3 and a sum over C(n, n/3); the text says |C| ≤ n/3 but uses equality). We prove the exact bound for n = 3k (theorem_3, theorem_3': |E| ≥ (n−1)(n−2)/(4n/3−2)) and, for every n and h ≤ n/3, |E| ≥ 3n/4 − 2 (theorem_3_asymptotic) as the precise form of (3/4)n(1 − o(1)). The statement of Theorem 3 (for every h with h ≤ n/3) is therefore true for every n; only the proof is restricted to 3 ∣ n. This is a proof-level gap (repaired, 🔧), not a statement issue.

  5. Lemma 6 / Theorem 5: parity (🔧). Lemma 6 uses n/2-sets (needs n even), and Theorem 5 applies it with n' = n − h and |B| = n'/2, not an integer when n − h is odd. We prove a general Lemma 6 for (i, b)-crossing graphs (lemma_6_general), the paper's form for even n (lemma_6), and Theorem 5 with b = ⌈n'/2⌉, in the explicit form n(n−1)(n−2) ≤ 24h²|E| for 1 ≤ h ≤ n/6 (theorem_5, theorem_5_T); the paper's arithmetic gives the slightly weaker |E| ≥ 25n⁴/(864h²(n−2)). Theorem 5 needs h ≥ 1 (implicit in the paper).

  6. Theorem 7 (upper bound Tₙ(h) = O(n³/h²)): gap in the existence of the expanders (🛠️). The paper builds L(G) from a near-Ramanujan graph G of degree d = Θ(n/h). It asserts the existence of Ramanujan graphs "for every n and d" via Friedman's theorem, but Friedman's theorem is about fixed d as n → ∞, while here d grows with n unless h = Θ(n) (spectral bounds for random regular graphs of growing degree came later). Moreover the required degree d > 6n/(k²h) ≈ 210·n/h (k = (√2−1)/√6) exceeds n − 1 for h ≲ 210, so for such h no such graph exists (the bound is then trivially true, as the complete hypergraph has ≤ n³/6 edges). The theorem is true: we prove Tₙ(h)·h² ≤ n³ for all 1 ≤ h ≤ n/3 by the counting argument the paper mentions but does not give (a greedy hitting set for the ≤ 4ⁿ crossing families, each of size ≥ h²n/3; theorem_7, T_le). The paper's own route is formalized conditionally: Lemmas 8 and 9 and Claim 10 hold under the conclusion of the expander mixing lemma, taken as a hypothesis MixingBound G d λ (the EML itself and the spectral definition of an (n,d,λ)-expander are not formalized), giving HDisjoint (lift G) h (hDisjoint_lift) and Tₙ(h) ≤ n·C(d,2) for a d-regular such G (T_le_of_expander). Small differences: Lemma 8 needs T ≠ ∅; Lemma 9 needs A, B ≠ ∅ and pairwise disjointness, but not a ≤ b ≤ c (the ordering is used only in Claim 10); Claim 10 holds with a + b + c ≥ (n+3h)/2 (the paper writes =). The algebra of Lemmas 8, 9 and Claim 10 is correct. Non-vacuity: the complete graph satisfies MixingBound with d = n − 1, λ = 1 (mixingBound_top).

Modelling choices

  • A 3-uniform hypergraph is a Finset (Finset V) of 3-sets over a Fintype V (n = |V|); the paper's ordered triple (x, y, z) ∈ E is the set {x, y, z} (its vertices are distinct wherever the paper uses it). The sufficiency number Uₙ(h) and Tₙ(h) are defined on Fin n.

  • Definitions 1 and 2 are transcribed literally: |A|+|B|+|C| ≥ (n+3h)/2 as n + 3h ≤ 2(|A|+|B|+|C|), |S| ≥ n − f as n ≤ |S| + f, Z ⊂ S ∪ T strict. The paper writes S, T, Z ⊂ V and A, B, C ⊂ V; we allow all subsets. For h ≥ 1 this makes no difference: the sets built in our proof of (⇒) have |S|, |T| = n − f < n and |Z| ≤ f < n, and (⇐) is only easier with fewer triples S, T, Z.

  • h and f are natural numbers (see item 3).

  • Byzantine agreement is not modelled. The paper's Theorem 2 is the purely combinatorial equivalence of f-tolerance and (n−2f)-disjointness; the statement "Byzantine agreement is possible in H iff H is f-tolerant (given (2f+1)-connectivity)" is cited from Ravikant et al. [38] (synchronous model) and Bracha–Toueg [9] (asynchronous), and is not formalized here. Accordingly Theorem 1 is formalized as the correctness of the reduction from balanced bipartite independent set (theorem_1_reduction, and through Theorem 2 theorem_1_reduction_fTolerant); NP-completeness of BBIS [3], co-NP membership and polynomial running time are not formalized. The reduction is proved for any 1 ≤ t ≤ |X|, |Y| (the paper has |X| = |Y| = n/2).

  • Asymptotic statements (Θ, Ω, O, o(1)) are replaced by explicit inequalities with explicit constants valid for all n (or all n divisible by 3, as indicated).

Not formalized

  • Theorem 11 (explicit construction for infinitely many n, via Cioabă–Murty expanders; its proof is a sketch: a fixed-degree sequence nᵢ must be matched with the degree Θ(nᵢ/h(nᵢ))), the expander mixing lemma itself, the existence of (near-)Ramanujan graphs, and the comparison statements of Section 2.1 (Tₙ(1) ≥ f(n,3), Tₙ(n/3) ≤ f(n,3) with f(n,3) = n(n−2)/3 from the literature), Section 1.2 (applications to other models).
