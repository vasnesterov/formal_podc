The Price of Equivocation in Byzantine Agreement — PODC 2012

Alexander Jaffe, Thomas Moscibroda, Siddhartha Sen. On the Price of Equivocation in Byzantine Agreement. PODC 2012. DOI 10.1145/2332432.2332491. Formalized version: the 10-page proceedings version (no arXiv version; the "full version" with the deferred proofs was not available). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The equivalence of f-tolerance and (n−2f)-disjointness (Theorem 2), the reduction behind co-NP-hardness (Theorem 1) and the tight bounds Tₙ(h) = Θ(n³/h²) (Theorems 3, 5, 7) all hold. The proof of the upper bound relies on expanders that the cited theorem does not provide and had to be replaced by a counting argument, and the exact formula of Theorem 13 for Uₙ(h) lacks a ceiling. The link to Byzantine agreement itself, taken from earlier work, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 13 (exact value of Uₙ(h) when n ≢ h (mod 2))

The claimed Uₙ(h) = C(n,3) − ((n−h)/2)·h² + 1 lacks a ceiling: for n − h odd the correct value is C(n,3) − h²⌈(n−h)/2⌉ + 1, h²/2 lower; e.g. U₄(1) = 3, while the formula gives 3.5 (theorem_13_false, U_four_one).

Impact. The extremal structure and every asymptotic consequence are unchanged, and we prove the corrected formula for all h ≤ n/3 (theorem_13).

Details

The proof minimises abc over integers a, b, c ≥ h with a + b + c ≥ (n+3h)/2. It only treats n ≡ h (mod 2) and says the other case "is proved similarly". When n − h is odd, c must be at least ⌈(n−h)/2⌉. For odd h the paper's formula is not even an integer. In the example, any three of the four triples on 4 vertices form a 1-disjoint hypergraph, two do not.

🔧 Proposition 15 (inside the proof of Theorem 13)

Its second bullet calls (n+h)²h/16 the minimum of g(a,b) = ab((n+3h)/2 − a − b) along each face of the triangle, but that value is the face maximum: n = 4, h = 1, (a,b) = (1,1) gives g = 3/2 < 25/16 (prop_15_face_false).

Impact. The final answer survives because the vertices are compared too; we replace the calculus by an integer inequality (mul_mul_ge).

Details

Along a face, g is a concave quadratic, so the face minimum is at the endpoints.

Repair

We replace the calculus by abc ≥ h²(a+b+c−2h) for a, b, c ≥ h, a two-line integer argument (mul_mul_ge).

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 2 fails for n = 0, where f-tolerance holds vacuously but no hypergraph is (−2f)-disjoint (theorem_2_card_zero); we prove it for n ≥ 1 (theorem_2).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 7 · Theorems 3 and 5

🛠️ Theorem 7 (expanders that are not known to exist)

The proof uses Ramanujan graphs of degree d = Θ(n/h) for every n, justified by Friedman's theorem, which is about fixed d as n → ∞.

Impact. The bound is true: we prove Tₙ(h)·h² ≤ n³ for all 1 ≤ h ≤ n/3 by a counting argument (theorem_7, T_le).

Details

Here d grows unless h = Θ(n). The degree the proof requires, d > 6n/(k²h) ≈ 210·n/h, also exceeds n − 1 when h ≲ 210.

Repair

The counting argument is the one the paper mentions but does not give. The expander route (Lemmas 8, 9, Claim 10, all algebraically correct) is formalized with the expander mixing lemma as a hypothesis (hDisjoint_lift).

🔧 Theorems 3 and 5 (divisibility and parity)

The proof of Theorem 3 silently assumes 3 ∣ n, and Theorem 5 applies Lemma 6 (which needs n even) with |B| = (n−h)/2, which need not be an integer.

Impact. Statements true for every n; repaired with explicit bounds.

Details

  • Theorem 3: we prove the exact bound for n = 3k, and |E| ≥ 3n/4 − 2 for all n and all h ≤ n/3.

  • Theorem 5: we use ⌈(n−h)/2⌉ and get n(n−1)(n−2) ≤ 24h²|E| for 1 ≤ h ≤ n/6.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Purely combinatorial, like the paper's own results. A system of n processors with 3-party partial broadcast channels is a 3-uniform hypergraph E : Finset (Finset V) on a finite type V.

  • FTolerant E f (Definition 1) and HDisjoint E h (Definition 2) are transcribed literally, with h, f ∈ ℕ.

  • Tₙ(h) and Uₙ(h) (Definitions 3 and 7) are defined as sInf over hypergraphs on Fin n.

  • The paper's setting is Byzantine agreement among n = 2f + h processors, at most f of them Byzantine; for [38] a synchronous, (2f+1)-connected network; the asynchronous and partially synchronous models are discussed through [9], [11]. None of this is modelled: Byzantine agreement itself is not formalized. The paper takes "BA is possible in H iff H is f-tolerant (with (2f+1)-connectivity)" from Ravikant et al. [38], and this is not formalized. Theorem 1 is therefore formalized as the correctness of the reduction from balanced bipartite independent set. Via Theorem 2 it is stated both for t-disjointness and for f-tolerance with n' = 2f + t.

  • Asymptotic statements become explicit inequalities valid for all n.

Sufficiency.lean

Proofs supplied. The proofs of Theorem 2 and Lemma 4 are deferred to an unavailable full version; we supply them (theorem_2, lemma_4).

  • Theorem 2: the (⇐) direction is the paper's Figure 1 and needs no hypothesis on f. The (⇒) direction shrinks a valid colouring to sizes p, q, c ≥ h with p + q + c = 2n − 3f and pads it with fresh faulty vertices. This is exactly where f ≥ n/3 is needed. (theorem_2)

  • Lemma 4 also holds for odd n. (lemma_4)

Not formalized. The expander mixing lemma and the existence of (near-)Ramanujan graphs (Lemmas 8, 9 and Claim 10 take the EML's conclusion MixingBound as a hypothesis); Theorem 11 (explicit construction; the printed proof stops at the Cioabă–Murty theorem, which has fixed d, while degree Θ(n/h(n)) is needed, and we did not investigate it further); the link between f-tolerance and Byzantine agreement ([38], [9]) and Section 1.2's applications to other models; NP-completeness of BBIS, co-NP membership and polynomial running time; the comparison with f(n,3) in Section 2.1.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: the complete hypergraph is h-disjoint for h ≥ 1 (hDisjoint_complete), Theorem 13's extremal hypergraph is not (exists_not_hDisjoint), and the complete graph satisfies the MixingBound hypothesis (mixingBound_top).
