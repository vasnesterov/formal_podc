import Papers.PODC2012.NonEquivocationLimits.SmallCases /-! # Non-equivocation *with* transferable authentication: r-broadcast for every `n` Section 3 states that non-equivocation and transferable authentication "together, but not individually, are sufficient to improve the resiliency bound to `n ≥ 2f + 1`" (the positive half is Section 4's translation, applied e.g. to the crash-tolerant r-broadcast of Figure 3). We check the r-broadcast instance directly. **Model.** Messages are *attestations* `valid_p(k, v)` (`Att`): transferable signatures by `p` on a key `k` and a value `v` (`attAuth`: an attestation is its own token, signed by its `signer`). Unforgeability is CSLib's (`Algorithm.Unforging`, `Run.AuthValid`). Non-equivocation (`AttNonEquivocating`): two attestations of the same faulty signer with the same key that the adversary shows to correct processes are equal. **Protocol** (`relay s`, the translation of Figure 3 in spirit). The sender attests `(0, v)` for its input `v` and delivers `v`. Every process that sees an attestation of `s` with key `0` for the first time delivers its value and relays the attestation to everybody, forever. `relay_isRBroadcast`: it solves r-broadcast in every asynchronous run, for **every** `n` and `f`. Together with `claim_3_1` and `claim_3_2`, this confirms the separation of Section 3. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace Podc.NonEquivocationLimits open Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync
