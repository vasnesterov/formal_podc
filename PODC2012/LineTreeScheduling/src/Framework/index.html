import Papers.PODC2012.LineTreeScheduling.Basic /-! # The two-phase primal–dual framework (Sections 3.2 and 6.1) The first phase starts from the zero dual assignment and performs a sequence of iterations `t = 0, 1, …, T-1`; iteration `t` raises every demand instance of a set `Is t`. Raising `d` at the current dual assignment `y` (Section 3.2, and its height variant in Section 6.1): * `δ(d) = s / (1 + h(d) c(d) |π(d)|)` where `s = p(d) - lhs(d)` is the slackness; * `α(a_d) += δ(d)` and `β(e) += c(d) δ(d)` for every critical edge `e ∈ π(d)`. With `h = c = 1` this is the rule of Section 3.2 (`δ(d) = s/(|π(d)|+1)`); with `h` the heights and `c(d) = 2|π(d)|` it is the rule of Section 6.1 (`δ(d) = s/(1 + 2h(d)|π(d)|²)`, `β(e) += 2|π(d)|δ(d)`). In both cases the dual constraint of `d` becomes tight. A `Rule` packages `h`, `c` and `π`. All instances of an iteration are raised simultaneously, each with the `δ` computed from the dual assignment at the beginning of the iteration; the paper does the same (its sets are independent, so the raises do not interact). Main results of this file: * `lhs_run`, `val_run` — closed forms of the dual constraints and of the dual objective after `t` iterations as sums over the earlier raises. * `profit_le_lhs_of_mem` and `not_mem_of_mem_of_lt` — a raised instance is satisfied afterwards and is never raised again. * `sum_raise_le_profitOf` — the charging argument of Lemmas 3.1 and 6.1: if every raised instance not in the final solution `S` is "covered" by later raises of instances of `S` (with total weight `≥ 1`), then `∑_{raised} δ ≤ p(S)`. * `lam_mul_lpValue_le` — the resulting approximation bound `λ · LP ≤ M ·
