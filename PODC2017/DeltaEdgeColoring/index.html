(Δ + o(Δ))-Edge-Coloring and Clique Decompositions — PODC 2017

Leonid Barenboim, Michael Elkin, Tzalik Maimon. Deterministic Distributed (Δ + o(Δ))-Edge-Coloring, and Vertex-Coloring of Graphs with Bounded Diversity. PODC 2017. DOI 10.1145/3087801.3087812. Formalized version: arXiv 1610.06759 v1 (full version with appendix); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The combinatorial correctness of the clique-decomposition colouring (Lemmas 2.1–2.3, Theorems 2.4–2.6), its colour count D^{x+1} S (Theorem 3.2), Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2 (Δ + O(a) colours) and Theorem 5.3 (Δ + o(Δ) colours when a = o(Δ)) all hold. But the last step of Theorem 5.4, which carries the abstract's polylogarithmic-time claim through Corollary 5.5, does not give the stated colour count, and it is not repaired. No running time was checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 5.4, last stage of the proof

The last stage colours subgraphs of in-degree ≤ Δ^{1/x} and out-degree ≤ â^{1/x} "with Δ^{1/x} + â^{1/x} + 3 colours by Theorem 5.2", but Theorem 5.2 does not give that many colours.

Impact. The bound (Δ^{1/x} + â^{1/x} + 3)^x feeds Corollary 5.5's Δ(1 + O(1/log Δ)) colours, and through it the polylogarithmic-time claim. Nothing about Theorem 5.4 is proved in Lean, so the step stands unrepaired.

Details

Theorem 5.2 only gives Δ' + O(a') colours (Δ' + (2+ε)a' − 1 with the paper's own procedure), and those subgraphs can have total degree up to Δ^{1/x} + â^{1/x}. So the exact constant +3 in (Δ^{1/x} + â^{1/x} + 3)^x is not justified as written.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 3.2 for D = 1: the proof assumes D ≥ 2, which the statement does not; 𝒜_1 produces up to ⌊√S⌋⌈S/⌊√S⌋⌉ colours, e.g. 6 > 5 for S = 5. The graph is a disjoint union of cliques, and the S colours of the statement are reached by the colour reduction the paper allows. Our theorem_3_2 assumes D ≥ 2.

  • The (p,q)-clique-decomposition is defined with clique size "is q"; "at most q" is meant.

Typos

  • The proof of Lemma 2.3 (i) speaks of "a clique Q of G'" where a clique of G_i is meant.

  • The heading of Appendix B reads "Proof of Lemma 3.2"; there is no Lemma 3.2, and the proof is that of Theorem 3.2.

  • "See Theorem 5.1" before Theorem 5.4: there is no Theorem 5.1, and Lemma 5.1 is meant.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 3.2, colour bound

🔧 Theorem 3.2, colour bound (appendix proof, inductive step for 𝒜_2)

The inductive step's inequality (D(t−1)+1)·D^{i+1}⌈S/t⌉ ≤ D^{i+2} S is justified only for i ≥ 2, and for 𝒜_2 (i = 1, t = ⌊√S⌋) it fails for arbitrarily large S (paper_inductive_step_fails).

Impact. The proof as written does not establish 𝒜_2, but the parameter the same proof states for the running time closes the step. We prove the bound with a stronger invariant, for every S ≥ 1 and without colour reduction (theorem_3_2, theorem_3_2_algA).

Details

The proof builds 𝒜_{i+1} from 𝒜_i with t = ⌊S^{1/(i+1)}⌋. It uses only the induction hypothesis "𝒜_i uses D^{i+1} S' colours". Then it claims (D(t−1)+1)·D^{i+1}⌈S/t⌉ ≤ D^{i+2} S "for S larger than some constant, since (D−1)(S/t) > Dt because t = ⌊S^{1/(i+1)}⌋ and i ≥ 2". But 𝒜_2 is the case i = 1. There t = ⌊√S⌋, and the inequality fails for arbitrarily large S. For example, D = 2, S = 31, t = 5 gives 9·4·7 = 252 > 248 = 8·31 (paper_inductive_step_fails). In the running-time part the proof uses a different parameter, t = ⌊S^{1/(i+2)}⌋. With that choice the step works for S above a constant (constant S is covered by §3's direct D(S−1)+1-colouring), so the gap is repairable from the paper itself.

Repair

The bound is true for the actual algorithm. For D ≥ 2, the invariant pal_j · (D·S_j − 1) ≤ D^j (D S − 1) holds whenever every level uses some t with t² ≤ S_cur. Here pal_j is the number of subgraphs after j levels and S_j the current clique bound. So 𝒜_x uses fewer than D^{x+1}S colours, for all S ≥ 1, with either choice of t and with no colour-reduction rounds (theorem_3_2, theorem_3_2_algA).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Deterministic synchronous message passing (LOCAL) with unique IDs (LinearOrder V), finite simple graphs. We verify the combinatorial correctness of the algorithms: properness, decomposition properties, colour counts, the round schedule, and the consistency of parallel choices. Running-time claims of the Õ(·) form are not formalized.

  • The subroutines are black boxes given by their output specifications:

    • the (Δ+1)-vertex colouring of [17] is IsDeltaColorer. Non-vacuity: identifiers followed by CSLib's standard colour reduction, isDeltaColorer_reductionColorer;

    • the H-partition of [4] is IsHPartition. It is instantiated with the peeling algorithm formalized in Papers/PODC2010/ArbdefectiveColoring/HPartition.lean: degree 3a (ε = 1) and L = 2(⌊log₂ n⌋+1) sets (exists_hPartition);

    • the edge colourings of the H-sets (via [17] or §4) are given as any proper colouring with k₀ colours. Greedy 2d − 1 colours: exists_hSet_coloring.

  • Clique identification: IsCliqueCover G 𝒬 D S. It is a family of cliques covering every edge, with every vertex in at most D members and every member of size at most S. This covers both the paper's "consistent identification" (e.g. stars of a line graph) and all maximal cliques (maximalCliques, diversity). The bound "every clique of G[U_i] has size ≤ q" is proved when 𝒬 contains every clique (CoversCliques, true for maximal cliques).

  • Algorithm 1 is unrolled level by level (stages). The parallel recursive calls on the colour classes G_i form one call on their disjoint union sameColor G φ. The output pair ⟨φ, ψ⟩ is encoded as φ·B + ψ. "Uses N colours" means all colours are < N.

  • The round-by-round procedures of Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 are formalized as a schedule (EdgeSchedule). Edges inside A/B, resp. inside the H-sets, are pre-coloured. The crossing edge ab (h a < h b) is coloured in round (ℓ−1−h a)·d + lab a b by b. A run is any colouring obeying the local rule (EdgeSchedule.Follows), so we quantify over all of the paper's "any assignment of distinct available colours". Every run is proper, runs exist, and two adjacent edges coloured in the same round are chosen by their common endpoint.

  • Theorem 5.3's Lean conclusion is existential ("some proper edge colouring with N colours", which alone would also follow from Vizing). The algorithm is captured by the colouring the proof builds (⟨φ, ψ⟩ from runs of Theorem 5.2) and by theorem_5_3_combine, which holds for every such φ, ψ.

  • Orientation connector (§5): the acyclic orientation is a rank r. Incoming/outgoing edges are grouped by ID into groups of sIn/sOut, with virtual vertices V × Fin (m+1). The paper's k = ⌈√Δ⌉ groups of size ⌈Δ/k⌉ become groups of size ⌊√Δ⌋+1, and similarly for √d. Asymptotically this is the same.

Remarks on the paper (not mistakes).

  • Theorem 3.2, base case 𝒜_1: the chain of inequalities is valid (the +(D−1)² term is absorbed by the −1 in 2⌊√S⌋ − 1, since t + k ≥ 2⌊√S⌋ and D(D−1) ≥ (D−1)²). It is not tight: the 2 colour-reduction rounds are unnecessary, the count is already ≤ D(DS − 1) < D²S (the case x = 1 of theorem_3_2).

  • Theorem 2.4's S/t^x + 2 can be sharpened to ⌈S/t^x⌉ ≤ S/t^x + 1.

  • Algorithm 1 passes ⌈S/t⌉ as the next S, and the nested ceilings equal ⌈S/t^x⌉.

  • §4: (2t−1)(2k−1) ≤ 4Δ already holds for t = ⌊√Δ⌋, so the extra reduction round is unnecessary (theorem_4_1_count_one).
