Vainstein, *Object Oriented Consensus*, PODC 2017 (brief announcement); full version (16 pp.) [AACV]. The objects of Section 2 are specified by the input/output behaviour of one invocation round: * `I` — the processes that invoke the object (with inputs `val`), * `O ⊆ I` — the processes that return from it (with outputs `out`; crashed processes do not return). `IsVAC I O val out` is the vacillate-adopt-commit object, `IsAC I O val out` the adopt-commit object. The templates of Section 3 are modelled at the level of these specifications: an execution of Algorithm 1 (`Exec1`) is a sequence of VAC rounds, the outputs of each round only constrained by `IsVAC`, and the reconciliator outputs `recon` arbitrary (validity and weak agreement are hypotheses of the theorems that need them). The processes invoking round `m + 1` are a subset of those that returned from round `m`; this covers both the paper's reading of Algorithm 1 (a process that commits halts) and the variant where deciding processes keep participating (`Exec1.Halting` distinguishes them). * Lemma 1 (Algorithm 1 is a correct consensus algorithm): `Exec1.lemma1_agreement`, `Exec1.lemma1_validity`, `Exec1.lemma1_termination`. * The Algorithm 2 template (adopt-commit + conciliator, "proved similarly"): `Exec2.agreement`, `Exec2.validity`, `Exec2.termination`. Its agreement needs the *validity of the conciliator* (`Exec2.ConciliatorValid`); Algorithm 4 (Phase-King's conciliator) does not have it, see `PhaseKing.lean`. -/ namespace
