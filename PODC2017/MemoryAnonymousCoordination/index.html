Coordination Without Prior Agreement — PODC 2017

Gadi Taubenfeld. Coordination Without Prior Agreement. PODC 2017. DOI 10.1145/3087801.3087807. Formalized version: the author's copy (10 pages, proofs included; no arXiv version). Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. All checked results hold: the impossibility results of Section 6 (Theorems 6.2, 6.3, 6.5), and correctness of the three algorithms (Figure 1 mutex, Theorems 3.2–3.3; Figure 2 consensus, Theorems 4.1–4.2; Figure 3 renaming, Theorems 5.1–5.3) for every adversarial numbering of the registers. But the correctness proofs of all three algorithms are sketches with false intermediate claims, and those of deadlock-freedom (Theorem 3.3) and of unique names (Theorem 5.2) needed new arguments. Figure 2 also breaks on an input equal to the initial value 0, which a local change fixes.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Figure 2, line 4 (an input equal to the initial value 0)

Figure 2 ignores the value 0 at line 4 but does not exclude 0 as an input, and with an input 0 agreement fails for n = 2.

Impact. Multi-valued consensus needs inputs kept apart from the empty value: Section 6.3 uses inputs in {0, 1} and refers back to this algorithm. We type inputs apart from the empty value, and consensus then holds (Consensus.consensus_solves).

Details

Process 1 with input 0 fills the registers and decides 0; then process 2 with input 1 ignores the 0 entries and decides 1.

✏️ Slips

  • Figure 2, line 6 and Figure 3, line 15 (the "arbitrary index"): the lines write to "an arbitrary index k such that myview[k] ≠ own record; clearly, such an entry exists", but in the deciding iteration no such index exists, because the until-test comes after the write. The prose of Section 4.1 has the intended order, deciding before the write. We let the write go anywhere, and the theorems hold for every admissible choice.

Typos

  • Theorem 6.5's proof says "in y" for "in x".

  • Theorem 5.1's item (3) says "increments mypref" for myround, and the prose of Section 5.1 cites line 19 for the round update, which is line 20.

Proof gaps in true statements (5)Theorem 5.2 · Theorem 3.3 · Theorem 4.1 · Theorem 5.1 · Theorem 5.3

🛠️ Theorem 5.2 (Figure 3, uniqueness)

The proof argues as if collects were atomic and ignores later rounds, so its claim that after the first decider all other processes find v in at least n entries of round r is false.

Impact. Statement true; the proof needs a new argument (Renaming.renaming_solves).

Details

A process whose collect started earlier sees a partly stale view, may keep another value and write it once more. Also, processes that already left round r keep writing records of later rounds, so the number of (v, r) records does drop below n, and the catch-up of lines 8–12 is ignored.

Repair

The counting invariant of Theorem 4.1 (below) is kept per round: records of later rounds force catch-up and carry (v, r) in their history. With well-formed histories this gives agreement per round. A ghost invariant on collects shows the invariant holds when the deciding collect starts. (Renaming.renaming_solves)

🛠️ Theorem 3.3 (Figure 1, deadlock-freedom)

The proof claims "exactly one of the two processes must find that less than ⌈m/2⌉ registers are set to its identifier, will give up, …, enabling the other … to enter its critical section", but with m = 3 both processes can give up with no CS entry in between.

Impact. Statement true for every odd m; the printed argument cannot be patched, and we replaced it (Mutex.theorem_3_3).

Details

The first loser is not blocked. The schedule: q scans while p is exiting and writes nothing; it then collects three 0s and gives up. Meanwhile p calls lock again, q leaves its wait loop on the empty memory, q writes two registers, p writes one, and p gives up too. (schedule in modelcheck/mutex_scenario.py)

Repair

At most two give-ups occur while no operation completes. Without give-ups the memory stabilizes, and oddness of m forces a decision. (Mutex.theorem_3_3)

🔧 Theorem 4.1 (proof treats collects as atomic)

The proof for Figure 2 argues as if collects were atomic: after the first decider i has seen all registers equal to (i, v), every other process is claimed to find v in at least n entries at line 4.

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a counting invariant (Consensus.consensus_solves).

Details

A process whose collect started earlier sees a partly stale view, may keep another value and write it once more.

Repair

The invariant is "registers not carrying v plus processes that may still write one stale value ≤ n − 1". A ghost invariant on collects shows the invariant holds when the deciding collect starts. (Consensus.consensus_solves)

🔧 Theorem 5.1 (Figure 3, termination)

Item (4) of the proof claims that a process continuing alone into the next round wins it, but earlier records of that round may hold another value at least n times, so the solo process adopts it and loses that round too.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Renaming.renaming_obstructionFree).

Details

Also, the proof never shows that myround stays below n.

Repair

Termination is proved by induction on n − myround, using the invariant myround + 1 ≤ |P|. (Renaming.renaming_obstructionFree)

🔧 Theorem 5.3 (Figure 3, adaptivity)

The proof's "the algorithm will terminate after k rounds" does not bound the names.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Renaming.renaming_solves).

Repair

A process acquiring name a sees a history naming a − 1 other distinct participants. Two processes cannot both get the name n by a counting argument.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory (CSLib Impl), atomic read/write registers, deterministic processes with arbitrary identifiers (any type P); process code may depend on the process, so the impossibility results also cover non-symmetric algorithms. No randomness.

  • Unnamed registers: each register is a separate base object; every process p uses its own adversarial numbering π p : Equiv.Perm ι of the registers, and all registers start with the same value (Impl.unnamed, Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Unnamed.lean). An algorithm is correct iff it is correct for every π.

  • Collects are not atomic: every read and every write is one step. "Arbitrary index" choices in the pseudocode are parameters, and the theorems hold for all admissible choices.

  • Failures: crash-stop is implicit (a process may stop taking steps); progress conditions are obstruction-freedom (consensus, renaming) and deadlock-freedom (mutex, CSLib's definition with fair client-well-formed infinite executions; participation not required). Tasks are CSLib one-shot tasks (consensus P V; adaptiveRenaming P = distinct names in {1..k} for k participants).

  • "Number of processes not a priori known" = an infinite set P of potential participants. Safety and obstruction-freedom only involve finite executions; for Theorem 6.2, deadlock-freedom is required only of infinite executions with finitely many participants (DeadlockFreeFin); with CSLib's DeadlockFree (also executions with infinitely many participants) it is the paper's "unbounded" case. "n processes" = Fintype.card P = n (or ≤ n for the algorithms).

The covering construction shared by Theorems 6.2, 6.3 and 6.5 is Impl.covering_construction (Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/UnnamedCovering.lean): since a process running alone from the initial memory reads only the common initial value until its first write, the adversary can choose its numbering so that this write goes to any prescribed register.

Proof remarks. The Section 6 proofs are correct. They leave implicit why the adversary may steer each process's first write: until then it reads only initial values, so its run does not depend on its numbering. For mutual exclusion, the covering processes must run round-robin, not solo, for deadlock-freedom to apply. Theorem 3.2 holds for every m ≥ 2.

Not formalized. Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.4 (they rely on results of [17] and the unpublished [25]); the k-set consensus remark; the election note. Theorems 3.1/3.4 (necessity of m odd, resp. gcd(m, ℓ) = 1) are covered, more generally, by theorem_5 of Papers/PODC2019/AnonymousMutex.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The algorithms are concrete, and admissible choice functions are exhibited (figure2_correct', figure3_correct'), so the correctness theorems are not vacuous. The algorithms also show that the hypotheses of the impossibility results are not trivially unsatisfiable: for example, Figure 1 gives deadlock-free mutual exclusion for 2 processes with 3 registers, consistent with mutex_card_lt. Bounded model checkers used for exploration are in modelcheck/ and are not part of the Lean development. CSLib candidates: Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/{Unnamed,UnnamedCovering}.lean; df_progress/df_enter in ImpMutex.lean (deadlock-freedom as a round-robin progress guarantee).
