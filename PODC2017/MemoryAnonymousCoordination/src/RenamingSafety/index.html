import Papers.PODC2017.MemoryAnonymousCoordination.RenamingLock /-! # Figure 3: uniqueness and adaptivity (Theorems 5.2 and 5.3) The invariant `Inv` (proved for every reachable configuration, `inv_of_run`) says: * every record `(val, round, history)` — in a register, in a local state or in a collected view — is well formed (`TupOK`): its history assigns exactly one winner to each round `1, …, round − 1` (`HistOK`), the winners are distinct, participants, different from `val`, and each of them is *locked* (`Locked`, see `RenamingLock.lean`) as the winner of its round; * the owner of a local state does not occur in its history; * the ghost invariant of a process in the middle of a collect: *if* everything it has read and everything it has not yet read is its own record `(p, s)`, then `s.val` is locked as the winner of round `s.rd`; in particular a process completing round `r` with preference `v` knows that `v` is locked as the winner of round `r`; * a process returning (or having returned) the name `a` knows (`OutOK`) a well-formed history `H` of the rounds `1, …, a − 1` not containing it, and either it is locked as the winner of round `a`, or `a = n`. Since two locks of the same round are for the same identifier, all such histories are equal, so two processes returning the same name `a` are both winners of round `a` (hence equal), or one of them returns `n` and then `H` together with both processes gives `n + 1` distinct processes. Every name `a` is bounded by the number of participants: `H` lists `a − 1` participants other than the process itself. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace
