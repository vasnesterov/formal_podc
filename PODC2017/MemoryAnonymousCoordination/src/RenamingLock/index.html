import Papers.PODC2017.MemoryAnonymousCoordination.RenamingSteps /-! # Figure 3: the per-round lock invariant `Lock π n v t C T` says that the identifier `v` is *locked* as the winner of round `t` in the configuration `C`: from now on every process that completes a collect in round `t` adopts `v`, except the "token holders" `T`, each of which may still write one *bad* record. A record is bad for `(v, t)` (`Bad`) if its round is `≤ t` and it is not `(v, t)`; records of a later round are harmless, because they all record `v` as the winner of round `t` in their history (`Hi`), and a process seeing one catches up beyond round `t`: * every process about to write a bad record is in `T`; * every process `p ∉ T` in the middle of a collect has fewer than `n − |T|` bad entries in its virtual view (entries read so far + current contents of the registers not yet read); * fewer than `n − |T|` registers are bad; * every record of a round `> t` (in a register, a local state or a view) has `(v, t)` in its history. The lock is preserved by every step (`lock_step`; `T` only shrinks), provided that a process completing round `t` has preference `v` (supplied by the global invariant). It is established when all registers hold the same record `(p, s)` and `p` starts a collect (`lock_establish`), and two locks of the same round are for the same identifier (`Lock.eq`): each forces at least `n` of the `2n − 1` registers to be good for it, and no register is good for two identifiers. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace Podc.MemoryAnonymousCoordination.Renaming
