Space Complexity of Fault-Tolerant Register Emulations — PODC 2017

Gregory Chockler, Alexander Spiegelman. Space Complexity of Fault-Tolerant Register Emulations. PODC 2017. DOI 10.1145/3087801.3087824. Formalized version: arXiv 1705.07212 v1; numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main results hold: the lower bound of Lemma 1 and Theorem 1, kf + ⌈kf/(n−(f+1))⌉(f+1) registers, is proved, and so are Theorems 3 and 5–7, after local repairs of the proofs of Lemmas 1, 3 and 7 and Theorem 1. Theorem 4 is false for dense domains such as ℚ and needs a condition on the value domain, Theorem 8 read literally (fixed k) contradicts Theorem 3 and needs a change of quantifiers, and Algorithm 2's writer mapping has an off-by-one. Theorem 2 is proved by a different, direct argument; the max-register/CAS upper bounds of Table 1 are not formalized.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 4 (CAS max register, Algorithm 1, wait-freedom)

Theorem 4 is stated for "some domain of ordered values V" but is false over ℚ: a write-max(1) can loop forever while another process completes ever larger writes below 1 (MaxRegisterCAS.ratImpl_not_waitFree).

Impact. The paper explicitly claims an arbitrary ordered domain, but its use with timestamps (over ℕ) is unaffected: Theorem 4 holds over ℕ (theorem4_nat), and Lemma 5 (linearizability) and lock-freedom hold over every linear order.

Details

Over ℚ, the other process completes write-max(1 − 1/(j+2)), j = 0, 1, …, each between the first process's read and its CAS. The proof's "assume w.l.o.g. there are k values between v′ and v" is an unstated hypothesis, which holds for the timestamps the paper uses but not for the stated domain.

Repair

The exact condition for wait-freedom is that no interval [v₀, c) contains an infinite increasing sequence (MaxRegisterCAS.waitFree_iff).

🔧 Theorem 8 (no emulation adaptive to point contention)

Read literally (k fixed, M may depend on the algorithm), Theorem 8 is false: Algorithm 2 (Theorem 3) uses finitely many registers, so it is adaptive with a constant M (theorem8_false_as_stated).

Impact. The intended version (bound independent of k) holds (Alg.theorem8).

Details

The proof only shows that consumption grows by f over each of the k writes.

Repair

True version: no M independent of k works. For every M there is a k such that every k-register emulation has a sequential run with more than M registers in use. (Alg.theorem8)

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 3 (Algorithm 2, Appendix D: writer-to-set mapping): client c_i, 1 ≤ i ≤ k, writes to the register set R_⌊i/z⌋, an off-by-one that overloads a set, and then Algorithm 2 is not even WS-Safe (Alg2.Counterexample.not_seqSafe_paperMapping, not_wsRegular_paperMapping, on our formalization of Algorithm 2). The intent is evident: the paper says each set serves (|R_j|−(f+1))/f clients. With j = ⌊(i−1)/z⌋, the mapping the paper's own counting describes, the algorithm is wait-free and WS-Regular with exactly kf + ⌈k/z⌉(f+1) registers, so Theorem 3 holds (Alg2.theorem3_wsRegular, Alg2.theorem3).

    • With 1-based i, R_0 gets z − 1 writers, a later set gets one writer more than it is sized for, and when z | k, client c_k maps to a set R_m that does not exist.

    • Smallest case: n = 4, f = 1, k = 3, so z = 2. Clients c_2 and c_3 share the overflow set R_1, which has 2f + 1 = 3 registers a, b, c on servers 0–2. A failure-free sequential run goes as follows. c_2 writes 1 to a and b, and its write to c stays pending. c_3 writes 2 to c and a, and its write to b stays pending. Then c_2's old write lands on c. A read that hears from servers 1–3 sees at most timestamp 1 and returns 1. So the counting of Lemmas 6/7 ("⌊(|R_j|−(f+1))/f⌋ writers per set") fails for the paper's own mapping.

  • The max-register specification "highest written value or v₀" needs v₀ = min V.

  • Lemma 1 says "k failure-free runs r_i, 0 ≤ i ≤ k".

Typos

  • Lemma 2.11 writes δ⁻¹ for δ.

  • Algorithm 2's initial state ⟨⟨⟨0,1⟩, v₀⟩, …⟩ is ill-typed.

Proof gaps in true statements (4)Lemma 3 · Lemma 1(b) · Theorem 1 · Lemma 7

🔧 Lemma 3 (the crash run)

The crash run "crashes Q_i ∪ M_i immediately after r". But in the adversary run, reads on these servers keep responding (Definition 3.3(a)), so the two runs differ for c_i.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Alg.round_main).

Repair

The servers must crash at the stabilization time. In our version the adversary freezes reads too, and its run is then itself fair (Alg.round_main). We simplified the adversary to G_i = F \ F_i, which removes M_i. We also replaced the paper's Lemma 4 by a stronger core statement (Alg.fresh_resp): after a complete write of a new value, the newly written, previously uncovered registers span more than f servers.

🔧 Lemma 1(b) (the final extension)

The final extension "by Definition 3.3(a)" until F is uncovered does not exist if c_i keeps writing to F after it returns.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Alg.lemma1_step).

Repair

One has to stop scheduling c_i and let only its pending writes respond (Alg.lemma1_step).

🔧 Theorem 1 (last step)

The last step applies Lemma 1 to G, the servers with many registers. But |G| may exceed f + 1, and Lemma 1 needs |F| = f + 1.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (load_bound).

Repair

Apply Lemma 1 to f + 1 servers of G (load_bound).

🔧 Lemma 7 (first claim of the proof)

"Every low-level write triggered after time t is associated with a bigger timestamp" is false: after W returns, an idle writer whose old write responds re-triggers (lines 30–32) with its own older timestamp (Alg2.Lemma7.retrigger_old_timestamp).

Impact. The lemma holds: such a re-trigger only re-covers a register that this writer already covered.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (Model.lean; Theorems 2 and 4 use CSLib's shared-memory model instead).

  • Asynchronous fault-prone shared memory with servers (Section 2, Appendix A). Clients are arbitrary deterministic automata. They trigger low-level reads and writes on base MWMR read/write registers B, which live on servers δ : B → S. They may have several pending operations, and they may act between high-level operations. A pending operation takes effect atomically at its response. The paper allows earlier linearization of writes but uses this convention in its proof (Assumption 1). Restricting runs only strengthens a lower bound.

  • Crash failures of clients and of at most f servers. Failures are not events: a failure pattern enters only fairness (Alg.Fair). Crashed clients take finitely many steps. Crashed servers respond finitely often. Every pending operation of a correct client on a correct server eventually responds, and correct clients are scheduled infinitely often. The scheduler/adversary is arbitrary; there is no randomness.

  • Hypotheses of the lower bounds. f-tolerant obstruction freedom (Alg.ObstructionFree, as in the paper). Safety only in sequential runs (Alg.SeqSafe), which is implied by the paper's WS-Safety. A k-register with writers w 0, …, w (k-1) and one further client acting as reader. Two distinct values suffice.

  • Adversary Ad_i (Round.lean): a solo strategy for the new writer, which freezes all its operations, reads included, on the servers Q_i ∪ G_i. We take G_i = F \ F_i while |Q_i| < |F_i|, which simplifies the paper's M_i. The frozen set is eventually constant and has at most f elements. Hence the infinite strategy run is fair with these servers crashed, and obstruction freedom applies to it directly (Lemma 3).

Remarks on the paper (not mistakes).

  • Theorem 2: the paper reduces it to Theorem 1 by running 2f + 1 copies of the max-register implementation plus multi-writer ABD; we did not formalize that composition. We prove Theorem 2 by a covering argument instead, with weaker hypotheses: obstruction freedom suffices, and linearizability is needed only on k-writer executions. It needs a reader distinct from the writers. (MaxRegLB.theorem2)

  • Superfluous hypotheses: Theorem 7 does not need |S| > 2f + 1. Theorem 1 needs only |S| ≥ f + 1. Lemma 1 needs only two values, not k distinct values.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: Algorithm 2 satisfies the hypotheses of all lower bounds (ObstructionFree, SeqSafe) for every f ≥ 1 and n > 2f (Alg2.theorem3).
