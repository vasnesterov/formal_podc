import Papers.PODC2017.ZigzagCausality.Zigzag /-! # Theorem 4 (Visible Zigzag Theorem), necessity direction, is false The network has four processes `i = 0`, `j = 1`, `k = 2`, `l = 3` and channels `i → j`, `i → k`, `k → j`, `j → l`; all lower bounds are `1`, and `U_ij = 2`, `U_ik = 1`, `U_kj = 1`, `U_jl = 2`. Processes follow a flooding full-information protocol. Let `s` be a non-initial `i`-node (e.g. `i` received an external message) and `θ₁ = ⟨s, i j l⟩`, `θ₂ = ⟨s, i k j l⟩`. * `knows_prec` — in every run in which `s` appears, `timer(θ₁) ≤ timer(θ₂)`: the chain `i → k → j` reaches `j` exactly 2 time units after `s`, the direct message `i → j` after 1 or 2. If both arrive at the same time they arrive at the *same* `j`-node, so `θ₁ = θ₂`; otherwise `θ₁ ≤ s + 3 ≤ θ₂`. Hence `(R, r) ⊨ K_s(θ₁ --0--> θ₂)`. * `wtZ_le_of_visible` — every `s`-visible zigzag pattern from `θ₁` to `θ₂` (in any run) has weight `≤ -1`: all its forks are rooted at `i`-nodes (the past of `s` contains only `i`-nodes, as `i` has no incoming channel), and the potential `φ⟨ρ, q⟩ = timer(ρ) + τ(q)` with `τ(ij) = 2, τ(ik) = 1, τ(ikj) = 2, τ(ijl) = 4, τ(ikjl) = 3` bounds every fork and junction. * `theorem_4_necessity_false` — the counterexample, with a concrete run. The knowledge comes from a case split over runs (the two chains merge at `j` or not), and neither case yields a visible zigzag: the "`+1`" of non-joined forks at `j` is not observable by `s`. -/
