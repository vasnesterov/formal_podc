Verdict: ✏️ Slip. In what we checked there are only slips: the 3-colouring lower bound (Theorem 9, with n ≤ 8T + 8 for every odd n) holds, and so do its ingredients, Theorem 10 (q-sum coordination is global) and Lemmas 12–14 (the parity invariant s), as well as Theorem 21 (no edge 2d-colouring for odd n) and Lemma 24 (no {1,3}-orientation for odd n). The third case of Theorem 22 gives the wrong reason for {0,3,4}-orientation, which stays global by the paper's unformalized Theorem 25. The paper's main results, the classification of complexities, undecidability and synthesis, were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 22, third case (X-orientations with no solution). The third case claims "no solution exists for infinitely many n", but X = {0,3,4} falls in it and every n × n torus with n ≥ 3 has a {0,3,4}-orientation (exists_orientation034, theorem22_third_bullet_false). Only the stated reason is wrong, not the complexity: {0,3,4} is still global, as the paper proves separately (Theorem 25, via q-sum coordination), and the introduction says "otherwise it is global". Theorem 22 classifies X-orientations: the third case is 2 ∉ X, {1,3,4} ⊄ X and {0,1,3} ⊄ X. The same counterexample works for {0,1,4}, by reversing all edges. The construction is invariant under the diagonal shift (x, y) ↦ (x+1, y+1). Diagonal k gets a value t_k: how many of the two edges between diagonals k and k+1 point to k+1. A node then has in-degree 2 − t_k + t_{k−1}. The cyclic sequence t = 0,2,0,2,…,0,2 (even n) or 0,2,…,0,2,1 (odd n) only produces in-degrees 0, 3 and 4.

  • Theorem 21, n = 1. "Any d-dimensional grid G_n with n odd admits no edge 2d-colouring" fails literally for n = 1, where the grid has no edges. All odd n ≥ 3 are proved (not_edgeColouring_torus).

Typos

  • In the proof of Theorem 10, d = A(F₁X₂F₂) − A(F₁X₂F₂) should be A(F₁X₂F₂) − A(F₁X₁F₂).

  • "The conditions in Section 10" means those of Theorem 10.

  • The proof of Lemma 14 swaps the coordinate names.

  • In the proof of Lemma 12, "southbound on r (S_{r1})" means "on r₁".

Model.

  • Network. The oriented toroidal n × n grid ZMod n × ZMod n. Nodes know n and which edge points north, east, south or west. They have unique identifiers in [0, B).

  • Algorithms. Deterministic LOCAL: a T-round algorithm is a map from the identifiers on the radius-T ball {d : |d₁| + |d₂| ≤ T} around a node to its output (GridLocal). Thanks to the orientation this is exactly a node's T-round knowledge. The lower bound only assumes correctness for identifiers in [0, 8n²), so it holds for every identifier range of size at least 8n² (eight times the number of nodes), in particular for the usual poly(n) ranges.

  • Directed cycles (q-sum). Same model on directed n-cycles, with identifiers in [0, 7n) (QSum.IsLocal, QSum.Solves).

  • Combinatorial results. Theorem 21 and Lemma 24 use the d-dimensional torus torusGraph d n, Mathlib's line-graph colourings and CSLib's SimpleGraph.Orientation.

  • What we prove differently. Theorem 10 has a new, shorter proof with a better constant: for 8T < n, any constant output sum Q on n-cycles must be a multiple of n (sum_eq_mul). Lemmas 12–14 are proved for every proper colouring by finite 3 × 3 block checks (decide) and flux counting, instead of a cycle decomposition of H.

  • Remark on the paper (not a mistake). The greedy post-processing before the reduction is unnecessary, because Lemmas 12–14 hold for every proper 3-colouring.

Not formalized. The classification theorem and the speed-up to O(log* n) (Theorem 2), the undecidability result (Theorem 3), the normal form and synthesis, the upper bounds (Theorems 4 and 15, Lemma 23), Theorem 25, which carries the corrected third case of Theorem 22, and the d-dimensional 3-colouring lower bound. Lemma 13's literal bound T < n/4: it ignores the O(1) extra radius needed to read ℓ, as the paper's conventions allow; we prove the lemma with 4(T+2) ≤ n and neither prove nor refute the literal bound. The identity between the paper's cycle-decomposition s(G) and the row sum of ℓ, which is routine.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. Non-vacuity checks:

  • globalAlg (NonVacuity.lean) is a 2n-round algorithm that properly 3-colours every odd torus. So the hypotheses of Theorem 9 are satisfiable, and n ≤ 8T + 8 is tight up to a constant factor.

  • torus44_edgeColourable shows that edge 2d-colourings exist for even n.
