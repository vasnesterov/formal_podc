Stateless Computation — PODC 2017

Danny Dolev, Michael Erdmann, Neil Lutz, Michael Schapira, Adva Zair. Stateless Computation. PODC 2017. DOI 10.1145/3087801.3087854. Formalized version: arXiv 1611.10068 v1 (the only version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Theorem 3.1 (two stable labelings rule out label (n−1)-stabilization), its tightness example, Propositions 2.1–2.3, Theorem 5.10, Theorem 6.2 and Corollaries 6.3–6.4 hold after local repairs to their proofs. The label covers only these: the hardness of verifying stabilization (Section 4) and the L/poly and P/poly characterizations (Section 5), which are also main results, were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 3.1 fails on graphs with self-loops: on the complete digraph with loops on 2 nodes, the protocol in which each node copies its loop label to all its outgoing arcs has 4 stable labelings but is label r-stabilizing for every r (SelfLoops.thm_3_1_false_with_loops). A node with a self-loop can store its state on that loop, which the stateless model is meant to rule out. The paper excludes self-loops only implicitly (a node communicates with "another node", and processors "cannot store information"). With loop-free graphs the theorem holds; we assume them.

  • Corollary 6.3 for odd n: the paper's EQ_n is constantly 0, so a protocol with a single label computes it, with label complexity 0 < (n−2)/8 (FoolingSet.cor_6_3_false_odd). The paper defines EQ_n = 1 only for even n. For even n the corollary holds, and in the stronger form L_n ≥ n/8 (FoolingSet.cor_6_3).

Typos

  • In the proofs of Corollaries 6.3/6.4 the names C and D are swapped.

Proof gaps in true statements (5)Theorem 3.1, final paragraph of the proof · Theorem 3.1, attractor regions and the proof of Lemma 3.2 · Theorem 5.10 · Theorem 5.10 · Corollary 6.3

🔧 Theorem 3.1, final paragraph of the proof (H-successors)

The paragraph after Lemma 3.4 claims that the H-successors of the critical state are all activation sets with |T| ≥ 2 containing the node with countdown 1, which is false.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (not_labelStabilizing, exists_nonconvergent_run). Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 themselves are fine.

Details

The proof works in the state space H of (labeling, countdown) pairs whose sorted countdown vector dominates (1, 2, …, r−1, r, r). Take n = 5, r = 4 and countdowns (2,2,4,4,4). Activating two of the nodes with countdown 4 gives (1,1,4,4,3), which is not in H. So the step that finds S and S ∪ {i} in different attractor regions is not justified as written.

Repair

It is enough that the admissible activation sets are upward closed, so that any two of them are connected through "activate everybody" by single-node insertions.

🔧 Theorem 3.1, attractor regions and the proof of Lemma 3.2

The paper never says whether attractor regions are defined by paths in H or in the full state graph: Lemma 3.4 and the final step need H, but Lemma 3.2's proof activates a single node, and that step leaves H.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (Impossibility.exists_biv).

Repair

We take valency in H. Lemma 3.2 then follows from Lemma 3.3 along a chain of hybrid labelings. We checked the counting step of Lemma 3.3 (activating j and the minimum-countdown node j' stays in H) in Lean.

🔧 Theorem 5.10 (quantifier over Σ)

The proof counts protocols for one fixed Σ, but the statement needs one function that is hard for all small Σ simultaneously.

Impact. Statement true; we prove it for n ≥ 6 (exists_hard_function).

Repair

Sum the same count over all sizes up to the bound.

🔧 Theorem 5.10 (algebra step)

The step log n + log(2|Σ|^k) + log log(2|Σ|^k) ≥ n ⇒ log n + 2k log|Σ| ≥ n fails for k log|Σ| < 3.

Impact. Statement true; a different estimate in the same chain keeps the bound (exists_hard_function).

🔧 Corollary 6.3 (fooling set violates Theorem 6.2's conditions)

The fooling set {(x,x) : x₁ = 1} of the proof violates the bullet conditions of Theorem 6.2, for example at the arc (m, m+1).

Impact. Statement true (for even n): the proof of Theorem 6.2 never uses the bullet conditions xᵢ = x'ᵢ (arcs of C) and yᵢ = y'ᵢ (arcs of D), and we prove the theorem without them (FoolingSet.card_le_of_isFoolingSet).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. (Model.lean)

  • A finite set of nodes V and a digraph G : Digraph V. Each arc carries a label from L (the paper's Σ).

  • A protocol is one reaction function per node, δ i : (incoming labels) → X → (outgoing labels) × Y. It is deterministic, and nodes have no other state. Inputs X and outputs Y are arbitrary; the paper uses {0,1}.

  • Asynchrony is a schedule σ : ℕ → Finset V. An activated node applies δ to its current incoming labels.

  • IsRFair r σ: every step activates a nonempty set, and every node is activated in every window of r consecutive steps (the shared BoundedFair r of Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SelfStabilization/Daemon.lean). Initial labelings and initial outputs are arbitrary (self-stabilization). There are no failures and no randomness.

  • LabelStabilizing r P, as in the paper: for every input, initial labeling and r-fair schedule, the outputs converge and so do the labelings.

  • Part II (Section 2.3 and Sections 5–6) uses synchronous executions (syncRun), with SyncLabelStabilizing and SyncComputes f (outputs converge to f x from every initial labeling). These are weaker than any r-fair notion, so the lower bounds are stronger than the paper's.

  • Theorem 3.1 holds for arbitrary (also infinite) label types and graphs that need not be strongly connected. It requires only that G has no self-loops.

Informal proofs, made precise.

  • Example 1 (tightness of Theorem 3.1): the argument ignores initial labelings in which a node's outgoing arcs carry different bits. The claim is true (Example1.or_tight).

  • Proposition 2.1: the proof is informal, and neither the round-complexity off-by-one nor the radius of a digraph is defined. The statement is true under a precise reading (exists_center).

Not formalized.

  • Section 4: communication complexity 2^{Ω(n)} and PSPACE-completeness of deciding r-stabilization.

  • The complexity-class characterizations of Section 5 (L/poly, P/poly), the counter protocols of Claims 5.5–5.6 (the notion of a counter is informal), and Corollaries 5.7–5.9.

Trust.

  • No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.

  • Non-vacuity:

    • Example 1 exhibits a protocol with two stable labelings that is label (n−2)-stabilizing.

    • prop_2_3 gives, for every f, a protocol satisfying the hypotheses of the lower bounds.

    • The synchronous schedule is r-fair for every r ≥ 1 (isRFair_sync).

  • A brute-force search over random small protocols (3–4 nodes) found no counterexample to Theorem 3.1. Example 1 was also brute-force checked for n = 3, 4.

  • The model is in Model.lean; the stone-age model in CSLib does not fit, since its nodes have states. Shared notions: BoundedFair (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SelfStabilization/Daemon.lean), Converges is Mathlib's Filter.EventuallyConst (converges_iff), WalkLen is Relation.RelatesInSteps of the arcs, strong connectivity is Digraph.StronglyConnected (stronglyConnected_iff, Cslib/Foundations/Combinatorics/Digraph/Walk.lean). CSLib candidates:

    • the stateless-protocol model with r-fair schedules (Model.lean);

    • the countdown-graph encoding of r-fair schedules (Impossibility.HPath.fair);

    • BFS trees in digraphs (UpperBound.exists_trees).
