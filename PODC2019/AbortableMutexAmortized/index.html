Constant Amortized RMR Abortable Mutex — PODC 2019

Prasad Jayanti, Siddhartha Jayanti. Constant Amortized RMR Abortable Mutex for CC and DSM. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331592. Formalized version: arXiv 1809.04561 v1 (full version with all proofs); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main results hold for Algorithm 1 as printed: mutual exclusion, starvation freedom, O(1) amortized RMRs per attempt in both the CC and DSM models, O(1) worst-case Exit, Fast Abort and O(n) space. The new fairness property AFCFS is false as defined, because the definition forgets passages that end with an abort, and one added condition gives a corrected version that holds. The distance function of the starvation proof misses the case of line 11, the invariant of Theorem 4.1 is true but needs an extra conjunct to be inductive, and a constant in the proof of Lemma 5.3 changes from 4 to 5.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 4.2 (AFCFS)

As defined, AFCFS fails whenever p aborts the last attempt of its passage: if p completes its doorway and then aborts for good, a later process p' splices out p's node and enters the CS although p never enters the CS in that passage (afcfs_literal_false, a 13-step run with n = 3).

Impact. Only the new fairness notion is affected; the algorithm is as fair as intended. Under the paper's definition no starvation-free abortable lock can satisfy AFCFS: a process that leaves after its doorway would block every later process forever. The evident fix is local, and we prove it: p' does not enter the CS before p enters the CS or the Abort section (theorem_4_2).

Details

A passage ends with a successful attempt "or the last attempt" (j = k). If p's last attempt is aborted and p never returns, the passage consists of aborted attempts only, and the A-precedence relation is based on the doorway of that last attempt. The failure needs the strict reading of "p' does not enter the CS before p enters the CS" (p' must wait for an entry of p that never comes). Under the weaker reading "if both enter, p enters first", the definition already says what is intended and Theorem 4.2 holds. Three things show the intended exception: the airline analogy ("people behind you occupy your original position"), the paper's proof (it only orders processes while they are in Q, and "leaves Q from any position" includes aborting), and the fact that the strict reading cannot be met by any starvation-free abortable lock. AFCFS is the paper's own new definition and the abstract lists it, but the fix only adds the missing case "unless p's passage ends with an abort", the proof reasons with the intended version, and the algorithm is unchanged.

Repair

theorem_4_2 assumes that p is at line 3 at time T (it has just completed lines 1–2) and that p' begins a passage at a time t' ≥ T (PassageStart: p' is in the Remainder, and its last step, if any, completed the Exit section). It concludes that whenever p' is at line 7, p was at line 7, 9, 10 or 11 at some time in between. The proof keeps p ahead of p' in the queue (before_step) and uses I10.

🔧 Definition 4.1 / Lemma 4.4 (the digit function f misses line 11)

Equation (1) gives no value of f at line 11, so δ(p) is undefined when the prefix leader q_m is an aborted process at line 11 (marked node, about to wake its successor), a case the proof of Lemma 4.4 itself lists.

Impact. This is a local fix. With f(11) = 4, the only value between f(10) = 5 and f(1) = 3, Lemma 4.4 and Theorem 4.5 hold (lemma_4_4_part1, lemma_4_4_part2, theorem_4_5).

✏️ Slips

  • Figure 1, I7: *mynode_p should be *mynode_{q_i}; I9: go_{q₁} should be &go_{q₁}.

  • Lemma 4.4 should exclude an aborting step of p itself, after which δ(p) is undefined.

  • Appendix C, Lemma 5.3, lines 3, 7, 9: the proof writes α_CC(11) for α_CC(9).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 4.1 · Lemma 5.3

🔧 Theorem 4.1 (the invariant I is not inductive)

The induction step for line 3 (case 3, splicing out an aborted predecessor) fails from a configuration satisfying I in which the new predecessor's node still holds the spliced process's go-address, after which I7 fails (paperInv_not_inductive: n = 3, Q = [r, x, π], r at line 3 with *a_r = &go_x, x aborted at line 11, π at line 3).

Impact. The statement is true: I holds in every reachable configuration (theorem_4_1). The appendix's "the rest of the invariants continue to hold since they are unaffected" is the gap.

Repair

We add one conjunct (Loc.x1): an aborted queue member at line 1, 10 or 11 never has its own go-address in its predecessor node, since it erased it at line 9. With this conjunct the invariant is inductive (inv_step), and it implies I verbatim (paperInv_of). An exhaustive model check of all 11 413 reachable configurations for n = 2, plus random runs for n = 3, 4, agrees.

🔧 Lemma 5.3 (CC constant for lines 8 and 11)

The proof claims α_CC(8), α_CC(11) ≤ 4, but the write *v ← true also invalidates go_q in q's cache, which adds one more unit of potential, so the true bound is 5.

Impact. The statement ("bounded by a constant") is true (lemma_5_3, with exact per-line bounds 0, 1, 2, 5). The bound 5 is attained for n = 2.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory with n processes Fin n, deterministic, no crashes, and an arbitrary scheduler (any schedule σ : ℕ → Fin n × Bool). Processes that have not "joined" yet are processes that stay in the Remainder section. The shared objects support Fetch&Store, read and write.

  • One step is one shared-memory operation plus the local actions after it, as in the paper. Line 1 is the Remainder section and line 7 is the CS.

  • Abort signal: a step carries an abort flag that is used only at lines 4–6. An aborting step there jumps to the Abort section and executes line 9 in the same step. This follows the paper's convention that a step is one shared-memory operation plus any local actions, and a jump is a local action. Lemma 4.4 also holds when the jump is attached to the preceding operation instead. It would fail only if the jump were a separate, memory-free step, which the paper's convention rules out; see differencies.md.

  • Starvation freedom assumes a fair schedule: every process outside the Remainder eventually takes a step. The process under consideration never aborts.

  • RMR cost models:

    • DSM: an arbitrary placement of variables with go_p in p's partition.

    • CC: per-process caches. Reads hit or miss; every Fetch&Store or write costs 1 and invalidates all copies. Caches start empty.

Proof remarks.

  • The invariant is stated through pointers rather than indices. Struct says that Q is a pred/mynode chain from a₀ to tail (Chain.lean). Membership in Q is read off locally: lines 3–7, 9, 10, or line 1/11 with a marked node. Loc holds the remaining conjuncts plus x1.

  • Each step changes Q in one of four ways (QStep): unchanged, append, splice, or removal of the head. Lemma 4.4 and AFCFS are proved from this.

  • In Appendix B, "one digit fewer ⇒ smaller" needs a nonzero leading digit, which follows from I6 and I8. Theorem 4.5 also needs Ψ(p) ≠ ∅, which follows from I11.

  • Theorem 5.4's "the lines outside the loop are executed at most once per attempt" becomes a remaining-budget potential Σ_p c(pc_p).

  • Theorem 5.4's Fast Abort bound of six shared-memory instructions is correct; we prove the sharper bound of five own steps (theorem_5_4_fastAbort), because line 1 belongs to the Remainder section.

Not formalized. Nothing of substance. "Space O(n)" is formalized as the count of shared variables.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Counterexamples are checked by decide +kernel. Non-vacuity checks:

  • fair_roundRobin: a fair schedule exists.

  • stdHome: a DSM placement with go_p local to p exists.

  • afcfsRun: the AFCFS hypotheses are met.

The Python model checkers used to validate the invariant, Lemma 4.4 and the per-line costs are in modelcheck/; they are not part of the Lean development. The model is paper-local and does not use CSLib's shared-memory framework. CSLib candidate: the generic pointer-chain lemmas of Chain.lean.
