Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Constant Amortized RMR Abortable Mutex — PODC 2019.

Paper: P. Jayanti, S. Jayanti, Constant Amortized RMR Abortable Mutex for CC and DSM, PODC 2019; formalized from the full version arXiv 1809.04561v1 (numbering as there).

Model

  • Processes. A fixed finite set Fin n. The paper allows an unbounded number of processes with arbitrary names that "join" over time; a process that has not joined yet is modelled as a process that stays in the Remainder section (line 1). Every result is stated for every n.

  • Granularity. One step = one shared-memory operation (the numbered line) plus all local actions that follow it, exactly the paper's convention (Section 3.2). Line 1 = Remainder, line 7 = CS (the paper's convention in I10 / Corollary 4.1.1; this also covers the "sidestep" of an aborting process that reads token at line 9 and goes to line 7 without using the CS).

  • Abort signal. A step carries a Boolean abort flag. It only matters at lines 4, 5, 6; there an aborting step jumps to the Abort section and performs line 9 in the same step (the jump is a local action, merged into the next shared operation). Elsewhere the flag is ignored (the paper: the jump is allowed "only after performing Line 3, 4, 5 or 6"; a signal arriving at line 2/3 takes effect after line 3). Remark: if the jump were a separate, memory-free step (so that a process can sit at line 6 and then jump to line 9), Lemma 4.4 would fail with the paper's f (f(6) = 2 < f(9) = 6): our exhaustive model checker finds 540 violating transitions for n = 2. This separate-step reading is not the paper's model: the paper says a step performs one shared-memory operation "but can perform any number of local actions", the jump (a goto) is a local action, and the paper requires the process "to execute Line 3 and all local actions associated with Line 3 before jumping". So the jump belongs to a shared-memory step, and the 540 violations are not an error of the paper. Merging the jump with either the preceding or the following shared operation is consistent with the paper's convention. We merge it with the following one. The review re-checked the preceding-merge variant (a step performing line 3, 4, 5 or 6 that leaves the process in the loop may end at line 9) for n = 2: 14 593 reachable configurations, the strengthened invariant holds, and Lemma 4.4 has 0 violations. The two models differ only in the local PC between the two steps.

  • Impossible values. Where the invariant shows a case cannot happen (*v ← true with v not a go-address; v_p = &go_q, q ≠ p, read at lines 3/6/9) the model does something harmless (no-op / no pred update). The invariant (Loc) proves these cases unreachable.

  • v_p initial value. "Arbitrarily initialized": we use nil; v_p is never read before it is written.

  • Executions. exec σ for an arbitrary schedule σ : ℕ → Fin n × Bool (every schedule is admissible, step is total and deterministic); finite runs run init ls.

  • Fairness (Theorem 4.5). Fair σ: every process outside the Remainder section eventually takes a step. This is the paper's "no process stays in the CS forever and no process stops taking steps while in the Try, Exit or Abort sections". A process at line 4 with go = false takes no-op steps (busy waiting).

  • RMR cost models. DSM: an arbitrary placement home of the shared variables with go_p in p's partition (the paper fixes only this); an access costs 1 iff the variable is not in the accessing process's partition. CC: per-process caches; a read is free iff the variable is cached (and then caches it); every non-read operation costs 1 and invalidates all copies (Section 1). CC caches start empty; the paper instead "thinks of go_p as initially in p's cache, at the cost of charging one unit to a joining process".

Statements

  • Theorem 4.1 (invariant). Stated verbatim as PaperInv (Figure 1) and proved for every reachable configuration (theorem_4_1), uniqueness of k, A, Q from I1–I4 (theorem_4_1_unique, unique_of_I1_I4). Lists are 0-based (a_i = A[i], q_{i+1} = Q[i]). Typos fixed: in I7 "*mynode_p" is *mynode_{q_i}; in I9 "go_{q₁}" is &go_{q₁}. I4 ("N = {a₀} ∪ {mynode_p}") is read as: every node is a₀ or some mynode_p (with |N| = n + 1 this forces mynode injective and a₀ nobody's node, cover_inj).

  • Proof gap in Theorem 4.1 (🔧). I is not inductive: paperInv_not_inductive gives a configuration satisfying I whose successor (line 3, case 3: splicing an aborted predecessor) satisfies no instance of I (I7 fails for the new predecessor, whose node still holds the spliced process's go-address). The appendix's "the rest of the invariants continue to hold since they are unaffected" misses this. Repair: the extra conjunct Loc.x1 ("an aborted queue member at line 1, 10 or 11 does not have its own go-address in its predecessor node"); with it the invariant is inductive (inv_step). Since I itself holds in all reachable configurations, the statement of Theorem 4.1 is true.

  • Corollary 4.1.1 (mutual_exclusion): as stated.

  • Theorem 4.2 (AFCFS) — false as stated (🔧). afcfs_literal_false: a run in which p completes the doorway of its only attempt, aborts it (lines 9, 10) and never returns; p' begins its first passage afterwards, splices out p's node and enters the CS. p's passage (one aborted attempt, the last one: j = k in the passage definition) A-precedes p''s, and p' enters the CS although p never does. The definition cannot be met by any starvation-free abortable lock (a process that leaves after its doorway would block all later processes forever). Corrected statement proved (theorem_4_2): p' does not enter the CS before p enters the CS or the Abort section. The definition of "attempt", "passage", "begins" is encoded semantically (PassageStart: p' in the Remainder, and its last step, if any, was an Exit step at line 7 or 8); the doorway completion is "p at line 3". The missing case ("unless p's passage ends with an abort") is evident from the airline analogy (people behind you may take your spot when you leave) and the proof (which only uses that p stays ahead in Q while it is there); the fix is local. AFCFS is the paper's own new definition and is listed in the abstract; the failure lies in the definition, not in the algorithm. The counterexample needs the strict reading of "p' does not enter the CS before p enters the CS" (p' must wait for an entry of p that never happens). Under the weaker reading "if both enter, p enters first", the literal definition is already the corrected one and Theorem 4.2 holds as stated. On the sidestep (line 9 reads token, then line 7): theorem_4_2 counts line 9 as "entering the Abort section". For a successful last attempt that sidesteps, it therefore only gives "p reached line 9 by the time p' is at line 7", not "p reached line 7 first". This is harmless because the sidestep is a degenerate CS entry by the paper's own convention (I10).

  • Definition 4.1 / Lemma 4.4 — f undefined at line 11 (🔧). f in eq. (1) has no value for PC = 11, but q_m can be at line 11 (aborted, node marked, about to wake its successor), and the proof of Lemma 4.4 itself lists 11 among the cases. We set f(11) = 4, the only value between f(10) = 5 and f(1) = 3.

  • Lemma 4.3 (lemma_4_3): as stated (for p in the Try section or CS; p ∈ Q follows from the invariant).

  • Lemma 4.4 (lemma_4_4_part1, lemma_4_4_part2): we exclude an aborting step of p itself, after which p is no longer in the Try section and δ(p) is undefined (the paper's statement silently assumes this). We also prove that Ψ(p) is nonempty and consists of processes outside the Remainder (promoters_spec), which Theorem 4.5's proof uses without proof (it needs I11: if q_m waits at line 4 with go = false then m = 1).

  • Theorem 4.5 (theorem_4_5): as stated, for fair schedules in which p never aborts; we also allow p at line 2. The hypothesis forbids aborting steps of p at all times, including later attempts. This is harmless: the conclusion depends only on the prefix up to p's CS entry, and the schedule can be changed after it.

  • Proof remark (Appendix B). "The m-th digit decreases" / "one fewer digit" needs the leading digit to be nonzero (f ≥ 1 on queue members, which needs I6/I8: no queue member is at line 2 or 8). Made explicit in StarvationDefs.lean.

  • Theorem 5.1 (theorem_5_1): "space O(n)" is formalized as the number of shared variables, |Var n| = 2n + 2 (tail, n + 1 nodes, n go-flags); each holds one word.

  • Lemma 5.2 (lemma_5_2): exactly the paper's constants (1 for lines 1, 2, 3, 7, 9; 2 for 8, 10, 11; 0 for 4, 5, 6), for every placement of nodes/tail, go_p local to p, and every configuration satisfying the invariant.

  • Lemma 5.3 — proof gap (🔧, statement true). The statement only claims "a constant"; the proof claims α_CC(8), α_CC(11) ≤ 4, but the write *v ← true also invalidates go_q in q's cache, raising 1{notcached(q)} too: the true bound is 5 (lemma_5_3, boundC; our exhaustive check for n = 2 attains 5). Also the proof's "α_CC(3), α_CC(7), α_CC(11) ≤ 1" should read α_CC(9). Neither affects Lemma 5.3 or Theorem 5.4.

  • Theorem 5.4. Amortized: theorem_5_4_dsm (≤ 8 RMRs per attempt) and theorem_5_4_cc (≤ 12 per attempt), for every finite execution from the initial configuration, where an attempt is a step at line 1 (the paper's ratio x/y; infinite runs: every prefix). The paper's proof ("lines outside the loop are executed at most once per attempt") is made precise by a remaining-budget potential Σ_p c(pc_p). CC with empty initial caches: the potential term 1{notcached(p)} is replaced by 1{notcached(p) ∧ pc_p ≠ 1}, which charges the missing unit to line 1 (the paper's "charging one unit to a process newly joining"); this costs nothing in the bound's form. Worst-case Exit: theorem_5_4_exit (2 own steps from line 7) and every step costs ≤ 1 RMR (dsmCost_le_one, ccCost_le_one). Fast Abort: theorem_5_4_fastAbort — from any configuration, a process taking only aborting steps returns to the Remainder within 5 own steps. The paper's "within six shared instructions" is a correct but weaker bound: it counts line 1, which is the Remainder section. This is not an error.
