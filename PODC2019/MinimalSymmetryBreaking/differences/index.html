Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Hardness of Minimal Symmetry Breaking — PODC 2019.

Paper: Balliu, Hirvonen, Olivetti, Suomela, Hardness of minimal symmetry breaking in distributed computing, PODC 2019, arXiv 1811.01643v3.

Model

  1. Randomness. The paper gives each node an infinite sequence of unbiased random bits. We give each node an independent value from an arbitrary finite probability space (Ω, μ), with any weights.

    • This covers every algorithm that reads a bounded number of random bits; Ω = bit strings of a fixed length with the uniform distribution is one instance.

    • Algorithms that read unboundedly many bits are not covered literally.

    • All probabilities are finite sums (Field.lean). Conditioning on a region is resampling outside it.

  2. Oriented regular tree. The 4-regular oriented tree with directions U/D/L/R (Section 5), and the 2k-regular tree with k oriented dimensions (Section 7), are modelled as the Cayley graph of the free group on k generators (Tree k). A t-round anonymous algorithm is a function of the random values within distance t of the origin; node v applies it to the field shifted by v. This matches "A(v) depends only on B_t(v)" with the same algorithm at every node.

  3. Edge algorithms. The output of the edge {v, v gᵈ} is out d (shift v x). It is a function of the values within distance t of either endpoint and may depend on the dimension d, as in the paper.

  4. Local failure. Local failure is evaluated at the origin. The paper's "for each node v, Pr[...] ≤ p" is the same thing by translation invariance.

  5. Finite instances (Claim 10, Theorems 6/13).

    • An instance is an n-node set V of nodes of the oriented tree containing the ball B_K, K = ⌊log_{2k+1} n⌋ / 3. The network is the induced subgraph.

    • The paper uses "a 4-regular tree of n nodes" whose ball B_k(v) of about n^{1/3} nodes contains no leaf; our choice is equivalent up to constants. Subtrees of every size exist (exists_subtree, theorem_6_instances).

    • theorem_6 quantifies over all V ⊇ B_K, including disconnected ones; this only strengthens it.

    • The algorithm sees the nodes of V within tree distance t, with identifiers, random values and orientation. For connected V this is the LOCAL view. It knows n.

    • Identifiers are exactly Fin n (injective on V, |V| = n), as in the paper ("identifiers exactly in {1..n}").

  6. Weak 2-colouring has output type Bool. Success means every node of V has a neighbour in V with the other colour.

  7. Lemma 2 and P*. These use CSLib's extensional LOCAL locality IsLocal R f: the graph and labels within radius R determine the output. Node names are the identifiers, and n = |V| is fixed and known.

    • Lemma 2 is proved for arbitrary simple graphs: no degree bound, finiteness or connectivity is needed. The algorithm is anonymous.

    • P*: d(v) ∈ ℕ with d(v) < Δ, and the pointer is the neighbour's name rather than a port number (equivalent). deg is the cardinality of the neighbour set.

  8. Δ. The paper assumes Δ ∈ {4, 6, ...} for P*. Our P* results hold for every Δ ≥ 3. The weak-colouring lower bound holds for every k ≥ 1 (Δ = 2k ≥ 2).

Statements

  1. Lemma 2.

    • Proved as stated (lemma_2), with an explicit radius k + 2c (lemma_2_explicit).

    • The paper's second step (Cole–Vishkin to 3 colours, then an MIS in a pseudoforest, O(log* c) rounds) is replaced by a greedy MIS over the 2c colour classes of the subgraph of differently-coloured edges (2c rounds). Both are O(1) for constant c.

    • Definition 1 is unsatisfiable for k = 0 and for graphs with isolated nodes, where the lemma is vacuous.

  2. Lemmas 7, 8, 14, 15.

    • Proved as stated for general Δ = 2k (lemma_14, lemma_15). Δ = 4 gives Lemmas 7 and 8.

    • The exponents of c are Δ/(Δ+1) = 1 − 1/(Δ+1) and 1 − 1/Δ.

    • The palettes are Finset α × Finset α (2^{2c} colours) and Fin k × Bool → Finset β (2^{Δc} colours).

    • Lemma 7 is stated for a (t+1)-round algorithm, i.e. t ≥ 1 in the paper's indexing.

    • We also prove the inequalities used by the proofs, speedup1_ineq (p ≥ (p' − Δcf) f^Δ) and speedup2_ineq (p ≥ (p' − (Δ−1)cf) f^{Δ−1}), for every threshold f ≥ 0.

  3. Claims 11/16. Proved (claim_16): (p₀/((Δ+1)c₀))^{(Δ+1)^{2t+1}} with p₀ = 1/c₀^Δ, for any c₀ that bounds the end of the palette chain c ↦ 2^{Δ·2^{2c}}. The paper's recursion (Claim 11) is lb, and nodeLB_lb is its validity.

  4. Claim 12.

    • The first step, "a 0-round algorithm fails with probability ≥ 1/c₀^Δ", is proved (failProb_zero_round) via the power-mean inequality Σ qᵢ^{Δ+1} ≥ c^{−Δ}.

    • The asymptotic part is proved with our own constants (qT_mul_large): for 2t + 2k + 15 ≤ log* n, the local failure probability is large compared with the number of independent nodes.

    • The paper's exact statement ("(½log* n − b − 3)-round algorithms fail with probability ≥ 1/log^{(2b)} n") is not checked. Its proof uses "for large enough n", which the statement does not mention. Not checked.

  5. Claim 10 / Lemma 9.

    • We prove a variant (succProb_le). If every anonymous t-round algorithm with randomness Fin n × Ω fails locally with probability ≥ q, then some identifier assignment gives success probability ≤ (1−q)^{|S|} + |B_K|²/n, for any S ⊆ B_{K−t−1} with pairwise disjoint (t+1)-balls.

    • With exists_separated, |S| ≥ |B_{K−t−1}| / |B_{2t+2}|.

    • The paper's exact bounds (1−p)^{n^{1/(3(2t+1))}} + 1/(2n^{1/3}) and the formula in Lemma 9 are not re-derived. Their proof has the independence error below; they are not refuted.

  6. Theorems 6 and 13.

    • Proved (theorem_6, theorem_13): constants C, N₀ exist such that for n ≥ N₀ and 2t + C ≤ log* n, every t-round algorithm has success probability < 1/2 under some identifier assignment. So Ω(log* n) rounds are needed for success probability ≥ 1/2.

    • Our C is 2k + 15, so t ≤ ½ log* n − (k + 7.5).

    • The paper's prose (Section 6) claims "at least ½ log* n − 4 rounds" for Δ = 4. That additive constant is not checked.

  7. Lemma 3. Proved for a repaired algorithm (lemma3_repaired).

    • Radius 6r + 3 (O(r)). "Irregularity within distance r" means a node of degree < Δ within distance r, or a cycle of degree-Δ nodes all within distance r. The paper's dist(v, C) = min dist + ℓ(C) ≤ r implies the latter.

    • Every such node is P*-happy. This is "solved in the 1-neighbourhood" read as happiness of the nodes concerned; apply it with r + 1 to cover 1-neighbourhoods.

  8. Lemma 17. Proved (lemma17): radius 6⌈log_{Δ−1} n⌉ + 3 for all graphs of maximum degree ≤ Δ on n nodes, Δ ≥ 3. The ball-counting step ("every node sees an irregularity within O(log_Δ n)") is Lemma 2.2 of the PODC 2026 SinklessOrientation formalization.

  9. Lemma 18.

    • Proved (lemma18): no r-round algorithm solves P* on both T and T', the balanced Δ-regular tree of radius r+1 and its modification with the same node set. The randomized form (lemma18_randomized) gives Pr[success on T] + Pr[success on T'] ≤ 1.

    • lemma18_lowerBound gives n ≤ Δ^{2r+2}, hence Ω(log_Δ n).

    • The paper's T' modification (re-attach a leaf of each depth-(r−1) node below another leaf) is implemented literally.

  10. Theorem 4. theorem4 combines Lemmas 17 and 18. The upper bound is in log_{Δ−1} n, the lower bound in log_Δ n; both are Θ(log n) for fixed Δ.

Proof repairs

  1. 🛠️ Lemma 3's algorithm is incorrect (Section 8.1).

    • Under "closest irregularity by distance": take a 4-cycle a < b < c < d of degree-4 nodes, a leaf x on b, and r = 2.

      • Ties going to the cycle: a outputs (p = b, d = 0) while b outputs (p = x, d = 1). Condition (3) fails at a.

      • Ties going to the node: d points to a with d = 0 ≠ d(a) = 1.

    • Under Section 8.1's own definition (distance + full cycle length), the same graph with x three degree-4 steps from b and r = 4 also fails.

    • Under "cycles take precedence": a degree-Δ node whose shortest path to a cycle starts with a low-degree neighbour w points to w with d = 0, but w is forced to output (⊥, deg w).

    • Root cause: the case irr(p(v)) ≠ C only checks non-backtracking, not d(v) = d(p(v)). The claim "each node v on C will have a cycle as their closest irregularity" is false.

    • Found by a brute-force search script (not included in the repository).

    • Repair: the mode-based algorithm described in the README, pstarAlg.

    • The algorithm appears only inside the proof of Lemma 3, and the lemma is existential.

  2. 🔧 Claim 10, independence.

    • The paper takes nodes at pairwise distance ≥ 2t+1 and says their failure events are independent. A node's failure depends on its (t+1)-ball, so this needs distance ≥ 2t+3.

    • Repaired with disjoint (t+1)-balls. The resulting count differs from n^{1/(3(2t+1))}; see item 13. The packing bound |S| ≥ |B_{K−t−1}| / |B_{2t+2}| (about n^{1/3} / 3^{O(t)}) is far larger than n^{1/(3(2t+1))} once t ≥ 1 and n is large. So the paper's count is very likely recoverable with the correct spacing; only its derivation is wrong.

    • The step Pr[X|Y] ≤ Pr[X'|Y'] conditions on different events, and X' is not determined by B_k(v). We replace it by an averaging argument over identifiers that are i.i.d. on the central ball and conditioned to be injective, then extend them to a bijection (exists_injOn_extend, exists_le_of_expect).

  3. 🔧 Lemmas 7/8/14/15, threshold choice.

    • The paper sets f = p'/(5c) (resp. p'/(4c), p'/((Δ+1)c), p'/(Δc)), where p' is the failure probability of the algorithm built with threshold f. This is circular.

    • Repaired by choosing f = q/((Δ+1)c) for the target bound q and arguing by contradiction (lemma_14, lemma_15). This needs p > 0, which we prove (NodeAlg.failProb_pos, EdgeAlg.failProb_pos) from the lower-bound chain.

    • Claim 11 only needs the lower-bound forms nodeLB_succ_of_edgeLB / edgeLB_of_nodeLB, which avoid this.

Minor imprecisions and typos

  1. In Claim 11 the recurrence for p̂_{t−1} uses ĉ_t; it should be ĉ_{t−1}. Claim 11's bound has exponent 5^{2t+1} after 2t steps; this is correct but not tight.

  2. Lemma 2's proof: "u must be the closest node of different colour to w" fails under ties, but only dist(w, ·) = dist(v, u) − 1 is used. The tie-breaking rule is irrelevant; only distances matter.

  3. Lemma 3: the tie-break "cycle with the smallest maximum identifier" is ambiguous when two cycles share their maximum-ID node. We use any fixed well-order on candidate cycle lists.

  4. Section 3.2 defines the distance to a cycle with ℓ(C) = |C|/2 or ⌊|C|/2⌋+1. Section 8.1 says "the length of the cycle".

Not formalized

  • Theorem 5 and Table 1 (classification of homogeneous LCLs; they depend on the external Theorems 19–24 in Appendix A.1).

  • The minimality argument of Section 8.3.

  • The exact numeric forms of Claim 12, Lemma 9 and Claim 10, and the "½ log* n − 4" remark.
