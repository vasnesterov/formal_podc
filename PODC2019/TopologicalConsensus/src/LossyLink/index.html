import Papers.PODC2019.TopologicalConsensus.DynamicNetworks /-! # The lossy link (Section 8.1 and Section 8.4 of the paper) Two processes `0, 1` and binary consensus. The graphs are `toR` (`0 → 1`), `toL` (`1 → 0`) and `both` (`0 ↔ 1`). * `MA3` (the oblivious message adversary choosing any graph of `{←, ↔, →}` in every round): consensus is impossible (Santoro–Widmayer). We prove it as the paper explains it in Section 8.1: for every valid, locally constant labelling (equivalently, every `d_u`-clopen partition with validity, `DN.dn_solvable_iff`) we construct a forever-bivalent execution, the common limit of `0`-labelled and `1`-labelled executions, and local constancy at that limit gives a contradiction (`not_solvable_MA3`). * `MA2` (choosing from `{←, →}`, Coulouma–Godard–Peters): consensus is solvable; the decision sets "the input of the round-1 sender is `v`" are clopen, so the universal algorithm of Theorem 5.2 solves consensus (`solvable_MA2`). -/
