Topological Characterization of Consensus under General Message Adversaries — PODC 2019

Thomas Nowak, Ulrich Schmid, Kyrill Winkler. Topological Characterization of Consensus under General Message Adversaries. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331624. Formalized version: the PODC'19 paper is arXiv 1905.09590 v1 (23 May 2019); numbering below follows v1. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf; note that this copy is arXiv v3 (2024), Topological Characterization of Consensus in Distributed Systems. v3 extends the paper to models with benign faults, and we formalized it as well (see the last section and the v1 ↔ v3 table).

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The core characterization (Theorem 5.5) is correct: under any message adversary, consensus is solvable iff the admissible executions split into decision sets that are open (hence clopen) in the minimum topology and contain the valent executions; its limit form (Corollary 5.18) holds too. But the paper's succinct characterizations built on it are false: Corollary 5.6 ("no connected component contains two valences"), Theorem 5.11 ("every component is broadcastable") and Theorem 6.7 (the non-compact case). For compact adversaries, Corollaries 6.1 and 6.5 are false as well and the characterization by ε-approximations (Theorem 6.6) is unestablished.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Corollary 5.6, Theorem 5.11 and Theorem 6.7 ("if" direction)

On three processes, the message adversary {π_k | k ≥ 1} ∪ {σ_∞} has only broadcastable components and no component with valent executions of two values, yet consensus is impossible (V1Cex.v1_theorem_5_11_false).

Impact. The paper's succinct characterizations built on Theorem 5.5 are false; Theorem 5.5 itself and the "only if" directions stand. v3 repairs Theorem 5.11 by adding "the strong broadcaster decision sets are clopen" (Theorem 7.9).

Details

  • Model. Three processes; message adversary {π_k | k ≥ 1} ∪ {σ_∞}.

    • σ_∞ has 1→0 and 1→2 in every round.

    • π_k has no edges in rounds 1..k-1, 2→0 in round k, and then 0→1, 0→2 forever.

  • The conditions hold.

    • The executions with pattern π_k fall into clopen sets, one per (x₀, x₂, k). Inside each, nobody ever hears of process 1, and process 0 broadcasts.

    • The component of (x, σ_∞) is {(y, σ_∞) | y₁ = x₁}, and process 1 broadcasts there.

    • So every component is broadcastable, and no component contains valent executions of two different values.

  • Consensus is impossible.

    • ((1,0,1), π_k) must decide like ((1,1,1), π_k), because process 0 never hears of process 1. So it decides 1.

    • For large k, process 1 cannot distinguish ((1,0,1), π_k) from ((0,0,0), σ_∞) before it has to decide in the latter.

  • Why the proofs fail. Theorem 5.11 "⇐" and the meta-procedure behind Corollary 5.6 assume that the union of the components of the v-valent executions is open. That holds only with finitely many components. Here the components {(x, σ_∞)} are not open.

  • The adversary is not limit-closed, which is why it also refutes Theorem 6.7 (the non-compact case).

❌ Corollaries 6.1 and 6.5 (finitely many connected components, ε-approximations of components)

The limit-closed adversary {σ_n} ∪ {σ_∞} on two processes, with σ_∞ = (1→0)^ω and σ_n equal to σ_∞ with 0↔1 in round n+1, makes consensus solvable but has infinitely many components (CanonicalCex.v1_corollary_6_1_false), and for every ε > 0 the ε-approximation of (y, σ_∞) contains executions (x, σ_n) from other components (CanonicalCex.v1_corollary_6_5_false).

Impact. Both statements are false, and nothing we proved recovers finiteness or a matching ε. This also invalidates the proofs of Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.6 (next item).

Details

Every (x, σ_n) is its own component. The proof's "open covering" by components is not open.

❌ Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.6 (unestablished)

Theorem 6.6 is derived from Theorem 5.11 and Corollary 6.5, both false, and the proof of Lemma 6.4 rests on Corollary 6.1 and on the step "setting ε < d/2", which gives no bound on the iteratively grown PS^ε, so the proofs are invalid.

Impact. Statements unestablished: we did not settle them.

❌ Corollary 5.15 ("⇐")

The proof relies on the triangle inequality, which d_min does not satisfy.

Impact. Unestablished; v3 drops this direction. We did not formalize Corollary 5.15. A related Lean example (ClosureCounterexample.closure_not_setDistZero) shows a point in the closure of a set at positive distance from it.

⚠️ Theorem 5.9 and Corollary 5.10 (connected broadcastable sets)

The proof builds U(a) by asymmetric forward reachability and treats it as an equivalence class, so the needed disjointness of U(a) and V(a) does not follow.

Impact. We prove the conclusion only in a weaker form: for message adversaries, every component of a solvable adversary has a common broadcaster with constant input (DN.dn_corollary_7_7). No Lean theorem states Theorem 5.9 itself, for an arbitrary connected broadcastable set and a given broadcaster.

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 4.7 (statement). It allows any d : X × X → {0,1} and fails without reflexivity (Lemma42Counterexample.lemma_4_2_false_without_refl); every distance the paper applies it to is reflexive.

  • Corollary 5.18. "Limit points in PT^ω" is ambiguous for these non-metric topologies; we state it in Σ.

🛠️ Theorem 5.11 "⇒" (broadcastable components)

The proof does not show that its execution c lies in the needed component.

Impact. The statement is true for message adversaries: every component of a solvable adversary has a common broadcaster with constant input (DN.dn_corollary_7_7).

Repair

A different argument: the first label flip along the inputs 1^k 0^{n-k} under a fixed pattern gives the broadcaster.

🔧 Lemma 4.7 (product topology, proof)

The proof claims that the balls B_1(C) are open, which fails when the zero-distance relation is not transitive, as for d_min on configurations, where v1 applies the lemma (BallCounterexample.ball_one_not_isOpen).

Impact. The statement is true for reflexive 0/1 distances.

Repair

A chain argument (lemma_4_2): replace B_1 by the set reachable through chains of zero-distance steps, moving one coordinate at a time.

Model (v1, DynamicNetworks.lean).

  • n lock-step synchronous, correct processes run a full-information protocol.

  • An arbitrary message adversary MA ⊆ (ℕ → Graph) chooses the round graphs (CSLib HeardOf.Graph convention). It need not be limit-closed.

  • All input assignments are admissible, independently of the pattern.

  • The view of p is compared by FIIndist: recursive equality of full-information views, equivalent to equal process-time-graph views (the shared Cslib.HeardOf.FIIndist of Cslib/Computability/Distributed/DynamicNetworks/FullInfo.lean with initial views given by the inputs). The heard-of relation Heard is the shared journey relation (Cslib.DynNet.Journey), and the generic model's indistinguishability relations form a Cslib.Knowledge.IndistSystem.

  • The minimum topology is τU, induced by d_u = min_p d_p. Decision sets are labellings Σ → V.

  • This is an instance of the generic model of v3, Model.lean.

Errors in the 2024 extension (arXiv v3 only)

v3 generalizes v1 to a generic model with obedient/disobedient processes and weak clocks. Its new statements contain further errors, all formalized:

  1. Theorem 5.2 "if" (uniform topology d_u) is false.

    • Background. d_u only counts processes obedient in both executions, which misses faulty processes that cannot detect their own fault.

    • Counterexample. Two non-communicating processes; process 0 is faulty from round 1 in the mixed execution. (CexUniform.theorem_5_2_false)

    • Fix. The asymmetric distance d_uc. (Model.theorem_5_2c)

    • Same error. Section 8.2's computation d_u(α, α₀) = 0 contradicts the definition of d_p.

  2. Theorems 5.2/5.3 "if" are false in the generic weak-clock model.

    • Counterexample. Global time is hidden; executions differ only by a time shift. (CexAsync.theorem_5_3_false_async)

    • Fix. We add LockstepRecall: views determine the global time and earlier views. With it, Theorem 5.3 is proved. (Model.theorem_5_3)

  3. Theorem 7.9 / Corollaries 8.2, 8.3 are false with the canonical (smallest common) broadcaster.

    • Counterexample. A limit-closed adversary where a canonical broadcaster decision set is not closed. (CanonicalCex.theorem_7_9_canonical_false)

    • What holds. With an existentially chosen broadcaster the claim is true for message adversaries. (DN.dn_theorem_7_9_exists)

  4. Lemma 8.6 / Corollary 8.7 are false. (CanonicalCex.corollary_8_7_false)

Also proved for v3:

  • Theorem 5.3 under LockstepRecall;

  • the strong-validity version of Theorem 5.2 (Model.theorem_5_2c_strong);

  • Theorem 7.12 (1)⇔(3)⇔(4) for message adversaries (DN.binary_iff_strong, DN.binary_iff_weak);

  • Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.5 (Limits.lean).
