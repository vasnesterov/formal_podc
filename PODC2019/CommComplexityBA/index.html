Communication Complexity of Byzantine Agreement, Revisited — PODC 2019

Ittai Abraham, T-H. Hubert Chan, Danny Dolev, Kartik Nayak, Rafael Pass, Ling Ren, Elaine Shi. Communication Complexity of Byzantine Agreement, Revisited. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331629. Formalized version: arXiv 1805.03391 v4 (15 Feb 2020); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. Both lower bounds hold with new arguments for their central steps: Theorem 4 / Theorem 1 (strongly adaptive adversary ⇒ Ω(f²) expected messages), with a better constant εf²/2 instead of (εf)², and Theorem 13 (no setup ⇒ no sublinear-multicast Byzantine broadcast), which needs n ≥ C + 2. The proof of Theorem 7, the expected constant rounds and O(λ) multicasts of the synchronous subquadratic protocol, has a wrong step whose apparent fix is not proved in Lean. The upper-bound sections (4–6, 9) were otherwise not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 7 and Lemma 2 (Section 5.3, wrong threshold; not formalized)

Theorem 7's proof claims that (1/2+ε/2)n so-far-honest nodes have not terminated, which needs fewer than εn/2 terminated honest nodes, not fewer than n/2.

Impact. Not formalized and not Lean-checked, so nothing we proved saves the step. With the case split at εn/2, Lemma 3 still holds with probability 1 − (1−λ/n)^{εn/2} ≥ 1 − e^{−ελ/2}; this is argued informally only.

Details

Lemma 2's statement is true, but its proof's n_h > n/2 is wrong: fewer than n/2 terminated forever-honest nodes only gives n_h > εn, because there are only about (1/2+ε)n honest nodes. This still gives a Θ(1) probability, so Lemma 2 survives. The real error is in Theorem 7.

🔧 Theorem 13 (Byzantine broadcast without setup) for n = 2

Theorem 13 is false for n = 2: with nodes {sender, S} and C = 1, the sender sends its bit once and S outputs it, which solves Byzantine broadcast with probability 1 with at most one speaker (theorem_13_false_n2).

Impact. The impossibility survives with one added condition: the theorem holds for n ≥ C + 2 (theorem_13).

Details

The protocol solves broadcast against one adaptive corruption. The proof needs a forever-honest node other than S in world W_{h,b}, so it needs n ≥ C + 2. The counterexample is Lean-checked against our restricted (injecting) adversary. It is also valid against the full Byzantine adversary: an honest S can only be fooled by a corrupt sender, and then validity is vacuous. That part is argued informally only.

Repair

We prove the theorem under 1 ≤ C, C + 2 ≤ n (theorem_13).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 1 (Section 5.3, not formalized) says that, except with probability exp(−Ω(λ)), fewer than λ/2 eventually-corrupt nodes are eligible for any given message; this is false as stated. In iteration 1 all honest nodes vote for their common input b, and about (1/2+ε)λ eligible honest nodes send (Vote, 1, b). The adaptive adversary then corrupts them all, after the fact; its budget f ≈ n/2 ≫ λ allows this. So more than λ/2 eventually-corrupt nodes are eligible with probability close to 1. The proof ("each so-far-honest node independently has a λ/n probability") ignores that corruption can depend on revealed eligibility. The intended term counts nodes that were already corrupt when they mined the message, the distinction the paper itself makes in Section 4.3. With it the consistency proof (Theorems 5 and 6) goes through, as argued informally: the node i* only needs to be honest when it sends Commit, not forever-honest. The same imprecision ("forever-honest" / "eventually-corrupt") appears in Theorems 5, 6 and 9.

  • Theorem 8 says "O(λ) messages" and Theorem 12 says "O(λ² log Δ) messages". Both mean multicasts; Theorems 7 and 11 say O(nλ) / O(nλ² log Δ) messages.

  • Proof of Theorem 4: V must avoid the designated sender, so one needs f/2 ≤ ⌈n/2⌉ − 1 rather than f/2 < ⌈n/2⌉. With f < n and |V| = ⌈f/2⌉ it is fine.

Typos

  • "Section 8 presents the model" / "for the reasons mentioned in Section 8" should refer to Section 2.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 4 · Theorem 13

🛠️ Theorem 4 (the key conditional-probability step)

The proof's claim Pr[Y₁ | X₁ ∩ X₂] ≥ 1/2, "by the definition of V", is unjustified: V only bounds the unconditional probability that p, receiving nothing, outputs 0.

Impact. The bound survives through a new argument, with a better constant εf²/2 instead of (εf)² (theorem_4, theorem_4_paper, Protocol.core_bound).

Details

The proof conditions on X₁ ∩ X₂: under adversary A, the randomly chosen p ∈ V receives at most f/2 messages. Both p's silent output and X₁ ∩ X₂ depend on p's own coins. Under A, p behaves honestly towards U, so it can reveal its coin, and U can then contact p exactly when p's silent output would be 1. With a plain union bound the paper's constants only give Pr[Y₁ ∩ Y₂] > 1/4 - 3ε, which is no contradiction.

Repair

Two steps.

  1. For every p ∈ V, the event "p does not output 0 when silent" is covered by three events: failure under A, failure under A'_p, and "U sends p more than f/2 messages".

  2. Markov's inequality is applied to each p separately, instead of the paper's Markov-plus-averaging step.

This gives E[#messages] ≥ 2ε·⌈f/2⌉·(⌊f/2⌋+1) ≥ εf²/2. Since ε ≤ 1/4, this is at least the paper's (εf)². (theorem_4, theorem_4_paper, Protocol.core_bound)

🛠️ Theorem 13 ("Pr[A_s] ≥ p" and the speaker count of A_r)

The proof's bound "Pr[A_s] ≥ p" is unjustified, and A_r is measured wrongly: the protocol's complexity guarantee counts only messages of honest nodes, and the simulated execution A_s is not an execution against any adversary.

Impact. For n ≥ C + 2 the threshold 5/6 survives: the bound Pr[A_r ∩ A_s] ≥ 2p − 1 is re-proved by a first-crossing argument (theorem_13_general, Protocol.not_E, Protocol.output_S_symm).

Details

In W_{h,b} the adversary has corrupted the simulated speakers, so the guarantee does not bound the number of speakers of the whole real copy, which is what the symmetry argument needs. No guarantee applies to A_s directly.

Repair

The first-crossing argument uses only the worlds W_{c,b} and W_{c,1−b} (there only S is corrupted): whichever copy exceeds C speakers first does so identically in the corresponding W_c world. The paper's "without loss of generality Pr[X | A_r ∩ A_s] ≥ 1/2" is also replaced by an explicit symmetry. A measure-preserving exchange of the two copies' tapes maps W_{h,0} to W_{h,1}. Measurability is never needed. (theorem_13_general, Protocol.not_E, Protocol.output_S_symm)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model for Theorems 4 and 1 (Model.lean).

  • n nodes, lock-step synchronous rounds, infinite executions. Each node's output is its first decision.

  • Randomness is one probability space (Ω, μ), and nodes may use ω arbitrarily. This covers private coins, shared coins and any setup (PKI, CRS, random oracle), as the paper claims.

  • The protocol is history-based: send p x ω h q : Option M and decide p x ω h : Option Bool.

  • Regularity: messages are countable, and send/decide are measurable in ω for each fixed history (Protocol.IsMeasurable). This is needed only for Markov's inequality and for the classification of silent outputs.

  • The adversary is a deterministic strongly adaptive omission adversary with budget f. It sees the communication pattern, including the current round. It then corrupts nodes and removes any current-round message whose sender or receiver is now corrupted (after-the-fact removal). Corrupted nodes otherwise follow the protocol.

  • This is the weakest adversary class the proof needs (the paper notes the bound holds even for omission adversaries). Against a weaker class, "the protocol is secure" is a weaker hypothesis, so the lower bound is stronger.

  • Failure events are measured by the outer measure.

  • The expected number of messages sent by forever-honest nodes is ∫⁻ honestMsgs.

Model for Theorem 13 (NoSetupModel.lean).

  • No setup: independent private tapes ω p ~ ν p.

  • Plain authenticated channels with multisets of messages per channel and round.

  • The adversary has independent fresh tapes ρ. It sees all past messages, corrupts adaptively (at most C nodes), and injects extra messages on channels with a corrupted endpoint. Corrupted nodes keep running the protocol.

  • This is a restriction of the paper's Byzantine adversary, which makes the impossibility stronger.

  • Complexity is "at most C forever-honest nodes ever speak". Multicast complexity ≤ C implies it.

  • We assume the BB guarantee and the complexity guarantee hold separately, each with probability p. This is weaker than assuming them jointly.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The hypotheses of Theorem 4 are satisfiable non-trivially: a send-and-relay protocol solves BB with probability 1 for n = 3, f = 1 (relay_solvesBB). The n = 2 counterexample shows that the Theorem 13 definitions are satisfiable. Not covered:

  • There is no non-vacuity witness for SolvesBA (Theorem 1's hypothesis).

  • C = 0 is excluded from theorem_13; that case is trivial but not proved.

The hypothesis ε ≤ 1/4 of theorem_4 / theorem_1 is not an issue in the paper: for ε > 1/4 the paper's hypothesis is unsatisfiable. It is needed in Lean only because ENNReal.ofReal truncates negative numbers at 0. The models are paper-local; inboxes, histories and the first decision (firstSome?) are those of CSLib's lock-step model (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/LockStep/Parallel.lean).
