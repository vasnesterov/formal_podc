Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Communication Complexity of Byzantine Agreement, Revisited — PODC 2019.

Paper: Abraham, Chan, Dolev, Nayak, Pass, Ren, Shi, Communication Complexity of Byzantine Agreement, Revisited, PODC 2019, arXiv 1805.03391v4.

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Theorem 4, proof: unjustified conditioning (proof gap; statement true, proved with a better constant).

    • The claim Pr[Y₁ | X₁ ∩ X₂] ≥ 1/2 does not follow from the definition of V, which bounds only the unconditional probability of p's silent output.

    • X₁ ∩ X₂ depends on p's coins through p's messages to U.

    • A union bound with the paper's constants yields only 1/4 − 3ε, which is no contradiction.

    • Repaired in LowerBound.lean: we use a union bound per p ∈ V together with Markov's inequality per node. The averaging over p and the event X₁ are no longer needed.

    • Result: E ≥ 2ε·|V|·(k+1) with |V| = ⌈f/2⌉, k = ⌊f/2⌋, hence ≥ εf²/2 ≥ (εf)².

  2. Theorem 13 (BB version): false for n = 2 (false statement; one added condition saves it).

    • Theorem 3 (BA) is true at n = 2 by the classical n ≤ 2t argument. The BA ⇒ BB transfer is not formalized.

    • Lean counterexample: theorem_13_false_n2 (C = 1, probability 1). It is checked against the restricted injecting adversary. It is also valid against the full Byzantine adversary, but that is argued informally only.

    • The proof needs a forever-honest node other than S in W_{h,b}, i.e. n ≥ C + 2.

    • We prove the theorem under 1 ≤ C and C + 2 ≤ n. C ≥ 1 is needed because W_{c,b} corrupts S. C = 0 is trivial but is not covered.

    • For 3 ≤ n ≤ C + 1 the statement is presumably true by the classical n ≤ 3t impossibility for broadcast without setup, but this is not proved here.

  3. Theorem 13, proof: Pr[A_s] ≥ p and the speaker count of A_r (proof gap; statement true).

    • The simulated copy is not an execution against any adversary.

    • In W_{h,b} the honest-message guarantee does not bound the real copy's speakers that the adversary corrupted.

    • Replaced by a first-crossing argument through the W_c worlds (Protocol.not_E), giving μ(E) ≥ 1 − 2(1 − q₂).

  4. Theorem 13, proof: "without loss of generality Pr[X | A_r ∩ A_s] ≥ 1/2" (imprecise).

    • Replaced by an explicit symmetry: the measure-preserving exchange σ of the two copies' tapes (swapE_mp) and Protocol.output_S_symm.

    • The resulting bound E ⊆ X₀ ∪ σ⁻¹X₁ gives μ(E) ≤ 4(1 − q₁).

    • General form (theorem_13_general): 1 ≤ 4(1 − q₁) + 2(1 − q₂). With q₁ = q₂ = p this reads p ≤ 5/6, the paper's threshold.

  5. Lemma 1 (Section 5.3) is false as stated (false statement; not formalized).

    • The adversary can corrupt, after the fact, the ≈ (1/2+ε)λ honest nodes that sent (Vote, 1, b). More than λ/2 eventually-corrupt nodes are then eligible, with probability close to 1.

    • The correct statement counts nodes corrupt at mining time. Theorems 5, 6 and 9 should use "honest when sending" instead of "forever-honest" / "eventually-corrupt". With that they go through.

  6. Theorem 7 (Section 5.3): threshold n/2 (proof gap; not formalized, not Lean-checked).

    • Lemma 2's statement is true. Its proof's n_h > n/2 should be n_h > εn, which still gives Θ(1).

    • The real error is Theorem 7's claim that (1/2+ε/2)n so-far-honest nodes have not terminated. The case split should be at εn/2.

  7. Typos.

    • Theorem 8 and Theorem 12 say "messages" where they mean multicasts.

    • References to "Section 8" for the model should be to Section 2.

Modelling choices (Theorems 4 and 1)

  • Randomness. One probability space (Ω, μ). Nodes may depend on all of ω (shared randomness, any setup). This is more general than the paper's private coins, so the lower bound is stronger.

  • Synchrony. Lock-step rounds, infinite executions. The output is the first decision, and "no output" = termination failure.

  • No self-messages. Self-messages are local computation and are not modelled.

  • Messages. At most one message per ordered pair and round; M countable.

  • Regularity. Protocol.IsMeasurable: send and decide are measurable in ω for each fixed history. The paper's model has no such regularity issues because executions are discrete ITMs. Only Markov's inequality (cntTo measurable) and the classification of silent outputs (disjoint events adding up to ≤ 1) use it. Failure probabilities are outer measures, so the hypothesis "error ≤ 1 − q" needs no measurability.

  • Adversary.

    • Deterministic, adaptive, strongly adaptive (after-the-fact removal) omission adversary.

    • It observes only the communication pattern (who sends to whom), including the current round.

    • It can omit messages from or to corrupted nodes and cannot forge.

    • This is the class the paper mentions ("even under a more constrained omission adversary"). The hypothesis is required only against it, which makes the theorem stronger.

    • The paper's adversaries are p.p.t.; ours are explicit strategies that are as efficient as the protocol. Computational bounds are not modelled.

  • Adversary A (deafAdv). Nodes of V ignore all incoming messages. The paper has them ignore the first f/2 messages from U plus all messages from V. The proof uses only the event "at most f/2 messages reach p", on which both versions coincide.

  • Adversary A' (blockAdv).

    • It blocks all U → p messages of a round as long as the cumulative count (including this round) is ≤ k. The paper blocks "the first f/2 attempts".

    • Our rule avoids ordering messages inside a round and corrupts at most k senders.

    • p is fixed. The paper picks p at random; averaging is replaced by per-node Markov.

  • Parameters. |V| = ⌈f/2⌉, k = ⌊f/2⌋, so |V| − 1 + k ≤ f and |V| + k = f < n.

  • Statement. There exist a sender input (for BA, an input configuration) and an adversary (static, corrupting ⌈f/2⌉ nodes) with E[messages by forever-honest nodes] ≥ εf²/2. Hypotheses: f < n and ε ≤ 1/4.

  • ε ≤ 1/4. This hypothesis is a Lean artifact: ENNReal.ofReal truncates 1 − (3/4 + ε) at 0. In the paper, ε > 1/4 makes the hypothesis unsatisfiable, so the statement is vacuous there.

  • Non-vacuity. SolvesBB has a witness (relay_solvesBB); SolvesBA has none.

  • Theorem 1 (BA). Proved directly with the same core, not via the BB reduction. Inputs: V gets false, U gets v, where v is the value that the silent V-nodes (input false) output with probability ≤ 1/2.

Modelling choices (Theorem 13)

  • No setup. Independent private tapes ω p ~ ν p; the adversary has an independent fresh copy ρ : P → T with the same law. The adversary's randomness is fixed to this form, a restriction that strengthens the impossibility.

  • Channels. Plain authenticated channels with multisets of messages per channel and round, so a corrupted node can deliver both its real and its simulated message to S.

  • Adversary.

    • It injects messages on channels with a corrupted endpoint; corrupted nodes keep running the protocol.

    • It sees all messages of previous rounds and is non-rushing.

    • This is a restriction of the paper's Byzantine adversary.

  • Complexity.

    • FewSpeakers: at most C forever-honest nodes ever send. This is implied by multicast complexity ≤ C and covers the paper's remark on "at most C nodes send".

    • The BB guarantee and the complexity guarantee are separate hypotheses (each with probability p), which is weaker than a joint one.

  • Truncation rule.

    • The adversary delivers simulated messages to S in round r iff at most C simulated nodes have spoken in rounds ≤ r. It corrupts exactly those speakers.

    • The paper corrupts "the first C" speakers. Ours may stop earlier within a round, which needs no intra-round order and is equivalent on the event used.

Not formalized

  • Sections 4–6: the warm-up protocols, the subquadratic protocols in the F_mine model, and their Chernoff-bound analyses (Lemmas 1–4, Theorems 5–12, Corollaries 1–2).

  • Section 9: cryptographic instantiation (adaptively secure VRF from NIZK and commitments).

  • The BA ⇒ BB reduction of Section 2. Theorem 1 is proved directly instead.
