Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Fast Concurrent Data Sketches — PODC 2019.

Paper: Rinberg, Spiegelman, Bortnikov, Hillel, Keidar, Rhodes, Serviansky. Fast Concurrent Data Sketches. PODC 2019 (brief announcement; we use the arXiv version 1902.10995v2, whose numbering is that of the PODC paper). The PODC version defers all proofs to "the full paper [24]"; the proofs we checked are those of the journal version (ACM TOPC 9(2), 2022, author copy iditkeidar.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/ConcurrentSketches.pdf): Section 6 (Lemmas 2–8, Invariants 1–3) and Section 7 (Claims 1–6, Lemmas 9–10).

Statement-level issues

  1. The initial hint 1 (Algorithm 2, lines 108–109; Lemma 1, Theorem 1). hint_i and prop_i start at the constant 1, but shouldAdd(h, a) is only required to be safe for hints h returned by calcHint() of a sketch that summarises a prefix of the stream. For a composable sketch whose empty-sketch hint is not 1, the first batch of every thread is filtered with an unconstrained hint and Lemma 1 / Theorem 1 fail: cntSketch (an exact counter, hints always 2, shouldAdd h a = (h ≠ 1)) satisfies every requirement of Section 5.1, yet with N = b = 1 two completed updates followed by two queries returning 0 is an execution of ParSketch with no linearization in SeqSketch_{Nb} (parSketch_hint_one_not_relaxed, parSketch_hint_one_not_stronglyLinearizable, Counterexample.lean). We prove Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 under the hypothesis S.Safe hint₀ [] (filtering by hint₀ is harmless on every stream), which holds for hint₀ = calcHint(empty sketch) (safe_init_hint). 🔧 The intended reading ("the hint of the empty sketch") is recoverable from the paper (hints are otherwise always produced by calcHint; for the only instantiation, the Θ sketch, Θ = 1 initially — theta_calcHint_init; the "trivial" implementation shouldAdd ≡ true does not care), and the fix is local (an initial value). The full version explicitly claims the algorithm works for "any composable sketch adhering to the API" (arXiv v2 only calls the algorithm "generic" and states Lemma 1 / Theorem 1 for "ParSketch instantiated with" the sketch). This is a wrong initial value, not a gap in the design: the repair (initialise hint_i, prop_i with globalS.calcHint() of the empty globalS) keeps the claim for every composable sketch, with no extra assumption on the sketch. The journal proof (Invariant 3, case shouldAdd false: "let H be the last hint returned to t_i") already reasons with hints produced by calcHint, i.e. with the intended version. Note that in OptParSketch the initial prop_i = 1 is also copied into hint_i at the first buffer switch (line 125 passes at once), so both initial values need the fix; the formalization uses hint₀ for both.

  2. Section 5.3: "the sequential Θ sketch answers queries with perfect accuracy in streams with up to k unique elements" (and Section 3: "Θ is 1 during the first k updates"). For Algorithm 1 (read with set semantics, the only reading under which the claim can hold) the query is exact only for at most k − 1 distinct hash values; with exactly k the initial 1 is evicted and the estimate is (k − 1)/Θ (thetaSketch_query_exact, thetaSketch_query_of_le, thetaSketch_not_exact_k). If "init k 1's" is read as a multiset, the estimate is (k−1)/Θ from the first update on and never exact. A remark motivating eager propagation, off by one; 🔧 The fix changes the number, but the intended meaning ("exact until the sample set is full", Section 3) is recoverable, it is an index-level slip caused by the sentinel 1 occupying a slot of Algorithm 1's sample set, and nothing in the paper depends on it. (For the library sketch of Section 7, whose exact mode is not tied to a sentinel, the sentence may well be true; it is false for the paper's own Algorithm 1.) The failure holds for every k (thetaSketch_query_of_le: estimate (k − 1)/Θ with Θ < 1); thetaSketch_not_exact_k is only the smallest instance (k = 1).

  3. Definition 2 is weaker than what Section 6 uses (not a false statement; it makes Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 weaker than the paper's use of them). (a) An r-relaxation may drop up to r entries, including queries, so up to r queries of a history in SeqSketch_r may return arbitrary values (isRelaxation_drop). (b) An entry of the relaxation may be preceded by arbitrarily many entries that follow it in H': U ++ [q] is a 1-relaxation of q :: U for every U (isRelaxation_future), so a query may reflect any number of later updates. Section 6 ("By Theorem 1, a query can miss up to r updates"; "Given Theorem 1, the optimised concurrent sketch's error is bounded by the relaxation's error bound") models only queries that see all but at most r of the preceding updates. This does not follow from Theorem 1 as stated, but it does follow from the paper's proof: we prove the stronger property SeqSketchRPast (no query dropped; a query is preceded in the relaxed history only by operations that precede it in the linearization), lemma1_pastOnly, theorem1_pastOnly. Proof gap in the step from Section 5 to Section 6, statements true. 🔧

Versions

The PODC 2019 publication (DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331567, pp. 207–208) is a two-page brief announcement. The file papers/2019/3293611.3331567_…pdf and our paper.pdf are arXiv 1902.10995v2 (Dec. 2019), which is the PPoPP 2020 text (it even cites the PODC brief announcement as "the full paper [24]"). Differences that matter for the checked claims:

  • Lemma 1 (Nb) and Theorem 1 (2Nb) and Algorithm 2 (including hint_i, prop_i init 1) are identical in v1 (Feb. 2019, contemporaneous with the brief announcement; proofs in its Appendix A), v2 and the journal version (which states Lemma 1 with 2Nb, item 6).

  • The Section 5.3 sentence "perfect accuracy … up to k unique elements" is in v2 / PPoPP / journal, not in v1; "Θ is 1 during the first k updates" (Section 3) is in v1 and v2.

  • The quantiles bound ε_r = ε − rε/n + r/n (Section 6.2) is from v2; v1 has a different quantiles analysis (its Lemma 3), which we did not check.

  • The phrase "any composable sketch adhering to the API" is only in the journal version. Our findings concern Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 (common to all versions) and the v2 claims listed above.

Proof-level issues (statements true)

  1. Lemma 6 of the full version says "the only updates without a visibility point are those in up_suff_i(σ)". An update that has been invoked but has not yet executed line 120 (shouldAdd) has no visibility point either and is not in up_suff_i. The bound |f(σ)| ≥ |l(σ)| − Nb still holds, because such an update can only be pending while counter_i < b, i.e. while |up_suff_i| ≤ b − 1. In our model shouldAdd(hint_i, a) is evaluated at the invocation (it is a local computation on the thread's own variable), so the issue disappears; the count is Inv.allButAtMost (from TI.length_le). This is a claim inside a proof of a true lemma, in the journal version only.

  2. Theorem 1's proof (full version, Section 6.3) simulates one OptParSketch thread by two ParSketch threads and appeals to "forward simulation ⇒ strong linearisability". The mapping does not preserve histories: the update that fills a buffer returns in OptParSketch right after line 229, while in the simulating ParSketch execution the corresponding update of t_i^cur is still waiting at line 125 (its response comes one batch later); in the first round the inactive simulated thread t_i^1 has no pending update at all when line 225 is mapped to its line 125. So the simulated execution is not an execution of ParSketch with the same history, and the generic simulation theorem does not apply as stated; the conclusion holds because the specific linearization (updates at invocation) ignores update responses. We do not formalize the simulation; we prove Theorem 1 directly with the same invariant method as Lemma 1 (OptParSketchInv.lean, OptParSketchStep.lean, OptParSketchMain.lean). 🛠️.

  3. Full version, Lemma 1 states r = 2Nb for ParSketch but proves Nb; the PODC version states Nb. We prove Nb.

  4. Error analysis (Section 6 / full version Section 7). Checked algebraically (ErrorBounds.lean): the quantiles bound ε_r = ε − rε/n + r/n (Lemma 10; the PODC version has no assumption on ε, the full version assumes ε < 1/2; the computation needs only ε ≤ 1, and with genuine ranks 0 ≤ ρ ≤ n − m and r ≤ n it holds for every ε: quantile_relaxed_rank_of_rank_le; the failure for ε > 1 in quantile_relaxed_rank_fails uses a "rank" larger than the stream, so it is outside the paper's model); Claim 1 (extremes at j = 0 or j = r; the full version writes M(0), M(j) for the extreme order statistics); Lemma 9 (an equality written as ≤, harmless); the weak-adversary bounds (eqs. (3), (4)) and the corollary "RSE ≤ 2/√(k−2) when r ≤ √(k−2)". All correct. Claims 4–6 of the full version write i, j > 0 where i, j ≥ 0 is meant (typo).

Modelling choices

  • Memory model. The paper assumes a non-sequentially-consistent memory with fences on atomic variables; its proofs reason about atomic steps in an interleaving semantics. We use CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (sequentially consistent interleaving of atomic accesses to base objects), as the proofs do.

  • Global sketch. The paper requires concurrent snapshot/merge executions of the composable sketch to be strongly linearisable and then treats them as atomic steps; we model the global sketch as one atomic base object (globObj). snapshot returns an element of an abstract type Snap with snapQuery (snapshot s) = query s (the paper's requirement on snapshot).

  • Derandomised sketch. As in Section 4, the sketch is deterministic (coins fixed by an oracle): Sketch with init/update/query; a state summarises a stream iff it equals run of the stream (the paper's definition). Composable adds merge with merge (run A) (run B) = run (A ++ B), snapshot, calcHint, shouldAdd with the paper's filtering requirement (shouldAdd_spec). The requirement calcHint() ≠ 0 is built in: prop_i : Option Hint, none = 0.

  • Propagator. "for all t_i with prop_i = 0" is a deterministic round-robin scan (pCheck j reads prop_j, then moves to j + 1); any other scan order would be a special case of nondeterminism the proof does not use. Since CSLib processes act only while they have a pending operation, the propagator is started by a never-returning operation propagate that only t₀ may invoke (it never appears in linearizations). globalS.merge(localS_i) is split into reading localS_i and merging the value read (localS_i is accessed only by t₀ at that time). prop_i ← globalS.calcHint() reads the hint, then writes it.

  • Update threads. shouldAdd(hint_i, a) is evaluated at the invocation; wait until prop_i ≠ 0; hint_i ← prop_i uses the value read by the last iteration of the wait loop (prop_i cannot change while it is non-zero). In OptParSketch, cur_i is a shared register written only by t_i and read by t₀; lines 226–228 are one step (the write of cur_i).

  • Processes. N ≥ 1 update threads upd i, the propagator, and any type Q of query threads, each of which may invoke only its kind of operation. The paper's "0 ≤ i ≤ N" for update threads is read as 1 ≤ i ≤ N (typo: t₀ is the propagator).

  • Hints and counters are of arbitrary types (the paper declares int hint_i, int atomic prop_i, although the Θ sketch's hints are reals in (0, 1]).

  • b ≥ 1 is assumed (with b = 0 no thread ever propagates; degenerate).

  • Strong linearisability w.r.t. a set of histories (Definition 1 with SeqSketch_r in place of SeqSpec) is Impl.HStronglyLinearizable (staged in Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/HistorySpecLin.lean): a prefix-preserving map from executions to linearizations (entries carry responses; completed operations included, real-time order respected, pending operations may be included) whose histories lie in the set. SeqSketch is the set of histories legal for the derandomised sketch.

  • Definition 2 is formalized literally on lists of entries (IsRelaxation): "all but at most r" is "up to a list D of at most r exceptions"; "y precedes x in H" is [y, x] <+ H.

  • Not formalized: the Θ sketch's order-statistic moments (taken as hypotheses in rse_weak_lt), the strong adversary (numerical only in the paper), Table 1, the experiments (Section 7), the adaptive eager phase of Section 5.3 (a variable b), and Quantiles/HLL sketches as instances.
