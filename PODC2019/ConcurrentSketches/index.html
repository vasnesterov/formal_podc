Fast Concurrent Data Sketches — PODC 2019

Arik Rinberg, Alexander Spiegelman, Edward Bortnikov, Eshcar Hillel, Idit Keidar, Lee Rhodes, Hadar Serviansky. Fast Concurrent Data Sketches. PODC 2019 (brief announcement). DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331567. Formalized version: arXiv 1902.10995 v2 (Dec. 2019; the PPoPP 2020 text). The PODC 2019 publication itself is a two-page brief announcement; Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 are stated identically in v1 (Feb. 2019, proofs in its Appendix A), v2 and the journal version. v2 defers its proofs to "the full paper"; the proofs we checked are those of the journal version (ACM TOPC 9(2), 2022), Sections 6–7. See differencies.md for version differences. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 hold: the generic algorithms are strongly linearisable with respect to SeqSketch_{Nb} and SeqSketch_{2Nb}, for every composable sketch, provided the threads' initial hint is the empty sketch's hint (Algorithm 2 hard-codes 1, which is right for the Θ sketch but not in general). The simulation behind Theorem 1, the paper's main correctness result, does not preserve histories, so Theorem 1 took a new direct invariant proof; we prove both results in a stronger form, which is the one the error analysis needs. The error bounds of Section 6 check out algebraically, with the order-statistic moments of §6.1 taken as hypotheses.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 (Algorithm 2's initial hint 1)

Algorithm 2 starts with hint_i = prop_i = 1, which breaks Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 for general composable sketches: an exact counter whose calcHint always returns 2 and with shouldAdd(h, a) = (h ≠ 1) meets all requirements, but drops every update forever (parSketch_hint_one_not_stronglyLinearizable).

Impact. For the paper's Θ sketch, 1 is exactly the empty sketch's Θ, so only the generic claim is affected. With "initial hint = hint of the empty sketch" both results hold (lemma1, theorem1).

Details

The pre-filtering requirement of Section 5.1 only constrains shouldAdd(h, ·) for hints h produced by calcHint(). So the first batch of every thread is filtered with a hint about which nothing is assumed. With the counter above and N = b = 1, two completed updates followed by two queries returning 0 is an execution with no linearization in SeqSketch_{Nb}.

Repair

We prove both results with "initial hint = hint of the empty sketch" (more generally, any hint that filters harmlessly on every stream). (lemma1, theorem1)

🔧 Section 5.3 (exactness of the sequential Θ sketch, off by one)

"The sequential Θ sketch answers queries with perfect accuracy in streams with up to k unique elements" holds for Algorithm 1 only up to k − 1 distinct hashes: with exactly k, the initial 1 is evicted and the estimate becomes (k−1)/Θ, for every k (thetaSketch_query_exact, thetaSketch_query_of_le; smallest instance thetaSketch_not_exact_k).

Impact. A remark motivating eager propagation; nothing depends on it. The same off-by-one appears in "Θ is 1 during the first k updates".

Typos

  • Update threads are indexed 0 ≤ i ≤ N (t₀ is the propagator).

  • hint_i and prop_i are declared int but carry real Θ values.

  • Section 6.1 has a broken reference, "the RSE bound of Lemma ??".

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Theorem 1 · Definition 2 vs. Section 6

🛠️ Theorem 1 (simulation by ParSketch)

Section 5.2 announces a proof of Theorem 1 by simulating N OptParSketch threads with 2N ParSketch threads, and the full version carries it out, but the mapping does not preserve histories.

Impact. Statement true; the simulation is not repaired, and we prove Theorem 1 directly instead, by a separate invariant argument (theorem1).

Details

An update that fills a buffer returns in OptParSketch while the simulating ParSketch update still waits at line 125, and in the first round line 225 is mapped to line 125 of a thread with no pending operation.

🔧 Definition 2 vs. Section 6 (what "strongly linearisable w.r.t. SeqSketch_r" gives)

Definition 2 is weaker than what Section 6 uses: an r-relaxation may drop queries and may place an operation after arbitrarily many operations that follow it (isRelaxation_drop, isRelaxation_future).

Impact. The algorithms satisfy the stronger property Section 6 needs; we prove Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 in this form (lemma1_pastOnly, theorem1_pastOnly).

Details

An r-relaxation may drop up to r operations altogether, including queries, which may then return anything. It may also put an operation after arbitrarily many operations that follow it in H': U, q is a 1-relaxation of q, U for every list of updates U. So "strongly linearisable w.r.t. SeqSketch_r" does not by itself give the model of Section 6 ("a query misses up to r of the updates preceding it"), on which the error bounds rest.

Repair

In the witnessing relaxation no query is dropped, and a query is preceded only by operations that precede it in the linearization. (lemma1_pastOnly, theorem1_pastOnly)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib's asynchronous shared memory: an interleaving of atomic accesses to base objects. The paper assumes a weaker memory with fences, but its proofs also reason with atomic steps. No failures. The scheduler is arbitrary; we prove safety only.

  • Processes: N ≥ 1 update threads, the propagator t₀ (started by a never-returning propagate operation), and any number of query threads. The propagator scans the threads round-robin.

  • Base objects: the composable global sketch is atomic (merge, snapshot, calcHint). The paper requires its snapshot/merge to be strongly linearisable and treats them as atomic steps. There are also local sketches (update, read, reset), atomic registers prop_i : Option Hint (none = 0), and for OptParSketch registers cur_i.

  • Sketches are derandomised, as in Section 4 (coins fixed by an oracle). A Composable sketch carries the paper's requirements as hypotheses: merge of summaries summarises the concatenation; snapshots answer like the sketch; shouldAdd(calcHint(S), a) = false ⇒ a can be removed from any extension of the stream summarised by S. Everything is generic in the sketch.

  • SeqSketch is the set of histories of the sequential sketch. SeqSketch_r is Definition 2, taken literally. Strong linearisability w.r.t. a set of histories (Definition 1) is a prefix-preserving map from executions to linearizations in that set (Impl.HStronglyLinearizable). The witness is the paper's l (updates at invocation, queries at their snapshot). The relaxed history is the paper's f (visibility points), computed by a ghost run.

Full version. These remarks concern the journal version only and are not findings about the formalized text:

  • Lemma 6 overlooks updates that are invoked but have not yet run shouldAdd: they have no visibility point and are not in up_suff_i. The statement is true: the bound Nb survives because such an update can only be pending while counter_i < b.

  • Lemma 1 is stated with r = 2Nb but proved with Nb (the PODC version says Nb).

  • The extreme order statistics of Claim 1 are written M(0), M(j), and Claims 4–6 write i, j > 0 for i, j ≥ 0.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: thetaComposable instantiates all hypotheses with the paper's Θ sketch, and lemma1_theta, theorem1_theta apply Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 to it with the paper's literal initial hint. The model is built on CSLib's Cslib.SharedMemory (Impl, executions, linearizations). The new generic notion of strong linearisability w.r.t. a set of histories is staged in Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/HistorySpecLin.lean (CSLib candidate). The step lemmas in ExecLemmas.lean and kSmallest in ThetaSketch.lean are also CSLib candidates.
