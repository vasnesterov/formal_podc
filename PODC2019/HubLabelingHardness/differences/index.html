Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Hardness of Hub Labeling in Sparse Graphs — PODC 2019.

Paper: Kosowski, Uznański, Viennot, Hardness of exact distance queries in sparse graphs through hub labeling, PODC 2019 (arXiv 1902.07055v3). Scope: Section 2 (Theorem 2.1, Lemma 2.2, Theorem 1.1).

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Wrong step in the proof of Lemma 2.2 (🛠️).

    • The paper says: "for any u ∈ V_i and v ∈ V_j with i < j, dist_G(u,v) = dist_H(u,v)", justified by "the construction" for j = i + 1 and by vertex cuts for j > i + 1.

    • It is false. In G a walk may turn around inside an in-tree (leaf → root → other leaf) without visiting the tree's vertex. That is shorter than the corresponding H-walk.

    • Counterexample (dist_claim_false): b = 1, ℓ = 2, u = v_{0,(0,0)}, v = v_{1,(0,1)}, with d_G(u, v) ≤ 71 < 73 ≤ d_H(u, v).

    • The consequence actually used, the case v_{0,x}, v_{2ℓ,z} with z − x even, is true: d_G = d_H = Lopt, and every shortest G-walk passes through v_{ℓ,(x+z)/2} (lemma_2_2_G).

    • The repaired proof uses a potential that changes by at most 1 per edge (pot, abs_pot_sub_le), the level reflection (refl) and the cut at level ℓ (exists_mem_support_midLevel); see Distances.lean. The upper bound d_G ≤ d_H between vertices of H does hold (edist_main_le_wlen).

  2. Lemma 2.2 for H: routine gaps (the statement is true). The proof does not show:

    • that a walk with exactly 2ℓ edges is monotone in the level;

    • that a² + b² with a + b fixed and even has the unique minimizer a = b;

    • that this determines every vertex of the path;

    • that Lopt < (2ℓ + 1)A, which needs ℓ ≥ 1.

    All four are proved in HLemma.lean.

  3. Inequality (1) |S*_v| ≤ diam(G)·|S_v| ≤ (3ℓ+1)s²·4ℓ·|S_v|. The diameter bound is asserted without proof (and diam should be diam + 1, counting vertices: ✏️). We avoid it: only hubs h of v with d(v, h) ≤ L := 8ℓ²s² can serve the counted pairs, and L ≥ Lopt. So S*_v := union of fixed shortest paths to those hubs has size ≤ (L + 1)|S_v| (SimpleGraph.hubPathSet, card_hubPathSet_le). The paper also uses shortest-path trees; we use arbitrary fixed shortest paths, which suffice.

  4. Typos. "a path of length w(e) − 2b + −2" should be w(e) − 2b − 2. In "V = ⋃_{r=0}^{2ℓ} V_i" the union index is mismatched. The proof of Theorem 2.1 (iii) prints z = 2x − y and x = 2z − y where z = 2y − x and x = 2y − z are meant (✏️).

Statement-level choices (no error in the paper)

  • Θ in lower bounds.

    • Theorem 2.1 (iii) "≥ 2^{bℓ}·2^{−Θ(b+ℓ)}" is proved as ≥ 2^{bℓ}/2^{10(b+ℓ)} (theorem_2_1).

    • Theorem 1.1 "≥ n/2^{Θ(√log n)}" is proved as ≥ n/2^{29√(log₂ n)} (theorem_1_1).

    • In a lower bound only the O direction of the Θ has content.

  • Theorem 2.1 (i) is proved as 2ℓ·s^{ℓ+1} ≤ |V| ≤ 16ℓ²·s^{ℓ+3}. Since ℓ·s = 2^{b + log₂ ℓ}, this is |V| = 2^{bℓ}·2^{Θ(b + log ℓ)}.

  • Theorem 1.1 ("graphs of max-degree 3 require …") is proved for infinitely many n: for every N there is a graph with ≥ N vertices, namely G_{m,m} with m = N + 1. The paper takes b = ℓ = √(log N) with an adjusted N and does not discuss rounding; we use the graphs G_{m,m} directly. "Every n" (by padding) is not formalized.

  • Hub labeling definition (SimpleGraph.IsHubLabeling). We require a common hub on a shortest path (edist u w + edist w v = edist u v) only for reachable pairs. This is identical on connected graphs and weaker in general, so our lower bounds are slightly stronger. We do not prove that G_{b,ℓ} is connected; it is not needed.

  • Factor 2 in the counting. card_le_mul_sum gives |P| ≤ 2(L+1)·Σ_v|S_v|, without using that the sources V_0 and targets V_{2ℓ} are disjoint. This costs a constant factor, absorbed in the explicit constants.

  • Weights. hw u v = A + Σ_k (u_k − v_k)². On edges this equals the paper's A + (j_c − j'_c)², because adjacent vectors differ only at c.

  • Coordinates are 0-indexed. The changed coordinate between levels i, i+1 is i for i < ℓ and 2ℓ − 1 − i for i ≥ ℓ (the paper's c = i + 1, resp. 2ℓ − i).

  • Heap numbering of the trees: node n has children 2n+1, 2n+2, and the leaf of value t is s − 1 + t. In-trees of V_0 and out-trees of V_{2ℓ} are omitted, as in the paper.

  • Degree. Δ(G_{b,ℓ}) = 3 is stated as maxDegree = 3 (for any DecidableRel instance), plus ncard_neighborSet_le, the version without a decidability instance.

  • Assumptions b, ℓ ≥ 1 throughout, matching "positive integers". For b = 0 the parity triples degenerate.

Not formalized

  • Uniqueness of the shortest v_{0,x}–v_{2ℓ,z} path in G (the "consequently also in G" part of Lemma 2.2). We prove the distance and that all shortest walks pass through v_{ℓ,(x+z)/2}, which is all the theorem uses.

  • Section 3 (Theorem 1.6 / Observation 3.1, the Sum-Index reduction) and Section 4 (Theorems 4.1, 1.4, Lemma 4.2, the Ruzsa–Szemerédi upper bound).
