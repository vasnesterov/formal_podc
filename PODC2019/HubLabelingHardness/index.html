Hardness of Hub Labeling in Sparse Graphs — PODC 2019

Adrian Kosowski, Przemysław Uznański, Laurent Viennot. Hardness of exact distance queries in sparse graphs through hub labeling. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331625. Formalized version: arXiv 1902.07055 v3; numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. The hub-labeling lower bound holds as stated: Lemma 2.2 for the weighted layered graph H_{b,ℓ}, the pass-through and distance part of Lemma 2.2 for the degree-3 graph G_{b,ℓ}, Theorem 2.1 (i)–(iii) and Theorem 1.1 are proved, all with explicit constants. One step of the proof of Lemma 2.2 is false: the claim that G_{b,ℓ} preserves all distances of H_{b,ℓ} between levels; only the special case the theorem needs is true, and it needs a new potential argument. The Sum-Index reduction and the Ruzsa–Szemerédi upper bound (Sections 3 and 4) were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 2.1, inequality (1): |S*_v| ≤ diam(G)·|S_v| is off by one, since a single hub at distance diam gives a subtree of diam + 1 vertices. The factor should be diam + 1; the loose second inequality absorbs this (card_hubPathSet_le).

  • Proof of Theorem 2.1 (iii), counting of triplets: it prints z = 2x − y and x = 2z − y; from y = (x+z)/2 the right formulas are z = 2y − x and x = 2y − z (card_triples_le_sum).

Typos

  • "path of length w(e) − 2b + −2" should be w(e) − 2b − 2.

  • "V = ⋃_{r=0}^{2ℓ} V_i" has a mismatched index.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 2.2 for G_{b,ℓ}

🛠️ Lemma 2.2 for G_{b,ℓ} (distances preserved between levels)

The proof asserts dist_G(u, v) = dist_H(u, v) for all u ∈ V_i, v ∈ V_j with i < j, which is false already for b = 1, ℓ = 2, j = i + 1 (dist_claim_false).

Impact. Lemma 2.2's use is fine: Theorem 2.1 only needs the case u = v_{0,x}, v = v_{2ℓ,z} with z − x even, and there the conclusion is true; we prove it by a different argument (lemma_2_2_G).

Details

To transfer Lemma 2.2 from H_{b,ℓ} to G_{b,ℓ}, the proof asserts that dist_G(u, v) = dist_H(u, v) for all u ∈ V_i, v ∈ V_j with i < j. For j = i + 1 it cites "the construction". This is false.

  • The mechanism: in G, a walk can enter the in-tree T_w^in of an upper vertex w through one leaf and leave it through another leaf without visiting w. That saves up to 2b steps. The corresponding H-walk u → w → u' must pay both full edge weights.

  • Counterexample: b = 1, ℓ = 2 (A = 24), u = v_{0,(0,0)}, v = v_{1,(0,1)}.

    • Every H-walk from u to v needs at least 3 edges, one of them changing coordinate 2, so it weighs ≥ 3A + 1 = 73.

    • G has a walk of length 71: u → v_{1,(0,0)} → in-tree of v_{2,(0,1)} (leaf 0, root, leaf 1) → v_{1,(0,1)}.

    (dist_claim_false)

  • The vertex-cut argument for j > i + 1 inherits the same gap.

  • For the pair the theorem needs, d_G = d_H = 2ℓA + Σ_k (z_k − x_k)²/2, and every shortest G-walk passes through v_{ℓ,(x+z)/2} (lemma_2_2_G).

Repair

The repaired proof uses potentials, not a walk-by-walk comparison with H:

  • An integer function on V(G) changes by at most 1 per edge. It is 0 at v_{0,x} and ℓA + Σ_k (j_k − x_k)² at v_{ℓ,j}. Coordinates not yet "reached" carry a correction term 2(s−1)|d| − d², and the potential interpolates linearly along trees and paths. The slack A = 3ℓs² ≫ s² + b absorbs the turnaround shortcuts.

  • The level reflection of G gives the same bound from v_{2ℓ,z}.

  • V_ℓ separates the bottom from the top. Summing the two bounds gives Lopt + 2 Σ_k (j_k − m_k)² for a walk through v_{ℓ,j}, with m = (x+z)/2.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. Pure graph combinatorics; there is no distributed model, randomness or adversary.

  • H_{b,ℓ} is a SimpleGraph on Fin (2ℓ+1) × ([0,s−1]^ℓ) (s = 2^b, A = 3ℓs²). It has integer weights A + Σ_k (u_k − v_k)², which is the paper's A + (j_c − j'_c)² on edges. Walk weight wlen coincides with CSLib's Walk.wlength, and wdist is CSLib's weighted distance.

  • G_{b,ℓ} is built exactly as in the paper: in- and out-trees as heaps, and every H-edge of weight w becomes a path of w − 2b − 2 edges. Coordinates are 0-indexed.

  • A hub labeling (SimpleGraph.IsHubLabeling) asks for a common hub on a shortest path (d(u,w) + d(w,v) = d(u,v)) only for reachable pairs. This is the paper's definition on connected graphs and a weaker hypothesis in general, so the lower bounds are slightly stronger.

  • Throughout, b, ℓ ≥ 1, as in the paper ("positive integers").

Claim "dist_G = dist_H between levels" in the proof of Lemma 2.2: false

Counterexample.lean

The proof of Lemma 2.2 for H_{b,ℓ} does not argue that a walk with exactly 2ℓ edges climbs one level per edge, nor why minimizing δ_i² + δ_{2ℓ+1−i}² under a fixed sum has a unique solution that pins down every vertex of the path. These steps are routine, and we fill them in (lemma_2_2).

Supporting facts: edist_main_le_wlen (d_G ≤ d_H between vertices of H), pot and abs_pot_sub_le (the potential), refl_adj (level reflection is a homomorphism), exists_mem_support_midLevel (the cut at level ℓ), lopt_le_length_and (in Distances.lean).

Asymptotic notation.

  • Theorem 2.1 (iii) "≥ 2^{bℓ}·2^{−Θ(b+ℓ)}" and Theorem 1.1 "n/2^{Θ(√log n)}" are lower bounds, so only the O(·) direction of the Θ is meaningful. We prove explicit constants: 2^{bℓ}/2^{10(b+ℓ)} and n/2^{29√(log₂ n)}.

  • Theorem 1.1 is proved for infinitely many n, the usual reading of such a lower bound.

Not formalized. Uniqueness of the shortest v_{0,x}–v_{2ℓ,z} path in G, which the paper states and never uses, and whose proof rests on the false distance claim; we prove the pass-through property and the distance. The diameter estimate diam(G) ≤ (3ℓ+1)s²·4ℓ of inequality (1) in Theorem 2.1, which the paper asserts without proof: we avoid it by counting only hubs within distance L = 8ℓ²s² ≥ Lopt of v. These are the only hubs that can serve the counted pairs, so |S*_v| ≤ (L+1)|S_v| without any diameter bound. Not formalized either: Section 3 (Theorem 1.6, the reduction from Sum-Index) and Section 4 (Theorems 4.1/1.4, the Ruzsa–Szemerédi upper bound).
