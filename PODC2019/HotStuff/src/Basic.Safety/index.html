import Papers.PODC2019.HotStuff.Basic.Invariants /-! # Basic HotStuff: safety (Theorem 2) **Theorem 2** of the paper: two conflicting nodes cannot both be committed by correct replicas. ## The paper's proof and its gap The paper fixes a commit QC `qc₁` on `w` (view `v₁`) and takes the *lowest view* `v_s ∈ (v₁, v₂]` with a valid `prepareQC` `qc_s` on a node *conflicting* with `w`. It then claims that a correct replica `r` in both quorums is still locked "on the branch led by `w`" when it votes for `qc_s`, and that the justification of `qc_s` cannot have a view `> v₁` "by minimality of `v_s`". Both steps are not justified by view-minimality over *conflicting* nodes: * `r`'s lock may have moved (at some view in `(v₁, v_s)`) to a `precommitQC` on a *strict ancestor* of `w` — such a node does not conflict with `w`, so minimality says nothing, and a proposal conflicting with `w` may well extend it; * the justification `m.justify` of the proposal may be a QC on a common ancestor of `w` and `qc_s.node` (not conflicting with `w`) with a view in `(v₁, v_s)`, or a QC with a view `> v_s` (correct replicas never check that `m.justify.viewNumber < curView`, and before GST replicas may be in different views), again not excluded by minimality. The statement is nevertheless true. We prove it with a different invariant: call a `prepareQC` *bad* if its view is `> v₁` and its node does *not extend* `w` (this includes ancestors of `w`). Argue about the **first moment in time** at which a bad QC becomes valid rather than about the smallest view: before that moment, a correct replica that voted `commit` for `w` at `v₁` is locked on a node extending `w`, and every justification with a view `> v₁` extends `w`, so it never votes for a node not extending `w`. Hence once a bad QC is valid, at most `f` correct replicas have voted (or can still vote) `commit` for `w` at `v₁`, and the commit QC on `w` can never form (`SInv.inv1`).
