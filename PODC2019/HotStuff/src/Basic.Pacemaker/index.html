import Mathlib /-! # The exponential back-off pacemaker of Section 4.4 does not by itself synchronize views The paper suggests (Section 4.4) to implement `nextView` by a timer whose interval is doubled whenever it expires without a decision, and argues: "Since the interval is doubled at each time, the waiting intervals of all correct replicas will eventually have at least `T_f` overlap in common". This file shows that the argument is not valid as stated: two replicas whose timers are offset by one view keep an overlap of exactly `α` in *every* view, forever, however long the run. Such an offset arises, e.g., when a Byzantine leader of view `1` sends its `decide` message only to replica `A` at time `α`: `A` moves to view `2` without doubling its interval `α`, while `B` times out at the same moment and doubles its interval to `2α`. From then on (no decisions), `A` is in view `k + 2` during `[2^k α, 2^(k+1) α)` and `B` during `[(2^(k+1) − 1) α, (2^(k+2) − 1) α)`. If `α < T_f`, no view ever gets `T_f` time with both replicas in it, so (with `n = 4`, `f = 1` and a silent Byzantine replica, where every QC needs all three correct replicas) no decision is reached in any later view. This is the well-known *view synchronization* gap; the paper's Algorithm 5 later simply *assumes* "synchronized leadership after GST".
