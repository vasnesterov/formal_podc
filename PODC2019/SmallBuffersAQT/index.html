Small Buffers on a Path (AQT) — PODC 2019

Avery Miller, Boaz Patt-Shamir, Will Rosenbaum. With Great Speed Come Small Buffers: Space-Bandwidth Tradeoffs for Routing. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331614. Formalized version: arXiv 1902.08069 v1 (the only version, with the appendix); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The space-bandwidth tradeoff survives, but only in the number n of nodes: we prove Theorem 4.1 (space ℓ n^{1/ℓ} + σ + 1 when ρℓ ≤ 1) for HPTS with the levels of each phase in decreasing order, and the abstract's O(ℓ d^{1/ℓ} + σ) upper bound in the number d of destinations is proved nowhere. Lemma 4.8 is false for HPTS as printed, and the one-line change of the level order repairs it. PTS/PPTS on paths and trees (Propositions 3.1, 3.2, 3.5, B.3) and the lower bound (Theorem 5.1) are proved.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Abstract and introduction (the O(ℓ d^{1/ℓ} + σ) upper bound in terms of destinations)

The abstract and introduction claim O(ℓ d^{1/ℓ} + σ) space for d destinations (and O(log d) space when ρ ≤ 1/log d), but the paper never proves it: Theorem 4.1 is in terms of the number n of nodes.

Impact. The tradeoff is established only with n in place of d; the d-form is not refuted. The hierarchy is built on node indices, so the d-form does not follow directly; our sharper form gives only min(ℓ n^{1/ℓ}, d) + σ + 1 (theorem_4_1, Run.occ_le_keys). The lower bound in terms of d follows from Theorem 5.1, since d ≤ n. Not formalized.

🔧 Lemma 4.8 (Algorithm 3, order of the levels in a phase)

Lemma 4.8 is false for HPTS as printed: Algorithm 3 runs the levels of a phase in increasing order, while the proof needs (and says) decreasing order, and with m = ℓ = 3 and 27 nodes a node's badness does not decrease over a phase (lemma_4_8_false_alg3, lemma_4_8_false_indexing).

Impact. With the levels of each phase in decreasing order (a one-line change to Algorithm 3) we prove Lemma 4.8 and Theorem 4.1 (lemma_4_8, theorem_4_1), and Theorem 4.1 only claims that some algorithm "HPTS" achieves the bound, so this variant proves it. For Algorithm 3 as printed, Theorem 4.1 is unverified (see Not formalized.).

Details

Algorithm 3 works at level λ = t mod ℓ and accepts new packets when λ = 0, so a phase runs the levels 0, 1, …, ℓ−1. The indexing in the statement of Lemma 4.8 (rounds t+1, …, t+ℓ) gives 1, …, ℓ−1, 0. The proof assumes the opposite: "levels are activated in decreasing order over the course of a phase". In the round of level λ, a bad packet can be traded for a bad packet at a lower level (Case 2 of Claim 2). If that lower level was already processed in this phase, the badness does not decrease.

Counterexample (m = ℓ = 3, 27 nodes, no injections): node 8 holds two packets to 15, node 9 one packet to 15, node 10 two packets to 20 and one packet to 15. Node 10 has badness 1: a bad level-2 packet with intermediate destination 18. The rounds at levels 0 and 1 do nothing. At level 2, node 8 sends a packet to its intermediate destination 9, where it becomes pre-bad at level 1. ActivatePreBad then pushes node 9's level-1 packet into node 10, which is busy forwarding at level 2. Node 10's badness stays 1, for both orders 0,1,2 and 1,2,0.

Here badness follows Definition 4.5 restricted to intermediate destinations w_k > i (see the slips below; the fix to Algorithm 5 line 5 listed there plays no role in this instance). Read literally, Definition 4.5 also counts node 8's bad level-2 packet at node 10, although that packet never crosses node 10; then node 10's badness drops from 2 to 1 in this instance. But the literal definition breaks the first step of the proof of Theorem 4.1, B(i) ≤ ξ(i) + 1: with m ≥ 6, three packets injected at once at node 0 for node 1 (σ = 2) give B(5) = 2 and ξ(5) = 0. The restricted definition is the one the proof needs.

This is a false lemma, and the proof of Theorem 4.1 rests on it.

Repair

With levels in decreasing order (ℓ−1, …, 0) we prove Lemma 4.8 and Theorem 4.1 (space ℓ n^{1/ℓ} + σ + 1 when ρℓ ≤ 1) (lemma_4_8, theorem_4_1).

🔧 Lemma 5.4 (used in Theorem 5.1)

Lemma 5.4 counts the β-stale packets at the end of round t by the load of [F(t+1)+1, F(t)] in round t+1, but such a packet may already be delivered then and occupy no buffer.

Impact. Theorem 5.1 is true: we prove load ≥ ((ℓ+1)ρ−1)(m−1)/(4(ℓ+1)) − 1 against every protocol (theorem_5_1, exists_load_ge), which with n ≈ (ℓ+1)m^ℓ is the paper's Ω(((ℓ+1)ρ−1)/ℓ · n^{1/ℓ}) up to constants.

Repair

We count them in round t, where they are still fresh and hence undelivered by Lemma 5.3 (card_becomeStale_le_sum_load). Theorem 5.1 then follows by a global telescoping count, which replaces Lemma 5.5. (theorem_5_1, exists_load_ge)

✏️ Slips

  • Definition 4.5 (badness): the sum must be restricted to intermediate destinations w_k > i, as in Definition 3.3. Without the restriction, the first step of the proof of Theorem 4.1 fails (see the Lemma 4.8 item). Formalized with this fix.

  • Definition B.4 / the PPTS-tree badness (Appendix B.2, Proposition 3.5) sums B_k(v) over all destinations, and with that definition the step "B(v) ≤ ξ(v) + 1" fails: inject 3 packets at a child of v with destination v; then ξ(v) = 0 but B(v) = 2. Restricting to destinations above v (as Definition 3.3 does on the path) repairs it; the statements are true (Tree.prop_3_5, Tree.prop_3_5_depth).

  • Algorithm 5 line 5 should read i < w_k: activating the empty pseudo-buffer at w_k itself would block the pre-bad run of a packet arriving at w_k one level lower. Formalized with this fix.

  • Claim 2, item 1 should say j > λ, not j < λ.

  • Propositions 3.2 and 3.5 omit ρ ≤ 1 (added).

  • The construction of Theorem 5.1 needs (ℓ+1)m^ℓ + 1 nodes, not (ℓ+1)m^ℓ ("destination n").

  • Section 4.1 says I_{j,r} has m^j nodes and I_j = {I_{j,r} : r ∈ ⟨m^j⟩}, and Algorithm 3 line 6 ranges over r ∈ ⟨m^{ℓ−λ}⟩; it should be m^{j+1} nodes and r ∈ ⟨m^{ℓ−j−1}⟩ (as in the definition of I_{j,r} and in Algorithm 5).

  • The phase boundaries in Section 4.2 (rounds (φ−1)ℓ … φℓ−1) and in Lemma 4.8 (t+1 … t+ℓ) disagree.

  • Algorithm 2 line 2 uses an undefined w_d.

  • The proof of Lemma 2.5 writes ℓ·s + 1 for ℓ(s−1) + 1.

  • The proof of Theorem 5.1 writes /2k for /2ℓ.

Typos

  • Definition 2.1 writes N_T(v) for an interval I.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Claim 2, item 3 · Lemma 4.8

🛠️ Claim 2, item 3 (badness never increases in a round)

The proof of Claim 2 is not a proof: item 3 is argued through events (a)–(c) and Cases 1–3, which do not account for each unit of badness.

Impact. Claim true; replaced by a new argument (Bad_low_le, Bad_lam_le, phase_decreasing).

Repair

Within a level-(λ−1) block, the nodes active at level λ form a suffix. A lower-level pseudo-buffer can gain a bad packet only at the first node of that suffix. So for every node i, the lower levels gain at most one unit of badness, and only when i is active at level λ, where it loses one unit (Lemma 3.4). (Bad_low_le, Bad_lam_le, phase_decreasing)

🔧 Lemma 4.8 (end of the proof)

The final step of the proof ("hence B^{s+}(i) = B^s(i), as desired") is garbled and does not prove a strict decrease in the phase.

Impact. Statement true for levels in decreasing order (lemma_4_8).

Repair

A short induction over the levels, in decreasing order, replaces it (phase_decreasing).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous rounds on the directed path 0 → ⋯ → n−1. Each edge carries one packet per round. A round is an injection step followed by a forwarding step, and buffer occupancy is measured between the two. Deterministic and centralized, with no failures.

  • Adversaries are count functions A t a b (round, source, destination), (ρ,σ)-bounded on every buffer and every interval of rounds (Cslib.AQT.IsBounded, staged in Podc/Computability/Distributed/AQT/Basic.lean), with real ρ ≥ 0 and σ.

  • Upper bounds. Configurations count the packets per node and destination. HPTS is formalized for n = m^ℓ (hptsStep): FormPaths in closed form (fpAct), then ActivatePreBad for levels λ−1, …, 0. The packet a pseudo-buffer sends is chosen arbitrarily (ValidSel), so every priority rule is covered. A Run uses one level per round according to a schedule, and new packets are accepted in batches at phase starts: the ℓ-reduction reduce A ℓ. Pending packets are not counted, as in the paper. PPTS and PTS are HPTS with ℓ = 1, m = n, accepting packets in their injection round.

  • Trees. A finite forest given by a parent map (Tree.DTree). Packets move towards ancestors, and destinations are ordered by a topological index. Algorithm 6 is formalized both as the loop and in closed form (Tree.act).

  • Lower bound. Arbitrary offline protocols: any trajectories that move each packet at most one hop per round towards its destination, with at most one packet per edge per round (LowerBound.Execution). The adversary lbAdv is a finite packet family. Injecting "ρm packets per phase" at once would not be (ρ,1)-bounded, so it spreads the injections at rate ρ, a routine reading of the paper's "during the phase".

Not formalized.

  • The d-destination form of the upper bound from the abstract.

  • General n ≠ m^ℓ (padding): the paper assumes n = m^ℓ "for simplicity", and Theorem 4.1 does not say so.

  • Theorem 4.1 for Algorithm 3 with the printed level order is open: nothing shows that the theorem itself fails, random simulations found no violation, and the bound has slack ℓ.

  • Lemma 5.5 is bypassed by a global telescoping count; its per-phase dichotomy was not checked.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound; the counterexample is checked by decide +kernel. Non-vacuity: every schedule and adversary has a run with a canonical packet choice (canonRun), executions of any protocol exist (LowerBound.Execution.idle), and the lower-bound adversary is (ρ,1)-bounded (lbAdv_isBounded).
