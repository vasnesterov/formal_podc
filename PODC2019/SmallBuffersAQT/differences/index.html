Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Small Buffers on a Path (AQT) — PODC 2019.

Paper: Miller, Patt-Shamir, Rosenbaum, With Great Speed Come Small Buffers: Space-Bandwidth Tradeoffs for Routing, PODC 2019; formalized from arXiv 1902.08069v1 (the only version; it contains the appendix with the proofs).

Model (Section 2)

  1. Rounds and intervals. Rounds are numbered from 0 and intervals are half-open (cross A n v s t counts rounds [s, t)); the excess ξ_t is the excess after the injections of rounds < t. Definition 2.1 writes N_T(v) for an interval I (typo). ρ and σ are arbitrary reals (the paper: σ ∈ ℕ).

  2. Routes. A packet with source a and destination b "contains" buffer v iff a ≤ v < b (it must be forwarded over the edge (v, v+1)); the paper writes v ∈ Path(i_P, w_P), which would also count the destination.

  3. Configurations are counts c i w (packets at i with destination w): packets of one pseudo-buffer are interchangeable for buffer sizes. Which packet a pseudo-buffer forwards is arbitrary in every round (sel, ValidSel), so the results hold for every priority rule (the paper suggests LIFO "for concreteness").

  4. ℓ-reduction (Definition 2.4). Batch φ = packets injected in rounds [ℓ(φ−1), ℓφ), accepted at round ℓφ (paper: rounds (k−1)ℓ+1 … kℓ treated as injected at kℓ; same up to the 0/1 indexing). The proof of Lemma 2.5 writes ℓ·s + 1 ≤ u ≤ ℓ·t; it should be ℓ(s−1)+1 ≤ u ≤ ℓt (typo, statement true).

  5. Non-accepted packets. As in the paper, packets injected during the current phase and not yet accepted are not counted in the buffer occupancy of Theorem 4.1 (they are "pending"). (At a node they number at most ℓρ + σ.)

HPTS (Section 4)

  1. n = m^ℓ is assumed, as in the paper ("for simplicity"); general n (padding) is not formalized.

  2. Pseudo-buffers L_{j,k}(i) are named by (level, intermediate destination) key i w instead of (j, k).

  3. ✏️ Badness (Definition 4.5). The sum over k ∈ ⟨m⟩ is restricted to intermediate destinations w_k > i (packets that still have to cross i), as in Definition 3.3 and as the prose says. Without the restriction, badness of packets that do not cross i would be counted, and the first inequality of the proof of Theorem 4.1, B(i) ≤ ξ(i) + 1, fails (m ≥ 6, σ = 2: three packets injected at once at node 0 for node 1 give B(5) = 2, ξ(5) = 0). The counterexample of item 11 is stated for the restricted badness; under the literal sum, node 10's badness in that instance drops from 2 to 1 (it also counts node 8's bad level-2 packet for intermediate destination 9). Checked by decide +kernel during review, not kept in the files.

  4. FormPaths (Algorithm 4) in closed form. Node v activates the level-λ pseudo-buffer whose intermediate destination is the largest among the bad level-λ pseudo-buffers at nodes u ≤ v with intermediate destination > v (fpAct). This is what the loop computes (the interval [i_k, min(i'−1, w_k−1)] contains v iff w_k is that largest one); the equivalence with the loop is proved (fpAct_eq_loop, FormPaths.lean; the loop is loopAct, run on the level-λ interval of the node with ws = [w_{m−1}, …, w_0] and pointer w_{m−1}). The same closed form is our PPTS (Algorithm 2, whose line 2 i ← w_d is undefined) and PTS (Algorithm 1).

  5. ✏️ ActivatePreBad (Algorithm 5). Line 5 w ← max{i ∈ I : i ≤ w_k and [a, i] inactive} is read with i < w_k: activating the (empty) pseudo-buffer at w_k itself is meaningless and would mark w_k as active, blocking the pre-bad run of a packet arriving there at a lower level. "∃ P pre-bad for a at level j" is read as: the packet forwarded into a by a−1 under the activation built so far has level j at a and its new pseudo-buffer at a is nonempty (Definition 4.6). The calls for j = λ−1, …, 0 are made in this order, as in Algorithm 3.

  6. 🔧 Schedule. Algorithm 3 sets λ ← t mod ℓ and accepts packets when λ = 0, so within a phase the levels are 0, 1, …, ℓ−1 (increasing); the indexing in the statement of Lemma 4.8 (phase = rounds t+1, …, t+ℓ) gives 1, …, ℓ−1, 0. The proof of Lemma 4.8 needs decreasing levels ("levels are activated in decreasing order over the course of a phase"). Lemma 4.8 is false for both orders (lemma_4_8_false_alg3, lemma_4_8_false_indexing, m = ℓ = 3; badness as in item 8; the instance does not depend on the reading of Algorithm 5 line 5 in item 10, since node 10 is active anyway). We formalize HPTS with the order ℓ−1, …, 0 (fixedSched) and prove Lemma 4.8 (lemma_4_8) and Theorem 4.1 (theorem_4_1) for it. Whether Theorem 4.1 holds for the literal schedule is open: its proof does not apply. Evidence that it may still hold: random simulations with m = 3, ℓ ∈ {2, 3} found no violation; a node has at most ℓ(m−1) pseudo-buffers in use, so the bound has slack ℓ; and in the abstract per-node game defined by our per-round facts (Bad_high_eq, Bad_lam_le, Bad_low_le, which hold for any level order), a brute-force search (ℓ ≤ 4, σ ≤ 2) finds badness at most ξ + ℓ for the increasing order, against ξ + 1 for the decreasing one. ξ + ℓ would still give ℓ(m−1) + σ + ℓ ≤ ℓm + σ + 1. Not proved.

  7. Lemma 4.7 (feasibility) holds by construction: an activation is a function node → Option pseudo-buffer, and ActivatePreBad only activates inactive nodes.

  8. 🛠️ Claim 2. Item 1 says "for all j < λ, B_j^{s+} = B_j^s"; it must be j > λ (as its proof says). The proof of item 3 (cases (a)–(c), Cases 1–3) is replaced by a different argument (RoundBad.lean, RoundMain.lean): for a node i and its level-(λ−1) block J, the nodes of J active at level λ form a suffix of J; a lower-level pseudo-buffer can gain a bad packet only at the first node of that suffix (fpAct_of_bad_arrival, BadArr_unique), so the lower levels gain at most one unit of badness of i, and only if i is active at level λ, where it loses one unit (Bad_low_le, Bad_lam_le).

  9. 🔧 Proof of Lemma 4.8. The last sentences ("Consider the largest s … hence B^{s+}(i) = B^s(i), as desired") do not prove the claim; replaced by an induction over the levels in decreasing order (phase_decreasing).

  10. Theorem 4.1 is proved in a slightly sharper form: occupancy ≤ (#pseudo-buffers in use at the node) + σ + 1 (Run.occ_le_keys), and #pseudo-buffers ≤ ℓm = ℓ n^{1/ℓ} (card_keysAt_le). The hypothesis 0 ≤ ρ is added (ρ ∈ ℝ⁺ in the paper).

  11. ⚠️ Abstract / introduction. The upper bound O(ℓ d^{1/ℓ} + σ) in terms of the number d of destinations (and the consequence "ρ ≤ 1/log d ⇒ O(log d) space") is not proved in the paper: Theorem 4.1 is in terms of n, not d. Not formalized. (Our sharper form gives min(ℓ n^{1/ℓ}, d) + σ + 1.) 16a. Indexing typos in Section 4. Section 4.1 says I_{j,r} "consists of all m^j nodes" and I_j = {I_{j,r} : r ∈ ⟨m^j⟩}; Algorithm 3 line 6 ranges over r ∈ ⟨m^{ℓ−λ}⟩. Correct: m^{j+1} nodes, r ∈ ⟨m^{ℓ−j−1}⟩ (as in the definition of I_{j,r} and Algorithm 5). Our level-j interval of v starts at lvl m j v = ⌊v / m^{j+1}⌋ m^{j+1}.

PTS / PPTS (Section 3)

  1. Propositions 3.1, 3.2 are derived from Theorem 4.1's machinery with ℓ = 1, m = n (then every packet has level 0 and intermediate destination = destination, ActivatePreBad never runs, FormPaths = PPTS); packets are accepted in their injection round (b = A). Lemma 3.4 and Claim 1 are Bad_lam_le / fpAct_of_Bad_lam_pos (in telescoping form). Lemma B.1 (feasibility) holds by construction.

  2. Trees (Section 3.3, Appendix B.2), Tree.lean. Model: a finite forest (DTree: parent map + depth), packets travel towards ancestors and are delivered at the destination; deterministic execution (exec), buffer bound after injection. Destinations are ordered by an index increasing along ≺ (a topological sort, as Algorithm 6 assumes); the loop of Algorithm 6 (alg6) is proved equal to the closed form act (alg6_eq_act). The paper's Path(u, w_k) includes w_k; this is harmless: the literal version forwards exactly the same packets (alg6Incl_fwd_iff). Badness: Definition B.4 / the tree PPTS badness sum over all destinations, which makes "B(v) ≤ ξ(v) + 1" false (3 packets injected at a child of v with destination v: ξ(v) = 0, B(v) = 2; no Lean counterexample); we sum only over destinations w ≻ v. Proposition 3.5 omits ρ ≤ 1 (added). We prove occupancy ≤ #{w ∈ W : v ≺ w} + σ + 1 (prop_3_5) and ≤ 1 + d′ + σ for d′ bounding the destination chains (prop_3_5_depth); Proposition B.3 for any single destination (not only the root), prop_B_3. The proofs of Lemma B.5 and Propositions 3.5/B.3 ("analogous") are written out (bad_step_le, bad_exec_le).

Lower bound (Section 5)

  1. Path length. The paper takes n = (ℓ+1)m^ℓ nodes but type-1 packets go to "destination n"; we use (ℓ+1)m^ℓ + 1 nodes (0 … (ℓ+1)m^ℓ).

  2. Injection pattern. "Inject ρm packets into buffer v during the phase" is made precise as: in every round t < (m−1)m^ℓ with ⌊ρ(t+1)⌋ ≠ ⌊ρt⌋, one packet of each type is injected. Injecting ρm packets at once would not be (ρ, 1)-bounded. Requires ρ ≤ 1 (stated by us; the paper only says ρ > 1/(ℓ+1)); the adversary stops at round (m−1)m^ℓ.

  3. Protocols. Arbitrary offline (clairvoyant, centralized) protocols: any packet trajectories moving at most one hop per round with at most one packet per edge per round (Execution). Delivered packets occupy no buffer.

  4. 🔧 Lemma 5.4 gap. The paper bounds the number of β-stale packets by the load of [F(t+1)+1, F(t)] in round t+1, but such a packet may be delivered in round t+1 and then occupies no buffer. We count them in round t in [F(t+1), F(t)] (they are fresh, hence undelivered by Lemma 5.3).

  5. Lemma 5.5 replaced. Instead of the per-phase dichotomy, a global telescoping count: with L the maximal load, at most 1 + 2L(F(t) − F(t+1)) packets become stale at the end of round t (card_injected_le_real). The paper's computation F(t) − F(t+1) = ∑_{j=0}^{k} m^j is checked (lemma_5_4_drop) but not needed.

  6. Constants. We prove: some buffer has load ≥ ((ℓ+1)ρ − 1)(m−1)/(4(ℓ+1)) − 1 (theorem_5_1), from the sharper ((ℓ+1)ρ−1)(m−1)m^ℓ − (ℓ+1) ≤ 2(ℓ+1)m^ℓ · load (exists_load_ge). With n ≈ (ℓ+1)m^ℓ this is the paper's Ω(((ℓ+1)ρ−1)/ℓ · n^{1/ℓ}) up to constant factors (n^{1/ℓ} ≤ 3m for ℓ ≥ 1). The paper's proof writes /2k for /2ℓ once (typo).

  7. ℓ ≥ 1 instead of ℓ ≥ 2; m ≥ 2.

  8. Claim 3 / Lemma 5.3 are stated with phase numbers P = t/m; the "highest differing digit" is the least k with P'/m^k = P/m^k.
