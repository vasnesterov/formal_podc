The Consensus Number of a Cryptocurrency — PODC 2019

Rachid Guerraoui, Petr Kuznetsov, Matteo Monti, Matej Pavlovič, Dragos-Adrian Seredinschi. The Consensus Number of a Cryptocurrency. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331589. Formalized version: arXiv 1906.05574 v1 (extended version, the only arXiv version; the journal version, Distributed Computing 2022, was not checked); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🛠️ Hard fix. Theorem 2 (consensus number exactly k, k ≥ 1), Corollary 1 and Lemma 2 hold, but the wait-freedom proof of Lemma 2 is false as stated and we replaced it with a new potential argument. Theorem 1 needs a local change to Figure 1, the linearizability proof of Lemma 2 needs local repairs, and Figure 4 needs a one-term fix, which we prove only on the counterexample. The safety of the message-passing algorithm (Theorem 3) was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Theorem 1 (Figure 1 is not linearizable as written)

Figure 1 keeps successful transfers in a set ops_p, so repeating an identical transfer is never debited: an owner with balance 2 calls transfer(a, b, 1) three times solo and all three return true (Fig1.Bug.literal_not_linearizable).

Impact. That is double-spending, the very thing the paper sets out to prevent, but the intent is evident: Figure 3 tags transfers with unique sequence numbers.

Details

Each process adds (a, b, x) to the set ops_p. Repeating an identical transfer therefore does not change ops_p, so the second withdrawal is never debited.

Repair

Keeping ops_p as a sequence is a two-line change, after which the paper's proof goes through with its linearization points (Fig1.fig1_linearizable, Fig1.fig1_waitFree).

🔧 Lemma 2, proof ("the linearization of a prefix of E is a prefix of L")

The side claim that prefixes linearize to prefixes is false, because failed transfers are linearized retroactively at the proposer's snapshot.

Impact. The claim is not needed for linearizability, and the paper's linearization points stay.

Repair

We drop the claim and insert such operations retroactively (Fig3.fig3_linearizable).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 3 and Theorem 3, liveness (Figure 4, line 25). Figure 4 as written blocks a correct process that spends money it has just received: process 0 (balance 5) pays 5 to process 1 (balance 0), and process 1's transfer of the 5 back never returns, even with two correct processes and a perfect broadcast (Fig4.lemma_3_false). A process may spend balance(a, hist[p] ∪ deps), i.e. including freshly received incoming transfers deps, and it ships them as the dependencies h. Validation (line 25), however, checks balance(c, hist[q]) without h, and incoming transfers enter hist[q] only after a later transfer of q has been validated. So process 1's message is invalid at every process forever. As written this falsifies Theorem 3, since Definition 1 requires every operation of a correct process to return; only the safety part of Theorem 3 is unaffected. The intended check is balance(c, hist[q] ∪ h) ≥ y: line 2 spends that amount, and Lemma 3's proof assumes the receiver's check passes because the sender checked its balance. With it the transfer completes (Fig4.fixed_completes). Lean shows this on the counterexample only, not Theorem 3 for the fixed algorithm.

  • Theorem 2 for k = 0. With "k-shared" defined as max_a |μ(a)| = k, an object whose accounts have no owner is 0-shared, but its consensus number is 1, not 0, since one process always solves consensus (atSolvable_one). For k ≥ 1 Theorem 2 holds (theorem_2).

  • The transition relation Δ is not total, contrary to the paper's assumption: a self-transfer transfer(a, a, x) with x > 0 has no successor state. We use "debit, then credit".

  • Figure 4's balance omits initial balances, so taken literally no transfer of a positive amount ever succeeds. The prose of Section 3 includes them.

  • Definition 1 (2) cannot hold for finite executions, and it also covers reads and failed transfers of other processes. The comment after the definition gives the intended reading.

Typos

  • Figure 3's proposal((a, b, q, x), ·) has the wrong arity.

  • The successful-transfer case of Lemma 2's proof writes L_k where L_s is meant.

Figure 3 implements asset transfer from a snapshot, registers and k-consensus objects. The algorithm is correct, and we proved it linearizable and wait-free (Fig3.fig3_linearizable, Fig3.fig3_waitFree).

🛠️ Lemma 2, wait-freedom

The argument "some instance will be only accessed with o" is false as stated: processes with stale collected sets keep proposing other transfers.

Impact. The paper's argument does not survive; the statement does.

Repair

We replace the wait-freedom argument with a potential argument. (Fig3.fig3_waitFree)

🔧 Lemma 2, incomplete transfers

The linearizability proof completes incomplete transfers "with response true" even when they were decided as failures.

Impact. One-line change: complete them with the decided response, or linearize at the proposer's snapshot. (Fig3.fig3_linearizable)

🔧 Lemma 2, non-owner transfers (lines 1–2)

The proof gives no linearization point to non-owner transfers.

Impact. One extra case: we linearize them at their invocation. (Fig3.fig3_linearizable)

🔧 Lemma 2, successful-transfer case

The proof leaves the key "no outgoing transfer in between" step unjustified.

Impact. A few lines from the round ordering the paper already mentions. (Fig3.fig3_linearizable)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Consensus numbers (System.lean) use CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model Cslib.SharedMemory.Recoverable, without crashes. There are n deterministic processes with binary inputs and wait-free consensus (WFConsensus; crash-stop = stop taking steps). The objects are any read/write objects (ObjSpec.RWLike: every operation is read-only or a blind write) plus any number of asset-transfer objects, each with its own owner map, with arbitrary initial values. ATSolvable n k means n-process consensus is solvable when every account has at most k owners.

  • Linearizability and wait-freedom (Theorem 1, Lemma 2) use CSLib's Impl model: deterministic processes, atomic base objects, IsLinearization (Herlihy–Wing, amended), WaitFree. Figure 1's base object is an atomic snapshot. Its read/write implementation (Afek et al.) is cited by the paper and not formalized.

  • CSLib object specifications do not see the caller, so transfer carries the invoking process as an argument. Algorithms must use their own identity (allowed / Honest). All own transfers and all reads are allowed, and Lemma 1's algorithm is proved honest.

  • Figure 4 is modelled only as far as the counterexample needs: two correct processes and per-sender FIFO exactly-once delivery (integrity and source order). The claim covers all extensions.

theorem_2 states ATSolvable k k ∧ ∀ n > k, ¬ ATSolvable n k. The upper bound is proved directly rather than through Lemma 2. At a critical configuration all processes are poised on one object. A read, a failing transfer or a blind write cannot be critical. Successful transfers from different accounts commute. So every two processes on opposite teams withdraw from one common account. Every process is on some team, so all n processes own that account, and n ≤ k (at_pair, no_consensus; generic lemmas Team.false_of_absorb, Team.false_of_commute, Crit.not_forall_poised_of_rwLike in Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Recoverable.lean). This needs neither the cited k-consensus hierarchy result nor composition of implementations, and it allows any number of objects with different owner maps.

Not formalized. The read/write implementation of atomic snapshots, and composition of implementations (so Lemma 2 is stated from an atomic snapshot). Theorem 3 (safety of Figure 4 under Byzantine faults with secure broadcast), also for the fixed check. Section 6 (BFT-based k-shared transfers in message passing; informal).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: Lemma 1's algorithm satisfies all hypotheses of the consensus model (atSolvable_self). Fig1.Example instantiates Theorem 1 for a concrete owner map, and Fig1.allowed_iff shows the access discipline allows all reads and own transfers. The valency machinery comes from CSLib (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Recoverable.lean).
