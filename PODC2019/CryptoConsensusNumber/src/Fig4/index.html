import Mathlib /-! # Figure 4 (message passing): a correct process's transfer can block forever Section 5.2, Figure 4 of the paper: the consensus-less asset-transfer algorithm on top of secure broadcast. Lemma 3 claims that every operation of a correct process eventually completes. We show that the pseudocode **as written** violates this, already with two correct processes, no Byzantine process and a perfectly behaved broadcast. The problem: a process `p` decides whether it may spend using `balance(a, hist[p] ∪ deps)` (line 2), i.e. including the incoming transfers `deps` it has received since its last outgoing transfer, and ships `deps` with the transfer (`h`, line 4). But the validation predicate (line 25) checks `balance(c, hist[q]) ≥ y` **without** `h`, and incoming transfers are only ever added to `hist[q]` (line 15) when a later transfer of `q` itself is validated. So a process that spends money it has just received can never get its transfer validated — at any process, including itself — and the operation never returns. Intended fix: `balance(c, hist[q] ∪ h) ≥ y`. ## Model (only what is needed for the counterexample) * processes `Fin 2`, all correct; account of process `q` is `q` (`μ(q) = {q}`); initial balances `q₀` (Figure 4's `balance` omits initial balances; we add `q₀`, otherwise no transfer of a positive amount could ever succeed); * secure broadcast: each process's broadcasts form a list `sent q`; process `p` delivers the messages of `q` one by one in that order (`dlv p q` = number delivered), each exactly once — integrity and source order hold by construction; agreement/validity are liveness properties and are irrelevant for a claim about *all* extensions; * events: `inv p b x` (`transfer(p, b, x)` by an idle `p`, lines 1–5), `dlv p q` (`p` delivers the next message of `q`, lines 8–12), `val p q m` (`p` validates `(q, m) ∈ toValidate`, lines 13–20). Reads do not change the state and are omitted.
