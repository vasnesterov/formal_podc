import Papers.PODC2019.CryptoConsensusNumber.Fig1Defs /-! # Figure 1 as written (`ops_p` a set) is not linearizable In Figure 1 of Guerraoui et al. (PODC 2019) every process keeps the *set* `ops_p` of its successful transfers and publishes it in the snapshot (`ops_p := ops_p ∪ {(a, b, x)}`), and `balance(a, S)` sums over the transfers in the snapshot. Two successful transfers with the same arguments `(a, b, x)` by the same process are then recorded only once, so the second one is not debited. Counterexample (`literalImpl`): one process (the owner of account `0`), accounts `0` and `1`, initial balances `2` and `0`. The process performs `transfer(0, 1, 1)` three times in a solo run. After the first one the snapshot holds `{(0, 1, 1)}` and `balance(0) = 1`, which stays so forever: all three transfers return `true`. Three successful withdrawals of `1` from a balance of `2` are not legal in any order, so the execution has no linearization at all. The fix (`impl` in `Fig1Defs.lean`, proved correct in `Fig1.lean`) records transfers in a sequence (equivalently: tag each transfer with a sequence number). -/ namespace CryptoCons.Fig1.Bug open
