On Termination of a Flooding Process — PODC 2019

Walter Hussak, Amitabh Trehan. On Termination of a Flooding Process. PODC 2019 (brief announcement). DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331586. Formalized version: arXiv 1907.07078 v1, the full version and the only arXiv version. Numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All results hold for finite connected graphs, as the abstract assumes: termination of synchronous amnesiac flooding (Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.4), termination time exactly e = ecc(source) iff the graph is bipartite (Theorem 3.5), e < T ≤ e + d + 1 otherwise (Theorem 3.7), and the Section 4 non-termination example. Beyond slips, the only repairs are a short gap in the proof of Theorem 3.5, closed by a parity argument or the paper's own Lemma 3.6, and dropping one node from Figure 7, which as drawn does not show sharpness.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Figure 7 (sharpness example)

The caption says flooding from b takes 7 = 2 + 4 + 1 = e + d + 1 rounds, but the drawn graph has an extra pendant node i (d – h – i), so e = 3, d = 5, and termination takes 7 < 9 rounds (Examples.figure_7_as_drawn).

Impact. Without i the caption's numbers are right (Examples.figure_7_without_i). So are the Petersen graph (from every node: 5 = e + d + 1) and the triangle; the upper bound of Theorem 3.7 is sharp (Examples.petersen_sharp, Examples.triangle_sharp).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 are stated for "a graph G", but both fail for disconnected graphs (Examples.lemma_3_3_needs_connected, Examples.lemma_3_4_needs_connected). The abstract assumes connectivity, and all results hold for finite connected graphs.

    • Lemma 3.3 fails on two isolated nodes: there is no ec node, yet node 1 lies in no round-set.

    • Lemma 3.4 fails when the source is isolated and there is one more edge 1 – 2: the graph is bipartite, yet 1 is an ec node. This counterexample relies on ∞ = ∞ counting as "equidistant", which is the paper's literal "usual distance" (Mathlib edist). If only finite distances count, Lemma 3.4 still fails: take an isolated source plus a triangle. The graph is not bipartite, but it has no ec nodes.

    • Theorems 3.5 and 3.7 need connectivity too, for e and d to be finite and for the bipartite/ec link.

  • In the proof of Lemma 3.3, "h ∈ D_i for some i ≥ 1" overlooks h = g₀, which would give the impossible g² ∈ R_1.

Typos

  • In the proof of Lemma 3.2(i), "Case j = 1" should be the inductive case.

  • In Lemma 3.2(iii), "Definition 2.2(i)" should be Definition 3.1(ii).

  • "Lemma 3." / "Lemma ." should be Lemma 3.2(iii).

  • §3 refers to Figures 3(a)/(b) for the cycles; it means Figure 6.

  • §1.2 refers to Figure 8 for the triangle; it means Figure 3.

  • "hypergraph" should be "hypercube".

  • Stray text "in D rounds on a bipartite graph" in §1.1.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 3.5

🔧 Theorem 3.5 (termination time e iff bipartite)

The last step "no node appears in 2 round-sets iff R_e is the last non-empty round-set (by Lemma 3.2)" does not follow from Lemma 3.2 in the direction ⇐: a node may receive twice, both times by round e.

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a parity argument (theorem_3_5).

Details

One must show that second appearances reach the farthest node, at a round after e. That needs Lemma 3.6 (which comes later) or a parity argument: in a connected non-bipartite graph, a node at distance e also has a walk of the other parity, so it receives again after round e.

Repair

The gap can be filled with the paper's own Lemma 3.6 (with Lemma 3.2(iii) and connectivity, it propagates a second appearance to the farthest node). We use the parity argument. (theorem_3_5)

Model.

  • G : SimpleGraph V with source s, in synchronous rounds 1, 2, …. In round 1, s sends to all its neighbours. In round t + 1, every node that received in round t sends to all neighbours that did not send to it in round t (sends, afStep; Algorithm 1.1). The round-sets are R_0 = {s} and R_t = the receivers in round t (Definition 2.1).

  • "Terminated after round T" means no message is sent in any round > T (TerminatesAfter). The termination time is the last active round (IsTerminationTime, which also needs R_T ≠ ∅).

  • Distances, eccentricity, diameter and bipartiteness come from Mathlib (edist/dist, eccent, diam, IsBipartite = Colorable 2). Section 2 holds for arbitrary graphs (Theorem 2.2) or finite ones (Corollary 2.4). Section 3 assumes finite and connected.

  • Single source only, as in the paper.

  • Asynchronous model (§1.1.1, §4): a delay function δ r u v, so the message sent from u to v in round r arrives in round r + δ r u v. δ = 0 gives the synchronous model (asends_zero_delay). The process is deterministic and sends at most one message per directed edge per round, so an adaptive adversary's choices in a run form such a function.

  • Key lemma (sends_succ_iff, mem_roundSet_iff): v ∈ R_t iff some walk from s to v has length t and no shorter walk has the same parity. So amnesiac flooding is BFS in the bipartite double cover. All of Section 3 follows from it. Theorem 2.2 is also proved separately by the paper's own argument (PaperProof.lean).

The PODC brief announcement. Two sentences of its abstract go beyond the formalized arXiv text, which says "an adaptive adversary can force" and "finite graph".

  • "An adversary can always ensure non-termination": on every finite forest, e.g. a single edge, every adversary's run terminates, because each message moves away from the source (asyncAF_terminates_of_isAcyclic, asyncAF_terminates_single_edge). Section 4 shows non-termination for one graph, the triangle (Figure 8), and that example holds. So non-termination is possible on some graphs, not on all.

  • "AF terminates on any arbitrary graph" is literally false for infinite graphs: on an infinite ray, flooding from the endpoint never stops.

Not formalized. The informal remarks: asymptotic time optimality for broadcast, and the applications and speculation in §1 and §5.

Trust. No sorry. All listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The examples are checked by decide (no native_decide) through simulate, a finite-set implementation proved equal to the model (mem_simulate). The same simulation computes distances (isFirstIn_iff), so the examples also certify eccent and diam. The hypotheses are satisfiable: the examples cover bipartite and non-bipartite connected graphs. The asynchronous model is checked against the synchronous one (asends_zero_delay).

An exhaustive check on all connected graphs with at most 6 nodes (not part of the repository) agrees with every claim of Sections 2–3.
