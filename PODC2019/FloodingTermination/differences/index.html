Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of On Termination of a Flooding Process — PODC 2019.

Paper: Hussak–Trehan, On Termination of a Flooding Process, arXiv 1907.07078v1 (full version of the PODC 2019 brief announcement).

Statements

  1. Connectivity (under-specified statement; false only for disconnected graphs). Section 3 says "Let G be a graph" but needs G connected. The abstract does assume connectivity.

    • Lemma 3.3 fails without connectivity: two isolated nodes have no ec node, but node 1 lies in no round-set (Examples.lemma_3_3_needs_connected).

    • Lemma 3.4 fails without connectivity: with the source isolated and an edge 1 – 2, the graph is bipartite and 1 is an ec node (Examples.lemma_3_4_needs_connected). This counterexample relies on edist treating ∞ = ∞ as equidistant. If ec nodes are read with finite distances only, Lemma 3.4 still fails in the other direction: an isolated source plus a triangle is non-bipartite and has no ec nodes (not formalized; it is immediate).

    • We assume G.Connected in Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 and Theorems 3.5 and 3.7, plus Finite V / Fintype V where eccentricity or termination is involved. Lemmas 3.2 and 3.6 and Theorem 2.2 hold for every graph, finite or not, and are stated that way.

  2. Theorem 3.5. "Flooding will have terminated after round e" is formalized as TerminatesAfter G s e (no message in any round > e). We also prove the exact form IsTerminationTime G s e ↔ G.IsBipartite (theorem_3_5'), using R_e ≠ ∅.

  3. Theorem 3.7. "Flooding terminates after j rounds" is formalized as IsTerminationTime G s j (last active round j). e enters as a hypothesis G.eccent s = e; d is Mathlib's G.diam.

  4. Corollary 2.4 ("fewer than 2|G| + 1 rounds"). We prove the slightly stronger R_t = ∅ for all t ≥ 2|G|, which the paper also states in the text. Hence the termination time is < 2|G| (exists_isTerminationTime_lt).

  5. Lemma 3.2(iii) is stated as IsSecondIn G s g (j + 1), i.e. g² ∈ R_{j+1}. The hypothesis j ≥ 1 is kept but not needed.

  6. Lemma 3.3 "each node is contained in exactly one round-set" is formalized as ∀ v, ∃! j, v ∈ R_j.

  7. Abstract (PODC version). "An adversary can always ensure non-termination" is an over-claim if "always" means "on every graph". On every finite forest every adversary's run terminates (asyncAF_terminates_of_isAcyclic, asyncAF_terminates_single_edge). The arXiv text only claims (and proves) existence: an example suffices, the triangle. That is what we formalize (asyncAF_triangle_nonterminating). Cosmetic: the PODC abstract's "AF terminates on any arbitrary graph" is literally false for infinite graphs (an infinite ray, flooding from its endpoint, never terminates). The full version says "finite graph", and we assume Finite V / Fintype V.

  8. Figure 7 (wrong example). As drawn (with the node i), e = 3, d = 5, and flooding takes 7 rounds, so the figure does not attain e + d + 1 = 9, contrary to the caption (Examples.figure_7_as_drawn). Without i, the caption's e = 2, d = 4, T = 7 is correct (Examples.figure_7_without_i). Sharpness of Theorem 3.7 itself is true: the Petersen graph (petersen_sharp) and the triangle (triangle_sharp) attain it.

Proofs

  1. Theorem 3.5, proof gap (statement true). The step "no node in 2 round-sets iff R_e is the last non-empty round-set (by Lemma 3.2)" is unjustified in the direction ⇐. If R_e is the last active round, some node might still receive twice, with both times ≤ e; Lemma 3.2 does not exclude this. A correct argument: in a connected non-bipartite graph, a node at distance e has a walk of the other parity and therefore receives again in a round > e. We formalize this argument (theorem_3_5, via exists_mem_roundSet_of_not_bipartite). The gap can also be filled from the paper's own Lemma 3.6. By Lemma 3.2(iii) an ec node has a second appearance. By Lemma 3.6 and connectivity, every node then has one, including a node at distance e, whose second appearance is after round e.

  2. Theorem 2.2. We prove it in two ways: from the parity-distance characterization (theorem_2_2), and by the paper's own minimal-counterexample argument (PaperProof.no_even_repeat). The paper's double minimality (duration, then start point) becomes a nested strong induction. The paper's proof is correct as written.

  3. Lemmas 3.2, 3.3, 3.4, 3.6 and Theorem 3.7. Our proofs go through the characterization v ∈ R_t ↔ t is a parity distance (mem_roundSet_iff), not through the paper's case analyses. We checked those case analyses by hand and found them sound, apart from the small slip in Lemma 3.3 ("h ∈ D_i for some i ≥ 1" ignores h = g₀, which gives the impossible g² ∈ R_1). For Theorem 3.7's upper bound we bound parity distances directly through an ec edge. This is the content of the paper's repeated use of Lemma 3.6, which we also prove separately (lemma_3_6).

Modelling choices

  1. Synchronous model. Messages are a relation sends t u v ("u sends to v in round t"), defined by recursion on rounds following Algorithm 1.1. There is no message in round 0; R_0 = {s}. Multiple copies on the same directed edge in one round cannot arise in the synchronous model.

  2. Distances. We use Mathlib's edist (ℕ∞, ⊤ when unreachable) for D_j and ec nodes. This matches the paper's "usual distance function". In connected graphs it agrees with dist.

  3. Asynchronous model (§1.1.1, §4). The adversary is a delay function δ : ℕ → V → V → ℕ: the message sent by u to v in round r is delivered in round r + δ r u v. A node receiving in round r from the set I sends in round r + 1 to N(v) \ I, exactly as in Algorithm 1.1. Messages on the same directed edge that are delivered in the same round are merged (Prop semantics); the paper does not specify this case. An adaptive adversary yields such a δ along its (deterministic) run, so "for all δ" covers adaptive adversaries and "there is a δ" is the paper's existence claim. Figure 8's adversary is triangleDelay: from round 3 on, c holds every message it sends in an odd round for one extra round. The resulting run has period 4.

  4. Not formalized. The informal claims of asymptotic time optimality for broadcast, the discussion and speculation in §1 and §5, and multi-source flooding, which the paper leaves open.

  5. Typos (not reflected in Lean).

    • In Lemma 3.2(i)'s proof, the "Case j = 1" label should mark the inductive case.

    • "Definition 2.2(i)" should be Definition 3.1(ii).

    • "Lemma 3." / "Lemma ." should be Lemma 3.2(iii).

    • Figures 3(a)/(b) in §3 should be Figure 6; Figure 8 in §1.2 should be Figure 3.

    • "hypergraph" should be "hypercube".

    • Stray "in D rounds on a bipartite graph" in §1.1.
