Paxos vs. Raft, and porting optimizations — PODC 2019

Zhaoguo Wang, Changgeng Zhao, Shuai Mu, Haibo Chen, Jinyang Li. On the Parallels between Paxos and Raft, and how to Port Optimizations. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331595. Formalized version: the extended version with Appendices A–C (TLA+ specifications and the refinement proof).

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The first half of the paper holds for a repaired Raft*: a Raft variant whose leader adopts the voters' extra entries refines repaired MultiPaxos under the Appendix C mapping, so it is safe, though this takes a new inductive invariant. The porting guarantee, the other half, is proved only for single-step refinements (the general theorem of Section 4), which excludes the multi-step Raft* refinement, and the ported protocols Raft*-PQL and Raft*-Mencius were not checked. The printed protocols are unsafe, and short local changes repair them.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Section 4.3 applied to Raft* (porting Paxos optimizations to Raft* "with guaranteed correctness")

The correctness argument for ported optimizations assumes single-step refinement (b_j ⇒ a_i), but the Raft* → Paxos refinement is multi-step (one AppendEntries is several Phase2a/Phase2b steps).

Impact. The abstract's claim that optimizations port from Paxos to Raft "with guaranteed correctness" is unestablished for Raft*, whose refinement of Paxos the paper itself proves only multi-step (Appendix C 2.4/2.5); Raft*-PQL and Raft*-Mencius are neither proved nor refuted (not formalized). The general theorem of Section 4.3 is true (port_correct). For multi-step refinements, evaluating the added clauses at the end points is unsound in general (naive_multistep_port_unsound).

🔧 TLA+ module MultiPaxos, theorem Invariance (Appendix B.1)

With 3 acceptors and majority quorums the spec reaches a state where one ballot has two proposals and both TRUE and FALSE are chosen at index 0, violating OneValuePerBallot, Inv and agreement (tla_invariance_false).

Impact. The paper's formal MultiPaxos is unsafe, and its stated invariance theorem is false. The pseudocode MultiPaxos (Figure 1) has the same flaw. A repaired MultiPaxos is safe (safety).

Details

Propose(a, i, v) records nothing in logs[a]. So a leader may propose TRUE and then FALSE for the same index in the same ballot. Then {a0, a2} accepts one value and {a1, a2} the other. The grey comment "⇒ v is safe at i, b" does not help: both values are safe at ballot 1. Figure 1's Phase2a also records nothing.

Repair

In Paxos.lean, a proposal per instance and ballot is recorded. Safety is proved in chosenAt_agree and safety.

🔧 Appendix B.1, Phase1b and BecomeLeader (two leaders of one ballot)

1b messages are not addressed to the proposer, so two acceptors can become leaders of the same ballot from the same 1b messages (tla_two_leaders_same_ballot).

Impact. This breaks the spec independently of the previous item; the repaired MultiPaxos is safe (safety).

Repair

Phase1Succeed needs the proposer's own report.

🔧 Figures 1 and 2 (leadership guards)

The pseudocode lacks leadership guards: Figure 1's Phase1a does not reset phase1Succeeded, and Phase1Succeed and BecomeLeader can re-run, which in Figure 2 can shrink lastIndex.

Impact. The repaired protocols are safe (safety, raftStar_commitSafe_raftVote).

Repair

In Paxos.lean, Phase1a resets leadership and Phase1Succeed needs ¬leader; BecomeLeader in RaftStar.lean is guarded in the same way.

🔧 Figure 2, ReceiveVote (lines 9–11, the vote rule)

Because a voter compares its entry at the candidate's last index (not its last entry) with the candidate's last term, it can vote for a candidate behind it, and a committed entry is overwritten (fig2_not_commitSafe; key step fig2_voter_reports_higher_term).

Impact. Raft* as printed is unsafe and refines no safe Paxos. Step 4.2 of Appendix C is false for it. The fix is local: Section 3 says Raft* is "identical to Raft" except for two differences unrelated to voting, and the TLA+ spec B.2 and Raft compare the voter's last entry, and with that rule the refinement holds (raftStar_commitSafe_fig2Fixed).

Details

The run uses five servers; every AppendEntries carries a value:

  1. A writes x@1 only to itself.

  2. C writes y@2 only to itself.

  3. A is re-elected in term 3, appends z@3, replicates [x@1, z@3] to B, D, E and commits x at index 0.

  4. C (log [y@2]) runs in term 4. B, D, E vote for it: their entry at index 0 has term 1 < 2, although their last term is 3.

  5. C keeps y at index 0 and commits it.

🔧 Figure 2, AppendEntries with no new value (heartbeats)

Figure 2 does not exclude vals = [], and with empty appends Raft*'s commit rule loses committed entries in Raft's "Figure 8" scenario, even with Raft's own vote rule (heartbeat_not_commitSafe).

Impact. Section 3 says re-stamping ballots makes Raft's §5.4.2 rule unnecessary. That holds only if every append carries an entry of the leader's term. The TLA+ spec B.2 always appends one value, and under that assumption the refinement holds (refinement_repaired).

✏️ Slips

  • Figure 3 maps instance.bal to the per-entry bal field, which gives no refinement even for repaired Raft* (scenario in differencies.md, in prose only). The intended mapping is in Section 3 and Appendix C, and the refinement is proved for it (refinement).

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Appendix C, steps 2.3 and 4.2 · Appendix C, step 2.5

🛠️ Appendix C, steps 2.3 and 4.2 (BecomeLeader ⇒ Phase1Succeed)

Step 4.2 ("the leader's entries are already the safe entries") has no argument.

Impact. For the repaired Raft* the step holds (sim_becomeLeader).

Details

Step 4.2 needs a real inductive invariant of Raft* (Raft.Inv, 18 clauses):

  • log matching;

  • one candidate with a quorum of votes per term;

  • a candidate's log is its own vote;

  • voters' last terms are at most the candidate's;

  • every entry is a proposal of its term and of its log's last term.

🛠️ Appendix C, step 2.5 (ReceiveAppend ⇒ Phase2b)

Step 2.5 maps only the new entries, although under the mapping a follower re-accepts its whole log in the leader's term, and it ignores the follower's commitIndex update.

Impact. For the repaired Raft* the step holds (sim_receiveAppend).

Details

The commit update maps to Paxos Learn steps. Showing that committed entries are never overwritten needs MultiPaxos safety itself, so the refinement and the invariant are proved together (inv_and_reachable). Raft* never truncates: a replaced entry is a Paxos acceptance in a higher ballot.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous message passing. Every message ever sent stays in a set, so messages may be lost, duplicated and reordered.

  • Crash-stop servers: a crashed server takes no further steps. No timing assumptions, deterministic protocols.

  • Any intersecting quorum system; majorities are QuorumSystem.majority.

  • Interleaving transition systems for MultiPaxos (Paxos.lean, Figure 1 repaired) and Raft* (RaftStar.lean, Figure 2 parametrised by the vote rule and by whether heartbeats are allowed).

  • Ghost history variables record the implied Paxos accept/acceptOK messages.

  • The mapping is Appendix C's: ballot = term, phase1Succeeded = isLeader, the accepted ballot of every entry is the term of the last entry, chosenSet = committed prefix.

  • Figure 3's per-entry bal mapping does not give a refinement even for repaired Raft* (scenario in differencies.md, not formalized). The repaired Raft* needs no bal field.

  • The refinement theorem covers every vote rule that never lets a server vote for a candidate with a smaller last term. This includes Raft's rule, the index-fixed Figure 2 rule and the permissive "last term ≤".
