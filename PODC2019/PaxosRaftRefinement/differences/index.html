Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Paxos vs. Raft, and porting optimizations — PODC 2019.

Paper: Wang, Zhao, Mu, Chen, Li. On the Parallels between Paxos and Raft, and how to Port Optimizations. PODC 2019, extended version (61 pp., Appendices A–C). The paper describes each protocol three times, and the three descriptions disagree: pseudocode (Figures 1–3), TLA+ (Appendix B) and the refinement proof (Appendix C). We formalize the protocols the Appendix C proof talks about (Figures 1 and 2, with the mapping table of Appendix C). We repair them where needed, taking each repair from another of the paper's descriptions where possible.

Statement-level issues

  1. TLA+ theorem Invariance of module MultiPaxos (B.1, line 177) is false — TLA.tla_invariance_false. There are three acceptors and majority quorums. One leader runs Propose(a1, 0, TRUE) and then Propose(a1, 0, FALSE), since Propose records nothing. Two quorums then accept, and OneValuePerBallot, Inv and agreement (chosen[0] = {TRUE, FALSE}) all fail. The grey comment "⇒ v is safe at i, b" does not help: both values are SafeAt ballot 1 because nothing was voted below it. Independently, two acceptors can become leaders of the same ballot, because 1b messages are not addressed (TLA.tla_two_leaders_same_ballot). 🔧. The fix (record proposals, address 1b messages) changes the protocol, it is not an index or symbol fix. The TLA+ specs are supporting material.

  2. Figure 2 vote rule (lines 9–11) compares the voter's entry at the candidate's last index with the candidate's last term. Raft* as printed then loses committed entries (Raft.fig2_not_commitSafe; isolated step: Raft.fig2_voter_reports_higher_term). So the printed Figure 2 refines no safe Paxos, and step 4.2 of Appendix C is false for it. 🔧. The fix is local (s.log[lIndex] → the voter's last entry), and the intent is recoverable from TLA+ B.2 (Phase1b uses raftlogs[a][lastIndex[a]]) and from Raft. The Appendix C proof does not reason with the printed rule; it gives no argument at all for 4.2. Raft* (Figure 2) is the paper's main protocol and is unsafe as printed. The intent is stated in the paper's own prose: Section 3 says "Raft* is identical to Raft, except for two introduced differences" (extra entries in votes, the bal field), neither of which concerns the vote rule, so Raft's last-entry check is intended; TLA+ B.2 Phase1b uses it too.

  3. Figure 2 allows AppendEntries with vals = [] (heartbeats). Then even Raft's own vote rule gives Raft's "Figure 8" loss of a committed entry (Raft.heartbeat_not_commitSafe). Section 3 claims that re-stamping ballots replaces Raft's §5.4.2 commit rule, but that only works if every append carries an entry of the current term. 🔧. TLA+ B.2's ProposeEntries always appends exactly one value, which recovers the assumption. Raft (which Raft* claims to equal "except two differences") does send empty AppendEntries, so a reader may be misled; but read the same way, Raft* would also keep Raft's §5.4.2 commit rule, which makes heartbeats safe. Either reading of the paper recovers a safe protocol; recorded here.

  4. Raft-PQL / Raft-Mencius "guaranteed correct" (Sections 4.3–4.4, Appendix A): unestablished (❌**). The Section 4.3 argument needs a single-step refinement (b_j ⇒ a_i). The Raft* → Paxos refinement is multi-step (Appendix C 2.4/2.5 map one step to "several Phase2a steps"). For multi-step refinements, the recipe of conjoining the added clauses at the end points is unsound in general (Porting.naive_multistep_port_unsound). The ported protocols themselves are not formalized, so they are neither proved nor refuted. The abstract presents porting "from Paxos to Raft with guaranteed correctness" as a primary contribution, and its proof for Raft* relies on b_j ⇒ a_i, which the paper's own Appendix C contradicts (one AppendEntries = several Paxos steps; "3. Q.E.D. … each step of Raft* implies a step of Paxos" is false as stated). Nothing is refuted for Raft*-PQL/Mencius.

Proof gaps of true statements (Appendix C)

  1. Step 2.3 / 4.2 ("s.instances[0, s.lastIndex] is already the safe entries") has no argument. For the repaired protocol it needs the invariant Raft.Inv (RaftInv.lean):

    • log matching;

    • at most one candidate with a quorum of votes per term (Inv.cand_unique);

    • a candidate's log equals its own vote (cand_log);

    • every voter's last term is ≤ the candidate's (rvok_vote);

    • every entry is a proposal of its own term and of its log's last term (entry_props, last_props), and proposals of a term come from that term's leader (props_leader).

  2. Step 2.5 (ReceiveAppend ⇒ Phase2b) maps only the entries prev+1 .. prev+|ents|. Under the Appendix C mapping (accepted ballot = term of the last entry), the follower re-accepts its whole log in the leader's term. This is justified by log matching: the follower's prefix equals the leader's, so Inv.receiveAppend_log gives that the new log is the leader's log. The step ignores the follower's commitIndex update, which changes chosenSet. We map it to Paxos Learn steps. Showing that the already committed entries are not overwritten needs MultiPaxos safety itself (PropSafe, supplied by Paxos.proposal_eq_of_chosenAt inside the induction, inv_and_reachable).

  3. Truncation (screening question): Raft* never truncates (s.lastIndex ≤ prev + length(ents)). A replaced entry corresponds to a Paxos acceptance in a higher ballot, never to un-accepting a value. This holds in our model.

  4. "Every Raft step implies a Paxos step or a stutter"* (Section 4.1 and step 3): it is a finite sequence of Paxos steps (refinement_step, batches in PaxosBatch.lean). The paper itself says so in 2.4/2.5 ("several Phase2a steps").

Modelling choices and repairs (not mistakes of the paper's results)

  • Mapping. We use Appendix C's mapping accBallot ↦ lastLogEntry.Term for every entry. Figure 3 maps instance.bal ↦ entry.bal (the per-entry bal field of Figure 2). With that mapping the refinement fails even for repaired Raft*, although safety holds. A candidate keeps its entry at index 0 with bal 1, while a voter reports a different value at index 0 with bal 3, so Paxos safeEntry would pick the voter's value. Scenario: 5 servers; term 1 writes x to C; term 3 writes u to V; term 5 extends C's log with prev = 0; C then wins term 6 with V's vote. This is only in prose, not formalized, because our Raft* has no bal field (it is behaviourally irrelevant). Recorded as a remark (✏️).

  • Figure 1 (MultiPaxos) repairs in Paxos.lean:

    • Phase2a must not propose a second value for the same instance and ballot. As printed it records nothing, the same flaw as TLA+ Propose.

    • Phase1a resets phase1Succeeded. As printed a leader keeps leadership after raising its ballot; TLA+ IncreaseHighestBallot resets it.

    • Phase1a sends the proposer's own prepareOK, and Phase1Succeed requires it and is guarded by ¬phase1Succeeded, as TLA+ BecomeLeader.

    • Phase1Succeed sets all instances (empty above the largest reported one). The unchosen optimisation is dropped, as Appendix C does.

    • Learn only adds to chosenSet; Figure 1 also overwrites the instance with (b, v). acceptOK messages are readable by every learner, which is the paper's "notify other servers".

  • Figure 2 (Raft) repairs* in RaftStar.lean:

    • RequestVote resets isLeader and casts the candidate's own vote, which carries its log.

    • BecomeLeader needs the own vote among a quorum of votes and is guarded by ¬isLeader, as TLA+ B.2. As printed it can re-run and shrink lastIndex.

    • Votes carry the whole log (Appendix C 3.5: "we can still assume Raft* includes the full log").

    • append messages carry the leader's log; the receiver uses only prevLen, pTerm and ents.

    • safeEntry among votes picks a vote with the largest last term, matching the mapping.

    • LeaderLearn commits any k at most the reported lengths. Figure 2 says exactly the minimum; allowing more nondeterminism only strengthens the safety result.

    • The vote rule is a parameter (Rules.voteOK). The theorem holds for every rule that never lets a voter vote for a candidate with a smaller last term (VoteMono). This covers Raft's/TLA+'s rule (raftStar_commitSafe_raftVote), the index-fixed Figure 2 rule (raftStar_commitSafe_fig2Fixed) and the most permissive rule "voter's last term ≤ candidate's" (refinement_repaired).

    • commitIndex is stored as a length (commit = commitIndex + 1). Indices are 0-based, as in the paper.

  • Ghost variables props/acks record the implied Paxos accept/acceptOK messages, like TLA+ proposedValues/votes.

  • Network and failures: every message ever sent stays in a set, so loss, duplication and reordering are all possible; crash-stop is a server that takes no more steps; quorums are any intersecting family. These are the paper's assumptions (Section 2, TLA+).

  • Not formalized:

    • the TLA+ module Raft* (B.2) — its ProposeEntries also records nothing in the leader's log, so its Invariance very likely fails the same way as B.1's; not checked;

    • PQL, Mencius and their Raft* ports (B.3–B.6, Appendix A);

    • Fig. 6's classification of Paxos variants;

    • the evaluation (Section 5).

  • Section 4 formalization (Porting.lean):

    • Subactions are relations on full states. A modified subaction conjoins an arbitrary clause, and an added subaction must leave the variables of A unchanged ("non-mutating").

    • The paper's syntactic "does not mutate Var_A" for the clauses of modified subactions is not needed: any conjunct only restricts behaviour.

    • Parameter mappings (f_args) are subsumed by letting clauses read the full pre/post states.

    • A stuttering b_j keeps the new variables, which the recipe leaves implicit.
