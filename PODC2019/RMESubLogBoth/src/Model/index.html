The paper has `n ≥ k` processes, at most one per port during a super-passage; a process only ever accesses `Node[p]` of its own port `p`, its local variables are wiped at every crash, and between two super-passages of a port it is in the remainder section, so the identity of the process using a port does not matter for the properties formalized here. * Node references are natural numbers; `0` is `SpecialNode`; `nxt` is the next fresh address (`new QNode` returns `nxt`). The pseudo-nodes `&Crash`, `&InCS`, `&Exit` and `NIL` are the constructors of `PV` (the values of a `Pred` field). * Signal objects are atomic bits: `set()` is one write, `wait()` busy-waits on the bit (one read per step). Theorem 1 (the implementation of Figure 2) is treated separately in `Signal.lean`. * `RLock` is abstract: a lock variable `holder`. Line 24 acquires it when it is free or already held by the caller (critical-section re-entry after a crash); otherwise the step is a busy-wait. The exit of `RLock` frees it. This allows every order of entries, so the safety results hold for every mutual exclusion lock with critical-section re-entry; starvation freedom of `RLock` is a fairness assumption on runs. * Every step performs at most one shared-memory access (or one `RLock` operation) together with the local computation that follows it. Lines 11–12 (allocation and the write of `Node[p]`) form one step; lines 16–17 are merged into line 10 (both read `Node[p]`, which only `p` writes). * Lines 39–41 and the order of the loop at line 42 are nondeterministic (`Step` is a relation): every maximal path containing `mynode` (resp. `tail`) may be chosen, and the loop may visit the maximal paths in any order.
