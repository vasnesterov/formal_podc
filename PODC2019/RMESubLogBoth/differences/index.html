Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Recoverable Mutex with Sub-logarithmic RMR on CC and DSM — PODC 2019.

Paper: Jayanti, Jayanti, Joshi, A Recoverable Mutex Algorithm with Sub-logarithmic RMR on Both CC and DSM, PODC 2019, arXiv 1904.02124v2.

Statement-level issues

  1. Lemma 9 is false as stated (🛠️). Three conditions of its invariant fail in reachable configurations of crash-free runs with k = 2:

    • Condition 5, qnode.CS_Signal = 0 ⇒ qnode.Pred ∈ {NIL, &Crash, &InCS}: false after line 14 (PaperInv.cond5_csSignal_false); also false at line 28 (Pred = &Exit, signal 0).

    • Condition 5, qnode.NonNil_Signal = 0 ⇒ qnode.Pred = NIL: false between lines 14 and 15 (PaperInv.cond5_nonNil_false) and between lines 18 and 23.

    • Condition 4, third conjunct (Pred iterated at most k times reaches a distinguished value): false with two queued processes (PaperInv.cond4_false); k + 1 would be needed.

    • Condition 18 uses PC_π' instead of \hat{PC}_π' (fails in the initial configuration); this one is a typo with evident intent (✏️; Condition 19(g) has the hat).

    The Condition 4 off-by-one and the Condition 18 typo look like local matters, but the two Condition 5 conjuncts have no evident local fix in the paper (adding all node values and &Exit to the right-hand side would make them nearly vacuous). The proof in Appendix E claims the whole conjunction is inductive. The correctness claims (Theorem 2's RME properties) are true and proved here.

    Reviewer check (rendered Figures 8–9). Condition 5 is quantified over all qnode ∈ 𝒩 with no PC guard and no restriction to a role; 𝒩 contains every node created at line 11, and CS_Signal = 0 / NonNil_Signal = 0 mean that no set() has been applied (a fresh node's signals have State = 0, Section 3.1). Condition 4's bound is the k of the k-ported algorithm. Reversing the two = 0 implications would give true statements, but they are just contrapositives of Condition 5's = 1 conjuncts, so that reading is not evidently intended; the printed conjuncts express the belief that the signals track Pred exactly ("NonNil_Signal = 0 iff Pred = NIL"), which ignores the windows 14–15, 18–23 and 27–28. The = 0 conjuncts are never used in Appendix E (only the = 1 conjuncts are cited), and the step for line 14 does not re-check Condition 5. For Condition 4 the proof does reason with the wrong bound (step of line 14: "b1 + b2 ≤ k", which forgets that a fragment's head may be SpecialNode or an exited node that is nobody's node_π); it is only used for acyclicity (Condition 23's proof), where any finite bound suffices. So Condition 5 is not a typo with evident intent, and Lemma 9 is false even though Lemmas 4–8 are true. The paper's corrected invariant (Conditions 4, 5, 18 patched, all others as printed) was not checked; we prove our own invariant instead.

  2. Lemmas 4 and 5: proofs not established (🛠️). Both proofs cite conditions of the false invariant (Conditions 15, 19), and Lemma 5's case "the queue is broken" is an informal induction over fragments. Statements proved with a new invariant and liveness argument.

  3. Theorem 2, RMR part (✏️, not formalized). "O(1) for a process that does not crash during its passage" is literally false for a passage that starts with recovery (it runs RLock and scans all of Node[]); the intent is crash-free super-passages. "O(fk)" should be "O(1 + fk)".

Modelling choices

  • Processes = ports. One process per port Fin k. The paper has n processes and at most one per port per super-passage. A process only touches Node[p] of its port and loses its local state at every crash, so the projection onto ports is faithful for ME, SF, CSR, wait-free exit and wait-free CSR. RMR accounting per process (CC caches) is not modelled.

  • Granularity. One shared access per step. Merged: lines 11–12 (allocation is local, so a crash between them only leaks a node); lines 16–17 into 10 (both read Node[p], written only by p); local lines 20–21, 34, 37–38, 39–41 (as one nondeterministic step), 48 (with the read of line 46). Each read in lines 43, 44 and 46 is its own step.

  • Pseudo-nodes. &Crash, &InCS, &Exit, NIL are constructors of PV. SpecialNode is node 0, with Pred = &Exit and both signals set. new QNode returns the next fresh address.

  • Nondeterminism of lines 39–42. mypath, tailpath may be any maximal path containing mynode, tail, and the loop of line 42 visits maximal paths in any order (done list). The paper says "the unique path"; we prove the invariant for every choice, and step_total shows a choice always exists (the scanned graph is acyclic).

  • Line 37 with curpred = NIL. It cannot happen (NonNil_Signal was awaited at line 35); we treat it like the distinguished values (vertex only).

  • Signals. Atomic bits inside the main algorithm. Theorem 1 is proved separately for the implementation of Figure 2, crash-free. The composition (Figure 2's non-atomic set()/wait() inside Figures 3–4, with crashes in the middle of a set() or wait()) is not verified; the paper does not prove it either beyond the remark that interrupted set()s are re-executed.

  • RLock. Abstract lock variable holder: acquire if free or held by the caller (CSR), else busy-wait; release in its exit. Any acquisition order is allowed (covers every mutual exclusion lock with re-entry for safety). For starvation freedom the paper assumes a starvation-free RLock; with our abstract lock no assumption is needed because after the last crash only finitely many acquisitions happen. A concrete RLock with its own steps is not modelled.

  • Crash. Sets the program counter to line 10 (non-idle) from any line, including the remainder section. This only adds obligations; it does not weaken the results.

  • Sections. CS = PC.cs (the paper's PC = 27); its step executes line 27. Exit section = lines 28–29 (b = 2 in Lemma 6). Try = everything else except the remainder section; the recovery code (including lines 22's execution of 28–29) is in Try, as in the paper.

  • Fairness. Fair: a process not in the remainder section later takes a step or crashes (conditions (i)–(ii) of Section 1.2). FinCrash: finitely many crashes overall.

  • Lemma 7/8 formulations. Lemma 7: after a crash in the CS, p is back in the CS within 3 own steps if it does not crash again. Lemma 8: from the crash until p re-enters the CS, no other process is in the CS (also with further crashes of p).

Theorem 1 (Signal)

  • Crash-free model, arbitrary invocations of set()/wait() by any processes, hypothesis that wait() executions never overlap. Linearization point of the first effective set(): the first completion of a set() or the first wait() reading Bit = 1 (as in Appendix D). (iii): set() takes ≤ 3 steps (lines 2–3 merged). (iv): ≤ 4 more steps of a wait() once State = 1. (v): RMR counts on DSM and CC (ghost cache).

  • With crashes and strict linearizability, (iv) can fail (Signal.crash_spin, Signal.crash_rewait): a set() crashing after line 1 leaves a waiter spinning with Bit = 1. The paper does not state Theorem 1 for crashes, and the main algorithm re-executes every interrupted set(); not counted as a mistake.

Not formalized

  • The RMR complexity bounds of Theorem 2 and all of Theorem 3 (arbitration tree via Golab–Hendler Section 5).

  • Appendix A's two scenarios against Golab–Hendler's PODC'17 algorithm.
