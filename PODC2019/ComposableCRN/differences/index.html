Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Composable CRN computation — PODC 2019.

Paper: E. E. Severson, D. Haley, D. Doty, Composable computation in discrete chemical reaction networks, PODC 2019 (arXiv:1903.02637v2, the version formalized; numbering follows it).

Errors in the paper

None found in the formalized part. All checked statements hold as written (see the README table). The only imprecisions are listed below; none of them changes a statement.

Outside the formalized part, §1.2 (second paragraph) says "X1 + X2 → W (computing w = x1 + x2)"; this reaction computes w = min(x1, x2), as the same sentence intends. ✏️

Model and definitions

  1. Inputs indexed by a finite type. ℕ^d is ι → ℕ for any finite ι (Fin d in the paper). This is a harmless generalization.

  2. Species distinctness. The paper names input species X₁, …, X_d, an output Y and a leader L without saying they are distinct. FunCRN requires X injective, Y ≠ Xᵢ, L ≠ Xᵢ, L ≠ Y (standard reading). The impossibility results (Impossibility.lean) never use these fields, so they hold even without distinctness.

  3. Leader as an option. FunCRN.L : Option S; ObliviouslyComputable requires a leader (the paper's model, §2.2), LeaderlessObliviouslyComputable requires none (§9). StablyComputable (only used to state the cited Lemma 2.7 as a hypothesis) allows both. The class is the same either way (the semilinear functions, [10, 17]), so the hypothesis remains a true theorem.

  4. Semilinear functions. We define "f semilinear" as "the graph of f is a semilinear set" with Mathlib's IsSemilinearSet (finite union of linear sets a + ⟨t⟩). The paper says this is the concise definition and then works with Definition 2.6 (a disjoint union of affine partial functions on Boolean combinations of threshold and mod sets, Definition 2.5). The definitions are equivalent by classical results (Ginsburg–Spanier; [10, Lemma 4.3]); Mathlib proves semilinear = Presburger-definable (definable_iff_isSemilinearSet). Our proof of Theorem 3.1 "⇐" uses the linear-set form directly, so it does not depend on the equivalence.

  5. Quilt-affine (Definition 5.1) is formalized literally: nondecreasing g : ℕ^ι → ℤ with g x = ∇g · x + B(x mod p), p > 0, ∇g ∈ ℚ^ι_{≥0}, B : (ℤ/pℤ)^ι → ℚ.

  6. Condition (ii) of Theorem 5.2 (EventuallyMin) uses m + 1 functions g₀, …, g_m, i.e. at least one; "the minimum of zero functions" is meaningless in the paper as well.

  7. Fixed-input restriction f[x(i)→j] is fixInput f i j x = f (Function.update x i j), with domain ℕ^d as in the paper's footnote 11.

Statements

  1. Lemma 2.7 ([10], cited) is not proved; its "⇒" half (stably computable ⇒ semilinear) is the hypothesis StablyComputableImpliesSemilinear of theorem_3_1 (and of theorem_9_2). The "⇐" directions of Theorems 3.1 and 9.2 need no hypothesis.

  2. Theorem 7.1 (necessity of condition (ii); §7, recession cones and a hyperplane-arrangement argument in ℝ^d) is not formalized. theorem_5_2 takes it as the hypothesis Theorem71; the "⇐" direction (lemma_6_2) and the parts (i), (iii) of "⇒" (Observations 2.1, 5.3) are proved unconditionally.

  3. Lemma 4.1. The paper asks the sequence (aᵢ) to be increasing; the proof never uses it, and not_obliviouslyComputable_of_seq drops the assumption (stronger statement). Differences are taken in ℤ, as in the paper.

  4. Observation 2.2 (composition). The paper composes by renaming C_f's output to C_g's input, plus a reaction L → L_f + L_g, for one upstream module g : ℕ → ℕ, and remarks that any number of upstream modules works. We prove the general remark (ObliviouslyComputable.comp, StablyComputable.comp_oblivious: κ upstream modules, fan-out reactions X_i → Σ_k X^{(k)}_i, one leader split L → Σ_k L_k + L_g). Instead of renaming we use a transfer reaction Y^{(k)} → X^{(g)}_k; this only delays the hand-over. The semantic content used by Lemma 6.2 (closure under composition) is the same; the renaming construction itself is not formalized. As in the paper, the downstream CRN need not be output-oblivious.

  5. Observation 5.3 is derived from composition (f composed with the constant j and the projections) instead of the paper's direct "hard-coding" CRN L → j X_i' + L'. Same statement.

  6. Lemma 6.2. Same identity (1) and the same pieces. Two harmless changes: n ∈ ℕ^d is replaced by the scalar N = maxᵢ nᵢ (the paper's own "without loss of generality"). The module for (x − n)⁺ is our leader-automaton CRN rather than (n+1)Xᵢ → nXᵢ + Yᵢ (same function). The other gadgets are the paper's CRNs literally: X₁ + … + X_k → Y for min and A → Y, (j+1)W + B → (j+1)W + Y for c(a, b, w).

  7. Theorem 3.1 / Lemma 6.1 constructions. Both are instances of one generic leader-automaton construction (obliviouslyComputable_of_automaton), proved correct once. For Theorem 3.1 the leader states are 0, …, N+P−1 with x ↦ x below N + P and x ↦ N + (x − N) mod P above. These are the paper's L_0, …, L_{n−1}, P_0, …, P_{p−1} up to renaming (the paper's P states start at n mod p; pdftotext drops the overbars P_{\bar n}, \bar a that mean "mod p").

  8. Proof of Theorem 3.1 "⇐". The paper's argument (threshold/mod decomposition, "the periodically repeated affine pieces must have the same slope since f is nondecreasing") is replaced by an argument on linear sets: every linear set a + ⟨t⟩ inside the graph with a period u = (u₁, u₂), u₁ > 0, gives f(x + P) − f(x) = (P/u₁)·u₂ on its projection, which is closed under +P. Hence f(x+2P) + f(x) = 2f(x+P) beyond a threshold. Monotonicity then makes f(x + P) − f(x) constant (eventually_affine_of_semilinear_of_monotone), the same "same slope" step as in the paper. No gap in the paper; just a different route.

  9. Theorem 9.2 construction. We use the paper's leaderless CRN without the leader-automaton reactions it inherits from Theorem 3.1 (the paper itself notes they are unnecessary): X → f(1)Y + T_{red 1} and merge reactions T_a + T_b → (f(a+b) − f(a) − f(b))Y + T_{red(a+b)}. Tokens are labelled by the same reduction red as in Theorem 3.1, so the paper's WLOG p | n is not needed. The correctness invariant tracks a multiset of consumed input blocks.

Not formalized

  1. Lemma 2.3 (composable ⇒ output-oblivious; splicing argument) and Observation 2.4 (output-monotonic = output-oblivious); Theorem 5.4; all of §7 (Theorem 7.1, Lemmas 7.3–7.20); §8 (Theorem 8.2, scaling limits); footnote 2 (equivalence of the "reachable stable configuration" definition with fair-execution and probability-1 semantics). We read the proofs of Lemma 2.3 and Observation 2.4 and found them sound (Lemma 2.3: the splicing terminates because each splice moves at least one W to the never-consumed Y, whose count is bounded by f(x)).

Scope notes

  1. Checked statements. The checked statements are Observations 2.1, 2.2 (in the generalized closure form), 5.3, 9.1, Lemmas 4.1, 6.1, 6.2, the ⇐ directions of Theorems 3.1, 5.2, 9.2, and the ⇒ directions of 3.1/9.2 modulo the cited Lemma 2.7. The main Theorem 5.2 is only half checked: the necessity of condition (ii) (Theorem 7.1, §7) is a hypothesis, so nothing is claimed about it. Lemma 6.2 uses the multi-input form of Observation 2.2, which the paper only asserts in a remark ("more generally, …") without proof. The extension is routine (fan-out plus the same additivity argument), so we count it as a routine detail rather than a gap.

  2. Hypotheses are the paper's own claims in the paper's model. StablyComputableImpliesSemilinear and Theorem71 are stated for our FunCRN, which, like the paper ((R, P) ∈ ℕ^S × ℕ^S), allows any reactions, including ones with no reactants. They are the paper's Lemma 2.7 ([10]) and Theorem 7.1 for exactly the paper's model, so they are not vacuous by a modelling artefact.
