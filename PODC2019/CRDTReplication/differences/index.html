Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Linearizable SMR of State-Based CRDTs without Logs — PODC 2019.

Paper: J. Skrzypczak, F. Schintke, T. Schütt, Linearizable State Machine Replication of State-Based CRDTs without Logs, PODC 2019; full version arXiv:1905.08733v2 (numbering as there).

Errors in the paper

  1. Lemma 3 is false. It states that two learned states are comparable if one is learned by consistent quorum. The counterexample Cex.run (Counterexample.lean) uses five processes, majority quorums, the G-set over {u₁, u₂} and incremental prepares only. In it, query B learns {u₁} by consistent quorum and query A learns {u₂} by vote (Cex.lemma3_false). The proof's step "it follows from C1 and C2 that a has received p₁'s PREPARE in between p₂'s PREPARE and VOTE" applies C1 (stated for Q₁ ∩ Q₂) to a voter a ∈ Q₁ that is not in Q₂. It also implicitly assumes that an acceptor which accepted another proposer's PREPARE first cannot assign p₂'s incremental prepare the same round number as the members of Q₂ did. Earlier prepares (query C in the run) make the numbers coincide.

  2. Theorem 3 (Consistency) is false: same execution (Cex.theorem3_false).

  3. Theorem 4 (Update Stability) is false. u₁ completes before u₂ is invoked, and A learns {u₂} (Cex.theorem4_false). The proof claims "∄Q: a.r = r for all a ∈ Q, as all a ∈ Q_u updated their round". But acceptors of Q_u that receive the PREPARE after the MERGE hold round r. They may vote although their payloads (containing u₁) were not among the ACKs whose LUB is proposed.

  4. The main claim (linearizability, Section III-E applied to Algorithm 2) is false. The history of Cex.run has no linearization for the G-set (Cex.runHist_not_linearizable, Cex.not_linearizable, Cex.linearizableClaim_false). For the paper's G-counter with the sum query this particular execution is linearizable; the counterexample needs a CRDT whose queries distinguish which updates are included.

  5. Lemma 4: correct, proof incomplete. The proof does not treat equal round numbers of different attempts, and it relies without proof on round numbers of accepted prepares strictly increasing at each acceptor. Our proof (lemma4) uses three acceptor invariants:

    • round numbers of ACKs are at most rnr;

    • two ACKs of one acceptor with equal numbers have equal ids;

    • a VOTED for round n is below every later ACK with a number > n.

  6. Repair (ours). An acceptor answers VOTE(r', s') only if r' = r and its payload before the merge is ⊑ s' (Variant.fixed, flag fix). With it, Theorems 1–5 and linearizability hold (fixed_* in Main.lean). Theorems 1, 2, 5, Lemmas 1, 2, 4 and Corollary 1 hold for both variants.

  7. Versions. The formalization follows arXiv v2 (2020). arXiv v1 (2019) has the same Algorithm 2 up to line numbering (updates and merges set rid ← write instead of ⊥; VOTE accepted iff r' = r; prepares compared by round number only), and the same proofs of Lemma 3.6 and Theorem 3.9, so items 1–4 apply to it as well.

Definitions and modelling choices

  • CRDT. CRDT follows Definition 3: S is a SemilatticeSup with decidable =/≤, app : U → S → S is inflationary, and queries query : Q → S → R. We add a decidability assumption for the line-13 and VOTE tests.

  • Equivalence ≡. Line 13 is modelled by a Boolean test cons (Variant). All results assume cons s t = true → s = t (equality, Variant.paper). The paper defines ≡ as query equivalence. With a coarser test the proof of Lemma 2(i) breaks, since it needs s' ⊑ a.s for the learned LUB s'. We did not investigate that reading further.

  • Causal histories. The paper treats C(s) as a function of the state, with C(s₀) = ∅, u ∈ C(u(s)), C(s₁ ⊔ s₂) = C(s₁) ∪ C(s₂), and quotes C(s₁) = C(s₂) ⇒ s₁ ≡ s₂. We assume a map H.C : S → Set U with these properties, strengthening the second to C(u(s)) = C(s) ∪ {u} (the paper's intended meaning; needed for Validity). Theorems 1, 4, 5 and the linearizability results are stated for CRDTs having such a map. The G-set has one (gsetHistory); a G-counter whose state forgets update identities in general does not.

  • "Includes u" is u ∈ H.C s; "update u₁ precedes u₂" is CSLib's real-time order Precedes on operation instances with commands upd u₁, upd u₂.

  • Unique updates. Assumed as a hypothesis UniqueUpd h in Theorem 4 and in the linearizability claim. The Section III-E theorem keeps the field unique of LearnedHistory for fidelity, although its proof does not use it.

  • Sequential specification. CRDT.spec: the state is a payload, an update applies its command and returns done, a query returns val (cmd_q s) and does not change the state. The paper's equation (1) (∃ s' ≡ q.s, C(s') = U_q) is implied: the state after the preceding updates has causal history U_q, and faithful gives the same query result.

  • System model. CSLib Cslib.AsyncMP:

    • channels are unordered and arbitrarily slow, with crash-stop processes;

    • message loss is not modelled separately, since safety concerns finite executions where undelivered messages are simply lost;

    • duplication is not modelled;

    • each process has at most one pending operation, as CSLib histories require. The paper allows many concurrent commands per proposer. The counterexample has one operation per process, so it is valid in the paper's model.

  • Processes and roles. Every process is proposer and acceptor ("we assume that all processes implement both roles"). Messages to self go through the network.

  • Updates. Lines 1–6: the local acceptor applies u (resetting rid), and MERGE(s) goes to the other acceptors. The update completes once a quorum has merged, counting the local acceptor as merged. This reading is more permissive than requiring a quorum of remote MERGEDs. The counterexample's update collects a quorum of remote MERGEDs anyway.

  • Queries.

    • Line 10 PREPARE carries s₀; retries (line 23 and retry) carry the LUB of the received payloads (or the proposal).

    • Request ids are attempt ids (p, k, j): process, operation index, attempt counter. The id j is fresh per attempt (new_id()); the round of an attempt is (n, id).

    • The proposer acts (advance) on all ACKs of the current attempt received so far, once they contain a quorum; the paper acts exactly when a quorum is reached. The scheduler can always take advance right after the quorum-completing reception, so the paper's behaviour is included.

    • Line 16 "all rounds equal": all received round numbers equal (ids are equal by construction).

    • Line 22: the fixed retry uses number max R̆ + 1.

  • Retries after NACK (Section III-B, not in Algorithm 2) are modelled by the internal action retry n, which a pending query may take at any time with any n. This is more permissive than "retry upon a NACK".

  • VOTED. Carries the round and the state of the VOTE. The proposer counts VOTED(s') only for its current attempt, round and proposal, as line 24 "VOTED, {s, …, s}" suggests.

  • Ghost state.

    • The learned state of each query and how it was learned (Loc.learned), written exactly at the response.

    • "Learned state" in Theorems 1–4 means the state learned by a query that has returned. A state learned and used for the response is the same thing in the model.

  • Statement forms.

    • Corollary 1 is stated as: an ACK present in C carries a state ⊑ every ACK of the same acceptor sent later in an execution from C.

    • Lemma 1 is stated for steps from reachable configurations.

    • Theorem 1 is stated as: every update in C(s) was invoked by some operation that is not preceded by the query.

  • Lemmas 5–8. Lemma 5 is the monotonicity of C (CausalHistory.C_mono). Lemmas 6–8 are replaced by an explicit construction (linOrder):

    • all invoked updates and all completed queries are sorted by level · N + invocation index;

    • a query that learned s has level 2·rank(s)+1, where rank(s) is the number of learned states not above s;

    • an update u has level 2·urank(u), where urank(u) is the number of learned states not containing u.

    This is equivalent to rules S1–S3 and avoids the auxiliary query r*. Pending queries are dropped and pending updates kept, which is how the paper extends H to H̄.

Not formalized

  • Section III-F (finite-writes termination / liveness) and Section III-G (optimizations, batching).

  • Section IV (experiments).
