Linearizable SMR of State-Based CRDTs without Logs — PODC 2019

Jan Skrzypczak, Florian Schintke, Thorsten Schütt. Linearizable State Machine Replication of State-Based CRDTs without Logs. PODC 2019 (brief announcement). DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331568. Formalized version: the full version, arXiv 1905.08733 v2; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Algorithm 2 is not linearizable as printed, and Theorem 3 (Consistency), Lemma 3 and Theorem 4 (Update Stability) are false: a five-process execution with majority quorums lets two queries learn the incomparable states {u₁} and {u₂}, although u₁ completed before u₂ was invoked. A one-line repair of the VOTE handler restores Theorems 1–5 and linearizability at no cost to updates or message size, which we prove for CRDTs with the causal history the paper assumes. Lemmas 1, 2, 4, Corollary 1 and Theorems 1, 2, 5 hold, and so does the Section III-E reduction (Theorems 1–5 ⇒ linearizability); finite-writes termination was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Algorithm 2 (linearizability), Theorem 3, Theorem 4 and Lemma 3

Algorithm 2 is not linearizable, and Lemma 3, Theorem 3 (Consistency) and Theorem 4 (Update Stability) are false: with five processes and majority quorums, one query learns {u₂} by vote while another learns {u₁} by consistent quorum, although u₁ completed before u₂ was invoked (Cex.not_linearizable, paper_false).

Impact. The paper's main claim fails for the protocol as printed, and the flaw is in the protocol (the VOTE handler), not only in the proofs. Lemmas 1, 2, 4, Corollary 1, Theorems 1, 2, 5 and the Section III-E reduction hold, and a one-line repair of the VOTE handler restores Theorems 1–5 and linearizability.

Details

Take five processes p₀…p₄, majority quorums, a G-set, and only incremental prepares; no message is lost, some are only late.

  1. A query C raises the round numbers of p₀, p₁, p₃, p₄ to 1.

  2. Query A (at p₀) gets ACK((2,A), ∅) from p₀ and p₁.

  3. Update u₁, issued at p₄, is merged at p₀, p₁, p₂ and completes on their three MERGEDs.

  4. Query B reads {u₁} from p₀, p₁, p₂ and learns it by consistent quorum.

  5. Update u₂ is applied at p₃ only.

  6. A's PREPARE reaches p₂, p₃, p₄, which all move to round (2, A). The round numbers coincide because of step 1. A takes the quorum {p₀, p₁, p₃} with states ∅, ∅, {u₂}, all in round (2, A), and sends VOTE((2,A), {u₂}).

  7. p₂, p₃, p₄ are still in round (2, A) and answer VOTED, so A learns {u₂} by vote, although p₂ and p₄ hold u₁.

So {u₁} and {u₂} are incomparable, which refutes Theorem 3 and Lemma 3. Since u₁ precedes u₂, Theorem 4 fails too. For the G-set, A returns {u₂} after u₁ completed and u₂ was invoked, so the history has no linearization. (Cex.lemma3_false, Cex.theorem3_false, Cex.theorem4_false, Cex.not_linearizable, paper_false)

Both proofs confuse the ACK quorum with the VOTED quorum. The proof of Lemma 3 applies condition C1, which holds only on Q₁ ∩ Q₂ (here {p₀, p₁}), to the voter a = p₂ ∈ Q₁ outside Q₂. It also tacitly assumes that a later PREPARE cannot get the same round number at all acceptors. The proof of Theorem 4 says "∄Q: a.r = r for all a ∈ Q, as all a ∈ Q_u updated their round". But the acceptors of Q_u that receive the PREPARE after the MERGE do hold round r, and their payloads (with u₁) are not part of the proposed LUB. The flaw is in the protocol, not only in the proofs: a VOTED certifies that the acceptor's round is unchanged, not that its payload is ⊑ the proposal.

The schedule respects the paper's own reading of line 47 too: the voters' payloads changed before they received A's PREPARE, not "in the meantime". The same flaw is in arXiv v1 (2019), whose handlers and proofs (Lemma 3.6, Theorem 3.9) are identical in the relevant lines.

The non-linearizability needs queries that can tell which updates are included. On the paper's own G-counter with the sum query, this particular execution is linearizable, although the same schedule still yields the incomparable learned vectors e₄ and e₃ (not formalized).

Repair

An acceptor answers VOTE(r', s') only if, in addition, its payload before the merge is ⊑ s' (Variant.fixed). Then every learned state was, at some moment, the exact payload of every acceptor of a quorum. Consistency follows from monotonicity alone, and Lemma 4's Paxos-style round argument is no longer needed. Update stability follows because a quorum acquired u₁ before u₂ existed. We prove Theorems 1–5 and linearizability for the repaired protocol. (fixed_theorem3, fixed_theorem4, fixed_linearizable)

✏️ Slips

  • Line 13 tests sᵢ ≡ s', and the paper defines ≡ as query equivalence. The proof of Lemma 2(i) needs s' ⊑ a.s, i.e. equality, so we use equality.

  • The causal history C(s) is treated as a function of the state, which is not well defined for every CRDT. Also u ∈ C(u(s)) is too weak for Theorem 1. We assume a causal-history map with C(s₀) = ∅, C(u(s)) = C(s) ∪ {u}, C(s ⊔ t) = C(s) ∪ C(t) and Shapiro et al.'s C(s) = C(t) ⇒ s ≡ t. The G-set has one.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib's asynchronous message-passing model Cslib.AsyncMP: unordered channels with arbitrary delays, crash-stop. Safety concerns finite executions, so a lost message is one that is never delivered. Histories are CSLib Labels, and linearizability is CSLib's IsLinearization w.r.t. the CRDT's sequential specification CRDT.spec (updates apply the command, queries return cmd_q(s)).

  • Every process is a proposer and an acceptor (alg V QS). Clients are the processes themselves, with one pending operation per process. Each handler is atomic.

  • The quorum system QS is an arbitrary pairwise-intersecting family of sets.

  • The CRDT is arbitrary (CRDT: join semilattice, inflationary updates, queries). The theorems mentioning "includes u" use a causal-history map H.

  • Proposer behaviour is generous, which makes the positive results stronger:

    • the internal action advance may act on all ACKs received so far, as soon as they contain a quorum;

    • retry n lets a pending query abandon its attempt at any time and restart with an incremental or arbitrary fixed prepare, which models NACK-triggered retries;

    • the local acceptor counts towards the MERGED quorum.

    The counterexample uses only the paper's literal behaviour.

Line 13 with ≡ read as query equivalence (only equality is formalized). The repaired protocol on CRDTs that have no causal-history map on states, such as a G-counter whose state forgets update identities: fixed_linearizable takes H : D.CausalHistory as a hypothesis.

Notes on the proofs. The paper's proof of Lemma 4 does not spell out two attempts with the same round number, or that round numbers of accepted prepares strictly increase at each acceptor; both follow from the code (lines 41–43). We prove the lemma with the Paxos-style invariant "every ACK with a larger round number carries a state above an earlier VOTED" (paper_lemma4).

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The counterexample execution is checked by evaluation (rfl), with no native_decide.

Non-vacuity:

  • Cex.qs5_isQuorumSystem shows the counterexample's quorums form a quorum system.

  • fixed_nonvacuous shows the repaired protocol has executions in which queries learn states, and that the counterexample run is not one of them.

  • gsetHistory is a causal history for the G-set.
