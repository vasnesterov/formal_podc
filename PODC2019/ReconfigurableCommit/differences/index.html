Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Reconfigurable Atomic Transaction Commit — PODC 2019.

Paper: M. Bravo, A. Gotsman, Reconfigurable Atomic Transaction Commit, PODC 2019 (arXiv:1906.01365v1, extended version). Scope: §2 (TCS specification), §3 (Figure 1), §4 Theorem 4.1 and its proof in App. A (Invariants 1–12, Lemma A.1, Proposition A.2, Lemma A.3).

Errors in the paper

  1. ✏️ Figure 1, lines 56–58: the check of NEW_CONFIG is only implicit. The handler head is NEW_CONFIG(new_epoch, M) with the state variable new_epoch in the pattern, and line 58 uses an unbound e. The paper's convention is that such patterns are matches (lines 45, 51; App. C cites "the checks in lines 141 and 148", whose heads NEW_CONFIG(new_epoch), NEW_STATE(new_epoch, …) carry no other precondition), so line 56 checks e = new_epoch: this is "the check in line 56" of the proof of Invariant 3. Read as a binder NEW_CONFIG(e, M) with no check, Theorem 4.1 is false: a delayed NEW_CONFIG(1) moves a process that already leads epoch 2 back to epoch 1, and two conflicting transactions commit in a FIFO, crash-free run of 32 steps (theorem_4_1_false_printed, Line56.acts, Line56.epoch_decreases). We formalize the check as e ≥ new_epoch (Params.fixNewConfig = true); this admits every execution of the e = new_epoch protocol (the handlers are otherwise identical), so theorem_4_1 covers the paper's protocol. The late message of the counterexample is rejected (Line56.fixed_blocks). Not a separate Lean statement: the inclusion of the = protocol's steps is immediate.

  2. 🔧 Conditions (1), (3)–(5) do not imply fₛ({ε}, l) = commit, and Theorem 4.1 / Lemma A.3 need it. (3) quantifies over all shards and projects payloads, so a committed transaction that does not touch s contributes ε to L | s. Counterexample with all stated conditions: lemma_A_3_false (spec level) and theorem_4_1_false_eps (a failure-free run of the protocol). Chockler–Gotsman (DISC 2018, condition (6)) project transactions on shards and do not have this problem. Our hypothesis: EpsNeutral (fₛ({ε}, l) = commit).

  3. ✏️ Figure 1, line 24 writes (txn, payload, vote, phase)[next]; must be [k] (followers do not maintain next; the proofs use slot k). Modelled as [k].

  4. 🔧 Invariant 8 (new_epoch ≥ epoch[s₀]) is false: a fresh member gets NEW_STATE(e) with new_epoch = 0 (Inv8.invariant_8_false, protocol with the line-56 check). Not needed; we use new_epoch ≤ epoch[s₀] for leaders/followers (Inv.loc_newEpoch).

  5. ✏️ Degenerate cases. (a) A configuration with a single member (f = 0): the wait at line 26 is vacuous for arbitrary kₛ, dₛ, and the coordinator commits unprepared conflicting transactions (Single.single_member_unsafe). The definition of posₛ in Lemma A.1 ("all followers ... received ACCEPT") is vacuous in the same case. (b) A transaction with shards(t) = ∅ can be decided without being certified. We assume at least two members per configuration (ValidInit, and in compute_membership) and shards(t) ≠ ∅.

Proof gaps (statements true)

  1. 🔧 Lemma A.1, (10)–(11). The witness Pₛ[t] = P \ {posₛ undefined} violates (11) when a transaction of P (prepared with vote commit when t's vote was computed) was later lost in a reconfiguration and re-prepared at a higher slot or with an abort vote. We filter Pₛ[t] by (11) and use monotonicity of gₛ (Inv.tcsll; witness PsW in Safety.lean).

  2. Lemma A.3 is only referred to "the proof of Theorem 1 in [5] with minimal adjustments". We prove it from scratch (lemma_A_3): order committed transactions topologically along ≺rt ∪ ≺dec (acyclic by (13)), and check legality shard by shard with (4), (5), (7)–(10). It needs neither well-formedness of the history nor constraint (12), but it needs EpsNeutral (item 2).

  3. Invariant 2. The paper only sketches the NEW_CONFIG transition. As a state invariant it must also survive acknowledgements and appends at an older epoch after a newer one was configured. We state it for every configured epoch E and its candidate state (base state if started, otherwise the frozen state of the chosen leader) and for slots that may still be accepted (AliveK). It needs extra invariants: frz (once E exists, some member of E − 1 moved past it), m_frozen (a chosen leader is frozen until NEW_CONFIG), cfg_mem (the probing record of each configuration, from which Invariant 5 follows by induction: Inv.inv5). The core argument (Inv.safe) is the paper's case analysis on the new leader's epoch.

  4. Proposition A.2 is replaced by the ghost record vinfo of the vote context at line 12 (slots of L₁, L₂ and the history length) and the origin tag of each log entry (elog), from which (9), (10), (13) follow (Inv.vote_ctx, Inv.edge in Safety.lean).

Modelling choices

  • Network. Channels are lists per ordered pair; any message may be delivered (deliver src dst i), FIFO is the special case i = 0. Messages are delivered at most once, and a handler whose precondition fails leaves its message in the channel. The safety theorem quantifies over all executions, so it covers FIFO and non-FIFO channels.

  • Crashes are not modelled (irrelevant for safety). Suspicions are arbitrary: reconfigure, retry may be called at any time by any process.

  • CS. A reliable shared list of configurations per shard; get_last, get, compare_and_swap as in §3 (the new epoch is recon_epoch = last + 1). CONFIG_CHANGE notifications are modelled by an over-approximating step configChange (adopt any stored configuration of another shard with a higher epoch).

  • Buffered acknowledgements. ACCEPT_ACK and PROBE_ACK messages are buffered at the receiver; the handlers at lines 26, 45, 51 fire on buffer contents (line 51 requires no buffered PROBE_ACK(true) for the current probing; line 45 does not check membership, as in the paper).

  • compute_membership returns any M containing the new leader, with |M| ≥ 2, whose members belong to the shard and either have a buffered PROBE_ACK for this probing or are fresh (members of no stored configuration of the shard).

  • Arrays. txn, payload, vote are always written together and are stored as one array of triples ent; arrays carry a length len so that max{k | phase[k] ≠ start} (line 59) is computable. Slots start at 1 (next starts at 0; the paper uses next ∈ ℤ).

  • Sends to "leader[s] for s ∈ shards(t)" (lines 3, 29, 73) are realized as: each process b with home b ∈ shards(t) that the sender considers the leader (resp. a member) of its own shard receives the message for that shard. This is equivalent because replica groups are disjoint.

  • Clients are not processes: certify is a step of the coordinator; decide(t, d) is appended to the history at the first DECISION(t, d) sent to client(t) (later ones carry the same d, Inv.decided_unique).

  • Specification. Payload sets are Finsets. A legal linearization of h | committed(h) is given by the order of committed transactions (IsLegalLinearization), equivalent to the paper's sequential history. TCS-LL omits constraint (12) (not needed for Lemma A.3; not proved for the protocol). The positions/votes/payloads of TCS-LL are defined from accepted entries (all followers of an epoch acknowledged an entry of that epoch's leader log).

  • Ghost variables (hist, sent, started, elog with origin tags, base, vinfo) are part of the state but never influence transitions.

  • Not formalized: constraint (12), liveness (Theorems 4.2–4.4, App. B), the RDMA protocol (§5, App. C, Theorem C.1).
