Bravo, A. Gotsman. Reconfigurable Atomic Transaction Commit* (arXiv 1906.01365v1). **Model.** * Asynchronous message passing. Every ordered pair of processes has a channel `net src dst` (a list, oldest first). A `deliver src dst i` step removes the `i`-th message of the channel and runs its handler, provided the handler's precondition (`pre:`) holds; otherwise the step is disabled and the message stays in the channel. Reliable FIFO channels are the special case `i = 0` (`Action.IsFIFO`); the safety proof allows any `i` (so it also covers non-FIFO channels), the counterexamples only use `i = 0`. * Crash-stop failures need no modelling for safety: a crashed process simply takes no further steps, and every finite execution with crashes is a finite execution of this system. * The configuration service (CS) is a reliable shared object `cs s` (the list of configurations of shard `s`, indexed by epoch). `CONFIG_CHANGE` messages from the CS are modelled by the step `configChange`, which lets a process adopt any stored configuration of another shard with a higher epoch (this over-approximates the CS's notifications). * Clients are not processes: `certify(t, l)` is a step at the coordinator `p` (any replica process), and the response `decide(t, d)` is recorded in the history when the first `DECISION(t, d)` message is sent to `client(t)` (line 27). * The arrays `txn`, `payload`, `vote` are always written together (lines 10–16, 24, 66), so they are stored as one array `ent` of triples. Arrays carry their length `len` (the largest slot ever written), which makes `max{k | phase[k] ≠ start}` (line 59) computable. * Line 24 writes slot `k` (the figure says `next`, an evident typo: a follower's `next` is never maintained). * Line 56 is printed as `when received NEW_CONFIG(new_epoch, M)`, with the state variable `new_epoch` in the message pattern and an unbound `e` at line 58. By the paper's convention (lines 45, 51; App. C lines 141, 148, whose "checks" are exactly such patterns) this is a match: the handler fires only if the message epoch equals the current `new_epoch`, which is "the check in line 56" cited in the proof of Invariant 3. `Params.fixNewConfig = true` adds the weaker precondition `e ≥ new_epoch`; every step of the paper's protocol (with `=`) is then a step of this model, so the safety theorem covers it. `fixNewConfig = false` is the binder reading (`NEW_CONFIG(e, M)`, no check), which is unsafe (`theorem_4_1_false_printed`). * `compute_membership` (line 48) is any set `M` containing the new leader, consisting of shard members that responded to this probing or fresh processes (never members of a configuration of the shard), as the paper prescribes; additionally `M` has at least two members (`f ≥ 1`: at least one follower). With a single member the coordinator's wait at line 26 is vacuous. Auxiliary (ghost) variables that never influence transitions: `hist` (the TCS history), `sent` (every message ever sent), `started s e` (the leader of epoch `e` of shard `s` has processed `NEW_CONFIG`), `elog s e` (the arrays `txn, payload, vote` of the leader of epoch `e` while it leads `e`; each entry is tagged with the epoch in which it was created), `base s e` (the same at the moment the leader started `e`), and `vinfo s e k` (when the leader of `e` computed the vote of slot `k` at line 12: the slots whose payloads formed `L₁`, `L₂`, and the length of the history). -/
