Reconfigurable Atomic Transaction Commit — PODC 2019

Manuel Bravo, Alexey Gotsman. Reconfigurable Atomic Transaction Commit. PODC 2019. DOI 10.1145/3293611.3331590. Formalized version: arXiv 1906.01365 v1 (extended version with Appendices A–C); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Theorem 4.1 (safety of the message-passing protocol) is false as stated: conditions (3)–(5) do not force fₛ({ε}, l) = commit, and without it two independent shards commit transactions that the global f forbids. Adding this one condition repairs it. With it, the whole safety proof is formalized: Invariants 1–12 in inductive form, Lemma A.1 (TCS-LL), Lemma A.3 and Theorem 4.1. Line 56 of Figure 1 must be read as a match on new_epoch (the paper's own convention); read as a binder, the protocol is unsafe.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Condition (3), §2 — missing fₛ({ε}, l) = commit (Theorem 4.1, Lemma A.3)

Conditions (1), (3)–(5) allow fₛ({ε}, l) = abort. Then a committed transaction that does not touch shard s forbids, through (3), every later transaction on s. The protocol never compares the two, so it commits both (theorem_4_1_false_eps, lemma_A_3_false).

Impact. Theorem 4.1 and Lemma A.3 are false as stated, for every protocol that does not coordinate independent shards. The paper's serializability example satisfies the missing condition. Chockler–Gotsman (DISC 2018), whose proof Lemma A.3 adapts, avoid the issue: their condition (6) projects transactions on shards rather than payloads.

Details

Two shards, t₁ on shard false, t₂ on shard true, payloads some s (non-empty on shard s) and ε = none. fₛ(L, l) = commit iff every x ∈ L is non-ε or l = ε; gₛ(L, l) = commit iff every x ∈ L is non-ε and l ≠ ε; f is defined from fₛ by (3). All of (1), (3), (4), (5) and fₛ(L, ε) = commit hold (LemmaA3Counterexample.matching). The protocol commits t₁ and then t₂ in a failure-free run (Eps.acts). But f({l₁}, l₂) = f_{true}({ε}, l₂|true) = abort, so the history is not correct.

Repair

Assume EpsNeutral (fₛ({ε}, l) = commit): lemma_A_3, theorem_4_1.

🔧 Invariant 8 (Figure 5) — new_epoch ≥ epoch[s₀] is false

A fresh process that joins a configuration receives NEW_STATE without any PROBE, and lines 61–66 do not update new_epoch, so it follows epoch 1 with new_epoch = 0 (Inv8.invariant_8_false, with the line-56 check).

Impact. No proof cites Invariant 8; the safety proof goes through without it (theorem_4_1), using new_epoch ≤ epoch[s₀] at leaders and followers (Inv.loc_newEpoch).

✏️ Slips

  • Figure 1, lines 56–58: the handler head NEW_CONFIG(new_epoch, M) puts the state variable new_epoch in the message pattern, and line 58 uses an unbound e. By the paper's convention (lines 45, 51; App. C cites "the checks in lines 141 and 148", which are such patterns) this is the check e = new_epoch that the proof of Invariant 3 cites as "the check in line 56". Read as a binder NEW_CONFIG(e, M) with no check, the protocol is unsafe: a delayed NEW_CONFIG(1) drags the leader of epoch 2 back to epoch 1, and two conflicting transactions commit in a FIFO, crash-free run of 32 steps (theorem_4_1_false_printed, Line56.epoch_decreases). We model the check as e ≥ new_epoch (Params.fixNewConfig = true), which allows more executions than e = new_epoch, so theorem_4_1 covers the paper's protocol (Line56.fixed_blocks).

  • Figure 1, line 24 writes the accepted entry at slot next; a follower never maintains next, and every proof uses slot k. We model [k].

  • Degenerate configurations. With a single member (f = 0), the wait of line 26 ("an ACCEPT_ACK from every follower") is vacuous for any kₛ, dₛ: a coordinator commits two conflicting transactions without preparing them (Single.single_member_unsafe). A transaction with shards(t) = ∅ can likewise be decided without being certified. We assume configurations with at least one follower (f ≥ 1) and transactions with at least one shard.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma A.1, constraint

🔧 Lemma A.1, constraint (11) — the witness Pₛ[t]

The proof sets Pₛ[t] = P \ {t' | posₛ[t'] undefined}, but a t' in the prepared set P of the vote may later be accepted at a higher slot, or with an abort vote. This happens when t' was lost in a reconfiguration and re-prepared by the next leader, and then (11) fails for that witness.

Impact. Statement true. Filtering Pₛ[t] by (11) works, since gₛ is monotone; repaired (Inv.tcsll).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous message passing. Each ordered pair of processes has a channel, and any message of a channel may be delivered when its handler's precondition holds. FIFO channels are the special case "deliver the head" (Action.IsFIFO): the safety proof covers all deliveries, the counterexamples only use FIFO ones. Messages are delivered at most once.

  • Crash-stop failures need no modelling for safety (a crashed process takes no more steps). Failure suspicion is arbitrary: any process may call reconfigure or retry at any time.

  • Shards with disjoint replica groups (home), epoch-indexed configurations at a reliable CS (compare_and_swap succeeds iff the last epoch is recon_epoch − 1). CONFIG_CHANGE is modelled as adopting any stored configuration of another shard with a higher epoch.

  • compute_membership: the new leader plus processes that replied to this probing or are fresh (never members of a stored configuration of the shard), at least two members.

  • Line 56 checks e ≥ new_epoch (Params.fixNewConfig = true), a relaxation of the paper's match e = new_epoch; fixNewConfig = false is the unchecked binder reading.

  • Clients are not processes: certify is a step of the coordinator, and decide(t, d) is recorded at the first DECISION(t, d) sent to the client.

  • The TCS specification (§2) is formalized literally, with linearizations of h | committed(h) given by the order of the committed transactions. TCS-LL is Figure 6 without (12).
