import Papers.PODC2019.AnonymousMutex.Alg1 /-! # Theorem 1: Algorithm 1 satisfies mutual exclusion Aghazadeh, Imbs, Raynal, Taubenfeld, Woelfel, PODC 2019 (arXiv:1810.03723), Section 3.2. **Theorem 1.** For `n` processes and `m > 1` registers with `m ∈ M(n)`, Algorithm 1 satisfies mutual exclusion. We prove it under the weaker hypothesis `n ≤ m` (`theorem_1`), which follows from `m > 1 ∧ m ∈ M(n)` (`theorem_1_of_M`). ## Proof A process is in a *CS-state* if its program counter is `retLock` or `cs` (it has seen a snapshot in which all `m` registers hold its identifier and has not started `unlock()` since). For a process `p` in a CS-state, a process `q ≠ p` is *dangerous* if its next step is a write (line 6 or the `⊥`-write of `shrink()`) into a physical register currently holding `p`'s identifier. The invariant (`Good`) is: * at most one process is in a CS-state; * if `p` is in a CS-state, then `#{registers not holding p} + #{dangerous processes} ≤ n - 1`. When `p` enters its CS-state, all registers hold `p` and there are at most `n - 1` other processes. Afterwards, only a dangerous process can overwrite a register holding `p` (and it then stops being dangerous), and a process never becomes dangerous: a write of line 6 is prepared from a snapshot entry `⊥`, and a `⊥`-write of `shrink()` from a read of the process's own identifier. Hence while `p` is in a CS-state, at most `n - 1 < m` registers do not hold `p`, so no other process can see a snapshot full of its own identifier, and no second process enters a CS-state. The invariant `RegInv` relates the client region (`regionOf`) of every process to its program counter; in particular a process in the critical section is in a CS-state. Neither invariant needs client well-formedness, so mutual exclusion even holds for all executions. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false open Cslib.SharedMemory namespace AnonymousMutex.Alg1
