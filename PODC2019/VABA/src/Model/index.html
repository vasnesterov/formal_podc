import Mathlib import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.Byzantine.Certificate /-! # VABA (Abraham, Malkhi, Spiegelman, PODC 2019): the protocol as a transition system We model Algorithms 1–7 of *Asymptotically Optimal Validated Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement* (arXiv:1811.01332v1) as an interleaving transition system. ## Model * `n = |P|` parties; the adversary corrupts parties **adaptively** (action `corrupt`), at most `f` of them in total. A corrupted party stops following the protocol and its local state is frozen. * **Asynchrony.** Every honest step is an action the adversary may schedule in any order whenever its guard holds; there is no notion of time. * **Ideal threshold signatures.** `tok p st` means that party `p` has produced a signature share on the statement `st`. A threshold signature (certificate, "QC") on `st` exists iff at least `Q` parties produced shares (`QC`); the paper uses `Q = 2f + 1` with `n = 3f + 1`, and we keep `Q` as a parameter. Corrupted parties may sign anything (`byzSign`), honest ones only what the protocol prescribes. Certificates cannot be forged, but anybody may forward an existing one. * **Ideal common coin.** The leader of view `j` is `c j` for a coin sequence `c : ℕ → P`. It is *revealed* (`rev j`) when the first honest party invokes `elect` for view `j` (with the `f` shares of the corrupted parties, one honest share suffices to compute the coin); an honest party learns it (`lead`) when its `elect` returns, which needs the coin to be revealed. * **f+1-Provable-Broadcast.** An honest party may deliver, in its current view and before abandoning it, a message of proposer `k` at stage `s ∈ {1,2,3,4}` if it passes the external validation (`ex-sbc-validation` / `ex-bc-validation`), the party has not delivered in that instance yet, and the message may have been sent (`SentBy`): a corrupted proposer may send anything, an honest one only its proposal of that view (stage `s ≥ 2` needs a stage-`(s-1)` certificate on it anyway). Delivering = sending the signature share. We do not model the proposer's collection of shares; the `done` messages are abstracted by "a stage-4 certificate exists" (`completed`), which over-approximates when they can be sent. * **Keys** `⟨r, σ⟩`: `σ` is a stage-1 certificate of the leader of view `r`; `r = 0` is the empty key of view 1. (Algorithm 6 line 4 says `j ≠ 1`; it must be the key view `≠ 0`, see `differencies.md`.) Since certificates are ideal, a key is represented by its view `r` only. * **View change.** View-change messages of view `j` are processed once the receiver knows the leader of view `j` (buffered until then). A message of an honest sender carries its deliveries `D_key, D_lock, D_commit` in the leader's broadcast (frozen, since the sender has abandoned it). A corrupted sender can send any combination of existing certificates; this is `recvQC`, which may also be used at any time for forwarded certificates. * Ghost fields (not used by the protocol's decisions): `kv` (the key view used when delivering a stage-1 message), `exitR` (the senders of the view-change messages that let a party leave a view), `prop` (the key proposed at the start of each
