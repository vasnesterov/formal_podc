import Papers.PODC2019.AutomaticSpeedup.Problem /-! # The speedup theorem for abstract view systems (Brandt, PODC 2019, §4.1–4.2) The proof of Theorem 1 only uses a few structural facts about neighbourhoods. We isolate them in `ViewSystem`: a class of port-numbered graphs (`Graph`, `Node`, `deg`, `nbr`, `rport`) together with * `nv G v` — the radius-`t` view `N^t(v)` of a node, * `ev G v i` — the radius-`t` view `N^t(e)` of the edge `e` behind port `i` of `v`, *seen from `v`* (i.e. with `v` marked as the endpoint we are interested in), * `pv G v` — the radius-`(t-1)` view `N^{t-1}(v)`, and the facts that `N^t(v)` determines each `N^t(e)` (`evOf`), that `N^t(e)` determines `N^{t-1}(v)` and the port of `e` at `v` (`pvOf`, `portOf`), that the view of `e` seen from the other endpoint is a function of the view seen from `v` (`flip`), and that views determine degrees. An algorithm running in time `t` is a function of `nv` (`SolvesN`), a "half-round" algorithm is a function of `ev` (`SolvesE`), and a `(t-1)`-round algorithm a function of `pv` (`SolvesP`). `Ind1`/`Ind2` are the two clauses of `t`-independence (§3). The concrete instance (port-numbering model, full-information views) is in `PortNumbering.lean`. Main results: * `solvesE_toEdge`, `solvesP_toPrev`: the forward half steps (proof of Theorem 1, (1) ⇒ (2)), * `solvesE_ofPrev`, `solvesN_ofEdge`: the backward half steps ((2) ⇒ (1)). The first one needs `EdgeAsym`: the two endpoints of every edge must have different views of the edge. The paper does not assume this, and without it the statement is false (`Counterexample.lean`). * `solvesE_max`, `solvesP_max`: the maximality simplification (Theorem 2). * `speedup_forward`,
