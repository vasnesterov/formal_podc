import Papers.PODC2022.WearLeveling.Counter /-! # Theorem 4.1: the counter of Figure 1 is strictly linearizable Invariant-based proof in the ideal model (no wear-out). The ghost state is the linearization `L` (operations in the order of their successful CAS at line 5) and the number `m` of its operations whose CAS is already persistent. The global invariant (`GInv`): * the cache holds `stair |L|`, persistent memory and the wear counters hold `stair m`; * `m ≤ |L| ≤ m + 1`: at most one successful CAS is not yet persistent, its cache line is the only dirty one; * operations of `L` at positions `≥ m` have not responded. The local invariants (`LocInv`) are the facts the helping flushes provide: a thread at line 5 with `iter, index` knows that persistent memory already holds at least `iter * k + index` increments (lines 2/13 and 9 flushed the words it passed). The key consequence: when a CAS succeeds, the persistent memory is up to date (`m = |L|`) and the CAS is the `|L|`-th increment with `|L| = iter * k + index` (the crucial claim of the proof of Theorem 4.1, with `⌊ℓ / k⌋` and `ℓ mod k` in place of the paper's `⌊ℓ / 2^k⌋`, `ℓ mod 2^k`).
