import Papers.PODC2022.WearLeveling.Counter /-! # Theorem 4.2: the counter of Figure 1 is lock-free Proof (following the paper's sketch): suppose that after some time there are no crashes and no responses, but running threads keep taking steps. 1. After a successful CAS a thread is at line 6 or 7 until it responds; so after the last response every thread performs at most one successful CAS. With finitely many threads, there is a suffix without successful CAS, in which the cache is constant (`c`). 2. With a constant cache, the potential `Φ = Σ_p φ_c(p)` strictly decreases at every step of a running thread (`μ`): a thread either reaches `CAS(A[k-1], c[k-1], …)` with `iter = c[k-1]` read at line 1/12, which succeeds, or fails at most `k` times with a stale `iter` and then rereads `A[k-1]`. A contradiction. The argument does not depend on persistent memory, so lock-freedom holds in every wear model.
