Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Internet Computer Consensus — PODC 2022.

Paper: Camenisch, Drijvers, Hanke, Pignolet, Shoup, Williams. Internet Computer Consensus. PODC 2022 (DOI 10.1145/3519270.3538430). We used the PODC version. It states Protocol ICC0 (Figures 1–2) and the three lemmas of Section 4 but contains no proofs; those are in the full version (IACR ePrint 2021/632), which was not available to us. All proofs here were reconstructed from the protocol description.

Statement-level issues

  1. Liveness lemma: "each honest party will have Q's round-k block in its pool as a finalized block" is false as written; the intended "committed" reading holds (🔧). "Finalized for P" is defined in Section 3.4 as "valid, and a finalization (combined S_final signature) is in P's pool". Figure 2 only combines finalization shares of a round-k block when k > kmax. After the two synchronous instants of hypothesis (iv) the network is asynchronous, so the adversary may deliver the round-k finalization shares to each honest party only after the round-(k+1) ones. Then every honest party first executes clause (ii) for the round-(k+1) block, sets kmax = k + 1 and outputs both blocks. It never combines the round-k shares, so no honest party ever broadcasts a finalization of B_Q, and corrupt parties need not either. When all round-k messages are delivered, every honest pool contains n − t finalization shares of B_Q, but no finalization. This is an informal counterexample: it satisfies all hypotheses (i)–(v), but it is not machine-checked; a concrete run needs 3 rounds and 4 parties. Its fix is local, and the paper itself shows the intent: Property P3 ("the block … will be finalized") and the discussion after it ("each honest party will output this command in round k"). We prove the intended version (liveness): every honest party has B_Q valid with a full set of n − t finalization shares in its pool (so it can form the finalization), has kmax ≥ k, and has committed B_Q (the chain ending at B_Q is a prefix of its output). A reader who takes "finalized" loosely as "committed" would see no error in the lemma.

No other statement problem was found. Lemma (Safety) and Lemma (Deadlock freeness) hold as stated, under the reading of "will have finished" described below. Their proofs are ours, since the paper gives none.

Model and idealization (not statement issues)

  • Cryptography is idealized.

    • The hash is injective: a block contains its parent block itself (Block.mk k α parent payload).

    • Signatures are unforgeable. The adversary can make an honest party receive: any block; an authenticator of a corrupt proposer, or one already broadcast by an honest party; shares of corrupt parties, or shares already broadcast; a notarization/finalization of B iff at least n − t parties signed it, corrupt parties counting as having signed everything (Avail, NotarCert, FinalCert).

    • This is the ideal functionality of a (t, n − t, n) threshold scheme. The paper's game-based definition (the adversary corrupts exactly t parties; a valid signature needs ≥ h − t honest shares) gives the same safety argument; see the comment on approach (ii) in Section 2.3.

  • Random beacon. The beacon signature is unique, so the permutation of round k is a fixed function rank k : P → ℕ. We only assume it is injective for each k, which is more general than a permutation onto {0,…,n−1}. Unpredictability of the beacon is irrelevant for P1–P3: P3 conditions on an honest leader. The beacon itself is modelled by its shares: a party starts round k after receiving t + 1 round-k shares, and immediately broadcasts its round-(k+1) share, as in Figure 1.

  • Corruption. Static set F, #F ≤ t, 3t < n. Corrupt parties take no explicit steps; their behaviour is everything the adversary can inject (above). This is the full Byzantine power under idealized cryptography.

  • Network. Every honest broadcast goes to the global set sent and into the sender's own pool (immediate self-delivery). The adversary delivers any available message to any honest pool at any time, in any order. This is the paper's insecure broadcast with adversarial scheduling. Self-delivery is immediate, whereas the paper only says "the pool contains messages received from all parties, including itself". We need it so that a party that finishes a round by combining shares keeps the combined notarization ("there will always be such a block", Section 3.5).

  • Time. Discrete global time (ℕ), the same clock for every party (the paper's clock()), delays Δprop, Δntry : ℕ → ℕ. The paper uses ℝ≥0 and local clocks. Its liveness lemma implicitly assumes local clocks run at real-time rate, which a global clock makes explicit.

  • "Wait for" semantics. In the paper a clause executes as soon as its condition holds, choosing arbitrarily among enabled clauses. We formalize it in two ways:

    • Safety holds for all interleavings (Step, time advances arbitrarily). This is stronger than needed.

    • For liveness, runs use UStep: time advances only when no honest party has an enabled clause.

    • In the deadlock lemma "will have finished" is read as "in a state where no honest party (in a round ≤ k) has an enabled Tree Building clause" (TBSettled).

  • Payloads. getPayload is an arbitrary choice: any payload may be proposed. Application-specific validity is omitted. Outputs are blocks rather than their payloads. The deadlock and liveness theorems assume Nonempty V: with an empty payload type no block can be proposed.

  • Initialization. All parties start at time 0 in round 1, waiting for the round-1 beacon. Their round-1 beacon shares are broadcast initially. This covers "all honest parties have been initiated at time T".

Formulation of the three lemmas

  • Safety (safety): for any reachable state, honest p, q, if B is finalized for p and B' (same round ≥ 1) is notarized for q, then B' = B. Stronger form safety_cert: at the level of signatures (a finalization of B and a notarization of B' can both exist). "Can be notarized" in the paper ranges over the future too; since sets only grow along an execution, a single state covers it.

  • Output consistency (output_prefix): the outputs of two honest parties are prefixes of one another. This is the argument after Property P2 in Section 3.3, which the paper presents informally; it is an addition.

  • Deadlock freeness (deadlock_freeness), for a reachable state and round k:

    • hypotheses: every honest message of a round ≤ k is in every honest pool; every honest party that has started a round ≤ k has all proposal and notarization delays elapsed; no honest party in a round ≤ k has an enabled Tree Building clause;

    • conclusion: every honest party is past round k. Leaving a round is only possible through clause (a), i.e. with a valid round-k block that is notarized or has n − t notarization shares, whose notarization the party then broadcasts; Inv.finished records that a notarization of some round-k block has been broadcast. The Lean conclusion itself is only "past round k".

    Message rounds are Msg.round: a beacon share of round j counts as a round-j message.

  • Liveness (liveness). Differences from the paper:

    • k ≥ 1 instead of k > 1 (more general);

    • δ ≥ 1 (in discrete time a zero delay bound makes hypothesis (iv) contradictory);

    • Δntry non-decreasing, as required in Section 3.5;

    • (ii) is formalized as "no honest party has entered round k before time T, and some has by time T";

    • δ-synchrony at T₀ is "every message broadcast by an honest party at a state of time ≤ T₀ is in every honest pool at every state of time ≥ T₀ + δ" (SyncAt);

    • "when all round-k messages from honest parties have been delivered" is formalized as a state j where every honest round-k message is in every honest pool, every honest party is settled, and time is ≥ T + 2δ + Δprop(0) and ≥ T + δ + Δntry(0). The time bounds make sure every round-k message that will ever be sent has been sent (the notarization delay for the leader's block has elapsed); the paper leaves this implicit.

    • Conclusion: see item 1.

Proof notes (no gaps in the paper to report, since it has no proofs)

  • Safety: an honest party casts a finalization share for B only if it cast no notarization share for another block of that round, and it casts no shares for round k after leaving it (Inv.fshare). Two n − t certificates share an honest signer since 3t < n (Inv.final_notar_eq).

  • Deadlock freeness:

    • Take an honest party in the smallest round j. If someone finished round j, its notarization arrives, and so does the chain below it, because every notarized block was echoed by an honest notarizer (Inv.share, Inv.valid_of_auth).

    • Otherwise every honest party has proposed and has cast a share for the valid, non-disqualified block of minimal rank. A disqualified rank always has two echoed blocks (Inv.D_spec), hence belongs to a corrupt party (Inv.D_corrupt). So all honest parties pick the same minimal rank and block (Stuck.rmin_le_rmin), and its n − |F| ≥ n − t shares finish the round (Stuck.false).

    • The beacon wait is handled with t + 1 ≤ n − t honest shares.

  • Liveness:

    • Catch-up: everything the first party in round k used was broadcast by time T, so all honest parties are in round k before T + δ (Inv.advance, LiveHyp.entered_of_ge). This needs t + 1 honest beacon shares of each round ≤ k, which the notarizers of round k − 1 provide (Inv.beacon_cert).

    • The leader proposes before T + δ + Δprop(0) (LiveHyp.proposal); its block is valid everywhere from T + 2δ + Δprop(0) ≤ T + Δntry(1) on (LiveHyp.valid_leader). Hence no honest party ever casts a share for another round-k block (LiveHyp.share_eq_leader), and every honest party leaves round k with N ⊆ {B_Q}, casting a finalization share for B_Q (LiveHyp.fshare_leader).

Typos

  • Section 3.5, prose for clause (c): "(iii) at least Δntry(t) time units have passed" should be Δntry(r) (Figure 1 has Δntry(r)).
