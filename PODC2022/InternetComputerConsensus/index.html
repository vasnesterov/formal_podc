Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Safety (Property P2) and Deadlock freeness (P1) of ICC0 hold as stated for t < n/3, and so does the consistency of the honest outputs. Liveness under partial synchrony (P3) holds in the intended sense, that the honest leader's block is committed by every honest party, but read literally its conclusion ("B_Q is in every honest pool as a finalized block") can fail because of Figure 2's kmax skipping, and rewording it keeps the intent. Protocols ICC1 and ICC2, the ones meant for large blocks, were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Liveness lemma (conclusion "as a finalized block")

The lemma promises that every honest party has Q's block B_Q "in its pool as a finalized block", but if the adversary delivers the round-(k+1) finalization shares before the round-k ones, every honest party skips B_Q via Figure 2's kmax and never forms a finalization of it (counterexample in words only).

Impact. The literal conclusion can fail, but what P3 and its discussion are after still holds: every honest party outputs B_Q.

Details

By Section 3.4, "finalized" means that a combined finalization is in the pool. But Figure 2 combines round-k finalization shares only if k > kmax. After the two synchronous instants of hypothesis (iv) the network is asynchronous. The adversary can deliver the round-(k+1) finalization shares before the round-k ones to every honest party. Then every honest party finalizes the round-(k+1) block first, jumps to kmax = k + 1, and outputs B_Q without ever forming a finalization of it. Nobody honest broadcasts one, so the literal conclusion fails. This counterexample is argued in words, not in Lean.

Repair

State the conclusion as commitment: every honest party holds n − t finalization shares of B_Q, has kmax ≥ k, and has committed B_Q: the chain ending at B_Q is a prefix of its output (liveness).

Typos

  • Section 3.5 describes clause (c) with "at least Δntry(t) time units"; it should be Δntry(r), as in Figure 1.

Model.

  • Asynchronous interleaving transition system for ICC0 (Model.lean). Parties run Figure 1 and Figure 2 concurrently, one clause per step, with arbitrary choice among enabled clauses.

  • Byzantine faults: a static corrupt set F with #F ≤ t and 3t < n. Corrupt parties are represented by everything the adversary can inject into honest pools.

  • Cryptography is idealized:

    • collision resistance: a block contains its parent;

    • authenticators and shares cannot be forged;

    • a notarization or finalization exists iff n − t parties signed, corrupt parties signing anything (the ideal (t, n−t, n) threshold signature, CSLib Byzantine.Certified);

    • the unique random beacon gives a fixed rank function rank k, assumed injective for each round.

  • Network: insecure broadcast. The adversary delivers any available message to any honest party at any time; self-delivery is immediate. No randomness is involved: the leader's honesty is a hypothesis.

  • Time is discrete, with one global clock read by all parties. Safety is proved for all interleavings and clock behaviours. For liveness, runs follow the paper's "wait for" semantics: time advances only when no honest party has an enabled clause (UStep), and δ-synchrony at a time T₀ is a property of the run (SyncAt).

  • Stronger or weaker than the paper: ranks only injective (more general); k ≥ 1 instead of k > 1; δ ≥ 1; arbitrary payloads. The deadlock lemma's "will have finished" is read as "in a state where no honest party in a round ≤ k has an enabled Tree Building clause".

  • Inv.valid_of_auth: every echoed block is valid once the honest messages are delivered.

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: NonVacuity.liveHyp_nonvacuous builds an explicit infinite run (one honest party, k = 2, T = 1, δ = 1) that satisfies all hypotheses of liveness, including the final-state conditions, and applies the theorem to it. With one party this is a degenerate instance, so it only shows the hypotheses are consistent. The model reuses CSLib's threshold certificates (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/Byzantine/Certificate.lean) and quorum arithmetic. Everything else is paper-local.
