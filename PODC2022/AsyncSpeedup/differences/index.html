Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Asynchronous Speedup Theorem — PODC 2022.

Paper: Fraigniaud, Paz, Rajsbaum, A Speedup Theorem for Asynchronous Computation with Applications to Consensus and Approximate Agreement, PODC 2022; formalized from arXiv:2206.05356v2 (full version, which contains the proofs of Claims 3, 4 and Section 5.3 that the proceedings version omits).

Classification: [F] false statement, [D] false only in a degenerate case, [G] proof gap (statement true), [U] under-specified definition / implicit assumption, [T] typo, [M] modelling choice of the formalization (no claim about the paper).

Model and definitions

  1. [M] Combinatorial topology replaced by executions. Complexes, simplicial maps and the carrier map Ξ are not formalized. A one-round model is a set of patterns Model.Pat I a (who sees whom and what the box returns, for a participating set I and box inputs a). Views are full-information views (View, step). A t-round algorithm is a pair (box-input function, decision function), i.e. the decision map f of the paper. "f : P^(t) → O agrees with Δ" becomes Solves: for every input simplex σ = (I, x) and every execution in which exactly I participates, the decisions of I form an element of Δ(σ). Executions are unique given the patterns, so simpliciality is automatic.

  2. [U] Meaning of "solvable" when Δ is not a carrier map. The paper explicitly drops the carrier-map requirement on Δ (Section 2.2) but keeps the complex-based definition f(P^(t)(σ)) ⊆ Δ(σ). If P^(t)(σ) is read literally as a complex containing P^(t)(σ') for faces σ' ⊂ σ, then an execution of two processes would also have to respect Δ of every 3-process superset. Under that reading the liberal approximate agreement (Definition 4) would be no different from approximate agreement and the 2-process clause of consensus3 would be void. We use the standard reading: participation is exact (Solves), and crashes during a round are faces of failure-free executions (item 3). All results of the paper hold in this reading.

  3. [U] Implicit model assumptions of Theorems 1/2. The proof of Theorem 1 uses P^(1)(τ') ⊆ P^(1)(τ) for faces τ' ⊂ τ (a round of a subset of processes extends to a round of all of them). This is explicit here as Model.Extendable. It also uses that every process sees itself and only participants (Model.WellFormed), besides solo executions (Model.AllowsSolo). All are proved for IIS, iterated snapshot, IIS+test&set and IIS+binary consensus (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/IteratedModel/Patterns.lean).

  4. [U] Black boxes (Section 4). The "consistent box" remark is formalized by letting patterns depend only on the participating set and the box inputs. The proof of Theorem 2 also needs that a process running solo gets an answer determined by its own box input ("b_i = B_t(a_i) ... whenever process i is running solo"). This is the explicit hypothesis Model.SoloDet b₀. It holds for test&set (the solo caller wins) and binary consensus (the solo caller gets its proposal). We did not determine whether Theorem 2 can fail for boxes with nondeterministic solo answers.

  5. [U] Placement of the box call under immediate snapshot. Algorithm 2 calls the box between the write and the collect, while an immediate snapshot is an atomic write+snapshot. We model the test&set winner (resp. the binary consensus proposal that wins) as a process of the first concurrency class of the round, i.e. a process seen by every participant. This is exactly the set of first callers compatible with write–call–read. It gives 7 vertices per colour in Figure 5 and makes the solo caller win. Only the executions ϱ_{i,j,k}, ϱ_{j,i,k} and the solo answer are used by the lower-bound proofs.

  6. [M] Tasks as relations. A task is (Inp, Δ); the output complex O and the vertex set V(O') = V(O) of Definition 2 are not represented (they play no role in solvability). Equalities of tasks ("CL_M(Π) = Π", Claims 2–4, 6) are stated as equalities of Δ(σ). Output vectors are total functions P → Y, and only their values on id(σ) matter.

  7. [M] Local task (Definition 1). Its inputs are the faces (J, τ) with ∅ ≠ J ⊆ id(σ). A single participant must output its input; for |J| > 1 the outputs must coincide on J with some element of Δ(σ) (= proj_J Δ(σ)). Hard-coding σ, τ, ρ into the one-round algorithm is allowed, as in the paper ("one can think of τ, σ, ρ and f as hard-coded").

  8. [M] "Solvable in at most one round" (Definition 2) is formalized as "solvable in one round"; these are equivalent (Solvable.succ).

  9. [M] Approximate agreement on the grid. Values are scaled by m: inputs/outputs in {0,…,m} ⊆ ℤ, precision ε = e/m with e ∈ ℤ. Outputs are automatically on the grid. Binary consensus uses Bool inputs and the (equivalent) validity + agreement specification. The participants are restricted to a set S (S = univ for the paper's tasks, smaller S in Section 5.3).

  10. [M] Algorithms in Section 5.3 "whose call to binary consensus depends only on the ID and the round" = IdOnly (box input γ r i); the closure "w.r.t. β" is closureβ.

Statements

  1. [U] Domain of ε. Definition 3 requires ε ∈ (0, 1], but the proofs of Corollary 3 and Theorems 3, 4 apply Claims 2–4, 6 to 3^k ε and 2^k ε, which may exceed 1. All claims are proved for every e ≥ 0 (inclusions Δ' ⊆ ... for every e).

  2. [M] Claim 2 holds for any n in the direction used. The inclusion Δ'_ε ⊆ Δ_{3ε} holds for every number of processes (closure_aa_subset); the equality is proved for two processes (claim2).

  3. [G] Claim 6 and the iteration in Theorem 4. Claim 6 assumes |S| ≥ 5. The iteration in the proof of Theorem 4 applies it to every S_k, k ≤ t − 2, but checks only |S_{t−1}| ≥ 3. The missing check holds (|S_k| ≥ n/2^k > 4). In fact the inclusion of Claim 6 needs no size hypothesis (for |S'| ≤ 2 it is trivial), and we iterate with the invariant |S| ≤ 2^(t+1) ∨ 1 ≤ 2^t ε (theorem4_aux). This yields exactly the paper's bound min(⌈log₂ n⌉ − 1, ⌈log₂ 1/ε⌉).

  4. [U] Scope of the binary-consensus result (abstract, Section 1.2, Conclusion). The abstract says test&set and binary consensus "are not more powerful as far as helping solving approximate agreement faster is concerned". The conclusion says binary consensus is "not useful ... (at least when n ≥ 1/ε)". Theorem 4, the only result about binary consensus, covers only algorithms whose consensus inputs depend on ids and rounds. Its bound ⌈log₂ n⌉ − 1 is one round below the IIS bound ⌈log₂ 1/ε⌉ whenever 1/ε ≤ n ≤ 2^⌈log₂ 1/ε⌉ (e.g. n = 4, ε = 1/4: bound 1 versus 2); it reaches the IIS bound only for n > 2^⌈log₂ 1/ε⌉ (e.g. n ≥ 2/ε). The claims are informal, and only this weaker form is proved (⚠️). Theorem 4 itself is proved as stated.

  5. [T] Proof of Claim 4. It says "the simplices of P^(1)(σ) considered in the proof of Corollary 2". They are simplices of P^(1)(τ), the protocol complex of the local task (as in Corollary 2's own proof, which also writes P^(1)(σ) and f : P^(1) → O).

  6. [T] Proof of Theorem 1 (proceedings version). "f(𝜚') ∈ Δ(σ). Since id(f(ρ')) = id(ϱ')" mixes ρ' and ϱ'.

  7. [M] Corollary 2 is proved via the paper's relaxed task (consensus3), including the fixed-point equality CL(Π) = Π (consensus3_fixedPoint), for any n ≥ 3.

  8. [M] Logarithmic forms. Corollary 3 and Theorems 3, 4 are proved in the scaled form (m ≤ 3^t e, m ≤ 2^t e, n ≤ 2^(t+1) ∨ m ≤ 2^t e) and then converted to the paper's ⌈log_b (1/ε)⌉ ≤ t with ε = e/m and Real.logb (assuming e, m > 0).

Additions (not claimed formally in the paper)

  1. Tightness (Remark after Corollary 3). We prove the upper bounds: ε-agreement is solvable in IIS in t rounds when 1 ≤ 2^t ε for any n (aa_solvable_many), and when 1 ≤ 3^t ε for two processes (aa_solvable_two). This uses a composition lemma for uniform one-round reductions (solvable_succ_of_reduction) and Equations (3) and (2). Equation (2) is used in a sign-symmetric form (clamp) that equals Equation (2) when y₁ ≤ y₂ and its mirror image otherwise.

  2. Figure 4. Two-process consensus is solvable in one round of IIS + test&set (twoConsensus_iisTS). This shows that n ≥ 3 is needed in Corollary 2 and that the model is non-vacuous.

Not formalized

  • The topological pictures (Figures 1–3, 5–7) and the iterated collect model (Theorem 1 is proved for an abstract model; instances: IIS, iterated snapshot, IIS + test&set, IIS + binary consensus).

  • The "essentially tight" remark for Theorem 4 (the ⌈log₂ n⌉-round consensus algorithm with binary consensus objects).
