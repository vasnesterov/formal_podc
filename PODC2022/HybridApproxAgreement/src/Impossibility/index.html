Partition the parties into `S₋`, `S₊` (non-empty, at most `max tₛ tₐ` each) and `Sₐ` (at most `tₐ`). * `R₁` (synchronous): `S₋` is Byzantine and silent, everybody else has input `x₁`. By termination and validity some `p ∈ S₊` outputs `x₁`, say by time `τ₁`. * `R₂` (synchronous): `S₊` is Byzantine and silent, everybody else has input `x₀`. Some `q ∈ S₋` outputs `x₀` by time `τ₂`. * `R₃` (asynchronous, `gst = T + 1`, `T = max τ₁ τ₂`; the library run `Run.merge` of `R₁` and `R₂`): `Sₐ` is Byzantine; towards `S₊` it replays its messages of `R₁`, towards `S₋` those of `R₂` (until `T`); messages between `S₊` and `S₋` are delayed past `T`. Then `S₊` cannot distinguish `R₃` from `R₁` and `S₋` cannot distinguish it from `R₂` up to `T`, so `p` outputs `x₁` and `q` outputs `x₀` in `R₃`, contradicting `ε`-agreement as `|x₁ - x₀| > ε`. The replayed messages respect unforgeability (`Run.tokens_replay`): `Sₐ` is honest in `R₁`/`R₂`, `S₊` behaves identically, and the silent parties emit nothing. So the impossibility holds **with signatures (any PKI)**, as claimed. If `tₐ > tₛ` the groups `S₋`, `S₊` may be larger than `tₛ`; then `R₁`, `R₂` are used as asynchronous runs (a synchronous run is in particular asynchronous). The proof needs **`n ≥ 2`**: for `n = 1` (where `2 tₛ + tₐ ≥ n` can hold) hybrid AA is trivially solvable (`hybridAASolvable_of_card_le_one`).
