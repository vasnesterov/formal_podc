When Is Recoverable Consensus Harder Than Consensus? — PODC 2022

Carole Delporte-Gallet, Panagiota Fatourou, Hugues Fauconnier, Eric Ruppert. When Is Recoverable Consensus Harder Than Consensus? PODC 2022. DOI 10.1145/3519270.3538418. Formalized version: arXiv 2205.14213 v1 (with appendices).

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All results of Section 3 hold and are formalized with the paper's proofs: n-recording suffices for recoverable consensus (Theorem 8, with the algorithm of Figure 2 and the tournament of Proposition 30), (n-1)-recording is necessary (Theorem 14), Theorem 16, Propositions 18, 19, 21, Corollaries 17, 20 and Theorem 22. Corollary 20 rests on an unproved claim (cons(T_n) ≤ n), which we prove; two "commute or overwrite" steps in the proofs of Propositions 19 and 21 are false as stated and a step of Lemma 11 is circular, but all are easily repaired. Section 4 and simultaneous crashes were not checked, and sufficiency is proved for binary consensus only.

Proof gaps in true statements (4)Corollary 20 · Proposition 19 · Proposition 21 · Lemma 11

🔧 Corollary 20 (cons(T_n) = n)

The paper derives rcons(T_n) < cons(T_n) = n "from Proposition 19 combined with Theorems 3 and 14", but cons(T_n) ≤ n needs that T_n is not (n+1)-discerning, which is nowhere shown.

Impact. Statement true; we prove the missing claim (TT.not_isDiscerning, corollary20).

Details

Proposition 19 gives cons(T_n) ≥ n (Theorem 3) and rcons(T_n) ≤ n - 1 (Theorem 14). The missing claim is true: with n + 1 processes one of the teams using op_A or op_B exceeds its counter (⌊n/2⌋ + 1 resp. ⌈n/2⌉ + 1 processes), so a schedule of that team returns the object to (⊥,0,0), after which a process of the other team cannot tell who went first; values other than (⊥,0,0) and read operations are excluded as in the paper.

🔧 Proposition 19 ("commute or overwrite" step)

The proof's claim "if q₀ ≠ (⊥,0,0) then any pair of operations either commute or overwrite" is false for q₀ = (w, ⌈n/2⌉−1, ⌊n/2⌋−1).

Impact. Statement true; the conclusion survives (TT.pair_of_ne_bot, TT.not_isRecording).

Details

From that q₀ both op_A and op_B reset the object to (⊥,0,0), and then neither commute (op_A op_B ends in (B,0,0), op_B op_A in (A,0,0)) nor overwrite each other. The conclusion survives because the two single operations reach the same value, which already violates condition 1 of 2-recording.

🔧 Proposition 21 (why q₀ must be (B,0))

The argument that q₀ must be (B,0) uses only "operations of opposite teams cannot commute or overwrite", but from q₀ = (w, n−1) the operations op_A and op_B neither commute nor overwrite each other and both lead to (B,0).

Impact. Statement true; this case is excluded by a third process k (SS.pair_disc, SS.not_isDiscerning).

Details

The schedules op_A op_k and op_B op_k give k the same response and value. (A Read assigned to a process is excluded by the paper's overwrite argument: any operation overwrites Read.)

🔧 Lemma 11 (circular step)

Lemma 11 uses "no update by the same process twice after s''" to show that O never returns to q₀, and conversely.

Impact. Statement true; a simultaneous induction resolves it (fig2_safe, theorem8).

Repair

Our proof of Theorem 8 replaces the execution-based Lemmas 9–13 by an invariant with ghost state (InvV), checked for every step and crash.

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (CSLib Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/Recoverable.lean, as in the Ovens'24 folder).

  • Asynchronous shared memory, n deterministic processes Fin n, individual crashes and recoveries: a crash resets the local state to the initial state with the same input, shared memory is non-volatile. Executions are arbitrary event sequences (any interleaving of steps and crashes).

  • Binary recoverable consensus (RecConsensus): agreement among all outputs (also of different incarnations), validity, recoverable wait-freedom (a process taking infinitely many steps and eventually not crashing outputs in its last run). Wait-free consensus (WFConsensus) is the crash-free special case.

  • Objects: deterministic sequential types; "with registers" = any read/write-like objects (withRW); unboundedly many objects (indexed by the nodes of the tournament tree). Readable = some read-only operation with injective responses (ObjSpec.Readable). IsRecording, IsDiscerning, RecSolvable, WFSolvable, (Rec)ConsensusNumber are those of the Ovens'24 folder.

  • Theorem 14 uses Ovens' valency sets E*_1 with the crashing process c = the largest identifier instead of the paper's E_A (only c crashes, at most as often as the others step); the proof only needs that one crash of c after any step of another process is admissible. This is internal to the proof: the statement of Theorem 14 quantifies over algorithms solving recoverable consensus on all executions, as in the paper.

  • Binary consensus makes the necessity results (Theorems 14, 22, Ruppert's necessity, the upper bounds of Corollary 20 and Proposition 21) stronger than the paper's; the sufficiency results (Theorem 8 and its uses) are proved for binary consensus only (the algorithms carry values only through registers).

Proof remarks. Two small points, with routine fixes (theorem8, theorem14):

  • Lemma 13, Case 3, claims without justification that an earlier run of the single process of B finds R_A = ⊥ at line 19 (true because R_A is never reset and that process later finds ⊥).

  • In Theorem 14, condition 3 in Case 2 is shown without using |A| > 1: no cycle starts on team B at all (we formalize this slightly stronger statement).

Remark (not an error). Theorem 22 and the "rcons(T) is n or n + 1" bound are not tight: by Ovens' Theorem 13 (PODC'24, formalized in RecoverableConsensus) recoverable consensus among n needs an n-recording type, so for readable types rcons(T) is exactly the largest n with T n-recording and rcons(𝒯) = max rcons(T) (theorem14_of_ovens, theorem22_ovens).

Not formalized. Theorem 1 and Appendix A (simultaneous crashes), Section 4 (universal construction, Theorem 23, Corollary 24), Appendix H (rcons(stack) = 1), multi-valued consensus.
