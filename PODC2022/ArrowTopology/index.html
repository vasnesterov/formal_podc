Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The topological proof of Arrow's theorem stands after small repairs: Arrow's theorem (Thm. 2.1), its topological form (Thm. 2.2), the index lemma, the orientability of N_I and the index-lemma proof of the base case are all correct. The promised characterization of domain restrictions does not hold as printed: Theorem 4.2 is false, and the corrected characterization of the full version (Theorem 6), which we verified, needs critical triples. The claim that every subdomain admits a non-dictatorial map, in Prop. 4.1 and Thm. 4.2, holds only for subdomains in which every pair of alternatives stays free.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2, "moreover" sentences (subdomains)

"Every domain contained in one of these maximal domains admits a non-dictatorial map" is false, e.g. for the one-profile domain {(ABC, ACB)}, where unanimity forces F(R) ∈ {ABC, ACB}, one voter's preference (prop41_moreover_false, thm42_moreover_false).

Impact. A missing hypothesis: non-dictatoriality is not inherited by subdomains that lose a vertex of N_I. The sentence holds for subdomains in which every pair of alternatives stays free, and the rest of Prop. 4.1 is correct (prop41_moreover_of_pairFree, thm42_moreover_of_pairFree).

🛠️ Theorem 4.2 (domain restrictions, two critical profiles)

Theorem 4.2 is false whenever the blue edges of the two removed critical profiles share a vertex, e.g. for R₁ = (BAC, ACB), R₂ = (CBA, ACB) every unanimous IIA map on N_I \ {R₁, R₂} is dictatorial (thm42_counterexample, thm42_not_admits_of_adjacent).

Impact. Section 4's characterization needs a new statement; Arrow's theorem and its topological proof are unaffected.

Details

The theorem considers n = 2, |X| = 3, and two critical profiles R₁ ∈ C₁, R₂ ∈ C₂ with different blue edges, and claims that N_I \ {R₁, R₂} admits a non-dictatorial unanimous IIA map. When the two blue edges share a vertex (12 of the 30 choices), the antiunanimity profile spanned by the two edges is still in the domain, and it forces dictatorship. In the explicit instance the remaining profile is (BCA, ACB).

Repair

The statement is true for disjoint blue edges (critical pairs) (thm42_admits_of_disjoint, thm42_maximal). The full version replaces it by a correct characterization (Theorem 6), which adds critical triples and which we verified (thm6).

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 3.2: "σ₃(i) = + (resp. +)" should be "− (resp. +)".

  • Lemma 5.2, Step 3: F^n(R) should be F^n(R').

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 5.1 · Lemma 3.2

🔧 Lemma 5.1 (reduction from |X| = 3 to |X| ≥ 3)

The auxiliary profile x R' b R' a R' y needs a, b, x, y pairwise distinct, but x, y are fixed in advance, so a = x breaks the construction.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (arrow_of_three).

Repair

If a, b both lie in the triple, IIA finishes the proof. Otherwise, run the construction with two elements of the triple different from a and b.

🔧 Lemma 3.2 (orientability of N_I)

The proof's pairing is not a pairing when ≥ 2 coordinates are free.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (lemma_3_2).

Repair

We pair by flipping one fixed free coordinate.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. Static combinatorics; no distributed system. The Section 6 analogy with tasks is informal.

  • Preferences. Strict total orders on an arbitrary, possibly infinite, type of alternatives. Voters form a finite type with at least 2 elements.

  • Axioms. Unanimity, IIA and dictatorship as in the paper. Domain-restricted versions are used for Section 4.

  • Complexes. A vertex of N_I is literally the set of profiles U^σ_{ab}; simplices are vertex sets with a common profile (nerves). The same holds for N_O.

    • Theorem 2.2 is stated without "chromatic", as in the PODC version. We prove that unanimous simplicial maps are automatically chromatic, so nothing is lost.

  • Three alternatives. Computations use a sign model, proved equivalent to the set-level complexes. The Section 4 statements are decided by the kernel after a proved reduction to sign functions on the 12 vertices.

Routine details the paper skips.

  • Section 3.3: "the two remaining contributions have to be −1 each" needs each of them to be ≥ −1, which the paper does not show (a priori a cycle that is not tricoloured could contribute ±2). Each contribution lies in {0, −1}, because a non-unanimous Condorcet cycle is oriented opposite to the unanimous ones (cyc_split).

  • Figure 7: the boundary-edge characterization is used without proof (isBoundary_iff).

Full version. Problems found only there:

  • The example of a critical triple uses (BCA, ABC), which lies in C₁, not C₂, so removing the three listed profiles leaves a dictatorial domain (paper_triple_example_not_critical). The intended profile is (CBA, ACB).

  • Section 4.2: "deleting a triangle on each of the concentric cylinders" is imprecise (two of the six profiles of each are deleted).
