import Papers.PODC2022.ArrowTopology.Domains /-! # The polarised domain `N''_I` (Section 4.2) Section 4.2 of S. Rajsbaum, A. Raventós-Pujol, *A Distributed Combinatorial Topology Approach to Arrow's Impossibility Theorem*, PODC 2022: a counterexample to the hypothesis (Baryshnikov, Lauwers, Baigent) that a domain admits Arrovian aggregation iff its induced complex is contractible. Two right-wing parties `A, B` (`T3.x, T3.y`) and a left-wing party `C` (`T3.z`); two voters; the polarised domain consists of the profiles in which no voter ranks `C` in the middle. The aggregation map has two local dictators: voter `0` on `{A, B}`, voter `1` on `{A, C}` and `{B, C}`. * `polarF_P_AB`, `polarF_P_AC`, `polarF_P_BC`: the defining equations of the paper's `F` (`F(R)` is a well-defined strict total order on the domain by construction). * `polarised_unanimity`, `polarised_iia`, `polarised_nonDictatorial`, `polarised_admits`: `F` is unanimous, IIA and non-dictatorial on the polarised domain. * Non-contractibility: Mathlib has no geometric realisation of abstract simplicial complexes, so we only prove the combinatorial certificate `polarised_fVector`: the complex generated by the 16 triangles of the domain has 12 vertices, 36 edges and 16 triangles, hence Euler characteristic `12 − 36 + 16 = −8 ≠ 1` (`polarised_euler`). That a contractible complex has Euler characteristic `1` is standard (homotopy invariance of homology) but **not** formalized here. * `polarised_covers`: the polarised domain contains all 12 vertices of `N_I`, so Theorem 6 of the full version applies to it (`polarised_misses_criticalPair`). -/ namespace Podc.ArrowTopology open Cslib.SocialChoice namespace ThreeAlts variable {X : Type*} (T3 :
