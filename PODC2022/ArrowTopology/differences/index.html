Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of A Combinatorial Topology Approach to Arrow's Theorem — PODC 2022.

Paper: S. Rajsbaum, A. Raventós-Pujol, A Distributed Combinatorial Topology Approach to Arrow's Impossibility Theorem, PODC 2022 ("PODC version"). Full version: A Combinatorial Topology Approach to Arrow's Impossibility Theorem, MPRA 120023 (2024) ("full version"). Numbering is the PODC numbering unless stated otherwise.

Overall. Arrow's theorem and its combinatorial-topology proof (Sections 2, 3, 5) are correct, up to small gaps that we repaired (items 6, 9, 10). Section 4 of the PODC version (domain restrictions) contains a false theorem (item 13) and missing hypotheses (item 12). The full version corrects them, and we verified its corrected theorem (item 15).

Model and definitions (Section 2)

  1. Preferences. Pref X is a bundled strict total order. X is an arbitrary type, possibly infinite, as Lemma 5.1 allows. Voters form an arbitrary type ι; Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 assume ι finite with 2 ≤ card ι (the paper's n ≥ 2).

  2. IIA is stated as: if (R i).P a b ↔ (S i).P a b for all i, then (F R).P a b ↔ (F S).P a b. For strict total orders this is the paper's "same order on {a, b}".

  3. Vertices are sets. As in the paper, a vertex of N_I is the set U^σ_{αβ} of profiles itself, so U^σ_{αβ} = U^{−σ}_{βα} holds automatically. Simplices are finite vertex sets with a common profile (nerves, PreAbstractSimplicialComplex). For computations with three alternatives we use an equivalent "sign model" (SignModel.lean) and prove the correspondence (ThreeAlt.lean, theorem_2_2_base).

  4. Theorem 2.2 without "chromatic". The PODC statement asks only for a simplicial map. The full version (Theorem 5) adds "chromatic", and the translation to Arrow's classical setting (full-version Prop. 3) needs it. We prove that every unanimous simplicial map N_I → N_O is automatically chromatic (isChromatic_of_unanimous). So the PODC statement is correct as written, and theorem_2_2 proves it without a chromaticity hypothesis.

  5. Propositions 3 and 4 of the full version. The PODC version only sketches them (Section 2.3.3). The forward direction (prop_3_forward) and the unanimity equivalence (prop_4_unanimity) need at least one voter. With no voters all the sets U^σ_{ab} coincide, so the induced map cannot be chromatic, and unanimity of F is unsatisfiable. This is irrelevant for n ≥ 2.

Index lemma and Lemma 3.2 (Section 3)

  1. Index lemma (Theorem 3.1). The paper's hypotheses are: "every edge lies in one or in an even number of triangles", and the orientation pairs the induced orientations. We formalize the orientation condition directly: on every edge that is not a boundary edge, the two directions are induced equally often (IsOriented). This implies the "even number" condition. The triangles form a finite family T → V × V × V (possibly with repetitions). The proof is the paper's double-counting argument.

  2. Lemma 3.2 (orientability).

    • The proof's pairing ("σ₃ and σ₃' are paired if they are equal on each component but one") is not a pairing when k ≥ 2 coordinates are free. We pair by flipping a fixed free coordinate (card_even_eq_card_odd).

    • Typo: "if σ₁(i) = σ₂(i) = + (resp. −), then σ₃(i) = + (resp. +)" should read "σ₃(i) = − (resp. +)".

    • We prove the lemma for any number of voters (as the paper's proof does) and also characterise the boundary edges: {U^α_j, U^β_{j'}} is a boundary edge iff α = β (isBoundary_iff). The paper uses this characterisation (Figure 7) without proof.

  3. Parity convention. The orientation AB → BC → CA is used when the product of all signs is +1 (additively: parity = 0 in ZMod 2), and AB → CA → BC otherwise. This is the paper's convention.

  4. Proof of Theorem 2.2 for n = 2, |X| = 3 (Section 3.3). The paper says "the two remaining contributions to I have to be −1 each". This needs each of them to lie in {0, −1}. That holds because a non-unanimous Condorcet cycle is oriented opposite to the unanimous ones, and its contribution is −1 exactly when its three signs are equal (cyc_split). The paper does not state this step; we prove it. The other steps are formalized as in the paper: C = 0 (content_eq_zero), I = 0 (index_eq_zero), the boundary is the four Condorcet cycles (index_eq, boundary_multiset, the latter checked by kernel decide), the unanimous cycles contribute +1 each (cyc_unanimous), and the triangle {U^{−+}_{AB}, U^{−+}_{BC}, U^{+−}_{CA}} separates the two split cycles.

Reduction (Section 5)

  1. Lemma 5.1 — gap (repaired). The auxiliary profile x R' b R' a R' y / a R' y R' x R' b needs a, b, x, y pairwise distinct. But a, b are arbitrary and x, y are fixed in advance, so e.g. a = x breaks the construction. Repair: the dictator k of F̄ dictates every pair inside the triple T = {x, y, z}.

    • If a, b ∈ T, IIA finishes the proof.

    • Otherwise at most one of a, b is in T. We run the construction with two elements u ≠ v of T \ {a, b} in place of x, y (arrow_of_three).

  2. Lemma 5.2 — typos. In Step 3, "x F^n(R) z" and "z F^n(R) y" should read "x F^n(R') z" and "z F^n(R') y". The conclusion x F^n(R') y is then transferred back to R by IIA, since R' and R agree on {x, y}. Otherwise the proof is formalized as written (arrow_of_two_voters). Voters are Fin n; the paper's voter n is Fin.last, and voters 1 and 2 are indices 0 and 1.

Domain restrictions (Section 4)

All Section 4 statements concern n = 2, |X| = 3. A domain is a set D of profiles. An aggregation map on D must satisfy unanimity and IIA for profiles in D; it is dictatorial if one voter dictates on D (AdmitsNonDictatorial). The cylinders C₁, C₂, critical profiles, blue edges, critical pairs and critical triples are given explicitly (Domains.lean). We obtained C₁ as the connected component of the torus T containing (ABC, ACB) and (BAC, ACB) (Figure 9) and checked it against Table 1 of the full version. The statements are reduced to sign functions on the 12 vertices (admitsNonDictatorial_iff_sign) and decided by the kernel.

  1. Proposition 4.1, last sentence — missing hypothesis. The sentence says "every domain contained in one of these maximal domains belongs to D_T". Read literally it is false, but only because "non-dictatorial" is not inherited by subdomains that lose a vertex of N_I.

    • Counterexample: the one-profile domain {(ABC, ACB)} lies in every T \ {R₁, R₂}, yet admits no non-dictatorial map. Unanimity forces F(R) ∈ {ABC, ACB}, which is one voter's preference (prop41_moreover_false). The empty domain is another trivial counterexample. Both drop vertices of N_I.

    • Correct statement: the sentence holds for subdomains in which every pair is free, i.e. which keep all 12 vertices (prop41_moreover_of_pairFree, via admits_of_subset_of_pairFree). This also follows from the full version's Theorem 6: the removed profiles form a critical pair, or two profiles of a critical triple whose third (antiunanimity) profile is not in T.

    • The rest of Prop. 4.1 is correct (prop41_admits, prop41_maximal), and the condition "different blue edges" is necessary (prop41_equal_blueEdge).

  2. Theorem 4.2 — false. It claims that N_I \ {R₁, R₂}, with R₁ ∈ C₁ and R₂ ∈ C₂ critical and having different blue edges, admits a non-dictatorial map.

    • This fails whenever the two blue edges are adjacent, i.e. share a vertex (12 of the 30 choices, thm42_not_admits_of_adjacent). The antiunanimity profile spanned by the two blue edges is still in the domain and forces dictatorship.

    • Explicit instance: R₁ = (BAC, ACB), R₂ = (CBA, ACB), with third profile (BCA, ACB) (thm42_counterexample, thm42_false).

    • The statement is true for disjoint blue edges (critical pairs: thm42_admits_of_disjoint, thm42_maximal).

    • The "moreover" sentence has the same missing hypothesis as in item 12: {(ABC, ABC)} is a counterexample (thm42_moreover_false). For disjoint blue edges it holds for subdomains in which all pairs are free (thm42_moreover_of_pairFree).

    • The full version replaces Theorem 4.2 by Theorem 6, which adds critical triples.

  3. Full version, example of a critical triple — wrong. "R₁ = (BAC, ACB), R₂ = (BCA, ABC), R₃ = (BCA, ACB) is a critical triple" is false: (BCA, ABC) lies in C₁, not C₂. Removing these three profiles leaves a dictatorial domain (paper_triple_example_not_critical). The intended R₂ is (CBA, ACB), as in case (f) of the full version's Table 1. This is a typo-level error.

  4. Full version, Theorem 6 (characterization) — verified. Let D be a domain in which every pair of alternatives is free. Then D admits a unanimous, IIA, non-dictatorial map iff D misses both profiles of some critical pair (18 of them) or all three profiles of some critical triple (12 of them) (thm6). The paper's words "does not intersect the interior of" are read as "misses these profiles".

  5. Section 4.2 (polarised domain). The aggregation map is well defined, unanimous, IIA and non-dictatorial (ThreeAlts.polarised_admits), for any three alternatives x, y, z with z the "left-wing" party. This is consistent with Theorem 6 (polarised_misses_criticalPair).

    • Non-contractibility is not formalized: Mathlib has no geometric realisation of abstract simplicial complexes. We prove the combinatorial certificate instead: the induced complex has 12 vertices, 36 edges and 16 triangles, so its Euler characteristic is −8 ≠ 1 (polarised_fVector, polarised_euler). The standard fact "contractible ⇒ χ = 1" is not formalized.

    • The paper's sentence "deleting a triangle on each of the concentric cylinders" is imprecise: the domain contains 4 of the 6 unanimous and 4 of the 6 opposite profiles.

  6. Section 4.1's index-lemma argument (full version, Figure 11) is not formalized as such. Its conclusion ("C₁ is dictatorial") is proved by exhaustive kernel computation (not_admits_C1).

Not formalized

  • Vertex counts and dimension (Section 2.3.2). We prove the vertex counts for every finite X: N_O has |X|(|X|−1) vertices (card_VO), and N_I has |X|(|X|−1)2^{n−1} vertices for n ≥ 1 (card_VI, stated as 2·#V = |X|(|X|−1)2^n). The dimension claim (|X|+1)(|X|−2)/2, cited from Baryshnikov, is not formalized.

  • The remark that Arrow's theorem fails for infinitely many voters (cited from [27]) is not formalized.

  • Ties / weak orders ([10, Lemma 1], cited) are not formalized.

  • Section 6 (relation to distributed tasks) is an informal analogy. Only its counting claims are checked (Counts.lean).

  • Remarks 2.1–2.3 (adjacent triangles differ by one transposition) are illustrative and not formalized.

  • The pivotal-voter proof (full version Section 5) is not part of the PODC paper and is not formalized.
