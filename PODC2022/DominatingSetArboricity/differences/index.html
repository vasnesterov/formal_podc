Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed Dominating Set in Bounded Arboricity Graphs — PODC 2022.

Paper: Dory, Ghaffari, Ilchi, Near-Optimal Distributed Dominating Set in Bounded Arboricity Graphs, PODC 2022 (arXiv:2206.05174v1). Each item is classified as one of: false statement, false only in a degenerate case, proof gap (statement true), under-specified definition, typo, or modelling choice.

Errors and gaps in the paper

  1. Theorem 1.3 (factor Δ^{1/k}(Δ^{1/k}+1)(k+1) in O(k²) rounds): proof gap, statement true. The proof uses Lemma 4.6 with γ = Δ^{1/k} and λ = 1/(Δ+1), which gives the factor γ(γ+1)⌈log_γ(Δ+1)⌉. But ⌈log_γ(Δ+1)⌉ = ⌈k log_Δ(Δ+1)⌉ ≤ k+1 only when (Δ+1)^k ≤ Δ^{k+1}, and γ > 1 needs Δ ≥ 2. For Δ = k = 2: ⌈log_{√2} 3⌉ = 4 > 3 (logb_gap), so the proof gives ≈ 13.7 instead of the claimed ≈ 10.2. The statement is still true. In the uncovered regime (Δ+1)^k > Δ^{k+1} (which forces k ≥ Δ), the claimed factor is at least Δ+1 (claimed_factor_ge). There the trivial one-round algorithm, where every node picks a minimum-weight node of N⁺_v, is a (Δ+1)-approximation (trivial_approx). Combined statement: theorem_1_3_full.

  2. Observation A.1: proof gap, statement true. The observation claims that some single-round 3-approximation exists on graphs of arboricity 1; this is true. But the algorithm of the proof (and of the introduction's sentence about trees), "take all non-leaf nodes", fails on a forest with a K₂ component: both endpoints are leaves, so they are not dominated (obsA1_proof_alg_fails_K2). If "non-leaf" means "degree ≥ 2", isolated vertices are missed too. Fix: also take the larger-ID endpoint of every K₂ component, and every isolated vertex. This is still one round, and the paper's parent/grandparent charging then gives the factor 3 (observation_A_1_fixed). The charging only needs an orientation with out-degree ≤ 1, so the result also holds for pseudoforests.

  3. Lemma 4.6 for λ = 1: under-specified hypothesis. The lemma says "for λ > 0" and takes "the output of Lemma 4.1", which forces λ < 1, but λ < 1 is never stated. Read literally with λ = 1, the number of phases t = ⌈log_γ λ⁻¹⌉ is 0, so S' = ∅ and S ∪ S' need not dominate. Example: a single vertex with w = 1, x = 1, S = ∅ (lemma_4_6_false_lam_one). We assume λ < 1 (lemma_4_6_dominating). Also, the lemma states E[w_{S'}] = γ(γ+1)⌈log_γ λ⁻¹⌉·OPT; this should be ≤ (typo).

  4. Lemma 4.7 (E[c_v] ≤ γ+1): missing justification, statement true. The proof takes "the sequence d_1 ≥ … ≥ d_r that maximizes E[c_v]" and bounds E[c_v] by ∑ β_i e^{-β̄_i} for a fixed sequence. The d_i are random and depend on earlier iterations, and the proof does not say why the fixed-sequence bound applies. Since the coins are fresh in every iteration, a standard argument closes the gap. We prove the lemma, combined with Lemma 4.8, by a potential-function / backward-induction argument. The potential is Φ_i = γ ∑_{v undominated} x_v (p_i d_v + γ), and the step uses (1-p)^d (1+pd) ≤ 1.

  5. Theorem 1.1 / Lemma 4.1 for α = 0: false only in a degenerate case (of the proof). Lemma 4.1 needs λ < 1/((α+1)(1+ε)). Theorem 1.1 uses λ = 1/((2α+1)(1+ε)), which violates this when α = 0, and bound (a) then has a zero denominator. Graphs of arboricity 0 are edgeless, so the theorem itself is trivial there. We assume α ≥ 1.

  6. Round complexity O(log(Δ/α)/ε): imprecise. The bound is 0 when Δ ≤ α, but at least one iteration (and O(1) rounds) is always needed. We prove r ≤ 1 + 2 log(max 1 ((Δ+1)/(2α+1)))/ε iterations and 2r + 2 rounds in total (numIter_detLam_le, distributed_theorem_1_1). For the general Lemma 4.1 we prove r ≤ 1 + 2 log(max 1 (λ(Δ+1)))/ε (numIter_le).

  7. Minor slip (notation):

    • Section 3 reuses the symbol λ for α/((2α+1)(1+ε)), which differs from the λ of Section 4.

Modelling choices and scope

  1. Arboricity vs. orientations (modelling choice). As footnote 2 of the paper allows, the main theorems take an orientation with out-degree ≤ α (G.OutOrientation α). The arboricity versions follow from Observation 3.5, which is proved (ArboricityLE.hasOutDegLE, theorem_1_1_arboricity, theorem_3_1). Our orientations may orient an edge both ways; this only makes the hypothesis stronger.

  2. Weights (modelling choice). Weights are arbitrary nonnegative reals, not positive integers bounded by n^c. OPT is expressed as "for every dominating set D".

  3. Unweighted Section 3 (modelling choice). The procedure of Section 3 equals that of Section 4.1 with w ≡ 1: r+1 iterations in Section 3 correspond to r' = r+1 in Section 4.1, and τ ≡ 1. Theorem 3.1 / Claim 3.3 are therefore obtained as theorem_3_1 from the weighted theorem. Lemmas 3.2 and 3.4 are not stated separately: they are the unit-weight cases of lemma_4_1 and of the feasibility invariant Inv.feas.

  4. Distributed model (modelling choice). The deterministic algorithm is implemented in CSLib's synchronous locally-iterative model (Cslib.LocallyIterative). There, a node's new state is a function of the round number, its own state, and the set of its neighbours' states. States contain node identifiers, so this is the LOCAL model with IDs. Global knowledge: Δ (instantiated with the true maximum degree), ε, and the iteration count r (a function of Δ, α, ε), as the paper assumes. CONGEST message sizes are not formalized. Each message is the sender's state: an ID, a weight, τ, an ID, x = τ(1+ε)^j/(Δ+1) with j < r (invariant Inv), and three bits. The minimum-weight dominator tauNode is an arbitrary one, chosen with Classical.epsilon. Real-number arithmetic is exact.

  5. Randomized algorithm (modelling choice). Lemma 4.6's algorithm is described centrally, round by round. Its randomness is modelled as independent per-node Bernoulli(p_k) coins in every step. The expectation is the finite backward recursion expect, which equals the expectation under the product measure. Γ is recomputed in every iteration as {u ∉ S∪S' : X_u ≥ w_u/γ}. The paper instead shrinks Γ by removing nodes whose X_u drops; the two agree because X_u only decreases (and sampled nodes have X_u = 0). A node whose sampling probability is ≥ 1 is sampled regardless of its coin. The packing values are multiplied by γ at the last iteration of each phase instead of at the start of the next phase, which is equivalent. No locally-iterative implementation of the randomized algorithm is given; the O(1) rounds per iteration are evident from the description.

  6. Theorem 1.2 (scope). Combined statement: theorem_1_2_full (Main.lean). It is formalized in an explicit form for every t ≥ 1 and γ > 1 (theorem_1_2). With ε = 1/(4t) and λ = ε/(α+1), the expected weight is at most (α(1+1/t) + γ(γ+1)⌈log_γ(4t(α+1))⌉)·OPT. The asymptotic part covers γ = max(2, α^{1/(2t)}), α ≥ 2 and t ln α ≤ α, i.e. t ≤ α/ln α:

    • the extension cost is at most 416 α/t (theorem_1_2_arith);

    • the number of iterations of Lemma 4.6 is at most 50 t (ln(Δ+1) + 1) (theorem_1_2_rounds_arith);

    • the iterations of Lemma 4.1 are bounded by numIter_le.

    The constants are explicit but not optimized. Lemma 4.1 and Lemma 4.6 are combined at the level of the centralized description, not as a single distributed run.

  7. Theorem 1.4 (scope). The KMW lower bound for fractional vertex cover (KMW16) is an external result and is not formalized. We formalize the combinatorial reduction:

    • the construction of H and its arboricity bound (we prove ≤ 2, which is what the lower bound needs);

    • the degree bound max K (max (Δ+1) 2);

    • OPT_MDS(H) ≤ K·OPT_VC(G) + n;

    • the conversion of any dominating set of H into a fractional vertex cover of G with ∑ y ≤ |S|/K;

    • the locality of the simulation: the host map H → G does not increase distances.

    The simulation itself is formalized only as this distance property, not as a transformation of algorithms. The paper uses two properties of the KMW graph: it is bipartite (so OPT_VC = OPT_FVC) and it has m ≥ n. We use instead the general facts OPT_VC ≤ 2·OPT_FVC (rounding) and n ≤ (Δ+1)·OPT_FVC, the latter for graphs without isolated vertices. This gives the factor 2c(1+1/Δ) instead of c(1+1/Δ) (reduction). The paper's footnote 3 mentions this alternative, and the change is immaterial for constant or polylogarithmic c.

  8. Not formalized: Remarks 4.4 and 4.5 (unknown Δ or α, which use the Barenboim–Elkin orientation algorithm). The NP-hardness results cited from [BU17] are only quoted in the paper.
