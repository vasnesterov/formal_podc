Fault Tolerant Coloring of the Asynchronous Cycle — PODC 2022

Pierre Fraigniaud, Patrick Lambein-Monette, Mikaël Rabie. Brief Announcement: Fault Tolerant Coloring of the Asynchronous Cycle. PODC 2022. DOI 10.1145/3519270.3538456. Formalized version: arXiv 2207.11198 v1 (the only version, the full version of the brief announcement). Numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The paper claims a wait-free 5-colouring of every asynchronous cycle C_n in O(log* n) rounds (Algorithm 3, Theorem 4.4), but neither 5-colouring algorithm is wait-free: Algorithm 2 (Theorem 3.11) and Algorithm 3 (Theorem 4.4) have executions on every cycle C_n, n ≥ 3, in which two processes take rounds forever without returning, and we proved no replacement. The 6-colour Algorithm 1 is correct: we prove Theorem 3.1, with a better bound. The Cole–Vishkin lemmas 4.1–4.3 and the safety Lemma 4.5 (the evolving identifiers of Algorithm 3 stay a proper colouring) also hold.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorems 3.11 and 4.4, Lemma 3.13 (Algorithms 2 and 3 are not wait-free)

On C_3 with identifiers 0, 1, 2, if all three processes take a round at time 1, process 1 then stops, and processes 0 and 2 keep taking rounds simultaneously, then neither of them ever returns, under both Algorithm 2 and Algorithm 3 (theorem_3_11_false, theorem_4_4_false), which also refutes Lemma 3.13 (lemma_3_13_false).

Impact. The wait-free 5-colouring in O(log* n) rounds is not established, and no bound on the number of rounds exists for either algorithm. The correctness and palette parts of both theorems do hold. Whether some wait-free 5-colouring of C_n exists is not settled here: we refute the algorithms, not the possibility.

Details

At time 1 each process writes (X, a, b) = (X, 0, 0) and recomputes: process 0 gets (1,1), process 1 gets (1,1), process 2 gets (0,1). Process 1 then crashes, or is just slow, and its register keeps showing its initial colours (0, 0). Processes 0 (the local minimum) and 2 (the local maximum) now take their rounds simultaneously, which the model explicitly allows. Each round they read the stale (0,0) of process 1 and each other's colours. Their states alternate forever between (1,1)/(0,1) and (2,2)/(0,2), and each of them always finds both of its colours in the conflict set C.

  • In Algorithm 3 the same execution works. Process 0 is a local minimum and process 2 a local maximum, so both set r ← ∞ in their first round and never change their identifiers again; after that the algorithm runs exactly like Algorithm 2.

  • The same pattern works on every C_n, n ≥ 3, with identifiers 0, …, n-1. Processes 0 and n-1 play the roles of 0 and 2. Their other neighbours 1 and n-2 stop after their first round (theorem_3_11_false_all, theorem_4_4_false_all).

  • Lemma 3.13 (non-minima return), on which the termination proofs of Theorems 3.11 and 4.4 rest, fails in the same run: process 2 is not a local minimum and its set A_2 stays empty, yet it never returns. The lemma's proof claims that the neighbours' a-values are positive "by time t_4", which ignores registers that are frozen at the initial value 0.

  • Two remarks from bounded model checking. If at most one process is activated per time step (no simultaneous rounds), Algorithm 2 terminates on all C_n with n ≤ 5. Leaving the initial colours unwritten (⊥) does not repair it.

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 3.3 is false as written with the open interval (t₁, t₂): a neighbour that takes a round exactly at t₂ can still conflict with p, and random simulation finds such runs on C_4. It holds for the half-open interval (t₁, t₂], which is the form we prove and use (fresh_of_conflict); it is used only inside the proof of Theorem 3.1.

  • Theorem 4.4 assumes only a proper colouring X, but its O(log* n) bound needs identifiers in [0, poly(n)], as Section 2.1 assumes for identifiers.

Typos

  • The proof of Theorem 3.1 says "min{ℓ, ℓ'} is bounded by ⌊3n/2⌋", which is true but not what is needed: the bound used is n/2. The final bound holds.

  • In the proof of Lemma 3.5, Y and τ should be x and t'.

  • The proof of Lemma 4.10 uses an undefined R.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 4.5

🛠️ Lemma 4.5 (Algorithm 3 keeps identifiers proper)

The proof's induction claim X_p ∉ {X_q, X̂_q} is not inductive on its own.

Impact. Statement true; repaired with a stronger invariant (lemma_4_5, alg3_X_ne_reg, alg3_X_proper).

Details

The paper's stale-read case concludes something about X̂_p where it needs the current X_p. The case X_q(T+1) = f(X_q(T), X_p(T)) omits the cases where q is a local maximum or reduces against its other neighbour.

Repair

The invariant also records two things. First, a register holds an earlier state with a counter r̂ ≤ r, equal only if the identifier is unchanged. Second, if a neighbour's last step changed its identifier and r̂_q = r_p, then that step read exactly the current X_p.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model (Model.lean).

  • Network and processes. n processes on ZMod n, where the neighbours of p are p ± 1. They are asynchronous, deterministic and crash-prone.

  • Registers. Each process owns a single-writer register, initially ⊥, that its two neighbours read. The full local state is written; the paper's algorithms write all their variables.

  • Rounds. A round is a local immediate snapshot: write, then read both neighbours, then compute or return. A schedule σ : ℕ → Finset (ZMod n) gives the processes activated at each time. Processes activated together all write before any of them reads (Section 2.1). Activating a returned process does nothing.

  • Faults and adversary. Crashes and slowness are schedules that stop or delay activating a process. The adversary is any schedule; no fairness is assumed.

  • Measures.

    • workActs counts the working activations of a process, which is the paper's round complexity.

    • WaitFreeWithin A init K means that in every schedule every process is activated at most K times while working.

    • ProperOutputs means that terminated neighbours have distinct outputs; OutputsIn is the palette.

  • Inputs. A proper colouring X (X p ≠ X (p+1)), as in the theorems. For Algorithm 1, X may take values in any linear order; the theorem itself uses ℕ.

  • Faithfulness. The model is exactly the paper's. The counterexamples use only schedules the paper explicitly allows: simultaneous rounds and a process that stops after one round.

Under-specified and minor points.

  • The pseudocode never says how a ⊥ register enters lines 11–19 of Algorithm 3. We follow the proof of Lemma 4.10: an unwritten r blocks the green light. The counterexample does not depend on this choice.

  • Theorem 4.4 assumes only a proper initial colouring, but its O(log* n) bound needs identifiers in [0, poly(n)], as Section 2.1 assumes for identifiers. Moot, since termination fails.

  • Lemma 4.1 is stated over the reals. We prove it over ℕ, with α = 3.

Supporting pieces:

Not formalized.

  • Properties 2.1–2.3: MIS impossibility, the Ω(log* n) lower bound and the 5-colour lower bound for C_3. They are reductions to cited results (strong symmetry breaking, Linial, renaming lower bounds).

  • Lemmas 3.4–3.8, 3.14 and 4.6–4.10, which are steps of proofs we replaced or of claims that are false.

  • The paper's proof of Theorem 3.1 leans on loose lemmas (Lemma 3.3 as written, the parity bookkeeping of Lemmas 3.4–3.8), which we did not check. We give a new, sharper proof with bound max(4, n) activations, where the paper has ⌊3n/2⌋ + 4 (theorem_3_1, alg1_workActs_le):

    • Local extrema return within 4 activations, as the paper claims. Our argument: after the first round of a local maximum p, each smaller neighbour can cause at most one more failure of p (workActs_le_four_of_max).

    • A non-extremal process returns within ℓ + ℓ' + 1 activations (Lemma 3.9, third term), where ℓ, ℓ' are the monotone distances to the closest local maximum and minimum. Our argument: every failed round changes the colour (Lemma 3.2), a_p changes at most ℓ times, and b_p at most ℓ' times (lemma_3_9).

    • The parity bookkeeping with the sets A_p, B_p (Lemmas 3.5–3.8) is not needed.

  • The terms 3ℓ + 4, 3ℓ' + 4 of Lemma 3.9 (via Lemma 3.4 for non-extremal processes). Exhaustive model checking for n ≤ 6 found no violation.

  • Appendix A (O(Δ²) colours on general graphs).

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The C_3 counterexamples are checked by kernel decide (2-periodic configuration); the all-n versions are symbolic. Correctness and palette are proved for every initial colouring. modelcheck/ holds the Python model checkers used to find the counterexample and to test the bounds: exhaustive over all schedules for small n (mc12.py, lasso.py, bounds1.py, scc3.py), and random/BFS search for Lemma 4.5 (alg3.py, rand3b.py, bfs3.py).
