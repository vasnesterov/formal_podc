The termination argument: by binding, before the coin of round `r` is revealed the adversary is bound to a value `b_r` that no party's BCA returns negated; the value can only depend on the coins of rounds `< r` (unpredictability: the coin of round `r` is revealed after the first non-faulty party finished its BCA). We model the adversary's binding choices as a strategy `β : List Bool → Bool` (coins so far ↦ bound value), so a deterministic adaptive adversary; a strong coin gives independent uniform coins. * `Hit β l`: in the coin prefix `l` there are rounds `r < r'` with `l[r] = β (l.take r)` (success: everybody starts round `r + 1` with `l[r]`, `binding_step`) and `l[r'] = l[r]` (then everybody commits in round `r'`, `commit_after_binding`). `hit_commits`: in every run whose binding values follow `β`, `Hit` on the first `k` coins implies that in some round `r' < k` every party finishing round `r'` commits. * `card_not_hit_le`: at most `k + 1` of the `2^k` coin prefixes of length `k` are not hits, so with a fair strong coin no round `< k` makes everybody commit with probability at most `(k + 1) / 2^k`; `tsum_succ_div_two_pow`: `∑ (k + 1) / 2^k = 4`, the paper's bound of 4 expected rounds (via `E[T] = ∑_k P(T > k)`), and the probability of never terminating is `0`.
