import Papers.PODC2022.FullyDefective.Robbins.Occ /-! # Algorithm 3 of [CCGS22]: the content-oblivious simulator on a Robbins cycle (network layer) K. Censor-Hillel, S. Cohen, R. Gelles, G. Sela. *Distributed Computations in Fully-Defective Networks.* PODC 2022 (full version arXiv:2205.11148, Section 4; Theorem 6 of the conference version = Theorems 10/12 of the full version). As for Algorithm 1 (`Cycle/Model.lean`), the simulated protocol `π` is abstracted away: queues receive arbitrary positive payloads (`push`), and a node decodes one payload per epoch into its `log`. The composition with `π` is in `Robbins/Simulation.lean`. ## The network The nodes are the vertices `V` of a graph `G` with a fixed Robbins cycle `R` (a closed walk `R.C : ZMod R.L → V` through all vertices, never traversing an edge in both directions). Channels are fully defective, so a channel `x → y` is a counter `net x y` of pulses in transit (not FIFO, which is irrelevant for indistinguishable pulses). ## Local knowledge of the nodes Node `u` knows, for each of its occurrences on the cycle, the previous and next nodes `prev_{u,i}`, `next_{u,i}`. We describe occurrences by their positions `p ∈ ZMod L` (`C p = u`): `prev = C (p - 1)`, `next = C (p + 1)`. The numbering of the occurrences (the rotation maintained by `RotateEdges()`) is stored as the position `o` of occurrence `0`; occurrence `i + 1` is `nextOcc` of occurrence `i`, and `RotateEdges()` is `o ← prevOcc o` (the new occurrence `0` is the old occurrence `k_u - 1`). Program points carry positions of occurrences instead of indices `i`. All actions of `u` only depend on the sequence of pairs `(prev_{u,i}, next_{u,i})` starting at its occurrence `0`, as in the paper. ## Program points (`Phase`) * `idle` — line 1; * `waitReq need` — line 3: `need x` request pulses are still expected on the channel from `x` (initially the number of `i` with `prev_{u,i} = x`; the paper's "wait until a request pulse is received on each `prev_{u,i}`", counted per physical channel); * `ready` — line 8; * `fdata c p` — lines 32–37 (non-holder): `c` data pulses received so far, waiting for a data pulse at occurrence `p` (from `C (p - 1)`); at occurrence `0` (`p = o`) also waiting for the `end` pulse (line 37); * `fend p` — lines 41–44 (non-holder): waiting for the `end` pulse at occurrence `p` (from `C (p + 1)`); * `hdata d t p` — lines 21–26 (holder, payload `d`): the `t`-th data pulse is on its way, waiting for it at occurrence `p`; * `hend d p` — lines 27–30 (holder): waiting for the `end` pulse at occurrence `p`. A node only reads pulses from the channels it waits on (`waits`): at lines 1 and 8 and the `until`-test of line 37 the paper does not restrict the sender, and we accept any channel of the right direction (clockwise channels `x → u` with `mult x u > 0`, counterclockwise ones with `mult u x > 0`); elsewhere the node reads the channel prescribed by the pseudo-code. The direction of a pulse is that of its channel, which is well defined on a Robbins cycle (`mult_eq_zero_of_pos`). ## Modelling choices (deviations from the pseudo-code are listed here) * Unary encoding only (Lines 20, 38–39); the binary encoding of Lemma 14 and the complexity bounds (Lemmas 13–14) are not modelled. * Line 3 is "wait until a request pulse was received on each `prev_{u,i}`"; since several occurrences may share the channel from `x`, the node counts request pulses per physical channel, with multiplicity `mult x u` (`waitReq need`). Extra pulses on a channel stay in the buffer and are read at line 8 as data pulses (the paper's comment on Line 8). * The token holder appends its own payload to its log when its `end` pulse returns (so that every node's log is the sequence of all payloads; the holder of a message addressed to itself delivers it like everybody else). * `RotateEdges()` is the update `o ← prevOcc o`; `prev_{u,i}`, `next_{u,i}` are `C (pᵢ ∓ 1)` where `p₀ = o`, `pᵢ₊₁ = nextOcc pᵢ`. * Asynchrony is modelled by interleaving single receptions; the network may delay any pulse arbitrarily (no fairness assumption: safety and deadlock-freedom only). ## Ghost state `ds` (payloads of the epochs started so far), `τ` (the position of the occurrence holding the token, or that received the token last) and `wv` (the channel carrying the unique token/data/end pulse in transit, if any; request pulses are not tracked) are ghost variables for the analysis; transitions never read them. -/ namespace
