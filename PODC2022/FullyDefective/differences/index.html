Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Distributed Computations in Fully-Defective Networks — PODC 2022.

Paper: Censor-Hillel, Cohen, Gelles, Sela, Distributed Computations in Fully-Defective Networks, PODC 2022; full version arXiv:2205.11148v1 (used for the proofs). Numbering: conference (Thm 2, 3, 4, Lemma 5, Thm 6 = Robbins-cycle simulator); arXiv numbers in brackets where they differ (Thm 3 = [Thm 20], conference Thm 6 = [Thm 10/12], the cycle-simulator correctness = [Thm 6]).

Scope

  • Formalized. Algorithm 1 (simple-cycle simulator, unary data phase) with Lemma 5 (safety and progress), the simulation theorem [Thm 6] (safety and liveness), Remark 2 (no starvation), quiescence; Theorem 3 (two-party impossibility); the existence of Robbins cycles in 2-edge-connected graphs (background fact used by Section 4) and its converse; Algorithm 3 on a Robbins cycle: safety (Lemma 11 (2), (3), simulation [Thm 12]) and deadlock freedom, but not liveness under fairness (last section).

  • Not formalized. The binary encoding (Algorithm 2, Lemmas 8, 9) and all communication-complexity claims (CC_overhead, CC_init, Lemmas 7, 9, 13, 14, 19); the distributed content-oblivious construction of a Robbins cycle (Section 5: Algorithms 4–6, Theorem 15, Lemmas 16–18, Remarks 4–6). Consequently Theorem 2 is covered only in the form "given a Robbins cycle (which exists), the simulator of Section 4 works"; the pre-processing phase is not verified. Algorithm 2 was only model-checked (modelcheck/alg2.py, rings of 1–4 nodes, L ∈ {2, 3}, no violation found).

  • [Remark 1] (the noiseless scheduler derived from the simulator is FIFO) and [Remark 3] (broadcast) are not formalized; our noiseless model is non-FIFO, which the paper says is also covered.

  • The reduction "a network with a bridge reduces to the two-party case" (Section 6, one sentence in the paper) is not formalized; only the two-party Theorem 3 is.

Model (all papers' sections)

  • Fully-defective channels are modelled as pulse counters: a node never sees message contents, so a link is the number of pulses sent and received in each direction. This is equivalent to the paper's alteration noise for the simulators, which ignore contents. Channels are not FIFO; for indistinguishable pulses this is irrelevant.

  • "wait until …" in the pseudo-code is a blocking receive on the named direction/edge: pulses arriving from other directions stay in the incoming buffer (polling semantics). The paper's proofs assume exactly this (e.g. "T₁ might get the token pulse before getting a request pulse, in which case it delays its actions").

  • Line 1 ("wait until Q_u is not empty or …") is an explicit step start u, enabled while u is at line 1 with a non-empty queue; weak fairness makes it happen.

  • Rings. Algorithm 1 is formalized on the oriented ring ZMod n for every n ≥ 1, including the degenerate n = 1 (self-loop) and n = 2 (double link); the paper assumes a simple cycle (n ≥ 3). Node identifiers are the ring positions; nodes know their clockwise/counterclockwise neighbours (as in Theorem 4).

  • The simulated protocol π (Protocol.lean) is a deterministic event-driven asynchronous protocol whose messages may go to any node (the paper: to neighbours in G; a special case). Inputs/IDs are folded into the per-node functions. Its noiseless executions deliver any in-transit message at any time (non-FIFO).

  • π as a black box. The paper lets π act "in parallel" and take arbitrary finite time. We model: delivery to π (Line 28) appends to an inbox; π processes inbox entries one at a time, each after an arbitrary delay (a proc step), atomically producing its outgoing messages, which are enqueued at once. The receive event of the transcript is the processing time. The initial messages of π are in the queues at time 0.

  • Encoding. The unary length of (m, u, v) is given by any injective coding enc : V × V × M → ℕ⁺ with a left inverse dec (Coding; Coding.ofEncodable for encodable types). Payloads must be positive: with d = 0 the holder would send its end pulse at once and the others would read it as a token pulse.

  • The holder logs its own message. In Algorithm 1(b) the token holder does not decode; we also append its own payload to its log, and deliver it to its own π if the destination is itself. This only matters for messages a node sends to itself, which never happens for π over a simple graph.

  • Simulation (Section 2, "noise-resilient simulator"). Safety (Cycle.simulation): for every reachable configuration of the simulator there is a reachable configuration of π over the noiseless network with the same states and the same local transcripts at every node; in addition, the messages in transit there are exactly the messages sent by π and not yet processed by their destination. The paper states the transcript condition for "an execution"; we state it for every finite prefix, which implies the paper's statement for finite executions, and add liveness separately. The witness D is determined by C (states, transcripts and D.net = sent − processed), but the theorem does not state that the witnesses of successive prefixes are reachable from one another (the proof builds them that way); for infinite executions this is the paper's own formulation ("simulates a prefix of a valid execution" at any time, plus liveness).

  • Fairness. Executions are infinite sequences of configurations, each step a simulator step or a stutter (SimExec); weak fairness (WeakFair): every step label (start u, reading a clockwise / counterclockwise pulse at u, π processing at u) enabled from some time on is eventually taken. This is the paper's "every message is eventually delivered" plus "π takes finite time". Non-vacuity: the round-robin scheduler is weakly fair (roundRobin_fair).

  • Liveness (Cycle.liveness): for every time t there is a later time by which every message sent by π by time t has been processed by its destination — the simulated noiseless execution is fair. The paper argues this informally in the last paragraphs of the proof of [Thm 6].

Lemma 5

  • (1) Progress is proved as rank_progress (the system moves token stage → data stage → end stage → next token stage unless it is quiet), epoch_completion (every node eventually completes every epoch started so far) and Inv.enabled_or_quiet (deadlock freedom). The paper's "at time t_k all nodes are in lines 1, 3 or 8" is the token stage of the invariant (TokStage).

  • The progress proof needs two facts the paper uses without stating them as invariants (Inv2): (a) a request pulse of the current token phase implies some node with a non-empty queue (the initiator keeps its message until it captures the token); (b) the released token has not passed such a node, so it is caught within n hops (the paper: "until it reaches a node v with a non-empty Q_v, which must exist").

  • (2) Single token holder: Inv.tok_unique; the holder clause of the invariant says who holds the token in each stage.

  • (3) Global consistency: every node's log of decoded payloads is a prefix of the global sequence ds of the payloads of the started epochs, i.e. every node decodes in epoch k the payload sent by T_k (Inv.log_prefix). Of the sent transcript P_{u,k} we formalize the clockwise part (one request and d_j data pulses per epoch, Common.cwS), not the exact interleaving with the counterclockwise pulses.

  • The paper's proof is an induction over epochs with real time; ours is a single inductive invariant over all reachable configurations (three stages, pulse counters as functions of local states, a "wave" condition for the end pulse, uniqueness of the counterclockwise pulse in transit).

Remark 2 (no starvation)

  • Proved (no_starvation): a node with a non-empty queue eventually broadcasts the message at its head. The proof uses the potential "counterclockwise distance from the current holder to u, plus the number of epochs", which never increases once a token phase has started with u's queue non-empty (TokenAhead, capture_cdist).

Theorem 3 (two-party impossibility)

  • Parties are general deterministic state machines over an arbitrary message alphabet; the adversary may replace each delivered message by any element of the alphabet. The reduction to pulse counts is proved (the paper says "w.l.o.g.").

  • "Gives output" = both parties eventually irrevocably output in every fair execution; termination is covered by this requirement only and not modelled separately.

  • The paper leaves the existence of a fair execution implicit; it is proved (round robin, rr_isFairExec).

  • The remark that revocable outputs make the problem solvable is proved for the paper's concrete protocol with X = Y = ℕ (estimateProtocol_stabilizes).

Robbins cycles

  • Definition (RobbinsCycle): a closed walk C : ZMod L → V with consecutive vertices adjacent, through every vertex, never traversing an edge in both directions — the paper's definition. Existence for finite, connected, bridgeless graphs with at least two vertices (RobbinsCycle.exists_of_bridgeless, which also traverses every edge) and the converse (nonempty_iff). The paper cites Robbins (1939); the proof goes through a strongly connected orientation built by ears.

Algorithm 3 (simulator on a Robbins cycle, Theorem 6 [Thm 10/12], Lemma 11)

  • Proved: safety (Lemma 11 (2), (3), the token-segment invariant of Challenge 1, the simulation theorem [Thm 12] in the same form as for the cycle) and deadlock freedom (Reach.deadlock_free: if no step of the network layer is enabled, every node idles at line 1 with an empty queue and has decoded every payload, and no pulse is in transit). Not proved: liveness/no starvation under fairness (Lemma 11(1) beyond deadlock freedom), Lemmas 13, 14.

  • Channels are counters per ordered pair of nodes; a node reads one pulse at a time from a channel it waits on. At lines 1, 8 and the until test of line 37 the paper does not restrict the sender; we accept any channel of the right direction (clockwise: x → u used by the cycle; counterclockwise: u → x used by the cycle). The direction of a channel is well defined because a Robbins cycle never uses an edge in both directions.

  • Occurrences are cycle positions; the numbering of u's occurrences is stored as the position o of occurrence 0; RotateEdges() is o ← prevOcc o. Program points carry occurrence positions instead of indices i.

  • Line 3 ("wait until a request pulse is received on each prev_{u,i}") counts request pulses per physical channel with multiplicity (several occurrences may share a channel); extra pulses stay buffered and are read at line 8.

  • Ghost variables ds, τ (position of the token) and wv (the channel of the unique token/data/end pulse in transit).

  • As for Algorithm 1: unary encoding only; the holder appends its own payload to its log.

  • The paper's proof of Lemma 11 is a sketch ("repeat the proof of Lemma 5"); it does not address why a node still in its data phase cannot read a next-epoch request pulse at its occurrence 0 (EndStage.read). The claim is true.

  • Model checking (modelcheck/alg3.py) on Robbins cycles with up to 5 vertices, repeated edges and nodes with 3 occurrences found no violation of safety or deadlock freedom.
