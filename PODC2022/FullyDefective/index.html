Distributed Computations in Fully-Defective Networks — PODC 2022

Keren Censor-Hillel, Shir Cohen, Ran Gelles, Gal Sela. Distributed Computations in Fully-Defective Networks. PODC 2022. DOI 10.1145/3519270.3538432. Verdict: ✅ Correct. We found no mistakes, but the paper's main result (Theorem 2) is only partly verified. The simple-cycle simulator (Algorithm 1, Theorem 4, Lemma 5, [Theorem 6]) is proved correct (safety, liveness under fair scheduling, no starvation and quiescence), the two-party impossibility (Theorem 3) holds as stated, Robbins cycles exist exactly in the 2-edge-connected graphs, and for the Robbins-cycle simulator (Algorithm 3, Theorem 6 [Theorems 10, 12]) safety and deadlock freedom are proved: single token holder, global consistency, consistent rotations, and the simulation of π. Its liveness under fairness is not formalized, nor are the distributed construction of the Robbins cycle (Section 5), the binary encoding and the complexity bounds.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Network. Asynchronous; every channel is fully defective, so only the existence of a message matters and a link is a pair of pulse counters. Channels are not FIFO.

  • Nodes. Deterministic. A wait until is a blocking receive on the named direction or edge.

  • Robbins cycles. For Algorithm 3, a node reads one pulse at a time from a channel it waits on. Channels are counters per ordered pair. A node's rotation is stored as the position of its occurrence 0.

  • Rings. Algorithm 1 runs on the oriented ring ZMod n, for every n ≥ 1, including n = 1, 2. A node knows its two neighbours.

  • The simulated protocol π (Protocol.lean): deterministic, event-driven and asynchronous; it may send to any node. It is a black box. Every message delivered to π is processed after an arbitrary delay, and its outputs are enqueued at once. Messages are encoded by any injective map into the positive integers (unary lengths).

  • Simulation. At every reachable configuration of the simulator there is a reachable configuration of π over the noiseless network with the same states and the same local transcripts at all nodes. Its in-transit messages are exactly those sent but not yet processed.

  • Liveness. Executions are infinite (stutters allowed) and weakly fair: every node step, and every π processing step, that stays enabled is eventually taken. In such executions every message sent by π is eventually processed. A round-robin scheduler is weakly fair, so the hypotheses are satisfiable.

  • Theorem 3. Two deterministic event-driven parties with any message alphabet. The adversary rewrites every delivered message. Executions are fair; outputs are irrevocable.

Proof remarks. The paper's proofs are informal, real-time inductions over epochs; the formal proofs need some invariants that the paper uses without stating them. Three points are only implicit in the paper and needed real work:

  • Why the token is caught (Lemma 5(1)). The paper says the token travels "until it reaches a node v with a non-empty Q_v, which must exist". Two invariants make this precise. First, a request pulse of the current token phase implies a node with a non-empty queue: the initiator keeps its message until it captures the token. Second, the released token has not passed such a node. Together they bound the token's hops by n. (Inv2, progress_step)

  • Lemma 11 [full version] is a sketch ("we can now repeat the proof of Lemma 5"). The formal invariant for Algorithm 3 needs facts the sketch does not mention. The token always lies in segment 0 of every node (RobbinsSim.Inv.rot; the paper states this one). At most one token, data or end pulse is in transit, and the ghost variable wv names its channel. A node still in its data phase cannot read a request pulse of the next epoch at its occurrence 0 (RobbinsSim.EndStage.read).

  • Implicit model conventions. Several conventions are only implicit. A wait until is a blocking receive: pulses from other directions stay buffered. Unary payloads must be positive. Fairness is needed for liveness. Theorem 3's proof also needs a fair execution to exist; we prove that a round-robin schedule gives one (TwoParty.Protocol.rr_isFairExec).

Not formalized. These parts are not formalized:

  • liveness and no starvation of Algorithm 3 under fairness (only deadlock freedom is proved);

  • the binary encoding (Algorithm 2, Lemmas 8–9), which was only model-checked;

  • all communication-complexity bounds (CC_init, CC_overhead, Lemmas 7, 9, 13, 14, 19);

  • the distributed construction of a Robbins cycle (Section 5: Algorithms 4–6, Theorem 15);

  • the reduction from networks with a bridge to Theorem 3;

  • [Remark 1] (the derived noiseless scheduler is FIFO; our noiseless model is non-FIFO) and [Remark 3] (broadcast).

Hence Theorem 2 is covered only for a given Robbins cycle, not for the preprocessing phase.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: a weakly fair execution of the simulator exists (Cycle.roundRobin_fair), and so does a fair two-party execution (TwoParty.Protocol.exists_fairExec); there is a concrete Robbins cycle (triangleRobbinsCycle). Before the proofs, we model-checked Algorithms 1, 2 and 3 exhaustively on small rings and Robbins cycles. This includes repeated edges and nodes with three occurrences. No violation was found. The checkers are in modelcheck/ and are not part of the Lean development. The content-oblivious model is new: CSLib's ContentOblivious rings have two ports per node and nodes that act only on start-up or on consuming a pulse, while the simulators need environment events (π enqueues messages at any time), spontaneous steps (line 1 fires on a non-empty queue) and, for Robbins cycles, arbitrary degree. CSLib candidates: Protocol.lean (generic asynchronous protocol with transcripts) and Robbins' theorem in orientation form with its helpers (exists_strongOrientation, exists_closed_chain, reachable_of_steps in Robbins.lean).
