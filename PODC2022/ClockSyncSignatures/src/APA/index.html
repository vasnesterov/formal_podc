Every node `w` has broadcast its value with an instance of crusader broadcast (CB), so it holds a vector `o w : P → Option ℝ` (`o w d` is the output of the CB instance with dealer `d`; `none` = `⊥`). Algorithm APA (Figure 1) then lets `b` be the number of `⊥` outputs, sorts the non-`⊥` values, discards the `f - b` lowest and the `f - b` highest ones and outputs the midpoint of the interval spanned by the remaining values. This file proves the combinatorial core, assuming only the two properties of crusader broadcast (Definition 6): validity and crusader consistency (`IsCrusader`). The protocol-level statement (signatures, two synchronous rounds) is in `Protocol.lean`. * `lemma_7`: replacing one `⊥` by any value `x` shrinks the retained interval. * `lemma_8`: the retained intervals of all honest nodes have a common point, proved by induction on the total number of `⊥` outputs at honest nodes, as in the paper (the induction step must replace a `⊥` by the value the other honest nodes received, to stay within executions satisfying crusader consistency). * `apa_step`: the outputs stay in every interval containing the honest inputs and are pairwise within half its length (Theorem 9, step form); `theorem_9_core`: the `(ℓ, ℓ/2)` form. -/ open Multiset set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false noncomputable section namespace Podc.ClockSyncSignatures variable {P : Type*}
