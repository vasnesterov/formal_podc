Values are `Bool`, every value is valid, `q = ⌈(n+t+1)/2⌉ = 4`. Inputs: `p3` has `false`, `p4` and `p0` have `true`. * **Phase 1** (Byzantine `p1`): proposes `true`; the three correct processes have `commit = ⊥` and vote. With the two Byzantine votes `p1` holds `QC_commit(true)` of level 1, which it keeps. * **Phase 2** (Byzantine `p2`): proposes `false`; the correct processes still have `commit = ⊥` and vote, so the adversary now also holds `QC_commit(false)` of **level 2**. In round 3, `p2` sends the old `QC_commit(true)` of level 1: its level `1 ≥ commit_level = 0`, so every correct process sends `⟨decide, true, 2⟩` and sets `commit = true`, `commit_level = 1`. `p2` forms `QC_finalized(true)` and sends it to `p0` only: **`p0` decides `true`**. * **Phase 3** (correct `p3`, undecided): `p3` proposes; the correct processes answer with their commit (level 1), and Byzantine `p1` answers with `QC_commit(false)` of level 2. `p3` relays the commit of maximal level, `false`/level 2; `2 ≥ 1`, so every correct process sends `⟨decide, false, 3⟩`; with the Byzantine decide messages `p3` forms and broadcasts `QC_finalized(false)`: **`p3` and `p4` decide `false`**. * Phases 4 and 5 are led by decided correct processes and are silent. No correct process asks for help and the fallback never runs.
