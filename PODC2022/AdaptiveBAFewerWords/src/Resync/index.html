import Papers.PODC2022.AdaptiveBAFewerWords.StrongBA /-! # Lemma 18 of [CKS22]: running a synchronous protocol from skewed start times [CKS22] S. Cohen, I. Keidar, A. Spiegelman. *Make Every Word Count: Adaptive Byzantine Agreement with Fewer Words.* PODC 2022; full version arXiv:2202.09123v2, Appendix B, Lemma 18. A synchronous protocol `A` is started by every correct process at a local time `s_p`, the start times being at most `δ` apart; rounds last `2δ`, so round `r` (counted from `0`) of `p` starts at `t^p_r = s_p + 2δ·r` (`roundStart`), and a correct process sends its round-`r` messages at `t^p_r`. Lemma 18 says that a round-`r` message of a correct process, with delay `≤ δ`, arrives at every correct receiver `q` in the window `[t^q_r − δ, t^q_r + 2δ]` in which `q` accepts round-`r` messages; in particular it has arrived when `q` ends round `r` at `t^q_{r+1} = t^q_r + 2δ` (`lemma18`, `lemma18_processed`). Hence the correct processes behave as in a lock-step run. What the lemma does *not* give (and the paper does not discuss): * the windows of rounds `r` and `r + 1` overlap, and a message may arrive before its receiver starts the round; messages must carry round numbers and be buffered (`windows_overlap`); * the adversary is stronger than the usual rushing adversary of a lock-step run: a Byzantine process may receive the round-`(r+1)` messages of an early starter and still deliver a round-`r` message to a late starter inside its round-`r` window (`skew_lookahead`).
