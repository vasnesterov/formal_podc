Make Every Word Count: Adaptive BA with Fewer Words — PODC 2022

Shir Cohen, Idit Keidar, Alexander Spiegelman. Brief Announcement: Make Every Word Count: Adaptive Byzantine Agreement with Fewer Words. PODC 2022. DOI 10.1145/3519270.3538458. Formalized version: the full version, arXiv 2202.09123v2 (January 2024, OPODIS 2022). Numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ❌ Intent lost. The headline claim is the first Byzantine Broadcast with O(n(f+1)) words for n = 2t + 1. It rests on the adaptive weak BA of the full version (Section 6). The version cited by the BA (v1) was withdrawn by the authors, and the corrected one (v2) violates agreement. Two independent attacks break v2:

  • an old commit certificate relayed by a Byzantine leader (two Byzantine processes; also when f < (n−t−1)/2);

  • a decision obtained from a help answer that is never passed on to the fallback (one Byzantine process, n = 3).

What does hold:

  • The BB-from-weak-BA reduction (Theorem 4), after a one-line fix of Algorithm 2.

  • Its O(n(f+1)) cost.

  • The binary strong BA with O(n) words when f = 0 (Theorem 7), modulo pseudocode slips.

Which version is judged. The label judges the claims of the PODC brief announcement (BA, 3 pages, June 2022).

  • The BA claims "the first Byzantine Broadcast algorithm with O(n(f+1)) word complexity" for n = 2t + 1 (abstract, Section 3, Conclusions). It contains the definitions, the reduction BB → weak BA (Algorithms 1–2, identical to v2's, including line 23) and no proofs. The adaptive weak BA that the headline needs is "presented in the full version [7]" (arXiv 2202.09123), which at that time was v1 (February 2022).

  • v1's weak BA differs from v2's: it has no commit levels. A process commits to the first commit certificate it receives, but sends decide for every certificate a leader relays. It runs t + 1 phases, and after the fallback it sets decision ← ⊥ at processes that had already decided. Elsheimy et al. found an error in it, and the authors replaced it in v2 (January 2024, "the weak BA algorithm has been corrected").

  • We formalized v2, the authors' corrected version; the numbering follows it. The Lean counterexamples refute v2. For v1 we checked by hand (not in Lean) that both attacks below also apply; see the v1 notes. So the headline of the BA rested on an algorithm the authors withdrew, and its replacement fails too.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Theorem 5 / Lemma 15 / Lemma 20 (Algorithms 3–4, agreement of weak BA)

Algorithm 3 violates agreement (theorem5_false). The run has n = 5, t = 2, Byzantine p1 and p2, and the fallback never runs:

  • Byzantine p1 (phase 1) forms QC_commit(true) at level 1 and keeps it.

  • Byzantine p2 (phase 2) collects votes for false, so it holds QC_commit(false) at level 2. It then relays the old level-1 certificate on true, finalizes true, and gives the finalize certificate to p0 only.

  • In phase 3 the correct leader p3 receives the level-2 commit on false from p1 and relays it as the highest level. Everybody accepts (2 ≥ 1), and p3 and p4 decide false.

Impact. The weak BA (Theorem 5) is refuted, so the paper's adaptive BB (Section 5 + Section 6) is not established. The same attack works with n = 11, t = 5 and only f = 2 < (n−t−1)/2 Byzantine processes (theorem5_false_small_f). This is the regime the paper calls adaptive. There the fallback can never repair anything, because no correct process ever asks for help. The run works for t + 1 or n phases and for both readings of line 15 and of the leader guard.

Details

The proof of Lemma 15 says that the processes that send decide in phase l set commit_level = l. Line 47, however, sets commit_level to the level of the certificate the leader relayed, which can be older than certificates the adversary has formed in the meantime. Correct processes with commit = ⊥ vote in every phase. So a Byzantine leader can harvest a commit certificate of a higher level on another value and withhold it. lemma15_false shows that two correct processes update decision in invokePhase to different values, and that finalize certificates on both values can be formed.

Line 33 says "for the first time". We read it per phase, as the proof of Lemma 16 requires. If it means "vote at most once in the whole run", this attack disappears, but then Lemma 16 and Lemma 6 fail: a Byzantine leader spends everybody's only vote, and nobody ever decides in the phases (lemma16_lemma6_false_voteOnce, f = 1). The attack of the next item breaks agreement under both readings.

Repair

We know no local fix. Setting commit_level to the current phase on acceptance blocks this attack, but it makes correct processes hold commits of different levels. Then a correct leader's relay is rejected by some of them, so Lemma 16 fails. A sound adaptive protocol for this setting is the subject of later work (Elsheimy et al., SODA 2024); we did not formalize one.

v1 (the version the BA cites; by hand, not in Lean). v1 has no commit levels, and its line 43 sends decide for every commit certificate the leader relays. So the attack is even simpler. Take n = 7, t = 3, Byzantine p1, p2, p4, so q = 6; the t + 1 = 4 phases are led by p1–p4.

  1. p1 and p2 harvest QC_commit(true) and QC_commit(false) from the votes of phases 1 and 2.

  2. p2 relays QC_commit(true), finalizes it with all decide messages, and gives it to p0 only.

  3. The correct leader p3 of phase 3 gets only the 4 correct decide messages, fewer than 6.

  4. p4 relays QC_commit(false) in phase 4. All correct processes send decide again (v1 line 43 is unconditional), and p4 finalizes false for p3, p5, p6.

This breaks v1's Lemma A.2, the counterpart of Lemma 15.

❌ Lemma 19 (Algorithm 3, hand-off of decisions to the fallback)

A decision taken at line 14, on a help answer, is never sent to the other processes with its proof. So the fallback can contradict it (lemma19_false: n = 3, t = 1, one Byzantine process).

Impact. This is a second, independent agreement violation of Theorem 5. It holds for every reading of lines 1, 15 and 33. The fix is probably local, but we did not prove a repaired Algorithm 3, since the previous item refutes it anyway.

Details

The run proceeds as follows:

  1. The Byzantine leader of phase 1 obtains QC_finalized(true) and keeps it.

  2. Afterwards no quorum forms without it, and both correct processes ask for help.

  3. Each correct process receives t + 1 help requests, broadcasts its fallback certificate with decision = undecided (line 11), and so never echoes (line 21).

  4. The Byzantine process answers only p2, with ⟨help, true, QC_finalized(true)⟩, and p2 decides true.

  5. p0 never sees true with a proof. It runs the fallback on its input false (or on undecided), while p2 runs it on true.

  6. Strong unanimity allows the output false, for example interactive consistency plus majority with the Byzantine input false, and p0 decides false.

The proof claims that "in the 2δ window … they receive v from p". The code broadcasts the fallback message before line 14, and a process that echoes later sends bu_proof = ⊥.

Repair (not formalized). After deciding at line 14, broadcast ⟨fallback, QC, v, decide_proof⟩ again.

v1 (by hand). Lines 5–24 of v1's Algorithm 3 are identical, and v1's Lemma A.6 has the same proof, so the same run breaks v1. The phases play out the same way: commits only to the first certificate, which is the same here, and t + 1 = 2 phases. In v1, p2 even ends with ⊥, because v1's lines 25–28 set decision ← ⊥ at every process that had decided before the fallback.

🔧 Lemmas 9 and 11 (Algorithm 2, line 23)

A correct leader relays only a value signed by the sender. A correct process holding an idk certificate answers with that certificate, which is neither sender-signed nor idk. So a correct leader may relay nothing, and correct processes enter the weak BA with ⊥ (lemma9_false, lemma11_false: n = 3, t = 1, Byzantine sender).

Impact. As printed, the precondition of the weak BA (valid inputs) fails, so the proof of Theorem 4 breaks. The key fact of Section 5.1, that later correct-led phases are silent, also fails (keyFact_false, n = 5, f = 1). A one-line fix saves everything.

Repair

Line 23 becomes "if received ⟨v′, j⟩ with BB_valid(v′)". With this rule we proved:

  • Lemmas 9–12 (lemma9, lemma10, lemma11, lemma12_values, lemma12_avail);

  • Theorem 4/13 for any black-box weak BA (theorem13);

  • the O(n(f+1)) bound n(f + 4) for round 1 and the phases (BB.words_le).

🔧 Lemma 16 (last clause)

"This decision is a proposal of a correct process" is false: a correct leader relays the highest commit it receives, and that commit may be on a value proposed only by a Byzantine leader.

Impact. Nothing uses this clause. The rest of Lemma 16 is proved (lemma16, per-phase reading of line 33).

✏️ Slips

  • Algorithm 3 line 1 runs t + 1 phases, but the text, Lemma 6 and Section 6.1 say n. With t + 1 phases Lemma 6 fails: n = 7, f = 1, and the fallback runs (lemma6_false_printed_phases). With n phases the same adversary is harmless (lemma6_n_phases_ok), and Lemma 6 is proved (lemma6). In v1, t + 1 phases was the design (text and proof of v1's Lemma 6.2 say so), so the run of lemma6_false_printed_phases (one commit certificate, so v1 behaves the same) refutes v1's Lemma 6.2: there, the guaranteed correct leader may already have decided and stay silent.

  • Algorithm 3 line 15 sets bu_decision ← decision, so undecided processes enter the fallback with undecided. Unique validity then fails with a single valid value (lemma22_false). The proof of Lemma 22 assumes the inputs; with that reading the run is fine (lemma22_intended_ok).

  • Algorithm 5 initializes bu_decision = ⊥ and sets it to decision unconditionally (line 19). The proof of Lemma 28 uses the inputs. We formalize the inputs.

  • Algorithm 5 line 26 echoes bu_proof, which is ⊥ for a decided process, so others cannot adopt its value. Agreement fails (printed_noProof_disagreement). The text says decided processes attach their proof.

  • while fallback_start > now (Algorithm 5 line 20; Algorithm 3 line 16) drops a message arriving exactly at fallback_start. With all delays exactly δ this breaks agreement (strictWindow_disagreement). We process the closed window.

  • The leader's rounds 3 and 5 of Algorithm 4 and round 3 of Algorithm 2 are not guarded by "the leader proposed / asked for help". A Byzantine process can then make every "silent" phase cost Θ(n) words (unguarded_silent_phases_cost: 132 words against the bound 49). The text says silent phases send nothing, and we formalize the guarded reading.

  • BB_valid(v), "signed by the sender or by t + 1 processes", must mean "sender-signed or an idk certificate". Read literally, the weak BA's own certificates would be valid values.

  • Definition 3 (weak BA) leaves implicit two assumptions that the reduction uses: correct inputs are valid, and decided values exist in the run.

Typos

  • Algorithm 3 line 2 does not pass decide_proof to invokePhase and overwrites it with later phases' results.

  • Algorithm 3 line 11 sends an undefined proof (read as decide_proof). Algorithm 1's header has vsenedr.

  • The proof of Lemma 27 speaks of a "certificate" that Algorithm 5 does not have. Lemma 25 has no proof ("similar to Lemma 17"); we proved it (lemma25).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Processes and adversary. Processes are Fin n, with n = 2t + 1; the reduction is proved for n ≥ 2t + 1. A static Byzantine set F has |F| ≤ t. All three algorithms are deterministic, so an adaptive adversary is simulated by corrupting the final faulty set from the start.

  • Rounds. Synchronous lock-step rounds, with messages received in the round they are sent. Algorithm 5's fallback hand-off uses real discrete time instead: delays in [0, δ], and first-hearing times computed by reachability.

  • Signatures. Ideal (threshold) signatures are symbolic. Unforgeability is a validity condition on the adversary (AdvValid): a certificate can be used only if enough shares are available, either Byzantine or sent by correct processes. The adversary is rushing and eavesdropping. The counterexamples only use shares sent to Byzantine processes.

  • States. Correct states are computed from the adversary's messages.

  • Black boxes.

    • The weak BA in the reduction is specified by Agreement, Termination and Unique validity, plus "decided values exist" (WeakBASpec).

    • A_fallback is a strong BA: if all correct processes run it, their common output satisfies strong unanimity w.r.t. their inputs (Admissible, FallbackSpec).

  • Lemma 18 (re-synchronisation). We prove its timing content (lemma18, lemma18_processed, lemma18_alg5). That the skewed run is "a correct run of A" is part of the black-box assumption. Two facts matter here: round windows overlap, so messages need round tags (windows_overlap); and a Byzantine process can see an early starter's round-r+1 messages before its round-r message reaches a late starter (skew_lookahead). This adversary is stronger than a rushing lock-step adversary, and the paper does not argue that Momose–Ren tolerates it.

  • Word counts. One word per message; messages to oneself are included.
