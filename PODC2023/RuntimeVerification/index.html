Wait-Free Runtime Verification of Linearizability — PODC 2023

Armando Castañeda, Gilde Valeria Rodríguez. Asynchronous Wait-Free Runtime Verification and Enforcement of Linearizability. PODC 2023. DOI 10.1145/3583668.3594563. Formalized version: arXiv 2301.02638 v3 (Nov 2025, full version); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All main results hold: the impossibility of runtime verification (Theorem 5.1), the impossibility of predictive runtime verification (Theorem A.1, under the black-box reading of the model), the predictive verifier for DRV and every O ∈ GenLin (Theorem 8.1) and self-enforced implementations (Theorem 8.2). Lemma 7.4 is false as stated: it fails for pending operations that no view has seen yet, and the proofs of Lemmas 8.1/8.2 routinely meet such operations, but their statements are still true once the comparison is made with a prefix, and we prove them that way. The progress and step-complexity claims were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Lemma 7.4 (tight execution vs. its sketch)

Lemma 7.4 claims that every tight execution E is equivalent to its sketch X(λ_E), but this fails when a pending operation has written to N and no view has seen it yet, i.e. its N.Write comes after every N.Snapshot (lemma_7_4_false).

Impact. The proofs of Lemmas 8.1 and 8.2 meet such operations routinely. The statements of Lemmas 8.1/8.2, and all main results, are true; we prove them with a prefix comparison.

Details

Such an operation is missing from X(λ_E). Example: a process completes one operation, then invokes a second one and writes it to N. The proofs of Lemmas 8.1 and 8.2 apply Lemma 7.4 to executions in which such operations are normal: the execution F of Lemma 8.1, obtained by removing snapshot steps, and T(E'|A*) in Lemma 8.2.

Repair

Compare with a prefix: truncate at the last relevant snapshot and use prefix closure. (Exec.sketch_mem_of_tight, Exec.cut_mem_of_sketch, sketch_isAStarHist)

🔧 Claim 7.2 (for all implementations)

Claim 7.2 is stated for all implementations, but it fails for abstract ones (sets of histories).

Impact. It holds for A*, the only implementation it is used for.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 8.2 (2) needs O ≠ ∅.

  • Theorem 8.2 (3) states the direction of similarity backwards.

  • Proof of Lemma 7.3: "E and T(E) are equivalent" should be E' and T(E).

  • Proof of Lemma 8.2: H should be a prefix of X(τ'').

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Histories. We use CSLib's single-object Herlihy–Wing histories. Linearizability is CSLib's amended definition (Sela–Herlihy–Petrank), which is the paper's Definition 4.2. Similarity (Def. 7.1) is defined literally (Obtain, Similar) and shown equivalent to a semantic form (similar_iff_simS). GenLin (Def. 7.2) means closed under prefixes and similarity.

  • Impossibility. n ≥ 2 asynchronous processes run a verifier with the structure of Fig. 2.

    • Lines 03–05 and 08–12 are atomic transitions on an arbitrary shared memory, so any base objects are allowed, of any consensus number. They may read a global clock.

    • The environment chooses operations; each process chooses each operation at most once.

    • The input A is an abstract implementation: a prefix-closed set of well-formed histories. It answers within this set.

    • Executions are infinite and fair: each process runs infinitely many iterations. There are no crashes; wait-freedom is reflected in the atomic blocks.

    • The verifier does not depend on A (black box). thm_5_1_nonuniform and trivialVerifier_predictive treat the other reading.

  • Positive part. An execution of V_O (Fig. 10) running A* (Fig. 7) over a black-box A is given by:

    • the operations each process invokes;

    • A's responses, which must form a history of the prefix-closed set 𝒜;

    • injective times of the 8 steps of each operation: A* invocation, N.Write, A invocation, A response, N.Snapshot, A* response, M.Write, M.Snapshot + test.

    Snapshots are atomic and return everything written before them. Executions of A* alone have 6 steps per operation. Histories of A*, of A, of the tight execution T(E) and of V_{O,A} are timed histories (TimedHist.lean); their list versions are obtained by sorting by time. The sketch X(τ) of Section 7.3.3 is the timed history XTH τ.

  • Infinite executions (thm_8_1_inf). A process may block forever inside A. The two standing assumptions are hypotheses:

    • a process that took its N.Snapshot also writes M;

    • tests occur at arbitrarily late times.

Reading of Section 3. Section 3 says the black-box requirement is "modeled as conceptually V_O taking A as its input". The intended reading is that one verifier works for every A (∃V ∀A), which is what we formalize. If V_O could depend on A, Definition 6.1 would be trivially solvable (Impossibility.trivialVerifier_predictive, a remark).

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The model builds on CSLib's Herlihy–Wing histories (Cslib/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/HerlihyWing/).

Non-vacuity:

  • linearizability w.r.t. any state machine is in GenLin (lemma_7_1);

  • the impossibility instances exhibit concrete objects satisfying the hypotheses;

  • lemma_7_4_false is a concrete execution.
