The MBroadcast Abstraction — PODC 2023

Mathilde Déprés, Achour Mostéfaoui, Matthieu Perrin, Michel Raynal. Brief Announcement: The MBroadcast Abstraction. PODC 2023. DOI 10.1145/3583668.3594569. Formalized version: the brief announcement (Theorems 1, 2, Property 1). For the converse direction we used the algorithm and proof of the extended version (DISC 2023, Send/Receive Patterns Versus Read/Write Patterns in Crash-Prone Asynchronous Distributed Systems, Algorithm 2 and App. A), since the brief announcement gives no algorithm for it.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ✏️ Slip. Every result we checked holds: Property 1, Theorem 1 (Algorithm 2 builds MBroadcast from causal message delivery when t < n/2), Theorem 2 (Algorithm 3 builds a linearizable, terminating MWMR register on top of MBroadcast, for any number of crashes), and the converse (MBroadcast, even a FIFO and reliable version, from atomic SWMR registers, for any number of crashes). The only flaw is the abstract's wording of mutual ordering, "p delivers first m and then m′": read literally (p delivers both messages), this property is too weak, and then Property 1 and Theorem 2 are false. Everything holds under the wording of Section 3, with the reading the paper itself uses elsewhere for "deliver m′ before m".

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Abstract and Section 1, paraphrase of mutual ordering (the meaning of "before"). The main text says "it is not possible that p mdelivers m before m′ and p′ mdelivers m′ before m". The abstract paraphrases it as "it is not possible for p to deliver first m and then m′ while p′ delivers first m′ and then m". The pattern MP1 of the full version is also drawn with both processes delivering both messages. The results need the stronger reading: "when p mdelivers its m, it has not (yet, or ever) mdelivered m′". This is the reading of the paper's own FIFO and causal properties ("no process mdelivers m′ before m"), of Property 1 ("p′ cannot return … before it has mdelivered m") and of the introduction ("p′ does not ignore p"). We formalize the strong reading (DelivBefore).

    Under the literal reading both claims fail. Take two processes. p₀ writes 1 with Algorithm 3: it mbroadcasts synch and write(1, ⟨1, p₀⟩), mdelivers both itself, returns, and crashes. Then p₁ reads: its synch and its write-back of (0, ⊥) are mdelivered only by p₁ itself, and the read returns 0. No process mdelivers both messages of a pair, so the literal mutual ordering holds vacuously. Validity and no-duplication hold too, and Global CS-termination is respected since p₀ is faulty. Yet Property 1 fails, and the history "write(1) completes, then read() returns 0" is not linearizable (property1_false_weak, alg3_weak_not_linearizable). Under the strong reading the same trace violates mutual ordering (weakTrace_not_mutualOrdering).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Specification (Spec.lean). An MBroadcast trace is the real-time list of events bc p m and dl p q m (p mdelivers m from q). Validity, No-duplication, Mutual ordering (strong reading), FIFO and causal ordering are properties of finite traces. Messages are identified by (sender, message), each mbroadcast at most once (the paper's "all messages are different", UniqueBc).

  • Theorem 2 (Register*.lean): CAMP_{n,t}[MB-broadcast].

    • Asynchronous crash-prone processes (any linear order of identities), communicating only through MBroadcast.

    • The layer is modelled by its specification: any process may mdeliver any message at any time. Safety is proved for every execution whose MBroadcast trace satisfies the safety properties (they are prefix-closed), i.e. for every implementation of MBroadcast.

    • Termination assumes weak fairness of local steps and Global CS-termination of the layer.

    • Each code block between two waits is atomic; line 2 may choose any date with ⟨d, i⟩ > clockᵢ.

    • Atomicity = CSLib's linearizability (IsLinearization) for register V v₀.

    • Any number of crashes.

  • Theorem 1 (Causal*.lean): CAMP_{n,t}[t < n/2] with causal delivery.

    • Reliable, non-FIFO channels, crash failures.

    • Causal delivery: a message is received only after every message causally preceding it with the same destination (one causal order across causal_broadcast and causal_send).

    • One append-only log of network and MBroadcast events; a causal broadcast is one atomic step and is never lost. Safety covers the paper's partial broadcasts, as finite prefixes.

    • Safety holds for any number of actual crashes provided 2t < n. Liveness assumes weak fairness and at most t crashes.

  • Converse (FromRegisters*.lean): CARW_{n,t}[∅].

    • n asynchronous processes, n atomic SWMR registers SENT[i] (the full version needs only regular ones), any number of crashes.

    • One step per register access (the local lines 6–7 together with the read). An invocation together with line 1 is one step, taken when no catch_up runs.

    • Liveness: every correct process takes infinitely many steps.

  • No randomness, no timing assumptions.

Proof remarks. The proofs of the full version for Theorem 2 (timestamps plus cycle-freeness) and for the register-based construction (write-then-read) go through as written. The brief announcement gives no proof of Theorem 1; ours follows the quorum-intersection argument the paper suggests. Small observations, none of them errors:

  • Algorithm 3 needs only validity and mutual ordering of the layer, not no-duplication (alg3_linearizable_of_validity_mutualOrdering).

  • The liveness part of Theorem 1 needs only "at most t crashes", not t < n/2.

  • The register-based Algorithm 2 of the full version is FIFO but not causal (FromRegisters.not_causalOrdering; the paper does not claim it is). The paper's remark that the causal variant has the same power is still true: we give a variant of our own that is causal (see the table).

Not formalized.

  • The message-complexity remark ("O(n) implementation messages").

  • The equivalence of reliable broadcast and causal broadcast with variants of MBroadcast, which is definitional.

  • A generic composition theorem ("same computability power" as a statement about simulations). We prove the two constructions separately.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity checks:

  • Alg3.alg3_nonvacuous: a write followed by a read returning the written value, whose MBroadcast trace is safe.

  • Causal.example_reachable (n = 3, t = 1).

  • FromRegisters.exampleExec_delivers: a fair execution exists.

Linearizability is CSLib's (Cslib.SharedMemory.IsLinearization), through the new staged timestamp criterion Cslib.SharedMemory.TSLin (Podc/Computability/Distributed/SharedMemory/TimestampLin.lean, CSLib candidate) on top of the staged MWMRReadsFrom. CSLib candidates (-- CSLib candidate comments):

  • TSLin (TimestampLin.lean);

  • the list lemmas exists_of_getElem?_filterMap (CausalModel.lean), exists_forall_of_mono (CausalLiveness.lean), eq_of_prefix_of_nodup (FromRegistersTrace.lean), getElem?_of_prefix (FromRegistersCausalVariantLiveness.lean).
