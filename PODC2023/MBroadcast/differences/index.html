Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The MBroadcast Abstraction — PODC 2023.

Paper: Déprés, Mostéfaoui, Perrin, Raynal, Brief Announcement: The MBroadcast Abstraction, PODC 2023. For the converse direction (MBroadcast from registers) the brief announcement gives no algorithm; we use Algorithm 2 and App. A.1 of the extended version (DISC 2023).

Definitions (Section 3, Spec.lean)

  1. Mutual ordering: reading of "before" (✏️). The paper (main text): "it is not possible that p mdelivers m before m′ and p′ mdelivers m′ before m"; abstract/introduction: "it is not possible for p to deliver first (its message) m and then m′ while p′ delivers first m′ and then m". We formalize the reading used by the paper's own FIFO/causal definitions and by Property 1: "when p mdelivers m, it has not (yet, or ever) mdelivered m′" (DelivBefore). The literal reading of the abstract ("p mdelivers both, m first", WeakMutualOrdering) is strictly weaker, and with it Property 1 and Theorem 2 are false (property1_false_weak, alg3_weak_not_linearizable). The intent is recoverable: Property 1, Fig. 1, the "p′ does not ignore p" remark of the introduction and the paper's usage of "deliver m′ before m" in the FIFO and causal properties all force the strong reading, under which every checked statement holds. The fix is local, one phrase. The definition is not a result claim, and the paper itself fixes the intended meaning.

  2. Pairs with p = p′. "For any pair of processes p and p′" — we quantify over all (p, m) ≠ (p′, m′); the case p = p′ holds automatically (not_delivBefore_same).

  3. All mbroadcast messages are different (the paper's WLOG assumption). Messages are identified by (sender, message); UniqueBc = each pair is mbroadcast at most once. In Algorithm 3 messages carry the index of the sender's operation, which makes them distinct by construction (reads may otherwise rebroadcast the same write(v, c) twice).

  4. Liveness properties (Local termination, Global CS-/CF-termination) are stated in each model file for its notion of correct process.

  5. FIFO / causal ordering are formalized with the same reading of "before" (FifoOrdering, CausalOrdering); causal ordering is the paper's one-step version (transitivity follows).

Algorithm 3 / Theorem 2 (Register*.lean)

  1. Model of CAMP_{n,t}[MB-broadcast]. The MBroadcast layer is modelled by its specification: deliveries are unconstrained steps, and the safety theorem assumes the final MBroadcast trace is safe (prefix-closed, so every intermediate trace is). Liveness of the layer (Global CS-termination) is a hypothesis of the termination theorem. This quantifies over every implementation of MBroadcast, as the paper intends.

  2. Atomicity convention. Each block between two waits is atomic: line 1 (invW), lines 2–3 (line2), line 4 (invR), lines 5–6 (line5), returns, and the handler of line 8 atomically with the mdelivery. The end of a wait is a separate step (other deliveries may interleave), which is the general case. The convention matters for lines 5–6: valᵢ (line 5) and clockᵢ (line 6) must be read with no handler of line 8 in between, otherwise a read could mbroadcast a mismatched pair write(v_old, c_new). This is the usual convention of the authors' pseudocode (handlers do not interrupt local code), not an error.

  3. Line 2 "let tᵢ such that ⟨tᵢ, i⟩ > clockᵢ": any such date is allowed (nondeterministic d).

  4. Timestamps WithBot (Lex (ℕ × P)), ⊥ for the initial value v₀ (the paper writes ⟨0, 0⟩); process identities form any linear order.

  5. Only validity and mutual ordering are used for linearizability (alg3_linearizable_of_validity_mutualOrdering); no-duplication is not needed. No assumption on the number of crashes (paper's "Important remark").

  6. Termination is proved for weakly fair executions (a continuously enabled end-of-wait step of a correct process is eventually taken) with Global CS-termination of the layer; only "a correct process eventually mdelivers its own messages" is used. Stated with the operation counter k (= number of responses, Inv.resps). No fair execution with a pending operation is constructed in Lean for this model (as for Algorithm 2 of the brief announcement, item 18). The hypotheses are evidently satisfiable, e.g. by a round-robin scheduler where every message is mdelivered to all processes right after it is mbroadcast.

  7. Proof of Theorem 2 (full version, App. A.2) is by a cycle-freeness argument on a relation →; we use the equivalent timestamp criterion (TSLin, writes ordered by timestamp, reads after the write they read from). The key step "→₁ implies ts(o₁) ≤ ts(o₂)" is Property 1 applied to the last message of o₁ and the synch of o₂ (Inv.tok_le_clock). No gap.

Algorithm 2 of the brief announcement / Theorem 1 (Causal*.lean)

  1. Network model. Reliable, unordered channels with causal delivery for causal_broadcast and causal_send together: a message is received only after every causally preceding message with the same destination (CausalReady, ghost causal past dep). This matches the paper's definition (one causal order, relations (i)–(iii)); the proof needs it (ack(m′) sent after receiving init(m) must arrive after init(m)).

  2. Self-delivery of one's own init(m) is omitted (the handler does nothing for i = j, line 4).

  3. Atomic broadcast, no loss. A causal broadcast is one atomic step and sent messages are never lost (also for crashed senders). For safety this covers the paper's crash in the middle of a broadcast (finite prefixes); for liveness, weak fairness makes every message to a correct process whose causal predecessors were received eventually received. The proofs use this only for messages in the causal past of a correct process's send, which a piggybacking causal-delivery layer provides.

  4. Threshold n − t − 1 acks (natural subtraction); safety needs only 2t < n, for any number of actual crashes; validity and no-duplication need no bound. Liveness needs at most t crashes (no 2t < n).

  5. Distinct messages per sender (invoke p m enabled only if p never mbroadcast m); a process invokes mbroadcast only when idle.

  6. No proof in the brief announcement ("proofs in the full version"; the HAL report was not accessible, and the DISC version cites the brief announcement for this algorithm). Our proof: quorum intersection of the two ack sets plus own processes, and a case analysis on the common process (other, p, or p′) using causal delivery. The existence of fair executions is not proved for this model (it is for the register model).

MBroadcast from registers (FromRegisters*.lean)

  1. Atomic SWMR registers (the brief announcement's claim). The full version claims the algorithm works with regular registers; not checked.

  2. Atomicity. One step per register access: a read of SENT[j] together with the local lines 6–7; the invocation together with the write of line 1, enabled only when no catch_up runs (the routine is uninterruptible and the invocation has no effect before line 1).

  3. Fairness: every correct process takes infinitely many local steps (its local step is always enabled); a round-robin fair execution exists (exampleExec_delivers).

  4. Properties proved: unique broadcasts, Validity, No-duplication, Mutual ordering, FIFO, Local termination, Global CF- and CS-termination, for any number of crashes (the full version's "reliable-MBroadcast").

  5. Not causal. The algorithm does not ensure causal ordering (not_causalOrdering, 3 processes); the paper does not claim it. The brief announcement's statement that the enriched variants (fifo-, causal-, reliable-MBroadcast) "do not change its computing power" is supported for the causal variant by an algorithm of ours (FromRegisters.Causal.*): each register entry carries the sender's delivery history, which a reader mdelivers first, stopping at its own pending message. Without that stop, mutual ordering fails: p could mdeliver its own pending m, found in another process's history, before reading SENT[p′].

  6. Same computability power is not stated as a single simulation theorem; it is the conjunction of Theorem 2 and the register construction (both for any number of crashes).

Not formalized

  1. Message complexity ("O(n) implementation messages"); the remarks identifying reliable broadcast and causal broadcast with variants of MBroadcast (definitional).
