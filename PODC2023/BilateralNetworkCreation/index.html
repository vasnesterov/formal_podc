The Impact of Cooperation in Bilateral Network Creation — PODC 2023

Tobias Friedrich, Hans Gawendowicz, Pascal Lenzner, Arthur Zahn. The Impact of Cooperation in Bilateral Network Creation. PODC 2023. DOI 10.1145/3583668.3594588. Formalized version: arXiv 2207.03798 v2 (full version with appendix); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Every statement we checked is true, and the proofs of Lemmas 2.4 and 3.5 are saved by local changes: this covers Lemma 2.4, Lemma B.1, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.2, Lemmas 3.3–3.5, Theorem 3.6 (trees in BSwE have ρ ≤ 2 + 2 log α), and Propositions 3.8 and 3.9 (stretched binary trees give the matching Ω(log α) lower bound for BGE). The paper's most significant result, the constant Price of Anarchy for 3-BSE on trees, was not checked, and neither were the bounds for general networks and the refutation of the conjecture of Corbo and Parkes.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • The proof of Lemma 2.4 misstates dist_{C_n}(u) for odd n as (n-1)(n-2)/4 instead of (n²-1)/4; the bound derived from it uses the correct value.

  • The proof of Lemma 2.4 says "if every node in G' has degree 2, then G' is isomorphic to C_n", which ignores disjoint unions of cycles. Those are disconnected and excluded by the penalty M.

  • The definition of the Bilateral k-Strong Equilibrium must require a nonempty coalition; otherwise the empty coalition "improves" vacuously and no graph is in BSE.

  • With M only required to exceed α n³, disconnecting is not always worse when α is tiny (e.g. n = 3, α = 0.01, M = 1: an agent of the triangle drops both edges). This never matters under the paper's standing assumption α ≥ 1.

Typos

  • Proposition 3.9 says "for sufficiently large η". The paper's proof (with k = ⌈α/η⌉) needs a threshold that depends on γ; η^γ ≥ 512 suffices (prop_3_9). The statement also holds with the γ-independent threshold η ≥ 512 (prop_3_9_uniform): if η^γ < 512, the claimed bound is below 1 ≤ ρ, and a complete binary tree of the right size is in BGE.

Proof gaps in true statements (3)Lemma 2.4 · Lemma 2.4 · Lemma 3.5

🔧 Lemma 2.4 (the explicit α-range for odd n)

The proof claims C_n is in BSE for (n+1)(n-1)/4 - (n-1) < α < (n+1)(n-1)/4 when n is odd, but for (n-1)²/4 < α < (n²-1)/4 the cycle is not even in RE (as for C_5 with α = 5; C5_not_BSE, cycle_odd_not_BSE, cycle_not_RE).

Impact. Statement true: with the right end (n-1)²/4 the range is still nonempty and of order n², so Lemma 2.4 as stated, "for some range of α ∈ Θ(n²)", holds (cycle_BSE, lemma_2_4). For even n the paper's range is correct.

Details

The proof cites [14] for Remove Equilibrium on this range. But an agent on C_n who drops one of its two edges saves α and loses only n(n-1)/2 - (n²-1)/4 = (n-1)²/4 in distance. C_5 with α = 5 lies inside the claimed range (2, 6).

🔧 Lemma 2.4 (path case)

The proof's path case assumes that "one of the end nodes must be part of the coalition", which is unjustified: the edge may have been dropped by the other endpoint, and then both end nodes can stay outside.

Impact. Statement true (cycle_BSE, lemma_2_4).

Repair

Our proof avoids the case analysis by counting distance levels: every coalition member of degree ≤ 1 in a path, and every member of degree 2, is not better off (cycle_BSE, lemma_2_4).

🔧 Lemma 3.5 (case α ≥ n/2)

The proof starts with "if α ≥ n/2, the claim holds by definition of the 1-median r", but |T_u| ≤ n/2 ≤ α does not give |T_u| ≤ α/(ℓ(u)-1) once ℓ(u) ≥ 3.

Impact. Statement true; the case split is unnecessary: the swap argument of the main case (u swaps its parent edge for an edge to r) works for every α (lemma_3_5).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model. (Model.lean)

  • A static, one-shot game; no distributed model and no randomness. Agents = vertices of a finite type V, n = |V|. A state is the created graph G (SimpleGraph V). The paper does the same: in equilibrium nobody pays for an edge that is not built.

  • cost(u) = α · deg(u) + ∑_v d(u,v), where d is the hop distance and M is the distance between disconnected agents. Theorems assume the paper's α ≥ 1 and M > α n³ (M ∈ ℕ).

  • Solution concepts are exactly the paper's: RE (one agent drops one edge), BAE, PS, BSwE (u replaces uv by uw, and both u and w must strictly gain), BGE = PS ∧ BSwE, k-BSE (coalition moves: dropped edges touch Γ, added edges lie inside Γ), BSE = n-BSE.

  • log is log₂ (Real.logb 2), as is standard; Lemma 3.4, Theorem 3.6 and Proposition 3.9 are stated with it.

  • ρ(G) = cost(G) / cost(OPT), where cost(OPT) is the infimum over all graphs on V. We prove cost(OPT) = 2(n-1)(α+n-1) for α ≥ 1 (lower bound socialCost_ge, star optCost_le_star).

  • Trees: layers and subtrees are metric, T_u = {x | d(r,x) = d(r,u) + d(u,x)}. A 1-median minimizes the distance cost (IsMedian, IsSubMedian). The k-stretched complete binary tree is an explicit graph on Σ ℓ : Fin (dk+1), Fin 2^⌈ℓ/k⌉ (stretchedTree d k).

Prop39.lean

How Proposition 3.8 is proved. The stretched binary tree is layer-sized: |T_x| depends only on the layer and halves every k layers (LayerSized). For such trees:

  • A swap is beneficial for both agents only if w lies on v's side C_v of the edge uv, v is strictly less central in C_v than w, and |C_u| · d(v,w) > α. This uses lower bounds on distances after the swap (d_swap_ge, Cut.lean).

  • The potential Φ_S(y) = ∑_{x∈S} d(y,x) along root paths (phi_eq) shows that neither happens for α ≥ 7kn.

  • For additions, a layer-cake argument bounds the gain of the shallower endpoint by 2kn.

Remark on Lemma 3.4 (not a mistake). The proof uses "for all w ∈ T_u with ℓ(w) ≥ ℓ(v)+1, |T_w| ≤ |T_u|/2" for the T_u-1-median v, which follows from the "equivalent" component-size definition of 1-medians stated in Section 3.2 without proof. We prove the step directly from minimality: otherwise moving from v towards w (to a child of v, or to its parent if w ∉ T_v) decreases the distance cost within T_u (two_mul_card_sub_le_of_subMedian).

Not formalized. Section 2 apart from Lemma 2.4, Theorem 3.10 and Sections 3.2.3–3.3, and the auxiliary Lemmas D.2, D.3, D.5, D.6 and the case analysis of Lemma D.7: our own, shorter proof of Proposition 3.8 does not need them, and they were not checked step by step.
