Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Impact of Cooperation in Bilateral Network Creation — PODC 2023.

Paper: T. Friedrich, H. Gawendowicz, P. Lenzner, A. Zahn, The Impact of Cooperation in Bilateral Network Creation, PODC 2023 (arXiv:2207.03798v2, numbering of the full version).

Errors in the paper

  1. 🔧 Lemma 2.4, explicit range in the proof (odd n). The proof claims that C_n is in BSE for (n+1)(n-1)/4 - (n-1) < α < (n+1)(n-1)/4 when n is odd. For (n-1)²/4 < α < (n²-1)/4 the cycle is not in RE. Dropping an edge saves α and costs n(n-1)/2 - (n²-1)/4 = (n-1)²/4. cycle_odd_not_BSE proves this for every odd n ≥ 3; C5_not_BSE is the instance n = 5, α = 5, M = 626, with claimed range (2, 6). The correct right end is n(n-1)/2 - ⌊n²/4⌋ (cycleHi): (n-1)²/4 for odd n, and n(n-2)/4 for even n, which matches the paper. The explicit range appears only inside the proof, and the lemma ("for some range of α ∈ Θ(n²)") is true (lemma_2_4).

  2. Lemma 2.4, other slips in the proof.

    • ✏️ dist_{C_n}(u) = (n-1)(n-2)/4 for odd n should be (n²-1)/4; the bound derived from it uses the correct value.

    • ✏️ "If every node in G' has degree 2, then G' is isomorphic to C_n" ignores disjoint unions of cycles. Those are disconnected and excluded by the penalty M.

    • 🔧 In the path case, "one of its end nodes u must be part of the coalition" is unjustified: the end node may have lost its edge through its neighbour. We replace the case analysis by a uniform argument (Levels.lean). In a connected graph of maximum degree 2, a vertex of degree c has at most c vertices at each distance, so its distance cost is at least ⌊n²/4⌋ (c = 2), n(n-1)/2 (c = 1) or n(n-1) (c = 0). Every coalition member is then not better off. A vertex of degree ≥ 3 in G' is necessarily in the coalition, and it pays α more while saving at most ⌊n²/4⌋ - (n-1).

  3. 🔧 Lemma 3.5, case α ≥ n/2. "The claim holds by definition of the 1-median" is false reasoning for ℓ(u) ≥ 3, since |T_u| ≤ n/2 ≤ α does not imply |T_u| ≤ α/(ℓ(u)-1). The swap argument of the other case works for all α, and lemma_3_5 uses it with no case split.

  4. Lemma 3.4, unproved step. "For all w ∈ T_u with ℓ(w) ≥ ℓ(v)+1 it holds that |T_w| ≤ |T_u|/2" is justified "by definition of the T_u-1-median". It follows from the component-size characterization of 1-medians that Section 3.2 states as "equivalent" without proof (T_w does not contain v, so it lies in one component of T_u - v). We prove it from the minimality of v (two_mul_card_sub_le_of_subMedian): otherwise moving from v to a child, or to the parent, of v lowers the distance cost within T_u. Note that w need not lie below v. Not counted as a mistake: the step follows from what Section 3.2 states.

Modelling choices and imprecisions

  1. States are graphs. As in the paper, a state is the created graph and an agent pays α per incident edge. Strategy vectors with unreciprocated requests are not modelled.

  2. Distance penalty M. d M G u v = M for disconnected pairs, M ∈ ℕ. All theorems assume the paper's M > α n³ together with the paper's standing assumption α ≥ 1. Without α ≥ 1, M > α n³ does not make disconnection worse than every connected state (e.g. n = 3, α = 0.01, M = 1: a vertex of the triangle drops both edges). For n = 3 the corrected range of Lemma 2.4 is (0, 1], so with α ≥ 1 only α = 1 remains. ✏️ Not used anywhere in the paper's results.

  3. BSE needs a nonempty coalition (KBSE). Literally, the empty coalition with the empty move satisfies "all members strictly benefit", and no graph would be in BSE. ✏️ Evident intent.

  4. ρ and the social optimum. optCost is the infimum of the social cost over all graphs on V. The paper takes the optimum value 2(n-1)(α+n-1) for α ≥ 1 from [14]; we prove both bounds (socialCost_ge, optCost_le_star). Statements about ρ assume n ≥ 2; for n = 1 the ratio is 0/0. Degenerate.

  5. 1-medians. A 1-median is a vertex of minimum distance cost (IsMedian), and a T_u-1-median minimizes ∑_{x∈T_u} d(v,x) over v ∈ T_u (IsSubMedian). Distances in T_u equal distances in G because T_u is convex. We never use the paper's "equivalently, removal leaves components of size ≤ n/2" as a definition; the needed consequences (|T_u| ≤ n/2 for u ≠ r, two_mul_card_sub_le) are proved from minimality.

  6. Lemma 3.3 is stated for the minimizing T_u-1-median we construct. The paper picks, among two medians, the one closer to u; we pick u itself if it is a median, else any median. The conclusion is the paper's.

  7. Theorem 3.6 assumes α ≥ 1 (the paper's standing assumption). For α < 1 the bound 2 + 2 log α is below 1 and false, since ρ ≥ 1. It also assumes n ≥ 2 (degenerate otherwise). Trees are in RE because M ≥ α + 1 (tree_RE).

  8. Lemma B.1 and Proposition A.2. The paper invokes "RE = NE" (Prop. A.2). We prove only the direction used: in RE no agent gains by dropping any set of incident edges (RE.cost_le_delAt). The reason is that a shortest path from v uses one edge at v, so the distance increases are superadditive. The BFS tree is CSLib's bfsParent.

  9. Corollary 3.2. The queue entry said 1 + n/α; the paper says ρ ≤ 1 + n²/α, which is what we prove.

  10. Proposition 3.8: different proof. We do not formalize Lemmas D.2, D.3, D.5, D.6 or the case analysis of the proof of D.7. Instead we prove the statements of Lemma D.4 (BAE) and Lemma D.7 (BSwE for α ≥ 7kn) for every tree whose subtree sizes depend only on the layer and halve every k layers (LayerSized, LayerTree.lean). The stretched binary tree satisfies this (stretched_layerSized).

    • Lemma D.4 holds already for α ≥ 2kn (paper: 5kn).

    • BSwE: if both u and w gain, then w lies on v's side C_v, v is strictly less central in C_v than w, and |C_u| d(v,w) > α (swap_core). The two orientations of uv are excluded by caseA_contra and caseB_contra. The paper's own proof of D.2–D.7 was not checked step by step.

  11. The stretched binary tree is the explicit graph stretchedTree d k on Σ ℓ : Fin (dk+1), Fin 2^⌈ℓ/k⌉. Layer ℓ has 2^⌈ℓ/k⌉ vertices, and the parent of (ℓ, j) is (ℓ-1, j/2) at the start of a stretched segment and (ℓ-1, j) otherwise. This is isomorphic to the paper's construction, a complete binary tree of depth d with every edge replaced by a path of k edges; it is a tree with (2^(d+1)-2)k + 1 nodes (stretchedTree_isTree, card_SVert).

  12. Proposition 3.9: explicit threshold. "For sufficiently large η" becomes η^γ ≥ 512 in prop_3_9, which follows the paper's proof. That threshold depends on γ, and so does the paper's proof (it needs 2k + 1 ≤ η/14 for k = ⌈α/η⌉ ≈ η^(1-γ)). The statement can also be read with η₀ independent of γ; this reading is true as well (prop_3_9_uniform, for all η ≥ 512): if η^γ < 512 then 25/32 + (γ/96) log₂ η < 1 ≤ ρ (one_le_rho), and the complete binary tree (k = 1) with η/42 ≤ n ≤ η/14 is in BGE by Proposition 3.8 since 7n ≤ η/2 ≤ α. The tree in BGE is obtained for every M > α n³.

    • The proof uses Lemma D.8 in the form t/3 ≤ n ≤ t and t < 2^(d+2) k, i.e. depth(B) > log(t/(4k)), slightly stronger than the paper's log(t/(6k)) (exists_depth).

    • The denominator is bounded by 15α/7 instead of 16α/7.

    • The step "lower bound n α/η by α/42" needs γ log η ≥ 9, which follows from η^γ ≥ 512.

    • The upper bound depth(T) ≤ k log t of Lemma D.8 is not needed and not formalized.

  13. Lemma 2.4's right end is included in our range (α ≤ cycleHi n); the paper uses strict inequalities. The theorem also requires α ≥ 1. lemma_2_4 states the order of magnitude as n²/8 ≤ cycleLo n (for n ≥ 8) and cycleHi n ≤ n²/4, and asserts that admissible α ≥ 1 exist for every n ≥ 3.

  14. Logarithms are binary (Real.logb 2). The paper does not fix the base; base 2 is the standard convention and matches its halving arguments (Lemmas 3.4, D.8).
