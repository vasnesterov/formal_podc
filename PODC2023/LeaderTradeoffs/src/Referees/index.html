import Papers.PODC2023.LeaderTradeoffs.Model /-! # The improved Afek–Gafni algorithm (Section 3.3, Theorem 3.10) Section 3.3 of Kutten, Robinson, Tan, Zhu, *Improved Tradeoffs for Leader Election* (PODC 2023). Synchronous KT0 clique, simultaneous wake-up, deterministic. The algorithm is parametrized by the number of iterations `K` (the paper's `k - 2`) and the referee counts `d i` of the iterations `i = 1, …, K` (the paper's `⌈n^{i/(k-1)}⌉`). Initially every node is a survivor. Iteration `i` (rounds `2(i-1)` and `2(i-1)+1`, 0-indexed): 1. every survivor sends its ID on its ports `0, …, d i - 1` (to `d i` distinct referees, or to all nodes if `d i ≥ n - 1`); 2. every node that received IDs replies (on the port of arrival) only to the highest ID received; a survivor stays a survivor iff it received replies on all of its ports `0, …, d i - 1`. Final round `2K`: every survivor sends its ID on all ports; a survivor outputs "leader" iff its ID exceeds all IDs it received, every other node outputs "non-leader". Time: `2K + 1`. Main results: * `electsBy`, `terminatesBy`: for every ID universe and every choice of `K`, `d`, the algorithm elects exactly one leader (the maximum ID) and terminates after `2K + 1` rounds; * `card_S_mul_le` (survivor bound): after iteration `i ≥ 1` at most `n / min(d i, n-1)` survivors remain (the referee sets of the remaining survivors are pairwise disjoint); * `msgs_le`: `msgs ≤ ∑_{i=1}^{K} 2·s_{i-1}·min(d i, n-1) + s_K·(n-1)` (`s_i` = #survivors after iteration `i`), `msgBound_general` (explicit worst-case bound); * `msgBound_real`: if `1 ≤ q`, `q^{K+1} = n` and `q^i ≤ d i ≤ 2 q^i`, at most `8(K+1)·n·q` messages; * `theorem_3_10`: for odd `ℓ = 2K + 1` and `d i = ⌈n^{i/(K+1)}⌉`, the algorithm elects a leader in `ℓ` rounds with at most `4(ℓ+1)·n^{1+2/(ℓ+1)}` messages. Remark on the paper's analysis: after the last two-round iteration `K = k - 2` the survivor bound only gives `n^{1/(k-1)}` survivors (not one); the last one-round "iteration" (the broadcast of the remaining survivors) is what reduces them to a single leader, at a cost of `≤ n^{1/(k-1)}·(n-1)` messages.
