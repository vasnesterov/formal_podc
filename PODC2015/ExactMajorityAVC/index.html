Fast and Exact Majority (AVC) — PODC 2015

Dan Alistarh, Rati Gelashvili, Milan Vojnović. Fast and Exact Majority in Population Protocols. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767429. Formalized version: the full version, Microsoft Research technical report MSR-TR-2015-13 (February 2015, with appendices). Numbering follows it.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. With a one-symbol typo in Figure 1 fixed, AVC solves exact majority with probability 1 from every configuration with non-zero sum (Lemma A.1, Theorem 4.1 first part), for all n, m, d. The four-state lower bound announced in the abstract (Theorem B.1) is proved only for unordered interactions, the convention of the paper's proof, while the paper states it for all protocols in its ordered model. The convergence-time bounds, the paper's main claim, and the Ω(log n) lower bound were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

⚠️ Theorem B.1 for ordered interactions (unestablished)

Theorem B.1 is stated for "any majority algorithm using four states" with ordered (initiator, responder) interactions, but its proof treats [S0, X] and [X, S0] as one interaction with a single outcome, so it does not cover protocols whose two orientations differ.

Impact. Not refuted, and very likely true: we prove Theorem B.1 for unordered interactions, the convention of the paper's proof (theorem_B_1, hypothesis Symmetric P); for ordered protocols the gap is closed only by a script, not in Lean. For unordered interactions, every correct four-state protocol preserves #S0 − #S1, so the probability of converging within t steps is at most 2t(ϵn+1)/(n(n−1)).

Details

Section 2 defines δ : Q × Q → Q × Q with ordered (initiator, responder) pairs, and AVC itself assigns different states to initiator and responder. Claims B.2–B.5 do not need symmetry (we prove them for both orientations), and an exhaustive search over ordered protocols with populations of at most 5 agents refutes every protocol that does not preserve #S0 − #S1. Since refuting certificates on small populations are valid for every n, this search would close the gap, but it is a script, not Lean. Symmetric P means that both orientations of a pair give the same multiset of states, though possibly to different agents. See differencies.md (item 7).

✏️ Slips

  • Figure 1, line 12 (value for weight). Line 12 reads "weight(x)·weight(y) = 0 and value(x) + value(y) > 0", so a weak node meeting a node of negative value skips lines 12–14 and falls through to line 19, which changes nothing: +0 never becomes −0, and AVC can fail when B is the majority (literal_fails, not_tendsto_literal).

    • Counterexample with m = d = 1, the four-state protocol, and inputs A, B, B (states 1₁, −1₁, −1₁). The first two agents interact by line 17 into −0, +0. The configuration −0, +0, −1₁ is silent, and agent 2 outputs A forever. This happens after one step with positive probability, so the probability of converging to B does not tend to 1.

    • A model checker finds the same failure for m = 3 (inputs A, B, B give +3, −3, −3) and every other small parameter set we tried, from many initial configurations.

    • The prose under Figure 1 describes the intended condition weight(x) + weight(y) > 0 ("zero meets non-zero"). With it, the protocol is sign-symmetric (update_neg) and solves exact majority with probability 1 (lemma_A_1, theorem_4_1_correct).

    • The proof of Lemma A.1 assumes "without loss of generality S > 0", and for S > 0 the literal rule happens to work; it is the sign symmetry behind this WLOG that the typo destroys.

Typos

Small typos in the case analysis of Appendix B (we checked the stated interaction sequences by script):

  • "two nodes in S9" (Case 1) should be S0.

  • "three nodes starting in C0" (Case 2.3) should be S0.

  • In Case 2.4.1 the configuration "two X, three S1" needs two applications of [X, Y] → [S1, X], not one.

  • At the end of Case 2.4.1 ("[X, Y] into [S0, Y]") the contradiction is with the second property, not the third.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Claim B.8

🔧 Claim B.8 (inside the proof of Theorem B.1: meeting probability)

The unique minority agent meets one of the ϵn + 1 majority agents with probability 2(ϵn+1)/(n(n−1)), not (ϵn+1)/n².

Impact. The paper's value is too small, the wrong direction for a lower bound, but only by a factor of about 2, so Ω(1/ϵ) is unaffected (hitProb_le, sum_tail_ge).

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Population protocols on the complete graph, CSLib's model: n labelled agents, a deterministic transition function δ on ordered (initiator, responder) pairs, no failures. AVC is avc d; its states are ±0, ±1_j and strong values v (odd, 3 ≤ |v| ≤ m). Figure 1 is transcribed line by line (update): lit = true is the printed version, lit = false the corrected one.

  • Randomness: the uniformly random scheduler of Section 2, i.e. an ordered pair of distinct agents drawn uniformly at each step (CSLib's uniformKernel, a Markov kernel on the finite set of configurations over valid states). "With probability 1" means that the probability of not being converged after t steps tends to 0; the converged set is proved closed.

  • Theorem B.1: four states S0, S1, X, Y, a CSLib protocol P : Protocol Q4 and an output map γ. The three correctness properties are stated for every population size on anonymous configurations (multisets of states, the Podc multiset model MStep/MReach). This is equivalent to labelled agents because protocols are anonymous. Interactions are unordered (Symmetric P: both orientations give the same multiset), as in Appendix B. The lower bound uses CSLib's labelled uniform scheduler.

  • The probability of convergence within t steps is the first-step recursion hitProb. "Expected parallel time Ω(1/ϵ)" is formalized as a tail bound plus a lower bound on the partial sums ∑_{t<K} P(τ > t), whose full sum is E[τ].

The case analysis (Claims B.6, B.7, Cases 1–3, with Claim B.9) is replaced by two certified case trees (FourTreeA.lean, FourTreeB.lean; 514 and 532 leaves, one tree per value of γ(X)). A script generates them (tools/gen2.py, tools/emit.py), and the kernel checks them with decide against the soundness theorem checkTree_sound (FourChecker.lean). Each leaf refutes a partial transition table with a certificate on at most 7 agents: a common configuration reachable from two initial configurations (Claim B.2), a closed set of wrong-output configurations (second property), or a closed set without correct-output configurations (third property, which covers the potential arguments of Claim B.9). The other leaves are complete tables preserving #S0 − #S1.

Not formalized. The convergence-time bounds of Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 (Claims A.2–A.4, 4.4, 4.5) and the n³(m+d+1) expected-time bound of Lemma A.1; Theorem C.1 (Ω(log n) for any number of states); ordered (asymmetric) four-state protocols in Theorem B.1 (only model-checked).

Trust. No sorry. The listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound; the case trees and the counterexample are checked by decide. Non-vacuity: the classical four-state protocol satisfies the three properties of Theorem B.1 for every population size (std_correct, FourExample.lean). The model checkers are in tools/ and are not part of the Lean development: avc.py (AVC, literal and corrected), b1.py (symmetric four-state protocols), genasym.py (ordered ones). Models come from CSLib (Cslib.Computability.Distributed.PopulationProtocols.{Basic,RandomScheduler}, Cslib.Computability.Distributed.SelfStabilization.Markov) and Podc (Podc/Computability/Distributed/PopulationProtocols/Multiset/Basic.lean).

CSLib candidates (marked -- CSLib candidate): sum_interact, reach_of_measure (AVCCorrect.lean), hitProb with hitProb_mono and hitProb_nonneg_le_one (FourLowerBound.lean), the ms_eq tactic and Reach.add_right (FourModel.lean).
