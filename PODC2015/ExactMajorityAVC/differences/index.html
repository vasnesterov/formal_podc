Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Fast and Exact Majority (AVC) — PODC 2015.

Paper: Alistarh, Gelashvili, Vojnović, Fast and Exact Majority in Population Protocols, PODC 2015, full version MSR-TR-2015-13.

Statement-level issues

  1. Figure 1, line 12 (✏️ slip). Printed: weight(x) · weight(y) = 0 and value(x) + value(y) > 0. Read literally, a weak state meeting a state of negative value is left unchanged (line 19), so +0 never turns into −0. AVC then fails to compute the majority when B is the majority: literal_fails (m = d = 1, inputs A, B, B: one interaction gives the silent configuration −0, +0, −1₁) and not_tendsto_literal (under the uniform random scheduler the probability of not having converged stays bounded away from 0). The script tools/avc.py shows failures for every tested parameter set (n ≤ 5, m ≤ 5, d ≤ 2), e.g. inputs A, B, B with m = 3. The intended condition is recoverable from the paper ("if a node with 0 weight meets a node with non-zero weight, then it will adopt the sign of the interaction partner", Section 3), the fix is local (value → weight), and the proof of Lemma A.1 never uses the printed condition: it argues "w.l.o.g. S > 0" with the prose semantics. We formalize the corrected protocol (avc, update false) and prove it correct. The corrected protocol is exactly sign-symmetric (update_neg), which justifies the paper's w.l.o.g. The published pseudocode of the main algorithm is wrong for half of the inputs; we treat it as a typo because of the explicit prose description.

No other statement was found false. Theorem B.1 is not refuted, but see item 7.

Modelling choices

  1. AVC states. St.zero pos (±0), St.one pos j (±1_j), St.strong v (value v). The transition function does not depend on m; m only enters through St.Valid m d (odd 3 ≤ |v| ≤ m, 1 ≤ j ≤ d), which is closed (valid_update, requires m odd and d ≥ 1, as in the paper). R↓/R↑ and ϕ are as in Figure 1. The initial states are ϕ(±m), i.e. ±1₁ when m = 1 (the paper: "nodes start in states +1 or −1").

  2. Probability 1. Lemma A.1 and Theorem 4.1 say "with probability 1". We prove that the probability of not being converged after t steps tends to 0 (lemma_A_1, theorem_4_1_correct), and separately that converged configurations are closed (converged_closed, lemma_A_1_closed). Together they give "w.p. 1 the execution reaches a configuration after which all outputs are correct forever". The probabilistic statements live on the finite type of valid states VSt m d.

  3. Lemma A.1, scope. The lemma holds for every valid configuration with S ≠ 0, not only initial ones, as in the paper. Its expected-time bound n³(m + d + 1) is not formalized.

  4. Section 2's definition of "solves majority within ℓ steps with probability 1 − φ" is not formalized; Theorem 4.1's correctness part is the limit statement above.

  5. Theorem B.1, configurations. The three correctness properties are stated on anonymous configurations (multisets of states, Four.Correct), for every population size; steps fire P.δ p q on two agents in states p, q in either role. This is the projection of the labelled model to counts and equivalent to it because protocols cannot see identities; the bridge labelled ↔ multiset is not formalized. The lower bound (theorem_B_1) is stated for CSLib's labelled uniform scheduler, so the two parts meet through Invariant P, a property of δ only.

  6. ⚠️ Theorem B.1, unordered interactions (hypothesis Symmetric P). Section 2 defines ordered δ; Appendix B says "interacting nodes are not ordered ([S0, X] is the same as [X, S0])" and its whole case analysis assigns one outcome per unordered pair. We add this as the hypothesis Symmetric P (both orientations produce the same multiset). Claims B.2–B.5 are proved without it, for both orientations. For ordered protocols the remaining case analysis is only model-checked (tools/genasym.py, populations ≤ 5, both values of γ(X)). The search tree has about 1.2 million leaves per case, every non-invariant ordered protocol is refuted, and 2490 leaves are invariant. That is evidence that the theorem holds for ordered protocols too, but the paper's proof does not cover them. This is a proof gap of the paper: the statement is not refuted. The restriction is not an implied assumption: Section 2 fixes ordered δ, and AVC itself distinguishes initiator and responder. In Lean the ordered case stays unestablished; Symmetric P is a multiset condition, so protocols whose two orientations differ only in which agent gets which state are covered.

  7. The output set. γ : Q4 → Bool (false = 0, true = 1). The paper's "w.l.o.g. γ assigns 0 or 1 to each state" is built in.

  8. Property 1 (C_i non-empty) is part of Correct but not needed by the proof (neither the paper's nor ours).

Proof-level differences

  1. Case analysis of Theorem B.1. We do not formalize Claims B.6, B.7, B.9 and Cases 1–3 one by one. Their combined consequence, every correct symmetric protocol preserves #S0 − #S1, is proved by two kernel-checked certified case trees (treeA, treeB, checkTree_sound) that split on the outcomes of the four "cross" pairs. Each leaf certificate uses at most 7 agents (at most 5 in treeB): a Claim B.2 collision, a reachable closed set violating the second property, or a reachable closed set violating the third property (the paper's potential arguments of Claim B.9 are instances of the last kind). We replayed the explicit interaction sequences of Claims B.6, B.7 and Cases 1.1–2.4 by script; they are correct up to the slips listed in item 13.

  2. Claim B.4 / B.5 are formalized as in the paper (n = 9, n = 5, n = 7; pigeonhole). Claim B.5 is stated as "same-output pairs keep or swap their states", for both orientations, and its first step (outcomes stay in {S_i, x_i}) uses the mixed absorbing configuration of Claim B.4, as in the paper.

  3. Claim B.8 / the Ω(1/ϵ) bound (🔧 for the meeting probability).

    • The probability that the unique minority agent meets one of the ϵn + 1 majority agents is 2(ϵn + 1)/(n(n − 1)) under the uniform ordered-pair scheduler; the paper writes (ϵn + 1)/n² (factor ≈ 2; harmless for Ω). Claim B.8 sits inside the proof of Theorem B.1 and its conclusion is true.

    • Only majority S0 is stated (theorem_B_1); majority S1 is the same after exchanging S0 ↔ S1 and X ↔ Y.

    • We prove the tail bound P(τ ≤ t) ≤ t · 2(D+1)/(n(n−1)) (hitProb_le; D = #S0 − #S1 ≥ 0 is preserved, at least one S1 initially), where τ is the first time without agents in S1. Configurations of C_0 have no S1 (since γ(S1) = 1), so theorem_B_1 bounds the probability of reaching any all-output-0 configuration. Instead of the expectation over the path space we prove ∑_{t<K} P(τ > t) ≥ K − p·K(K−1)/2 (sum_tail_ge), a lower bound for E[τ] = ∑_t P(τ > t). Taking K ≈ 1/p gives E[τ] ≳ n(n−1)/(4(ϵn+1)) steps, i.e. Ω(1/ϵ) parallel time. The paper counts "clutch rounds" (a geometric number), which gives the same order.

    • P(τ ≤ t) is defined by the first-step recursion hitProb (probability of visiting a set within t steps), not via a measure on executions.

  4. Typos in Appendix B (no effect): "two nodes in S9" (Case 1) means S0; "three nodes starting in C0" (Case 2.3) means S0; in Case 2.4.1 "this interaction from I would lead to a configuration with two nodes in X and three nodes in S1" needs two applications of [X, Y] → [S1, X]; in Case 2.4.1 ("[X, Y] into [S0, Y]") the contradiction is with the second property (an absorbing configuration of C_0 reached from majority S1), not the third.

  5. Lemma A.1 proof. Formalized along the paper's three phases, as a reachability argument (phase1, phase2, phase3) with the measures "total negative weight", "number of negative agents" and "number of −0 agents". Phase 1 picks a strong node and a node of opposite sign and non-zero value, as in the paper; phase 2 lets one fixed pair −1_i, +1_j interact until both are weak (at most d interactions). The w.l.o.g. S > 0 is justified by update_neg / reachable_neg (sign symmetry of the corrected protocol).

Not formalized

  • Theorem 4.1's convergence-time bounds and Corollary 4.2 (Claims A.2, A.3, A.4, 4.4, 4.5; Chernoff-type concentration).

  • The expected-time bound of Lemma A.1.

  • Theorem C.1 (Ω(log n) for any number of states).

  • Theorem B.1 for ordered (asymmetric) four-state protocols (only model-checked, item 7).
