import Cslib.Computability.Distributed.Byzantine.PartialSync.Model /-! # Figures 1–4 as a partially synchronous algorithm The consensus algorithm of Figure 4, with its building blocks CB-broadcast (Figure 1), adopt-commit (Figure 2) and eventual agreement (Figure 3), written as an `Algorithm` of CSLib's partially synchronous Byzantine model `Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync`: processes `Fin n`, authenticated point-to-point channels, discrete time, every correct process takes one step per tick; a run fixes the faulty set, what faulty processes send, and the delivery time of every batch of messages. The per-channel timing assumption of the paper (an eventual `⟨t+1⟩bisource`) is expressed on runs (`Cslib.Byzantine.PartialSync.Run.BisourceValid`). **Reliable broadcast is a primitive**, as in the paper (which only uses its properties): an RB-broadcast of `v` in instance `k` is the message `rb k v` sent to all; a process RB-delivers the *first* `rb k _` message of each sender (RB-Unicity). For correct senders this gives RB-Validity and RB-Termination-1/2 on reliable channels; for faulty senders the properties are constraints on what they send (`RBAdmissible`). A correct process keeps (`St`) all received messages with their senders, its program counter (`PC`: where the main thread of Figure 4 waits), its estimate `est`, the value `val` ea-proposed in the current round, the rounds for which it sent `EA_COORD`/`EA_RELAY`, its running timers, logs of its EA and AC returns, its decision, and its outbox for the current tick. One step: 1. receive the inbox (appended to `rcv` in sender order) and advance the timers by one tick; 2. lines 11–14 of Figure 3 (`coordSt`): if it is `coord r` and received `EA_PROP2[r]` from a process of `F r`, broadcast `EA_COORD[r](w)` once, `w` from the first such message; 3. lines 15–19 (`relaySt`): on `EA_COORD[r](v)` from `coord r`, or on expiry of `timer[r]`, broadcast `EA_RELAY[r]` once (`v`, resp. `⊥`) and disable the timer; 4. line 9 of Figure 4 (`decideSt`): decide `v` once `DECIDE(v)` was RB-delivered from `t + 1` processes; 5. the main thread (`mainSt`), run until it blocks (at most `fuel` transitions per tick). All "pick any" choices of the paper are resolved by arrival order: CB returns the value of `cb_valid` whose first `rb` message arrived first; the `n − t` messages of a wait statement are the first `n − t` qualifying ones; the most frequent value is the first one with maximal count. In the counterexamples every such choice is forced (only one candidate). `Params.eaRound = some r` runs the EA object alone (Figure 3, round `r`, the input is `val`); after returning, the process
