import Papers.PODC2015.LoopFreeUpdates.Basic /-! # Peacock (§4): the shortcut selection of odd rounds In an odd round, Peacock (Algorithm 1, lines 6–9) considers the nodes whose new (dashed) rule points forward on the current line, in order of non-increasing *distance* (the number of solid edges the dashed edge skips), and greedily selects a node `u` unless some already selected `v` *blocks* it: `v < u < out₂ v` or `v < out₂ u < out₂ v` (positions on the current line = the old route). We abstract the outcome of the greedy procedure as `GoodSelection I C`: * every selected node has a forward new rule; * the intervals `[pos₁ c, pos₁ (out₂ c)]` of selected nodes do not overlap (they may touch); * every forward node that was not selected is blocked by a selected node of at least its distance. `greedy` is the concrete greedy procedure and `greedy_good` shows that it produces a good selection. Everything downstream (Lemmas 2, 3 and Theorem 3) is proved for an arbitrary good selection, hence for any tie-breaking among edges of equal distance. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false
