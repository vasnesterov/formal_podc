Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Fast Distributed Almost Stable Matchings — PODC 2015.

Paper: R. Ostrovsky, W. Rosenbaum, Fast Distributed Almost Stable Matchings, PODC 2015, arXiv 1408.2782v2. Scope: the deterministic algorithm ASM (Sections 2–4). The randomized variants (Section 5, Appendix A) are not formalized.

Model and definitions

  1. Preferences (Prefs): duplicate-free preference lists, symmetric acceptability (w ∈ P^m ↔ m ∈ P^w), as in §2.1. We do not assume |X| = |Y|. Men and women are any finite types.

  2. Quantiles. The paper says only that Q_1 holds the deg v / k favourites, Q_2 the next deg v / k, and so on; it does not say how to round. We use q^v(u) = ⌈k · P^v(u) / deg v⌉ ∈ {1, …, k} (quant). Then two items in the same quantile have ranks less than deg v / k apart (lt_of_quant_le). This is the only property of the rounding the proofs use.

  3. Blocking pairs / Definition 2.3. IsBlocking follows §2.1: an unmatched player prefers every acceptable partner to being alone. IsEpsBlocking follows Definition 2.3. For an unmatched player it uses the convention P^v(∅) = deg v + 1, which the paper introduces only in the proof of Lemma 4.3.

  4. The woman's list Q^w is not stored. In Step 4 a woman sends REJECT to all acceptable men in a lesser or equal quantile than her new partner. The paper sends it only to those still in Q^w. A man outside Q^w has already removed her, so the extra messages change no state. She never reads Q^w anyway: proposals come only from men in Q^w.

  5. Who runs the outer-loop test. Algorithm 3 has every processor test |Q| ≥ 2^i. We let only the men test it, once at the start of iteration i. Women always take part, and so does every man as a receiver of REJECT messages: an inactive man still drops rejecting women and loses his partner if she rejects him. This is the only reading under which the algorithm makes sense, and §3.4 says so ("we only allow men … to participate"). An inactive man does not set A and does not propose.

  6. MaximalMatching (Theorem 2.7) is a black box MMOracle. It is an arbitrary function from bipartite graphs G₀ to maximal matchings of G₀ (Definition 2.6 in its "every edge has a matched endpoint" form), plus a round count. All theorems hold for every such oracle. This covers any deterministic subroutine, in particular [HKP]'s. The oracle sees only G₀; an adversary that also depends on the history is not covered. The analysis never uses this restriction.

  7. Parameters. k = ⌈8/ε⌉ (kOf; Algorithm 3 writes 8ε⁻¹, the proof of Theorem 4.1 ⌈8/ε⌉). δ = ε/8. The inner loop runs ℓ = ⌈2δ⁻¹k⌉ = ⌈16k/ε⌉ times (ellOf). The outer loop runs i = 0, …, ⌊log₂ n⌋, where n is any bound on the degrees (the paper assumes log n is an integer). The general theorem blocking_le allows any k ≥ 1, any ℓ and any L with all degrees < 2^{L+1}.

  8. Granularity. The state is updated once per ProposalRound, through local rules: proposers (step 1), accepts (step 2), the oracle (step 3), rejects/womanUpdate/manUpdate (steps 4–5). Each node's new state is a function of its own state, the one-bit messages from its neighbours and its own partner in M₀. There is no formal CONGEST semantics with message passing. In particular, the composition with a CONGEST implementation of the subroutine is not formalized.

  9. Running time (Theorem 4.7) is proved for the explicit round count asmRounds = (L+1) · ℓ · k · (3 + T): one round each for PROPOSE, ACCEPT and REJECT, plus T for the subroutine. Local steps cost 0 rounds. ASM run with a round counter (stageC: each ProposalRound adds 3 + mm.rounds) computes the output of asm and ends with counter asmRounds k ℓ L mm.rounds, i.e. ASM makes exactly (L+1) ℓ k ProposalRound calls (stageC_asm); theorem_4_7_run bounds this counter for ASM(P, ε, n). The asymptotic bound is explicit: ≤ 2610 (3 + c) ε⁻³ log⁵ n for 0 < ε ≤ 1, n ≥ 2, T ≤ c log⁴ n, with log = ⌊log₂⌋.

Statements

  1. Theorem 4.1 is proved as stated, with the paper's constants (theorem_4_1). We also prove that the output is a matching in E (implicit in the paper). The general form is k ℓ · #blocking ≤ 4ℓ|E| + 8k²|E| (blocking_le).

  2. Lemma 4.4. The paper assumes δ ≤ 1/2 and gets 2δ/(1-δ) |E| ≤ 4δ|E|. We prove 4δ|E| directly for every δ > 0 with ℓ ≥ 2k/δ (lemma_4_4); the core form is ℓ · ∑_{m bad} |Q^m| ≤ 8k|E| (sum_bad_card_le).

  3. Lemma 4.5 is stated as ℓ · #(bad active men) ≤ 2k · #(active men) (lemma_4_5), i.e. at most a 2k/ℓ ≤ δ fraction. It holds for every iteration started in a reachable state with all A empty, as every iteration of ASM is.

  4. Lemma 4.6 is proved in the form used by the proof of Lemma 4.4: every (2/k)-blocking pair (m, w) has m bad and w ∈ Q^m (lemma_4_2_4_6). Hence the (2/k)-blocking pairs number at most ∑_{m bad} |Q^m| (card_strongBlocking_le). The paper's "fewer than 2^i for m ∈ B_i" depends on its definition of B_i (see item 16) and is not formalized separately.

  5. Lemma 3.1 is proved for one ProposalRound: a matched woman stays matched, and a change of partner is a strict improvement in quantile (lemma_3_1). Lemma 3.2 says k ProposalRounds empty every A, from any reachable state (lemma_3_2).

  6. Lemma 4.3 holds for arbitrary partner functions (lemma_4_3). The paper's display (1) has the differences reversed: it should read P^m(p(m)) − P^m(w) < 2 deg m / k or P^w(p(w)) − P^w(m) < 2 deg w / k.

Proofs

  1. Proof of Lemma 4.4 (gap; we use a different argument). The paper charges every man who is bad at the end to the set B_i of men that "became bad during the i-th iteration and |Q^m| < 2^i". It then bounds |B_i ∪ … ∪ B_log n| with Lemma 4.5, which concerns only the men active in iteration i. A man can, however, become bad while inactive. He is matched when he leaves the active set, and later an active man displaces him (Example.bad_while_inactive: k = 2, ℓ = 1, 5 men, 5 women). Such men are not covered by the application of Lemma 4.5. The construction of the disjoint sets H_i and inequality (2) are also not justified as written. Our proof (BadMen.lean) charges a displaced inactive man to the previous iteration:

    • the number of unmatched men never increases;

    • so the inactive men displaced in iteration j number at most the bad active men at the start of iteration j (matched_inactive_le);

    • each such man has |Q| < 2^j;

    • the per-man potential "2^j if matched, |Q| otherwise" of an inactive man never increases (reach_Fpot, inactive_mass). This gives ∑_{m bad} |Q^m| ≤ ∑_i 2^{i+1} b_i (accounting), where b_i is the number of bad active men at the end of iteration i. Lemma 4.5 and ∑_i 2^i |act_i| ≤ 2|E| (sum_pow_card_ac_le) finish the proof. The constant matches the paper's final 4δ|E|. In random searches with the paper's parameters (k = 2, ℓ = 8, up to 30 × 30 players) we found no run with such a displaced man. These parameters leave very few bad active men at the end of an iteration. The paper's argument does not rule such men out, though, and our proof does not need to.

  2. Proof of Lemma 4.5. "The number of matched players (and hence matched men) can only increase … therefore the number of good players can only increase" is used for the active men. That step also needs: inactive men are never newly matched, and matched men = matched women in number. Then the number of matched active men cannot decrease, although individual active men may lose their partners (card_matched_act_le, card_bad_act_le). The counting of quantile rejections is formalized as the potential ∑_{m ∈ act} #(quantiles of Q^m) + ∑_w ψ_w (Pot, pot_prStep, pot_le).

  3. Proof of Lemma 3.2 is formalized as an invariant: after t rounds every proposal (m, w) has q^w(m) > t (prStep_quantile_gt).

  4. Proof of Lemma 4.2 uses only two invariants of reachable states: a woman who rejected m is matched in the same or a better quantile, and a matched man's partner is in his best remaining quantile (Inv.witness, Inv.p_best). With our quantile rounding, (1/k)-blocking would already suffice.

  5. Typos. In the proof of Lemma 4.2, "contradicts that (m, w) is ε-blocking" should say (2/k)-blocking. §3.1 says Q_1 holds "v's deg v / k favorite men" for every player v; for a man these are women. Theorem 2.7 states log⁴ n rounds; [HKP] give O(log⁴ n).
