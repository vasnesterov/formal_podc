This overlooks *inactive* men: a man who is matched when he becomes inactive (in iteration `j`, `|Q^m| < 2^j`) can later be rejected by his partner when an active man proposes to her; he then stays unmatched with a nonempty list forever, i.e. he becomes bad without being active. We account for such men with the per-man potential `F(m) = 2^j` (matched) / `|Q^m|` (unmatched) of an inactive man, which never increases during iteration `j` (`reach_Fpot`), together with `matched_inactive_le`: the number of inactive men displaced in iteration `j` is at most the number of bad active men at the start of iteration `j`. Summing over the iterations gives the paper's bound `∑_{m bad} |Q^m| ≤ ∑_i 2^{i+1} b_i`, where `b_i` is the number of bad active men at the end of iteration `i`, and Lemma 4.5 bounds `ℓ b_i ≤ 2k |act_i|`; finally `∑_i 2^i |act_i| ≤ 2 |E|`. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace Papers.PODC2015.AlmostStableMatchings open Finset variable {M W : Type*} [Fintype M] [Fintype W]
