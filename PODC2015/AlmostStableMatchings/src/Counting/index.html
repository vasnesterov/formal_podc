import Papers.PODC2015.AlmostStableMatchings.Invariants /-! # Counting bad men: Lemma 4.5 (and the per-iteration facts used for Lemma 4.4) We fix one iteration `j` of the outer loop of `ASM`, started in a reachable state `s₀` in which every `A` is empty, with active set `act = {m | |Q^m| ≥ 2^j}`. Every state of the iteration is reached from `s₀` by `IStep`s (a `ProposalRound`, or the initialization of `QuantileMatch` by the men of `act`), and satisfies `IInv`: the invariants `Inv`, inactive men never propose, and inactive men have `|Q| < 2^j`. * A man is *good* if he is matched or has been rejected by all his acceptable partners (`Q = ∅`); otherwise he is *bad* (`badSet`). * `card_bad_act_antitone`: the number of bad active men never increases (matched men never decrease in number, inactive men are never newly matched). * `lemma_4_5`: with `ℓ` calls of `QuantileMatch`, at the end of the iteration `ℓ · #(bad ∩ act) ≤ 2k |act|`; for `ℓ ≥ 2k/δ` this is "at most a `δ`-fraction of the active men are bad". The proof is the paper's: every bad active man witnesses, in every call, a quantile rejection (a woman getting a strictly better partner, or one of his own quantiles being emptied), and there are at most `2k |act|` of those per iteration. potential of `BadMen.lean` (`reach_Fpot`) this is the step of Lemma 4.4 that the paper's proof misses. -/ set_option linter.unusedSectionVars false namespace Papers.PODC2015.AlmostStableMatchings open
