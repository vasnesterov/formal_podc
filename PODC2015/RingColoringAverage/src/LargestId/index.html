In the *largest-ID* problem every node outputs `Yes` (`true`) iff it carries the largest identifier of the ring (`LargestId.Correct`). * **Worst case is linear** (`LargestId.half_le_radAt_of_max`, `LargestId.worst_case`): for every correct algorithm, on every ring the node with the largest identifier has radius `≥ ⌊n/2⌋`: if its window `[-r, r]` with `2r + 1 < n` misses a node, giving that node a larger identifier produces a ring where the same view must answer `No`. * **Logarithmic average** (`LargestId.straightforward`, `LargestId.straightforward_correct`, `LargestId.sum_radAt_straightforward_le`): the straightforward algorithm — increase the radius until a larger identifier is seen (answer `No`) or until the whole ring has been seen, detected by a repeated identifier in the window (answer `Yes`) — is correct, and on every ring of size `n` its total radius is at most `n (⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1)`, i.e. its average radius is `O(log n)`. The paper argues the `O(n log n)` total via the recurrence `a(p) = max_{1 ≤ k ≤ ⌈p/2⌉} {k + a(k-1) + a(p-k)}` and OEIS A000788 (`a(p) = Θ(p log p)`), without proof. We only define the recurrence (`segA`) and check that its first values are those of A000788 (`segA_eq_A000788`); we do **not** formalize the claim that `a(p)` is the maximal total radius on a segment, nor its asymptotics.
