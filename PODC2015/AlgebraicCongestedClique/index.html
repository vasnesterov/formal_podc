Algebraic Methods in the Congested Clique — PODC 2015

Keren Censor-Hillel, Petteri Kaski, Janne H. Korhonen, Christoph Lenzen, Ami Paz, Jukka Suomela. Algebraic Methods in the Congested Clique. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767414. Formalized version: arXiv 1503.04963 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The checked results hold after local changes: O(1)-round 4-cycle detection (Theorem 4, with Lemmas 12 and 13), O(n^{1/3})-round semiring matrix multiplication (Theorem 1), and Lemma 14 and the case split of Theorem 15. They need one short repair in the proof of Lemma 12 and two slips corrected. The paper's main point, fast ring matrix multiplication and its applications, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Section 2.1, Step 1 (3D matrix multiplication: recipients of the T-blocks). Step 1 sends the T-blocks to the wrong nodes, so for n^{1/3} ≥ 2 some node never receives a row of T it needs (step1_as_written_wrong). The paper says node v sends T[v, w₃∗∗] to every w ∈ ∗v₂∗, and that v receives T[w, v₃∗∗] from every w ∈ ∗v₂∗. But node v multiplies S[v₁∗∗, v₂∗∗] T[v₂∗∗, v₃∗∗], so it needs the rows w ∈ v₂∗∗ of T; the paper itself says "each node v receives … T[v₂∗∗, v₃∗∗]". Corrected, v sends to w ∈ ∗v₁∗ and receives from w ∈ v₂∗∗. We implement this pattern; the algorithm is then correct and Theorem 1 (semiring part) holds (step1_corrected, theorem_1_semiring).

  • Lemma 12 (missing hypothesis ∑ deg(y)² < 2n²). As stated, Lemma 12 claims disjoint tiles A(y) × B(y) ⊆ V × V with |A(y)| ≥ deg(y)/8 for every graph, which fails for the complete graph K_n once n ≥ 67: the tiles would need total area at least n(n−1)²/64 > n². The lemma is meant under the bound ∑_y deg(y)² < 2n² derived just before it ("Using this bound, we obtain the following lemma"), and it is only used there. We state it with that hypothesis, and then it holds (lemma_12).

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 12, proof

🔧 Lemma 12, proof (f(y) for degrees 1–3)

f(y) is "deg(y)/4 rounded down to the nearest power of 2", which is undefined for 1 ≤ deg(y) ≤ 3, and the natural fix f(y) = 1 breaks the first step of the paper's chain.

Impact. Statement true; repaired (sixteen_mul_tileSize_sq_le, sum_tileSize_sq_le).

Details

For 1 ≤ deg(y) ≤ 3 no positive integer power of two is ≤ deg(y)/4. Yet |A(y)| = f(y) ≥ deg(y)/8 > 0 must hold. With f(y) = 1, the first step of the chain ∑ f(y)² ≤ ∑ deg(y)²/16 < n²/8 < k² fails.

Repair

The conclusion ∑ f(y)² ≤ k² still holds: 16 f² ≤ deg² + 15 gives 16 ∑ f² < 2n² + 15n ≤ 16k² for n ≥ 3, and n ≤ 2 is direct. (sixteen_mul_tileSize_sq_le, sum_tileSize_sq_le)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Network (Podc/Computability/Distributed/CongestedClique/Phases.lean, shared). Synchronous congested clique on a finite node set with known identifiers. The algorithms are deterministic, with no failures and unbounded local computation. An algorithm is a Prog: a sequence of communication steps. In a step, every node computes from its own state a list of words for every node; every node then computes its new state from its old state and the received lists. So locality holds by construction.

  • Direct steps. A direct step is sent over the links, one word per ordered pair per round. It costs the length of its longest list. This is the plain congested clique.

  • Routed steps. A routed step has the same semantics. It requires every node to send and receive at most n words and costs 16 rounds. This is the black box of Lenzen's routing (PODC'13, Theorem 3.7), formalized separately in Papers/PODC2013/CongestedCliqueRouting for opaque messages but not composed with this model. Only the final step of the 4-cycle algorithm uses routed steps. The 3D algorithm uses direct steps only.

  • Words. For 4-cycle detection a word is a triple of numbers ≤ 64n (O(log n) bits). For matrix multiplication a word is one semiring element, as in the paper ("entries of O(log n) bits").

  • Inputs and outputs. For 4-cycle detection, node v starts with N(v) of a simple undirected graph on Fin n, and every node must output whether a 4-cycle exists. For matrix multiplication, n = q³ (the paper's assumption that n^{1/3} is an integer); node v starts with rows S[v,∗] and T[v,∗] and must output row (ST)[v,∗], over any (even non-associative, non-unital) semiring.

Girth.lean

Routing with O(n) loads (Theorem 4, last step). The paper says "each node needs to send O(n) words and receive O(n) words, and the running time is therefore O(1) rounds" [46]. But Lenzen's routing (Theorem 3.7 of [46]) needs loads of at most n, while here a node sends up to 64n walks and receives up to 2n − 2. Splitting the instance needs coordination, because a node does not know the loads of the others. A referee may consider this folklore. We give a deterministic reduction (Prog.exists_routedBig): one round of counts, one round of receiver-side offsets, then a·b Lenzen instances of load ≤ n each, for send loads ≤ a·n and receive loads ≤ b·n.

Not formalized.

  • Fast (ring) matrix multiplication: Lemma 10, the second part of Theorem 1, ρ.

  • Corollary 2, Theorem 3 (colour coding), Lemma 11, Corollaries 6–8, Theorem 9, Section 3.4, and the lower bounds of Section 4.

  • Theorem 15 as an algorithm: it needs Theorem 3. Only its combinatorial core is checked, and the ρ = 0 case is not covered.

Trust. No sorry. theorem_4, theorem_1_semiring, lemma_12, lemma_13, lemma_14, theorem_15_dense, Prog.exists_oblivious and Prog.exists_routedBig use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Theorems 4 and 1 are existential statements about explicit programs (fourCycleProg, built from the paper's steps). Their conclusions are exact input/output specifications, so no hypothesis can be vacuous. The tiles of Lemma 12 are computed explicitly by a Morton-order packing, not chosen.
