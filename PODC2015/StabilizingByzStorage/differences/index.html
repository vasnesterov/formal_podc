Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Stabilizing Byzantine Server-Based Storage — PODC 2015.

Paper: Bonomi, Dolev, Potop-Butucaru, Raynal, Stabilizing Server-Based Storage in Byzantine Asynchronous Message-Passing Systems, PODC 2015; formalized from the full version arXiv:1503.00140v1 (numbering of lemmas/theorems/figures follows it).

Mistakes of the paper (labels: mistakes-labeling.md)

  1. ❌ Theorem 3 / Lemma 13 (Fig. 3, practically stabilizing SWSR atomic register) are false. Atomic.theorem3_false: a fair infinite execution (n = 9, t = 1, one Byzantine server, sequence numbers mod 2^64 + 1) with a new/old inversion between two regular reads in every 158-step cycle, forever. The step of Lemma 13 that fails: "the local sequence number can be reset to a value smaller than sn1 only if [...] N6 [...] the writer sequence number wrapped around" — N6 lowers pwsn whenever 2t + 1 servers still hold an older helping pair, which is the normal situation (Atomic.Cex.sanity_check_lowers_pwsn). Every choice at a threshold test in the counterexample is the freshest candidate (Atomic.Cex.cycle_allFresh), so the ambiguity of line 12 ("let v be this value" when two values have 2t + 1 copies) is resolved in the most natural way. A variant (Atomic.Cex.variant_labels, Robust.lean; a finite execution up to the inverted read) has the same inversion when every choice is also the strictly most frequent candidate (Atomic.Cex.variant_allMostFrequent). The execution respects the "system-life-span" bound of Section 4 (four writes between successive write-free reads) and the bound of Lemma 13 (each read overlaps at most four writes). No repair proved; removing N6 breaks stabilization from a corrupted pwsn, which is what N6 is for.

  2. (consequence of 1, not a separate mistake) Theorem 4 (MWMR register) is built from the SWMR registers of Section 5.1, themselves built from Fig. 3; its premise fails. Fig. 4 itself was not checked.

  3. ✏️ Lemma 9 claims 4t + 1 correct servers share the helping pair when a write returns; when the test of line 03 fails because 4t + 1 acks agree, up to t of them are Byzantine, so only 3t + 1 correct servers are guaranteed (Atomic.Cex.lemma9_false). 3t + 1 (stated correctly in Claim C(b) of Lemma 2) suffices for the later uses.

Proof gaps of true statements

  1. 🔧 Lemma 2 (read termination, Fig. 2). The many-writes case assumes that the first write after the reset sees 4t + 1 acks ⊥ and refreshes; false when NEW_HELP_VALs of concurrent writes are delivered after the reset. The proof also refers to τ_stab (defined in Lemma 3) and confuses "2t + 1 replies carry x or y" with "2t + 1 replies carry the same value". Repaired with a different argument (Regular.lemma2_read_terminates), which needs only n > 6t.

  2. 🔧 Lemma 3 (regularity, Fig. 2). Returns through the helping values (line 15) are not argued, and the "and so on" induction over concurrent writes is not given. Repaired with an invariant (Regular.lemma3_regular, Regular.lemma3_regular_exec); holds for every n and from the return of the first write on (τ_stab = τ_1w).

Stronger / different statements in the formalization

  • Theorem 1 is proved with 6t < n (paper: 8t < n); regularity needs no bound on n.

  • Theorem 1 assumes assumption (b) of the paper (at least one write is invoked) only for read termination; it is used in the form "some invWrite occurs in the execution".

  • Liveness is stated for fair infinite executions (Regular.Fair): every correct server eventually delivers each pending message (its link is empty or shrinks infinitely often), and each client's enabled step is eventually taken (weak fairness). Byzantine servers are not required to do anything.

Modelling choices and their effect

  • Asynchronous message passing, interleaving semantics. One step = one server delivering the head of one of its two incoming FIFO links, or one client action. Processing is instantaneous (as in the paper).

  • ss-broadcast axiomatised, as in the paper: a broadcast appends the message to the link of every server; no duplication, validity and FIFO order hold by construction; synchronized delivery (n − 2t correct servers deliver during the call) is the guard of the client step that ends the broadcast (syncW, syncR); termination and eventual delivery are fairness conditions. The bounded-capacity data-link implementation (footnote 3) is not modelled.

  • Acknowledgment matching (favourable to the paper). The paper relies on FIFO order to match ACK_WRITE/ACK_READ with the client's broadcasts, without sequence numbers (Remark of Section 3.1). We record a server's reply in a ghost slot only if it answers the client's current broadcast; stale replies (to older broadcasts, or initial garbage in the reverse links) are never used. Without this, garbage acknowledgments in the initial link contents (allowed by the paper's failure model) could shift the reply streams permanently; we do not claim this as a mistake.

  • Transient failures. τ_no_tr = 0. The initial configuration is arbitrary for the servers and for the links from the clients (garbage messages are delivered before the messages broadcast later on the same link, as with the stabilizing data link of footnote 3). For Fig. 2 the clients start between operations (an operation in progress at τ_no_tr is not modelled); their local variables are reset at each invocation anyway. The counterexample to Theorem 3 starts from a clean configuration, which only makes it stronger.

  • Byzantine servers (B, #B ≤ t) are modelled by arbitrary replies in every collection; their local states are irrelevant. They may answer the writer and the reader differently.

  • Threshold tests with several candidates (lines N4, 12, 14 when two values both have 2t + 1 copies): the pseudo-code does not say which one is taken; the model allows any. See item 1 for why the counterexample does not depend on this.

  • Sequence numbers: Fin m with m = 2^64 + 1 in the counterexample, compared with >cd (gtcd x y := (x − y) mod m < (y − x) mod m, the paper's clockwise-distance order).

  • Values in the counterexample are natural numbers, each written once (write j writes j), so inversions are unambiguous.

  • Specifications. Regularity and new/old inversion are stated on the sequence of invocation / response events (ReadOp, WriteOp, RegularRead, Inversion); "eventual" = there is a finite τ_stab per execution (EventuallyNoInversion). For Fig. 2, regularity is proved with ghost variables (vals, nDone, base) recording the write history; they are never read by the algorithm.

  • Mobile Byzantine faults (footnote 1) are not modelled.

Not formalized

  • Theorem 2 / Lemmas 4–6 (synchronous links, Fig. 5, t < n/3).

  • Lemmas 7, 8, 10–12 (liveness and eventual regularity of Fig. 3) individually: Theorem 3 is refuted through Lemma 13. The "practical" stabilization from a corrupted pwsn (Lemma 12) is not checked.

  • Section 5.1 (SWMR from SWSR registers, one paragraph) and Fig. 4 / Lemmas 14–18 (MWMR from SWMR, bounded epochs of [1]).
