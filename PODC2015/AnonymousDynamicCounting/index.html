The Cost of Anonymity on Dynamic Networks — PODC 2015

Giuseppe Antonio Di Luna, Roberto Baldoni. Brief Announcement: Investigating the Cost of Anonymity on Dynamic Networks. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767442. Formalized version: arXiv 1505.03509 v1 (the full version, with all proofs); numbering follows it.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. All main results hold: counting in G(PD)₂ takes Ω(log |V|) rounds (Theorem 2), and with constant dynamic diameter D it takes D + Ω(log |V|) rounds (Corollary 1). The linear-algebra core (Lemmas 2–4) is right. Lemma 4's formula and Lemma 5's round bound are each printed off by one; their proofs and Theorem 1 use the right values. Two proofs have local gaps, which we closed: the rank argument of Lemma 2 and the chain argument of Corollary 1.

Mistakes in the paper

✏️ Slips

  • Lemma 4 states Σ⁻ k_r = ½(3^r + 1) − 1, but its proof derives ½(3^(r+1) + 1) − 1, the true value (lemma_4). The text before Lemma 2 itself lists Σ⁻ k_1 = 4, while the printed formula gives 1 (lemma_4_statement_false).

  • Lemma 5 claims indistinguishability up to round r ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋. In the paper's own indexing (the leader knows m_r = M_r s_r at round r) this is one round too many. For n = 3, r = 1 every pair of multigraphs of sizes 3 and 4 is distinguished, as the text after Equation 5 says itself (lemma_5_paper_indexing_false, from the sharp form lemma_5_sharp). The proof gives r ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋ − 1, which is what Theorem 1 uses. (Read with Definition 7's indexing, where S(v_l, r) holds rounds 0..r−1, the printed bound is exactly right (lemma_5); the slip is then in the proof's first step, which derives ½(3^(r+1)+1) − 1 ≤ n from r ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋.) The lemma also says "Let us consider M, M′" where it means "there exist M, M′".

  • Discussion: counting in the restricted G(PD)₂ "still requires Ω(|V|) rounds" should read Ω(log |V|).

Typos

  • Lemma 2, base case: "det(M_0) = 1" for the 2×3 matrix M_0.

  • Before Equation 5: "9 variables (i.e. 3² rows)" should be columns; after Equation 2, s′_0 = s_0 + t k_0 needs t ∈ ℤ, not t ∈ ℕ.

  • Proof of Lemma 3, last step: the block form of k_{r−1} comes from Lemma 3's own construction, not from Lemma 2.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Lemma 2 · Corollary 1

🔧 Lemma 2 (rows of M_r are linearly independent)

The proof shows three facts: the rows of M′_{r−1} are independent, no row of U is a combination of rows of M′_{r−1}, and the rows of U are independent. These three facts do not imply that all rows together are independent.

Impact. Statement true; proved (lemma_2).

Details

For example, e₁ and {e₂, e₁+e₂} satisfy all three facts, yet e₁, e₂, e₁+e₂ are dependent. The proof needs span U ∩ span M′_{r−1} = 0. This follows from the paper's block observation: on every 3-column block a combination of U-rows is a(1,0,1) + b(0,1,1), which is constant only for a = b = 0. The independence argument for M′_{r−1} also treats only combinations of two rows.

Repair

We compute the kernel directly: the integer, rational or any-ring solutions of M_r d = 0 are exactly the multiples of k_r (inKer_iff, by induction on the first letter, i.e. the paper's block form k_r = [k_{r−1}, k_{r−1}, −k_{r−1}]). Rank-nullity then gives rank = number of rows = 3^(r+1) − 1 (lemma_2).

🔧 Corollary 1 (D + Ω(log |V|) for constant D)

The proof attaches the G(PD)₂ part to the leader by "a static chain of D − 1 nodes" and says the corollary follows from Theorem 2. Theorem 2 alone does not give the additive D, and with D − 1 chain nodes the dynamic diameter is D + 1, not D.

Impact. Statement true; proved with explicit constants (corollary_1).

Details

The additive D needs a causality argument. Information from W reaches the leader only through the chain, so the leader's state at round T depends only on what happened in W before round T − D + 1.

Repair

We use a chain of D − 1 nodes counting the leader. This network is 1-interval connected, has dynamic diameter D (chainNet_diam), and the leader cannot tell apart two multigraphs whose views agree for t rounds until round t + D − 1 (leader_agree). Hence counting needs D + ⌊log₃(2(|V| − D − 1) + 1)⌋ rounds (corollary_1), and at least D + ⌊log₃ |V|⌋ for |V| ≥ 2D + 1 (corollary_1_log).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Synchronous rounds 0, 1, …, deterministic algorithms, no failures, no randomness. All non-leader nodes run the same code from the same initial state; the leader starts in its own state.

  • Dynamic networks (DynNet, NAlgo): in round r an adversary picks an arbitrary graph E r. Every node broadcasts one message of unbounded size and receives the multiset of its neighbours' messages (anonymous broadcast; the degree is not known before receiving). Counting (Definition 2): the leader's first output is |V|. A class of networks is counted within f if this happens by round f |V| on every network of the class (NAlgo.CountsWithin). This is weaker than "outputs and terminates", so the lower bounds are stronger. G(PD)_h (InPD): every round is connected and every node keeps a fixed distance ≤ h from the leader. Dynamic diameter ≤ D (DiamLE): a temporal journey (CSLib DynNet.Journey) from every (x, r) to every (y, r + D).

  • M(DBL_k) (DBL, DAlgo): in round r node w is joined to the leader by edges with the nonempty label set L r w ⊆ {1..k}, and every message arrives with its edge label. hist and view are the paper's node state S(w, t) and leader multiset C(v_l, t) (Definitions 6, 7). leader_eq_of_view_eq proves the paper's remark that the leader's state is a function of the views. fullInfo attains them, so the views are exactly what a leader can know.

  • Indexing. Our t counts completed rounds. The paper's M_r, k_r (knowledge at the end of round r) are our objects for t = r + 1.

  • The reduction network chainNet k L M has a static path of L + 1 nodes ending in the leader, k relays (the paper's V₁), and W. Relay j and w are adjacent in round r iff j ∈ L r w. We make the relays pairwise adjacent; the paper leaves this open, and either choice is in G(PD)₂. For L = 0 this is the paper's G (chainNet_mem_PD); for L = D − 2 it is the network of Corollary 1.

Our bounds are slightly stronger than the paper's. Theorem 1: the leader of an n-node multigraph needs more than ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋ rounds. The paper says at least ⌊log₃(2|W|+1)⌋. Theorem 2: on G(PD)₂, f |V| ≥ ⌊log₃(2|V| − 5)⌋ + 2. This gains one round over the paper's route (Lemma 1 + Theorem 1, theorem_2_via_lemma_1), because the G(PD)₂ leader hears from V₂ only through V₁. lemma_5_sharp shows that Lemma 5 is tight. For 3^t > 2n + 1, any two multigraphs of sizes n and n + 1 give different leader states after t rounds. This is the uniqueness half of the paper's kernel analysis, and the bound of the method cannot be improved for the pair (n, n+1).

Not formalized. The Discussion's O(1) algorithm when nodes know their degree in advance, and the remark that the lower bound survives in the restricted model without edges inside a layer (our relays are adjacent to each other). The abstract's "D ≤ 2h in G(PD)_h". Lemmas 2–5 are formalized for k = 2, as in the paper. Theorem 1 is proved for all k ≥ 2 through M(DBL₂) ⊆ M(DBL_k); for k = 1 counting takes one round.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. The lower-bound hypotheses are not vacuous: the classes contain the explicit networks chainNet (chainNet_mem_PD, chainNet_connected, chainNet_diam). Journeys come from CSLib (Cslib.Computability.Distributed.DynamicNetworks.Journey); everything else is self-contained, in namespace Podc.AnonymousDynamicCounting.
