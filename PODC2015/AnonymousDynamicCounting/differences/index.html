Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of The Cost of Anonymity on Dynamic Networks — PODC 2015.

Paper: G. A. Di Luna, R. Baldoni, Investigating the Cost of Anonymity on Dynamic Networks, PODC 2015 brief announcement; formalized from the full version arXiv:1505.03509v1.

Mistakes in the paper (with labels)

  1. ✏️ Lemma 4, statement. It says min(Σ⁺k_r, Σ⁻k_r) = Σ⁻k_r = ½(3^r + 1) − 1. The proof derives Σ⁺k_r = ½(3^(r+1) + 1), hence Σ⁻k_r = ½(3^(r+1) + 1) − 1 = ½(3^(r+1) − 1), which is correct (lemma_4; e.g. Σ⁻k_1 = 4, as the paper itself states before Lemma 2). The printed exponent is wrong (lemma_4_statement_false). The proof of Lemma 5 uses the correct value.

  2. ✏️ Lemma 5, round bound and quantifier. The paper's round r means that the leader knows m_r = M_r s_r (rounds 0..r). In that indexing indistinguishability holds iff Σ⁻k_r ≤ n, i.e. r ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋ − 1, not r ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋ as printed. For n = 3, r = 1 all pairs of sizes 3 and 4 are distinguished (lemma_5_paper_indexing_false), as the paper notes itself after Equation 5 ("if n ≤ 3 it is possible to obtain the count in 2 rounds"). The proof of Lemma 5 uses ½(3^(r+1)+1) − 1 ≤ n, the correct condition, and Theorem 1 states r < ⌊log₃(2|W|+1)⌋ − 1, consistent with it. (In the round count of Definition 7, S(v_l, t) after t rounds, the printed bound t ≤ ⌊log₃(2n+1)⌋ is exactly right: lemma_5 with 3^t ≤ 2n+1.) The statement also reads "Let us consider M, M′ … does not exist an algorithm that distinguishes", which literally quantifies over all pairs; the intended (and proved) claim is existential.

  3. ✏️ Discussion. "counting in G(PD)₂ still requires Ω(|V|) rounds" should be Ω(log |V|).

  4. 🔧 Proof of Lemma 2 (gap, statement true). Independence of the rows of M′_{r−1}, "no row of U is a combination of rows of M′_{r−1}", and independence of the rows of U do not together imply independence of all rows (counterexample in ℚ²: {e₁}, {e₂, e₁+e₂}). The needed fact, span U ∩ span M′_{r−1} = 0, follows from the block structure. The independence of the rows of M′_{r−1} is argued only for combinations of two rows. We prove Lemma 2 differently: the kernel is computed directly (inKer_iff), then rank-nullity gives rank = number of rows (lemma_2).

  5. 🔧 Proof of Corollary 1 (gap, statement true). "From this observation and Theorem 2 the corollary follows" omits the causality argument that yields the additive D (Theorem 2 alone gives only Ω(log |V|) on the chain network, which is not in G(PD)₂). With a chain of D − 1 nodes between v_l and v₁, v₂, the leader is at distance D + 1 from W, so the network has dynamic diameter D + 1. Repaired: leader_agree (states of a chain node at distance d from the relays agree d rounds longer) and chainNet_diam (our chain has D − 1 nodes including the leader, giving diameter D); corollary_1.

Typos (no claim affected)

  • Lemma 2, base case: "det(M_0) = 1" for a non-square matrix.

  • Before Equation 5: "9 variables (i.e. 3² rows)" (columns).

  • After Equation 2: s′_0 = s_0 + t k_0 with t ∈ ℕ (should be ℤ, e.g. the example uses s_0 = s′_0 − 2k_0).

  • Proof of Lemma 3, last paragraph: "for Lemma 2, k_{r−1} = [(k_{r−2})_1 k_0 …]" refers to the construction in Lemma 3's own induction step.

  • Footnote/Definition 7: "S(v_l, r) = [C(v_l, 0), …, C(v_l, r − 1)]", but Section 4.2 writes the state after round 0 as S(v_l, 0) = [{(1,[⊥]), (2,[⊥])}]. The two indexings differ by one (source of item 2).

Modelling choices and differences

  • Rounds. Our t counts completed rounds (DAlgo.leader M t, NAlgo.run G t v). The paper's M_r, k_r, m_r are our objects for t = r + 1 (words of length r + 1).

  • Counting (Definition 2). "outputs |V| and terminates" is formalized as "the first output of the leader is |V|" (FirstOutput), with a deadline f |V| for a whole class of networks (CountsWithin, CountsBy). A per-instance lower bound (the literal reading of Lemma 1 / Theorem 1: "on an instance M") is meaningless for deterministic algorithms (an algorithm can hard-code one answer), so all lower bounds are stated for classes with a uniform deadline function.

  • Theorem 1 is stated as 2|W| + 1 < 3^(f |W|) (theorem_1), i.e. f |W| > ⌊log₃(2|W|+1)⌋ (theorem_1_log). The paper: no solution at a round r < ⌊log₃(2|W|+1)⌋ − 1, i.e. at least ⌊log₃(2|W|+1)⌋ rounds; ours is stronger by one round.

  • M(DBL_k). Parallel edges between w and the leader carry distinct labels, so a round of M is a nonempty label set L r w ⊆ Fin k; labels 1, 2 of the paper are 0, 1 in Lean. A node receives the leader's message once per incident edge, with the label (DAlgo.run); the leader receives (label, message) for every edge.

  • Leader state (Definition 7). The paper defines the leader state as the sequence of multisets C(v_l, i) and remarks that it can be built by a message-passing protocol. We prove both directions: every algorithm's leader state is a function of these views (DAlgo.leader_eq_of_view_eq), and the full-information algorithm fullInfo computes them (fullInfo_leader).

  • Lemma 5 construction. Nodes following the words with k_r = −1 (resp. +1), one per word, plus n − Σ⁻k_r extra nodes with label set {1,2} in every round (kGraph). The paper's s_r with (s_r)_j ≥ 1 exactly on the negative positions and Σ s_r = n is the same up to where the extra mass is put. Views are compared by counting (card_rowMem_pos_eq_neg).

  • Lemma 5 sharpness (lemma_5_sharp) is an addition: it is the uniqueness part of the paper's kernel discussion ("under which condition of the kernel space we have a unique solution").

  • Lemmas 2–4 are stated for k = 2, as in the paper. Lemma 2 is stated both as a kernel characterization over any commutative ring (inKer_iff) and in matrix form over ℚ (lemma_2: ker = span{k_r}, finrank ker = 1, rank = #rows = 3^(r+1) − 1, rows linearly independent). Columns and rows are indexed by words (List Lab), not by positions in the lexicographic order. Lemma 3's block form k_r = [k_{r−1}, k_{r−1}, −k_{r−1}] is the first-letter recursion kvec_cons (lemma_3).

  • Lemma 1 is proved for every k and every chain length L (L = 0 is the paper's G). The relays (V₁) are pairwise adjacent in our G; the paper does not fix edges inside V₁. The paper's G_id (relays with identifiers) is skipped: we simulate the anonymous G directly (the M(DBL_k)-leader knows each relay's identity from the labels). The simulation is round-for-round (simAlgo_output): there is no delay, since the M-leader simulates the relays in the same round.

  • Theorem 2 is proved directly (chain_lower_bound with L = 0) and also by the paper's route (theorem_2_via_lemma_1). The direct proof gains one round because the G(PD)₂ leader hears from V₂ one round late. The class is InPD 2 (connected rounds, persistent distances ≤ 2); the hard instances are chainNet 2 0 M.

  • Corollary 1. The class is "every round connected and dynamic diameter ≤ D" for D ≥ 3 (DiamLE, via CSLib journeys; "a flood from (x, r) reaches y by the end of round r + D − 1", matching Figure 1, where D = 4 and the flood started at round 0 arrives at round 3). For D = 2 the construction does not apply (W-to-W floods need 3 rounds), and D + Ω(log |V|) is then the same as Theorem 2.

  • Not formalized: the Discussion's O(1) algorithm with degree knowledge; the claim that the bound also holds in the model without edges inside a layer; the abstract's "D ≤ 2h in G(PD)_h"; Figure examples.
