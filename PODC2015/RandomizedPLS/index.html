Randomized Proof-Labeling Schemes — PODC 2015

Mor Baruch, Pierre Fraigniaud, Boaz Patt-Shamir. Randomized Proof-Labeling Schemes. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767421. Formalized version: the 10-page author copy (no arXiv version; the proof of Theorem 3.6 is omitted there).

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. The main message holds: Theorem 3.1 (a PLS with κ-bit labels becomes a one-sided RPLS with 2 log₂ κ + 6-bit certificates) and Corollary 3.5 (O(log n + log k) certificates for every predicate). Proposition 4.3 and Theorems 4.4 and 4.7, stated for any G with P(G) ≠ P(σ(G)), are false when G is illegal and all its crossings are legal, but they hold for a legal G (as do Propositions 4.6 and 4.8), which is all that the acyclicity bounds of Theorem 5.1 and the other applications use. The MST parts of Theorem 5.1 and the upper bounds of Theorems 5.2 and 5.3 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 Proposition 4.3, Theorems 4.4 and 4.7 (the crossing tool for an illegal G)

The crossing lower bounds are stated for any G with P(G) ≠ P(σ(G)), but they are false when G is illegal and all its crossings are legal: on a windmill graph a PLS with 3 + log₂ log₂ r bits exists, while Theorem 4.4 promises Ω(log r) bits (thm_4_4_false, thm_4_7_false, prop_4_3_false).

Impact. The tools are over-general as stated; all applications in Section 5 use a legal G, and in that case we prove the tools (prop_4_3, thm_4_4, thm_4_7_correct, prop_4_8). The paper's main message (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.5) is unaffected.

Details

Theorems 4.4 and 4.7 only ask that every crossing flips the predicate: P(G_s) ≠ P(σ^{ij}₁(G)_s). The proof of Proposition 4.3 covers the case "G_s is rejected" with a single labelling of G (the pigeonholed prover labels). Rejection of the crossing, though, is about every labelling, and a legal crossing comes with its own prover labels.

Counterexample. Take the windmill: a hub joined to L_k and R_k (k < r), plus the edges L_k R_k. These r edges are independent port-preserving copies of one edge (s = 1). States are identities, F is G together with its crossings, and P says "some (k,·) has a neighbour (k',·) with k' ≠ k". So P(G) is false and every crossing is legal, and all configurations are connected. A PLS with 3 + log₂ log₂ r bits exists:

  • a crossed L_k raises a flag, and the hub checks that some flag is up;

  • each R_m publishes a bit position t and the bit m_t, and checks them;

  • a flagged L_k checks that its port-0 neighbour's bit differs from k_t.

In G, the flagged L_k faces R_k, so G is rejected under every labelling.

  • Theorem 4.4 promises Ω(log r / s) = Ω(log r) bits (thm_4_4_false).

  • Compiling this PLS with Theorem 3.1 gives an edge-independent two-sided RPLS with O(log log log r) bits. Theorem 4.7 promises Ω(log log r) bits (thm_4_7_false).

  • For r = 2^32 and 8-bit labels, Proposition 4.3 fails for every pair: G is rejected and the crossing is accepted (prop_4_3_false).

Repair

Add the condition P(G) = TRUE, which Propositions 4.6 and 4.8 already carry (prop_4_3, thm_4_4, thm_4_7_correct).

✏️ Slips

  • Propositions 4.3, 4.6 and 4.8 count as if every label or certificate had exactly κ bits. With "at most κ bits" there are 2^(κ+1) − 1 strings, so our thresholds read r ≤ (2^(κ+1)−1)^(2s) (Prop. 4.3) and r ≤ 2^((2^(κ+1)−1)·2s) (Prop. 4.8). The asymptotics are the same.

  • In the proof of Theorem 5.4 the first copy is printed as H_1 = G_e{v₀, v₁}. v₀ is adjacent to every other node, so this copy is not independent of the others (printed_H1_not_independent); {v₁, v₂} as in Theorem 5.2 is clearly meant.

  • Theorem 5.6's graph ("n/c cycles, except one of at most c nodes") is vague when the leftover has 1–2 nodes; we take n = m·c.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Proposition 4.6 · Theorem 5.1

🔧 Proposition 4.6 (rounding the certificate distributions)

The proof rounds the joint distribution of the certificates of a copy, but Eq. (6) uses that one arc's marginal is ε-close, while equal joint roundings only give closeness ε·2^((2s−1)κ).

Impact. Statement true; repaired (prop_4_6, thm_4_7_loglog). The chosen ε happens to absorb this factor (the total error is exactly 1/3), so Eqs. (6)–(8) need fixing, but the conclusion survives.

Repair

We round each arc separately and get log₂ log₂ r ≤ 2κ + 5 + 2 log₂ s.

🔧 Theorem 5.1 (Theorem 4.7 applied outside its hypotheses)

The crossed path is not in F; the proof uses its cycle component instead.

Impact. Statement true. This is sound because verification is local. We state the tools for crossings whose union of components is an illegal member of F (Refuted).

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Configurations. A configuration is port-numbered: a finite node type, neighbour lists by port, and states, which carry identities when the paper assumes them (Config, Basic.lean). Networks are connected wherever the paper needs it. The Section 5 lower bounds hold for every family that contains the connected configurations with distinct identities (hFF).

  • Verification. One synchronous round, no failures.

    • PLS: the verifier sees its state, its label and its neighbours' labels by port. Labels are bit strings.

    • RPLS: every node draws private uniform coins. The coin space may depend on the node's state, label and degree, and so may the certificate it sends on each port. The decision is a function of the state, the label and the certificates received.

    • One-sided means acceptance with probability 1 and rejection with probability ≥ 1/2; two-sided means 2/3 on both sides. Edge independence means one coin per port (EIScheme).

  • Complexity. Verification complexity is a bound on label (resp. certificate) length on legal configurations. It may depend on the configuration, e.g. on n.

  • Crossings. Crossings are built from r pairwise independent port-preserving copies of a pattern graph (Copies.cross). They keep ports, states and degrees.

  • Lemma 3.4 (cited) silently needs connected configurations, distinct identities and an isomorphism-invariant P; all three hold in the paper's model.
