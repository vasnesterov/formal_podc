We build the slightly more general `Wheel.G n c`: a `c`-cycle `v₀ … v_{c-1}`, `v₀` adjacent to every other node `v₁ … v_{n-1}` (so `v_c, …, v_{n-1}` are pendant nodes); `Wheel.G n n` is the graph of Theorem 5.2 and `Wheel.G n c` the graph of Theorem 5.4. States are the identities `0, …, n-1`. Port numbers are "consistently ordered": a cycle node `v_x` (`1 ≤ x ≤ c-1`) has its successor at port `0` and its predecessor at port `1`. The copies are the single edges `H_k = {v_{3k+1}, v_{3k+2}}`, `k < ⌊c/3⌋` (the paper uses `{v₁, v₂}` and `{v_{3i}, v_{3i+1}}`, `2 ≤ i ≤ ⌊c/3⌋ - 1`, i.e. one copy less; any family of single cycle edges at distance `≥ 2` works). Crossing `{v_a, v_{a+1}}` and `{v_b, v_{b+1}}` (`a < b`) replaces them by `{v_a, v_{b+1}}` and `{v_b, v_{a+1}}`: the cycle splits into `v_{a+1} … v_b` and the rest, which are only joined through `v₀`.
