import Papers.PODC2015.RandomizedPLS.Compile import Cslib.Foundations.Data.List.BitEncoding /-! # The universal scheme (Lemma 3.4, Corollary 3.5) Lemma 3.4 (cited from [20, 30]): with states of `k` bits, every predicate has a PLS whose label is a description of the whole configuration. We formalize the classical scheme: * the label of `v` is `lab (index of v) D`, where `D` lists, for every node in some enumeration, its state and the indices of its neighbours ordered by port (bit-encoded with CSLib's self-delimiting `encList`); * the verifier at `v` checks that its own row of `D` carries its state, that the neighbour at each port `p` carries the label `lab (D[i].2[p]) D`, and that `D` describes a connected legal configuration (an existential check: the local computation is unbounded, as in the paper). Soundness needs the implicit assumptions of the paper's model: configurations are connected, the states contain distinct identities, and `P` is invariant under port-preserving isomorphisms (`Config.Iso`). Label length (`universal_labelBound`): `2 log n + 4 + 2n(4k + 3) + 4(2 log n + 3) · Σ_v deg v = O(nk + m log n)`; the paper states `O(nk + min{n², m log n})` (we do not formalize the adjacency-matrix variant for dense graphs).
