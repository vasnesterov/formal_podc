Patt-Shamir. *Randomized Proof-Labeling Schemes.* PODC 2015 [BFP15]. The proof follows the paper's idea (round the certificate distributions of the copies, pigeonhole, hybrid argument over the arcs of the two crossed copies) with a cleaner bookkeeping: * `EIScheme.accProb_eq_sum` (edge independence): the acceptance probability is the expectation of the acceptance event `accEvent` under a product distribution over the *receiving slots* `(x, q)` (`Config.Ports`); slot `(x, q)` independently receives a certificate distributed as the certificates that the neighbour `sender (x, q)` sends on its port towards `x` (`slotDist`). The event only depends on states, labels and degrees, so it is the same in `c` and in the crossing; only the slot distributions change, and only on the `≤ 4s` slots on arcs of the two crossed copies (`Copies.slotDist_cross`, `Copies.card_arcSlots_le`). * `abs_sum_mul_prod_sub_le` (hybrid argument): two product distributions give expectations of a `[-1, 1]`-valued function that differ by at most `∑_slots ‖μ_slot - ν_slot‖₁`. * Pigeonhole on the `1/m`-rounded *marginal* distributions of the `2s` arcs of each copy (`m = 12 s 2^(κ+1) + 1`), instead of the paper's rounding of the joint distribution over all `2^(2sκ)` certificate vectors of a copy. ## Main statements * `prop_4_6`: if `r > crossingBound κ s = (12 s 2^(κ+1) + 2)^(2 s 2^(κ+1))`, two copies can be crossed changing the acceptance probability by less than `1/3`. * `thm_4_7_correct`: for a two-sided edge-independent RPLS, a legal configuration whose crossings are all illegal has `r ≤ crossingBound (κ c) s`;
