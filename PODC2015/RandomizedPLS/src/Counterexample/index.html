import Papers.PODC2015.RandomizedPLS.LowerBound /-! # Theorem 4.4 (and Proposition 4.3) fail when `P(G) = FALSE` Theorem 4.4 assumes `P(G_s) ≠ P(σ^{ij}₁(G)_s)` for all `i ≠ j`; the paper only proves (and only uses) the case `P(G) = TRUE`. Proposition 4.3's proof also treats the case where `G` is rejected, but it transfers *one* labelling of `G` to the crossing, whereas rejection of the crossing concerns *all* labellings — and when `P(G) = FALSE` the crossings are legal and carry their own prover labels. **Counterexample.** The windmill `G_r`: a hub adjacent to `L_k, R_k` (`k < r`), plus the edges `L_k R_k`. The `r` edges `L_k R_k` are pairwise independent port-preserving copies of a single edge (`s = 1`). States are identities. Let `F = {G} ∪ {crossings}` and `P` = "some node `(k, ·)` has a neighbour `(k', ·)` with `k' ≠ k`": `P(G) = FALSE` and every crossing is legal. Yet `(F, P)` has a PLS with `O(log log r)`-bit labels: * a crossed `L_k` (its port-0 neighbour is `R_{k'}`, `k' ≠ k`) raises a flag `1`; * every `R_m` carries a bit position `t` (in binary) and the bit `m_t`, and checks it (and that its own flag is `0`); a flagged `L_k` checks that its port-0 neighbour's bit differs from `k_t` (so that neighbour is not `R_k`); the prover picks a position where `k` and `k'` differ; * the hub checks that some neighbour is flagged. In `G` the flagged `L_k` would face `R_k`, whose bit equals `k_t` — rejected for every labelling. Theorem 4.4 would demand `Ω(log r / s) = Ω(log r)` bits (`thm_4_4_false`), and Proposition 4.3's conclusion fails for every pair (`prop_4_3_false`).
