Differences between the formalization and the paper

Rendered from differencies.md of Randomized Proof-Labeling Schemes — PODC 2015.

Paper: M. Baruch, P. Fraigniaud, B. Patt-Shamir, Randomized Proof-Labeling Schemes, PODC 2015 (10-page author copy; the proof of Theorem 3.6 is omitted there).

Model

  • Configurations (Config, Basic.lean): any finite node type, neighbours listed by port (ports 0, …, deg-1 instead of 1, …, deg), states in an arbitrary type S (identities are part of the state when needed). Connectivity is a property families may require (Config.Connected), not built in. The paper always takes networks connected; every configuration we use in a counterexample is connected.

  • PLS (IsPLS): verifier = function of (own state, own label, neighbours' labels by port); labels are bit strings (List Bool); verification complexity = a bound κ c on label lengths on legal configurations (LabelBound), so κ may depend on n or on anything else.

  • RPLS (RScheme, IsRPLS): each node draws private coins uniformly from a finite space that may depend on its state, label and degree (the paper: "independent random bits"; uniform finite spaces are slightly more general and needed because adversarial labels are unbounded); the certificate on each port is a function of the coins (we also allow it to depend on the state and degree, which only strengthens our lower bounds); the decision is a deterministic function of state, label and received certificates, as in the paper. Labels of an RPLS can be of any type (their size is not measured, Definition 2.1); our upper bounds use List (List Bool) (a list of bit strings, which could be bit-encoded without affecting certificate sizes). One-sided: pacc = 1, prej = 1/2; two-sided: pacc = prej = 2/3. Edge independence (Definition 4.5) is structural (EIScheme).

  • Copies and crossings (Copies, Copies.cross): r copies emb i : W → V of a pattern graph H, pairwise vertex-disjoint and non-adjacent (Definition 4.1), port-preserving. Crossing (Definition 4.2) keeps port numbers. The paper counts |V(H_i)| ≤ 2s; we pigeonhole on the non-isolated pattern vertices (card_supp_le), which gives the same count.

  • Refuted crossings (Refuted): Theorems 4.4/4.7/Proposition 4.8 are stated with the hypothesis that each crossing, or a union of its connected components, is an illegal member of F. The paper's theorems require the crossing itself to be in F, but the proof of Theorem 5.1 applies them to a component ("σ₁(P) ∉ F, but C is a connected component of σ₁(P) and C ∈ F"). The restriction lemmas (Restrict.lean) justify this (locality; for probabilities, a marginal lemma). Proof gap in Theorem 5.1, repaired (🔧).

Statement-level issues

  1. 🔧 Proposition 4.3, Theorem 4.4 and Theorem 4.7 are false when P(G) = FALSE (see "Notes on item 1" below). Theorems 4.4 and 4.7 assume P(G_s) ≠ P(σ^{ij}₁(G)_s) (checked on the rendered page 5). Proposition 4.3's proof also treats "G_s is rejected" explicitly, but transfers one labelling of G to the crossing, while rejection of the crossing concerns all of its labellings. Counterexample (Counterexample.lean, Refutations.lean): windmill G_r (hub + triangles hub, L_k, R_k), copies L_k R_k (s = 1), states = identities, F = {G_r} ∪ {crossings}, P = "some node (k,·) has a neighbour (k',·) with k' ≠ k". All hypotheses hold, all configurations are connected, and (F, P) has a PLS with 3 + ⌊log₂ log₂ r⌋-bit labels (isPLS, labelBound):

    • thm_4_4_false: no C > 0, D with κ ≥ C log₂ r / s − D for all instances;

    • thm_4_7_false: compiling with Theorem 3.1 gives an edge-independent one-sided (hence two-sided) RPLS with O(log log log r)-bit certificates, so no κ ≥ C log₂ log₂ r / s − D;

    • prop_4_3_false: for r = 2^32, 8-bit labels (so κ < log r / 2s), every pair i ≠ j has G rejected and the crossing accepted with the prover's labels. The case P(G) = TRUE (the only one used in Section 5) is proved: prop_4_3, thm_4_4, thm_4_7_correct (TwoSided.lean), and Proposition 4.8 (which the paper states only for P(G) = TRUE).

  2. ✏️ Proposition 4.3 and 4.8: exact constants. The paper counts 2s labels of < log r / 2s bits as < r bit strings, i.e. as if all labels had exactly κ bits ("w.l.o.g. exactly κ bits" in Prop. 4.6/4.8). With labels/certificates of at most κ bits there are 2^(κ+1) − 1 of them. We prove r ≤ (2^(κ+1) − 1)^(2s) (Prop. 4.3/Thm 4.4, i.e. κ + 1 ≥ log₂ r / 2s) and r ≤ (2^(2^(κ+1) − 1))^(2s) (Prop. 4.8, i.e. 2s(2^(κ+1) − 1) ≥ log₂ r). The printed thresholds κ < log r / 2s, κ < (1/2s) log log r are not proved as such (a refutation would need a scheme exploiting variable lengths; not attempted). Same asymptotics; proof imprecision.

  3. Theorem 3.1: O(log κ) made explicit: 2⌈log₂ 3(κ+1)⌉ ≤ 2 log₂ κ + 6 bits; the scheme is one-sided with rejection probability > 2/3, so it is also two-sided, and edge-independent. Fingerprints over GF(2^m) (Fingerprint.lean) instead of an unspecified protocol from [32]; the message has a length that depends on the label length, and labels of different lengths with different field sizes are rejected deterministically.

  4. Lemma 3.4 / Corollary 3.5 (Universal.lean): Lemma 3.4 is cited by the paper. Our universal scheme needs three assumptions implicit in the paper's model: connected configurations, distinct identities in the states, and P invariant under port-preserving isomorphisms (Config.Iso); states are encoded injectively with ≤ k bits. Label bound 2 log n + 4 + 2n(4k+3) + 4(2 log n + 3) Σ deg = O(nk + m log n); the paper's O(nk + min{n², m log n}) also uses an adjacency-matrix variant for dense graphs (not formalized; irrelevant for Corollary 3.5). Corollary 3.5: certificates of 2 log₂(32(k+1)n³) + 6 bits.

  5. Proposition 4.6 / Theorem 4.7 (TwoSided.lean): explicit threshold r > (12 s 2^(κ+1) + 2)^(2s·2^(κ+1)), giving log₂ log₂ r ≤ 2κ + 5 + 2 log₂ s (thm_4_7_loglog), i.e. Ω(log log r) for fixed s (stronger than the printed Ω(log log r / s)). The paper's (1/2s − o(1)) log log r threshold with its "technical observation" is not reproduced literally.

Proof-level issues (true statements)

  1. 🔧 Proposition 4.6 proof. It rounds the joint distribution of the 2s certificates of a copy, but Eq. (6) uses that a single arc's marginal is ε-close; equal joint roundings only give marginal closeness ε·2^((2s−1)κ). With the chosen ε = 1/(12s·2^(2sκ)) the total error is still 4s · 2^κ · ε · 2^((2s−1)κ) = 1/3, so the argument survives after correcting Eqs. (6)–(8). We round every arc's distribution separately. Also "w.l.o.g. exactly κ bits" (see 2).

  2. 🔧 Theorem 5.1 applies Theorem 4.7 to a connected component of the crossing, outside the theorem's hypotheses (see "Refuted crossings" above); repaired by the restriction lemmas.

  3. Theorem 5.1 is stated for MST; we formalize acyclicity (the predicate its lower-bound proof uses) on lines/cycles (LinesCycles; as for Theorems 5.2–5.6 the lower bounds are stated for every family F containing the lines and cycles with distinct identities, e.g. F_con) and the acyclicity upper bound on F_con (thm_5_1_upper, deterministic ⌊log₂ n⌋ + 1-bit scheme + Theorem 3.1). The MST upper bound (KKP's O(log² n) MST scheme) is not formalized. The lower bounds use the paper's copies {u_{3k}, u_{3k+1}} (0-based, k < ⌊n/3⌋).

  4. Theorems 5.2, 5.4, 5.6 (Biconnectivity.lean, CycleAtLeast.lean, CycleAtMost.lean): only the lower bounds, deterministic (Theorem 4.4) and one-sided (Proposition 4.8); the two-sided edge-independent versions follow from thm_4_7_loglog as for Theorem 5.1 but are not instantiated. The upper bounds of Theorems 5.2 and 5.3 are only sketched in the paper and are not formalized; Theorem 5.5 is not formalized. Families: the lower bounds hold for every family F containing the connected configurations whose states (in ℕ) are distinct identities (hypothesis hFF; F = F_con with or without the distinctness requirement are instances), so they apply to schemes that only need to be sound in the paper's identifier model; κ is a function of n.

    • Copies are {v_{3k+1}, v_{3k+2}}, k < ⌊n/3⌋ (resp. ⌊c/3⌋), one more copy than the paper's range; the paper's ranges are consistent.

    • ✏️ Theorem 5.4: printed H_1 = G_e{v₀, v₁} is not independent of the other copies (v₀ is adjacent to all nodes; printed_H1_not_independent). Typo with evident intent ({v₁,v₂} as in Theorem 5.2), inside a proof.

    • Theorem 5.4: the claim "every simple cycle of the crossing has < c nodes" is proved via a cut-vertex/block argument (cycle_support_of_cut); the paper only asserts it.

    • ✏️ Theorem 5.6: the graph is a chain of m cycles of c nodes (n = m·c); the paper's "except one of at most c nodes" is not a cycle when 1–2 nodes are left over.

Notes on item 1

  • Proposition 4.3 and Theorems 4.4, 4.7 are false as printed (item 1). The paper explicitly states the general condition P(G) ≠ P(σ(G)), and Proposition 4.3's proof explicitly (and wrongly) argues the rejected case. The counterexample satisfies every hypothesis of the paper's model (connected networks, distinct identities, port-preserving independent copies).

  • Is "G legal" a standing assumption? No. Propositions 4.6 and 4.8 explicitly assume P(G_s) = TRUE, while Proposition 4.3 has no legality hypothesis and its proof argues the case "G_s is rejected" explicitly, and Theorems 4.4 and 4.7 ask only P(G_s) ≠ P(σ(G)_s). The authors distinguished the two forms. For Theorem 4.7 alone one could argue that legality is meant (it is announced as a corollary of Proposition 4.6, which assumes P(G) = TRUE), but Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4 are argued with the general version.

  • Role in the paper. The abstract's primary claim is the exponential saving of randomization (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.5), which is correct. The crossing technique is introduced "In addition" (abstract and contributions), and is correct in the form used everywhere in the paper (P(G) = TRUE). What the abstract claims of it is that the technique exists and applies to deterministic and randomized schemes; that claim survives in the P(G) = TRUE form, which yields every bound of Section 5. Only the over-general formal statements are false.
