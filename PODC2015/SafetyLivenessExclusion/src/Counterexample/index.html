import Papers.PODC2015.SafetyLivenessExclusion.Theorem49 /-! # Theorem 4.9 fails when processes may take steps while idle In the paper's I/O-automaton model a process automaton `A_{I_i}` is arbitrary; nothing forbids internal actions of a process that is not executing an operation. With such *idle steps* the commutation claim of the proof of Theorem 4.9 ("because `A_{I'}` is input-enabled, every history `h'` with a single response and `h'|p_i = inv_i · res_i` is a history of `A_{I'}`") is false, and so is the theorem. **The counterexample** (`n = 2`, one invocation `()` and one response `()`): * Safety property `S` (`S`): a well-formed history is in `S` unless `p₀` receives a response after `p₁` crashed *before `p₀`'s first invocation and without ever having invoked*. It is prefix- and limit-closed, every invocation can be answered sequentially, and every history of `S` is a history of the implementation `Is` below, which ensures `S`. * `Is` (`Is`): base objects are a counter and a flag. `p₁` increments the counter at every step, also while idle (a heartbeat), and sets the flag at the first step of each operation. On its first invocation `p₀` reads the counter and waits until the counter changes or the flag is set (evidence that `p₁` was alive after `p₀`'s invocation, or invoked); later invocations are answered at once. * `h₀ = crash₁ · inv₀` is fair for *every* valid implementation ensuring `S`: after it no response to `p₀` can ever be given (`h0_mem_fairH`). * With `Lmax = U \ {h₀}` (every finite well-formed history has an extension in it) the strongest liveness property not excluding `S` is `U ≠ Lmax` (`theorem_4_9_false`). `CounterexampleWF.lean` upgrades this to `Lmax =` wait-freedom. -/
