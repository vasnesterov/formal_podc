Safety-Liveness Exclusion in Distributed Computing — PODC 2015

Victor Bushkov, Rachid Guerraoui. Safety-Liveness Exclusion in Distributed Computing. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767401. Formalized version: the authors' full version with proofs (EPFL Infoscience record 207929, 19 pages); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. Theorem 4.4 (when a weakest excluded liveness property exists) and Lemma 4.8 are correct, and Theorem 4.9 ("a strongest non-excluded liveness property must be Lmax") holds, with a locally repaired proof, if processes take steps only while they execute an operation. That is the convention of the paper's prose, but its formal I/O-automaton model does not impose it, and there the theorem is false even with Lmax = wait-freedom. The (l,k)-freedom theorems of §5 follow from the cited (im)possibility results once "progress" for TM is read as the authors' earlier work defines it; those cited results, the TM model and Lemma 5.4 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

🔧 §3.2 (the order on liveness properties)

The claim "L₁ is weaker than L₂ iff L₂ ⊆ L₁" is false: for reactive implementations, wait-freedom and wait-freedom plus the never-fair history inv₀ (with p₁ idle forever) are ensured by exactly the same implementations (weaker_iff_subset_false).

Impact. All proofs of the paper use ⊆, and we formalize every result with ⊆. With the paper's semantic definition, strongest and weakest properties are determined only up to equivalence: "it should be Lmax" in Theorem 4.9 then holds only up to equivalence.

🔧 §5.1 (the remark on LF_l ∪ OF_k, also "a union" in the Introduction)

"LF_l ∪ OF_k is the set of all executions that ensure (l,k)-freedom" is false: only ⊆ holds (lkFree_imp_lf_or_of), because two correct processes, one of them stepping and progressing alone, give an execution that is 2-obstruction-free but not (2,2)-free (lf_union_of_not_iff).

Impact. The remark is not used: all results rest on Definition 5.1.

✏️ Slips

  • Theorem 4.9, implied assumption: in the paper's I/O-automaton model, which lets a process take steps while it executes no operation, Theorem 4.9 is false even for Lmax = wait-freedom: for two processes, a safety property S meeting all the paper's assumptions has a strongest non-excluded liveness property strictly weaker than wait-freedom (theorem_4_9_false_waitFree). §2 describes processes as running their algorithm when invoked, and every implementation in the paper does so. For such reactive implementations Theorem 4.9 holds (theorem_4_9), and Corollaries 4.10–4.11 follow from the cited impossibilities (corollary_4_10). Whether this is an implied assumption is a borderline call; see the details below and differencies.md.

  • Theorem 4.9, proof: it ignores the case where every process crashed in h, in which there is no correct pending process to answer. Such a history has no well-formed extension, so the assumption on Lmax puts it into Lmax (core_subset_Lmax).

  • Theorem 4.9 with no invocations: the empty history is then fair for every reactive implementation, so an Lmax omitting it makes the theorem fail. We assume [Nonempty Inv].

  • Definition 5.1 and Theorem 5.3: read literally, Definition 5.1 makes Theorem 5.3 false. If "good" responses are those that do not abort, a TM that aborts every commit request is opaque and (n,n)-free, and if they are commits, a correct process that never invokes tryC makes every (1,k)-freedom unimplementable (argued, not formalized). The intent can be recovered from §4 ("the set of 'good' events is restricted to commit events") and from the authors' [4], which exempts parasitic processes. With that reading, the cited results give Theorem 5.3. We formalize it from those results, taken as hypotheses (theorem_5_3).

  • §4.1, example before Corollary 4.5: some histories listed in F₁ (e.g. propose₁(v) · v₁ · propose₂(v')) are never fair, because correct idle processes must keep invoking. For n ≥ 3, F₁ must also crash the other processes. The corollary is unaffected.

  • §5.3: the second requirement of S says the transactions "should be aborted in h". This is not prefix-closed; "none of them commits" is meant.

  • §2: input-enabledness is stated for every state at which pᵢ is "not pending". Read literally, this includes unreachable states and the crashed state, where nothing may be enabled. We require it along executions (see differencies.md).

Details (Theorem 4.9 with idle steps). S contains every well-formed history except those in which p₀ receives a response after p₁ crashed before p₀'s first invocation without ever having invoked. S is prefix- and limit-closed, every invocation can be answered sequentially, and every history of S is a history of an implementation ensuring S, namely Is.

The implementation Is has a single-writer register (counter, flag) written by p₁. p₁ increments the counter at every step, also while idle (a heartbeat), and sets the flag in each operation. On its first invocation p₀ reads the counter, then waits until the counter changes or the flag is set. Either event proves that p₁ was alive after p₀'s invocation, or had invoked. Later invocations of p₀ are answered at once.

Two facts make the counterexample work:

  • In every valid implementation ensuring S, the histories Y are fair (Y_subset_fairH). In these histories p₁ crashes, possibly after invoking, p₀ invokes and waits forever, and nobody responds. The reason: if the inputs of Y are applied without internal steps, the state is the one reached after crash₁ · inv₀, because p₁'s inputs touch only p₁'s local state and the crash erases it. From that state S forbids any answer to p₀.

  • Every other fair history of Is is wait-free (Is_fairH_subset).

So WaitFree ∪ Y is the strongest non-excluded liveness property (isStrongestNonExcl_waitFree_Y), and it is not wait-freedom.

The proof breaks at its claim "because A_I' is input-enabled, every history h' with a single response and h'|pᵢ = invᵢ · resᵢ is a history of A_I'". This needs the other processes' invocations and crashes to commute with pᵢ's run. That fails as soon as pᵢ waits for an idle process. Is is valid but not reactive (Is_not_reactive).

Repair (Theorem 4.9). We assume that processes take no step while idle, and repair the proof (see Proof gaps). theorem_4_9 holds for every class of reactive valid implementations that contains the two trivial implementations of the paper's proof. One example is the reactive implementations from registers. The hypotheses can be met (nonvacuous_4_9).

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 4.9

🔧 Theorem 4.9 (proof, reactive implementations)

The proof derives invₗ · resₗ ∈ S from h · resₗ ∈ S, which does not follow.

Impact. Statement true for reactive implementations; repaired (core_subset_Lmax, theorem_4_9).

Details

For consensus, propose₂(1) · propose₁(0) · decide₁(1) satisfies agreement and validity, but propose₁(0) · decide₁(1) violates validity. The paper's second and third steps pick the response resₗ in opposite orders.

Repair

We use the characterization Setting.isStrongestNonExcl_iff: a strongest non-excluded property is Lmax ∪ core, where the core is the set of histories fair for every implementation ensuring S. It then suffices to show core ⊆ Lmax.

  • Take a core history h. The silent implementation ensures S, so h has no response, and every process is pending or crashed at its end.

  • If all processes crashed, h has no well-formed extension, so the assumption on Lmax puts h into Lmax.

  • Otherwise take a pending correct p_l and its invocation x. First choose r with inv_l x · res_l r ∈ S (the paper's assumption on S), then an implementation I' ensuring S with this history. The one-shot responder that answers r ensures S: each of its histories is a history of I' by the frame lemma Run.frame, which uses reactivity. Yet h is not fair for the responder.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • Asynchronous shared memory, n processes, crash failures (any number), deterministic adversary (scheduler and environment), no randomness. An implementation is the paper's composition A_{I₁} × … × A_{Iₙ} × A_B (Impl). Each process has a local state, or the crashed state. The base objects have a state. Invocations, responses and crashes touch only their process's local state. Internal steps of a process may access the base objects. A_B may have internal actions.

  • Fairness is the paper's (§3.2), and fairH I is fair(A_I). Histories are finite or infinite sequences of inv/res/crash events. Well-formedness also forbids events after a crash.

  • Valid implementations (Impl.Valid) have an initial state, produce only well-formed histories, and are input-enabled along executions. Reactive implementations (Impl.Reactive) take no step of a process that is not pending.

  • Safety and liveness properties follow Definitions 3.1–3.2. The paper's assumptions on S and Lmax are SafetyAssumptions and LmaxAssumptions. The class of admissible implementations, e.g. "from registers", is a parameter 𝓒.

  • Sections 3–4 are proved in an abstract setting (Abstract.lean) and instantiated in the model (setting).

  • Section 5 works on what Definition 5.1 sees of a fair execution: the correct, infinitely-stepping and progressing processes. The cited (im)possibility results are hypotheses.

Remarks. Theorem 4.4 needs no property of the model except that fair histories of an implementation ensuring S lie in S (limit closure), and that some implementation ensures S; both follow from the paper's assumptions. Its statement is also correct when there is no adversary set (Gmax is then everything). Theorem 4.9 is equivalent to "core ⊆ Lmax" whenever a strongest non-excluded property exists (Setting.core_subset_of_eq_Lmax).

Not formalized. Lemma 5.4 (Algorithm I(1,2): opacity, the second requirement of S, (1,2)-freedom) and the TM model. The cited results [4], [5], [9], [17], [20] enter only as hypotheses: the concrete adversary sets of Corollaries 4.5–4.6, and "Lmax excludes S" in Corollaries 4.10–4.11.

Trust. No sorry; the listed theorems use only the axioms propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Non-vacuity: nonvacuous_4_9.
