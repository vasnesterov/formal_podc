Differences between the formalization and [AEM15]

Rendered from differencies.md of Limitations of Highly-Available Eventually-Consistent Data Stores — PODC 2015.

Paper: Attiya, Ellen, Morrison, Limitations of Highly-Available Eventually-Consistent Data Stores, PODC 2015 (authors' copy). Lemma 5, Lemma 7 and parts of Theorem 12 are proved only in the full version (not available); all their proofs here are ours.

Model

  1. Replicas (Store): one partial transition function per replica, step i σ e : Option St, with a common state type. The paper's assumption that send is deterministic (the message is a function of the state) is not needed and not assumed.

  2. Executions (IsExec, Def. 1) are built event by event; a receipt of m at i needs an earlier send(m) at some j ≠ i. Infinite executions are sequences all of whose prefixes are executions (IsInfExec).

  3. Happens-before (Def. 2) and Proposition 1 are not formalized: we never need them. The constructions build the needed sub-executions explicitly.

  4. Implicit assumptions made explicit. Available: in every state, every operation on every object is enabled with some response ("replicas handle client operations immediately"). RecvEnabled: every receipt is enabled in every state ("messages may be delivered at any time"). The paper uses both (e.g. "Let ê be the transition in which op(e) is invoked", "we deliver it to R by appending a receive").

  5. Abstract executions are indexed by ℕ with a domain predicate (AE). ord is the order H: a strict total order on the domain in which every event has finitely many predecessors (H is a sequence). Compliance (Def. 9) is formalized as AE.On: the abstract execution lives on the do-events of the execution and agrees with its per-replica order. This is Def. 9 up to renaming events. For Theorem 6, Complies/AE.Equiv give the renaming version.

  6. MVR specification (Fig. 1b): AE.mvr; returned values are the values of the writes of the context that are not visible to another write of the context. Values need not be distinct in general; the lemmas that identify writes with values assume distinct values, as the paper does ("we assume that each write event writes a different value").

  7. Eventual consistency of a store (Def. 14, see 13 below): EventuallyConsistent P = every infinite sufficiently connected execution complies with a correct, eventually consistent abstract execution satisfying P. Causal consistency of a store (CausallyConsistent, Defs. 10–12): every finite execution complies with a causally consistent abstract execution. Theorem 12 assumes both (P arbitrary), as the paper does ("causally consistent and eventually consistent").

  8. Consistency models (Section 3.2) and satisfaction (Def. 11) appear only in Theorem6Statement, restricted to finite executions and finite abstract executions. In the paper a consistency model may contain infinite abstract executions. For those, "strictly stronger" can come from excluding infinite executions that are not eventually consistent (e.g. a model like OCC ∩ {finite or eventually consistent}), which has nothing to do with the construction of the proof (which builds finite executions). So the finite reading is the one the proof addresses.

Statements

  1. Lemma 3 (Store.lemma3): as stated, for any responses (not only sets) of the two reads. Op-driven messages are not needed.

  2. Corollary 4 (corollary4): as stated. The extension sends every pending message and then delivers every message to every other replica. Needs SendClears, OpDriven, RecvEnabled.

  3. Lemma 5 (lemma5). Changed hypothesis (🔧). "e →hb w ⇒ e →rcv w for any do event e" is unsatisfiable for earlier events of R (no replica receives its own messages). We assume instead: no replica has a message pending at the end of α (before w), and R has received every message sent in α by another replica. This is what the uses need (Theorem 6's construction delivers exactly these messages; Theorem 12's writers have sent everything). We also assume the written values distinct and w's value fresh (paper-wide assumption). Added hypothesis n ≥ 2 (✏️ slip, degenerate). With one replica the lemma is false (lemma5_false_one_replica): a store that never sends satisfies all hypotheses.

  4. Lemma 7 (lemma7): as stated (writes of the operation context of r), with "β contains r_w, w" formalized by a map φ into the other abstract execution Â preserving replica, operation and response, keeping r_w before w. Â has pairwise distinct written values. We only need from "revealing" that w's visible events are visible to r_w and that r_w precedes w at the same replica.

  5. Definition 14 literal (Store.literalEC_false): with "every abstract execution that α complies with", even restricted to correct and causally consistent ones, no store with two replicas, available invisible reads, op-driven messages and correct finite executions is eventually consistent (✏️ slip). It is reported because it makes Theorems 6 and 12 vacuous as written. We use "some" (item 7), which is what the proofs use ("β∞ complies with some causally consistent MVR abstract execution Â such that …").

  6. Definition 18 (AE.OCC): condition 1's w_{i−1} is read as w_{1−i} (typo). We add w₀ ≠ w₁: for w₀ = w₁ conditions 1 and 3 contradict each other, so a read returning a single write would violate OCC. "rval(r) ⊇ {w₀, w₁}" is formalized on writes: w₀, w₁ are maximal writes of the context of r. Condition 4 quantifies over writes ŵ (the formula says "any ŵ", the prose "no write").

  7. Theorem 6 (Theorem6Statement): stated (finite abstract executions, equivalence-closed models, with the model assumptions of item 4 and SendClears), not proved.

    • Gap 1 (❌ w.l.o.g. revealing). The proof needs the revealing extension of A to be OCC (Lemma 11 is applied to every read, including the added r_w). The literal extension of Section 5.2.1 is not OCC in general (Gap.revealing_extension_not_OCC: writes a, b concurrent, both visible to c; r_c returns {a, b} with no witnesses). A different revealing abstract execution with the same per-replica histories might be OCC (in the example, order a before b). We do not know whether one always exists.

    • Gap 2 (❌ Lemma 7 across objects). The proof of Lemma 11 derives w'_i →vis^ w₀ "from w'_i →vis w₀ and Lemma 7", with w'_i a write to another object than w₀. Lemma 7 does not cover this (its H' contains only writes to obj(r)). The claim "w'_i ∈ ctxt(A, e)" is false by Definition 7. Visibility between writes of different objects is not revealed by the reads r_w (same object), and eventual consistency only forces same-object visibility. Example: R₁ reads x (∅) and writes x; R₂ reads y (∅) and writes y, with w_x →vis r_y, w_y. This is revealing and causally consistent, and so is the abstract execution with the same responses and only per-replica visibility, in which w_x ↛vis w_y. So the step needs a new argument.

    • The other steps of Lemmas 10 and 11 are sound modulo Propositions 2, 8, 9, which we did not formalize. Our proof of Lemma 5 shows how the paper's "quiescent completion" arguments go through.

    • Status. No statement is refuted. Gap 1 is a wrong implicit claim inside the proof ("the revealing extension is still OCC"); gap 2 applies Lemma 7 outside its statement (Lemma 7 itself is true, lemma7; the cross-object form the proof needs is an unstated claim inside a proof). Lemma 11 is only unestablished: we have no store and OCC execution where the construction of Section 5.2.2 gives ê ≠ e. So Theorem 6 is unestablished, not refuted.

    • The formal statement adds the paper's standing assumption (Section 4) that written values are pairwise distinct in A; without it a store might "hide" the concurrency of two writes of the same value, which OCC does not account for.

  8. Theorem 12 (theorem12).

    • ✏️ Bound ⌊n' lg k⌋ (Nat.log 2 (k ^ n')) instead of n' lg k. The counting argument gives only the floor (bit strings of different lengths). Messages are bit strings via an injective enc : M → List Bool.

    • Hypotheses n ≥ 3, s ≥ 2 (so that n' ≥ 1) and k ≥ 1, instead of "k ≥ min{n − 2, s − 1}" (irrelevant to the proof; for n' ≤ 0 the claim is degenerate). Written values: V infinite (to have n' k + 1 distinct values).

    • 🛠️ New proof. (a) The writers' messages m^i_j and m_g exist by Lemma 5, applied to sub-executions where the writer (resp. R_{n−1}) has heard everything. (b) "w^i_j ∈ rval(r^i_j)" (full version) is proved by Cx.a_read: R_{n−1}'s state after receiving m^i_{g(i)} is final in the sub-execution where the writers stop after the delivered rounds, so by eventual consistency its read returns exactly {⟨g(i), i⟩} (Store.final_read). (c) Decoding: the paper's justification ("R_n cannot return w_y … since R_n does not know this value") is wrong, because m_g may carry R_i's writes. We replace the sequential decoder by a direct injectivity argument (Cx.decode, Cx.key, Cx.mg_injective), see README.md. R_n receives the first g(p) messages of every writer (not all k of the other writers' messages) and m_g.

    • Indices: rounds j = 0, …, k − 1 (the paper: 1, …, k), R_{n−1} = a, R_n = b.

  9. Abstract (and Section 6 intro): "an Ω(min{n, s} k)-bit message" for every k. Section 1 and Theorem 12 have lg k. Both readings follow from Theorem 12 (apply it with 2^k), since the execution length is unbounded. Not an error.

Not formalized

  1. Propositions 1, 2, 8, 9; Lemmas 10, 11 (and hence Theorem 6); Section 5.3 (CAC theorem comparison; the example store without invisible reads); the read/write register remarks at the end of Section 6; Section 7's full-version extensions.
