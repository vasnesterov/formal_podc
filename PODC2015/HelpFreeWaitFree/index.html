Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The definition of help, the impossibility for exact order types such as the queue (Theorem 4.3, after a repair of Claim 4.5.10) and for snapshots (Theorem 4.7), Claim 4.8.1 and the help-free wait-free set and max register all stand. But the stack is not an exact order type, Theorem 4.8 is proved only when a view tells which competing operations precede it, which leaves out the claimed fetch&increment and increment, and the proof of Theorem 4.11 (read/write max registers) has a gap we could not repair. Universality of fetch-and-cons was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Examples after Definition 4.5.1 (the stack is an exact order type)

The stack is not an exact order type: a stack can hide the swap of op and W_{n+1}, so no operation of R(m) tells the two executions apart (stack_exists_equal, stack_not_exactOrder, stack_not_exactOrderStrong).

Impact. Theorem 4.3 therefore says nothing about stacks; the conclusion "no wait-free help-free stack" remains true by the direct proof of V. Aksenov, P. Kuznetsov and A. Shalyto (On Helping and Stacks, NETYS 2018), who observed the error. The mistake concerns an example: the queue is an exact order type and Theorem 4.3 holds.

Details

The claim appears after Definition 4.5.1, in Sections 4.1 and 4.5.1, and in the PODC version ("Natural examples of exact order types are FIFO queues and LIFO stacks" in Section 1.1 and "Examples of such types are a queue, a stack, and the fetch-and-cons" after Definition 4.1). Exact order types must tell apart every execution of W(n+1) ∘ (R(m) + op?) from every execution of W(n) ∘ op ∘ (R(m) + W_{n+1}?) by the results of R(m). Since W_{n+1} may be moved into R(m), a stack can hide the swap. For example, with op = push(a) and W_{n+1} = push(b), compare W(n)∘push(b)∘push(a)∘R(m) with W(n)∘push(a)∘R(m) where push(b) is re-inserted right after the pop that removes a: all operations of R(m) return the same values. The other three op/W_{n+1} cases are similar. This holds for every choice of op, W, R, n, m, so the stack is not exact order in the paper's literal form nor in the weaker form.

❌ Theorem 4.11 (read/write max registers): unestablished

The proof (Claim 4.7.1, second case for lines 4/7) infers "R₁ cannot possibly return a value ≥ 3, hence R₁ is decided before W₃" for a pending ReadMax R₁, which is invalid (claim_4_7_1_inference_fails).

Impact. We found no repair; whether Theorem 4.11 holds is open here. It is not formalized.

Details

A linearization function may leave a pending read out of the linearization until it returns, so R₁ is not decided before W₃ although its result is fixed. Once R₁ returns, it is decided before W₃, but that decision is taken by R₁'s owner, which help-freedom allows, so the intended contradiction disappears. Lean witness on the help-free CAS max register of Figure 4.5: after R₁ reads 0 and then WriteMax(3) completes, every extension makes R₁ return 0, yet R₁ is not decided before W₃ for a valid linearization function. (claim_4_7_1_inference_fails)

⚠️ Theorem 4.8 for global view types as defined (fetch&increment, increment)

The proof of Theorem 4.8 (Claim 4.6.8) needs a view to tell which competing operation precedes it, but Definition 4.6.18 only tells how many, so the paper's claimed instances fetch&increment and increment are not covered (fetchInc_not_strongGlobalView).

Impact. Unestablished, not refuted: we have no counterexample. We prove Theorem 4.8 for strong global view types (theorem_4_8), which covers snapshots (Theorem 4.7, theorem_4_7), but not fetch&increment (fetchInc_globalView) or increment, which the paper explicitly claims.

Details

Definition 4.6.18 only requires a view's result to tell how many of op₁ and the next modifier op₂ precede it, and the paper stresses that the proof "relies only on" this, so that it covers increment and fetch&increment objects. But Claim 4.6.8 infers from "op₁ is decided before op₃ in h∘p₁∘p₃ but not in h∘p₃∘p₁" that a solo run of p₃ returns different results in the two histories. With three result classes both may fall in the middle class: op₁ ≺ op₃ ≺ op₂ in one, op₂ ≺ op₃ ≺ op₁ in the other (for fetch&increment both views return the same count). The same inference is used to exclude reads, failed cas and writes.

Repair

We prove Theorem 4.8 for strong global view types, where the view also tells which operation precedes it. Snapshots are such types, so Theorem 4.7 holds (for snapshot implementations correct in all executions). (theorem_4_8, theorem_4_7, Ctx.caseA)

🛠️ Claim 4.6.12 (Theorem 4.8: op_k not decided before op_j)

"op_k is not decided before op_j, because op_j may still be before op₃, which may still be before op_k" combines two different extensions, and non-decidedness is not transitive.

Impact. Repaired by changing the construction, within our proof of Theorem 4.8 for strong global view types (Ctx.cas_move).

Repair

When p_k's step at line 25 would decide op_k before the next view, the next steps of p₁, p₂ are competing successful cas, and lines 17–20 are applied instead. (Ctx.cas_move)

✏️ Slips

  • Claim 4.8.1 needs that the linearization grows only by appending, that the implementation is obstruction-free and that processes can invoke operations (all true in the paper's uses).

  • Observation 4.4.4 (2) and Claim 4.4.5 silently use solo termination.

  • "op₂ ≺ op₁ in f(s)" is not defined when op₁ is missing from f(s). The paper's own proofs (Claims 4.5.2, 4.8.1) need it to count as true, and we use this reading.

Proof gaps in true statements (2)Claim 4.5.10 · Claim 4.5.13

🔧 Claim 4.5.10 (Theorem 4.3: eliminating reads and failed cas)

At the critical point the paper rules out that p₁'s next step is a read or a cas that changes nothing by comparing h∘p₁∘p₂ with h∘p₂∘p₁ (Claim 4.5.3 (1)), but a cas of p₁ that fails at h may succeed after p₂'s step.

Impact. Statement true; repaired, and Theorem 4.3 is proved as stated, in fact for a weaker notion of help-freedom (Ctx.claim_4_5_10, theorem_4_3).

Details

If p₂ writes the expected value, p₁'s cas succeeds in h∘p₂∘p₁, and then the two histories differ.

Repair

Compare h with h∘p₁, which p₂ and p₃ cannot tell apart, and use Claim 4.5.3 (2); symmetrically for p₂ with Claim 4.5.3 (3). So the remark "(3) is not needed in the proof" is wrong. Theorem 4.3 is proved for weak help-freedom: the deciding step must be taken by the owner of the operation, not necessarily inside it. (Ctx.claim_4_5_10, theorem_4_3)

🔧 Claim 4.5.13 (Theorem 4.3: the failed cas)

The proof says the failed cas "contradicts Claim 4.5.3 (2)", but that claim concerns op₁ against op₂, while this one concerns op₁ against op₃.

Impact. Statement true; repaired inside the proof of Theorem 4.3 (Ctx.critical_step).

Repair

Use Claim 4.5.3 (2) for the next competition (op₁ vs W_{n+2}), reached via Claim 4.4.5. (Ctx.critical_step)

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib asynchronous shared memory: deterministic processes, no crashes, adversarial scheduler, no randomness. Executions are label lists (inv, base-object step tau, ret). The set of histories H is the set of all finite executions with arbitrary invocations. Operation instances are (process, index), which also names future operations.

  • Help (Definitions 4.4.1–4.4.3): DecidedBefore I f h a b (no finite extension s has b ≺ a in f (h ++ s), with operations missing from f (h ++ s) counted as last) and HelpFree I. Impossibility results are proved for the weaker WeaklyHelpFree I (the deciding step is taken by the owner), so they are stronger than the paper's.

  • Theorems 4.3, 4.7, 4.8: base objects are read/write/cas registers (casObject, any value type per location), wait-freedom is CSLib's WaitFree, there are at least three processes, and every process may invoke every operation.

  • Exact order types: ExactOrderStrong is the paper's literal Definition 4.5.1 and ExactOrder the weaker form of Aksenov et al. (ExactOrderStrong.exactOrder). Theorem 4.3 is proved for the weaker form, i.e. for more types.
