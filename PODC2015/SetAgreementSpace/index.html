On the Space Complexity of Set Agreement — PODC 2015

Carole Delporte-Gallet, Hugues Fauconnier, Petr Kuznetsov, Eric Ruppert. On the Space Complexity of Set Agreement. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767406. Formalized version: arXiv 1505.02690 v2 (full version with appendices); numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

Verdict: ⚠️ Weakened. The repeated lower bound, Theorem 2 (n + m − k registers), is correct; we prove it from the wait-free set-agreement impossibility, which is a hypothesis. The algorithms of Figure 3 (one-shot) and Figure 4 (repeated) are safe, but they are not m-obstruction-free, and not even obstruction-free, for every 1 ≤ m ≤ k < n; a one-line change to the adoption rule repairs Figure 3, and for the repaired one-shot algorithm we prove the full statement of Theorem 7, in its snapshot version. The repeated upper bound of Theorem 8 stays unproved, because the same repair of Figure 4 is only model-checked, and the anonymous results of Sections 5 and 6 were not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Figure 4, Lemma 14, Theorem 8 (repeated upper bound)

Figure 4 inherits the adoption rule of Figure 3 and livelocks already inside the first instance, so it is not m-obstruction-free for any 1 ≤ m ≤ k < n and the termination part of Theorem 8, as proved through it, is false (fig4_not_mObstructionFree, fig4_livelock; summary theorem8_figure4_false).

Impact. The paper's proofs of the upper bound of Theorem 8, of the upper bounds for repeated set agreement in Figure 1, and of the "exactly n registers for obstruction-free repeated consensus" corollary are invalid. The repeated upper bound remains unverified: Theorem 8 as a bare existence statement is neither proved nor refuted. Safety and the lower bound (Theorem 2) are unaffected.

Details

Figure 4 inherits the bug verbatim: run the livelock of the next item inside the first instance.

Repair

The repair of Figure 3 carried over to Figure 4 passes bounded exhaustive model checking (n = 2, k = m = 1, two instances per process; see differencies.md). It is not proved in Lean, so the repeated upper bound min(n + 2m − k, n) (Theorem 8) remains unverified.

🔧 Figure 3, Lemma 5 (termination part of Theorem 7)

The algorithm of Figure 3 is not m-obstruction-free, not even obstruction-free: already for n = 2, k = m = 1, r = 3, a process running solo keeps re-adopting the same value forever, so Lemma 5 and the termination part of Theorem 7 (as proved through this algorithm) are false (fig3_not_mObstructionFree, fig3_not_obstructionFree; summary theorem7_figure3_false).

Impact. The paper's proof of the upper bound of Theorem 7 is invalid. A one-line change repairs Figure 3, and the repaired one-shot algorithm satisfies Theorem 7 in its snapshot version. Safety and the lower bound (Theorem 2) are unaffected.

Details

The adoption rule (lines 11–13) makes a process adopt the value w of a duplicated pair (w, q) written by another process q. The process then writes (w, id). This is a different pair, so it does not destroy the duplicate.

  • Smallest case: n = 2, k = m = 1, r = 3 components. p₁ runs solo and decides, leaving A = [(v,p₁),(v,p₁),(v,p₁)]. Then p₀ runs solo forever. It writes (v₀,p₀) into A[0] and sees two different pairs (more than m = 1), so it does not decide. Line 11 holds: there is no ⊥, no copy of its own pair, and a duplicated pair. It adopts v, writes (v,p₀) into A[0], and is in the same situation again, without ever changing i. Both processes are correct, and p₀ never terminates.

  • For general m we use crashed processes 1,…,m that leave m different pairs and a duplicate in A[1..r−1]. With them the livelock exists for every 1 ≤ m ≤ k < n with r = n + 2m − k, even when all inputs are equal.

The proof of Lemma 5 breaks in Case 2: "as p changes its preferred value … it must have found another pair that appears twice". In the example, p re-adopts the same value forever. arXiv v2 is the latest version and has the same Figure 3. The authors' PODC'15 talk slides give the same rule: "if … some other pair (v, id′) appears twice then pref ← v".

Repair

At line 11, also require that the value of the first duplicated pair differs from pref; otherwise advance i (line 14). The repaired Figure 3 is m-obstruction-free. The new Case 2 of Lemma 5: after some time all duplicates lie in the frozen components, so the first duplicated value w* is constant. A process that adopted w* must then advance. Safety (Lemmas 3–4, k-agreement) is unaffected. (theorem7_corrected)

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Theorem 2

🔧 Theorem 2 (Lemma 1 used outside its statement)

Lemma 1 talks about executions from the initial configuration, but Theorem 2 applies it from a reachable configuration D_j, to the fresh (s+1)-th instance.

Impact. Statement true. The reduction to the wait-free impossibility works there too: restart the algorithm from D_j. We prove that generalized form (lemma1).

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • CSLib's asynchronous shared-memory model (Cslib.SharedMemory.Impl): n deterministic processes, atomic base objects, invocations by the environment, executions as label lists, crash = stop taking steps.

  • High-level object saObj V: Propose v returns a value.

    • One-shot k-set agreement: kSetAgreement, validity plus at most k outputs, on one-shot executions.

    • Repeated k-set agreement: Impl.SolvesRepeated, the conditions Out_i ⊆ In_i and |Out_i| ≤ k for every instance i, in every execution.

  • m-obstruction-freedom (Taubenfeld): Impl.MObstructionFreeOn m E. In every infinite execution where at most m processes take infinitely many steps (invocations count as steps), a process whose operation stays pending forever takes only finitely many steps. One-shot algorithms use one-shot executions; the repeated problem uses all executions.

  • Upper bounds: the algorithms use one atomic snapshot object with r = n + 2m − k components (snapObjOpt). Implementing the snapshot from registers ([5], [1,13] in the paper) is not formalized.

  • Lemma 3 holds only for one-shot executions (not a mistake): if a decided process were invoked again, its old and new pairs could coexist. The algorithm is one-shot, so the paper's statement and proof are fine. Our Lean proof uses a stronger inductive invariant (lemma3).

  • Lower bound: an arbitrary algorithm over a finite family of read/write registers (RegFamily ι W w₀) with arbitrary local states and values. It assumes all invocations are allowed and that the inputs include |P| distinct values, as in the paper's proof, where processes propose their ids.

  • The wait-free set-agreement impossibility (Borowsky–Gafni, Herlihy–Shavit, Saks–Zaharoglou) is the explicit hypothesis BGImpossibility. It says that no wait-free register algorithm, from any initial state, solves (q−1)-set agreement among q processes with fixed distinct inputs.
