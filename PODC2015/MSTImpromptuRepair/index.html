MST construction and impromptu repair with o(m) messages — PODC 2015

Valerie King, Shay Kutten, Mikkel Thorup. Construction and impromptu repair of an MST in a distributed network with o(m) communication. PODC 2015. DOI 10.1145/2767386.2767405. Formalized version: arXiv 1502.03320 v1; numbering follows it. Copy of the paper: paper.pdf.

DOI · arXiv · PDF · All differences · Lean source · GitHub

Verdict: 🔧 Easy fix. HP-TestOut, FindMin and FindAny are each wrong as printed and saved by a local change proved in Lean, and the one step we did not repair, the Markov bound for FindMin-C, changes only a constant. The combinatorial core is sound: the cut-parity identity behind TestOut, Observation 1, the fingerprint test HP-TestOut, Lemma 4 (isolation by a pairwise independent hash), and the correctness of the w-ary search FindMin and of FindAny/FindAny-C (Lemma 5). The paper's main point, the message bounds of Build MST, Build ST and the repair, was not checked.

Mistakes in the paper

❌ Lemma 2, proof (FindMin-C, Markov step)

The budget (2c/q + 1)L is only 2c + q times the expected L/q trials, so Markov gives 1/(2c + q) ≈ 0.47 for c = 1, q = 1/8, not "less than 1/3 for c ≥ 1" (that needs c ≥ 1.44).

Impact. This step of the proof is wrong and we did not repair it in Lean. The statement is still true, by concentration of sums of geometric variables or a larger budget, and only the constant C of Lemma 3 depends on it.

🔧 HP-TestOut (Section 2.2, edge numbers)

HP-TestOut as written always answers "no leaving edge", because the edge number of the undirected edge is used for the ordered pairs in E↑(T), E↓(T), so both fingerprints are equal for every α (hpTestOut_symm_never, hpTestOut_edgeNumber_never).

Impact. Local fix with evident intent (Observation 1 makes it clear): number the dart (u, v) by the ordered concatenation ID(u)·ID(v) and pick p above the largest dart number. With this fix we prove inequality (2): error ≤ (D−1)/p < ε with D = ∑_{y∈T} deg y (hpTestOut_dartNumber_error_lt).

Details

The polynomial 𝒫(D)(z) = ∏_{e∈D} (z − edge_number(e)) is applied to the sets of ordered pairs, but the edge number concatenates the IDs smallest first. Then (u, v) and (v, u) get the same number, already Local↑(y) = Local↓(y) at every node, and the two fingerprints are equal whatever T is.

🔧 FindMin (Section 3.1, Lemma 2: the heaviest weight is missed)

FindMin returns ∅ although an edge leaves T_x when the lightest leaving edge is also the heaviest edge at T_x and w | maxWt − 1, e.g. on the path 0 − 1 − 2 with weights 1, 3 and T = {0} (run_none_of_max, p3_counterexample).

Impact. Off-by-one with evident intent (step 7(a) treats the range as half-open): with k ← maxWt + 1 the search is correct (findMinFixed_correct).

Details

Step 3 sets k ← maxWt(T_x). The w subranges of step 5 cover [j, j + w⌈(k−j)/w⌉ − 1], which is [j, k−1] when w | k − j (empty when k = j). Step 7(a) narrows to (j_min, k_min + 1), so the range is meant to be half-open. In the failing case TestOut (which never answers 1 wrongly) never answers 1. In the example, a singleton fragment in Build MST's first phase, maxWt(T) = 1 and every subrange is empty.

🔧 Lemma 5 (Section 4.1, FindAny's hash range)

Lemma 5 is false for some pairwise independent hash families: on the star K_{1,3} with T = {center}, FindAny's range r > ∑ deg gives r = 4, too small for Lemma 4, and FindAny-C succeeds with probability 0 instead of ≥ 1/16 (FindAnyCex.lemma5_findAnyC_false, FindAnyCex.findAny_eq_none).

Impact. Local parameter fix: with r ≥ 4 ∑ deg, or r > 2 ∑ deg plus index 0, Lemma 5 holds (lemma5_findAnyC, lemma5_findAny_whp). The failing family is artificial: the linear family over GF(2) succeeds here.

Details

Lemma 4 assumes |W| < 2^(ℓ−1) = r/2 for the set W of leaving edges, and its witness j can be 0. Step 3a only asks r > ∑ deg, and step 3b only uses the indices 1, …, lg r. The proof of Lemma 5 never checks Lemma 4's hypothesis. In the star example ∑ deg = 3, so r = 4 and the indices are {1, 2}, and the edge numbers are 10, 11, 12. We build a pairwise independent family on all keys [0, 12]: the high bits of the three hash values are s₁, s₂, ¬(s₁ ⊕ s₂), and everything else is independent and uniform. With it, the number of leaving edges hashing into [2] is always 0 or 2. So min = 2, w(T) = 10 ⊕ 11 ⊕ 12 = 13 is not the number of any edge, and every round fails. FindAny never finds an edge.

✏️ Slips

  • Inequality (2) needs B = the sum of the degrees in T, as in step 0, not "the number of edges incident to T". The fingerprints agree at the number of every dart inside T (card_inter_le_card_hpTestOut_fail).

  • Inequality (2) is stated unconditionally but holds only when E↑(T) ≠ E↓(T) (hpTestOut_error_lt).

  • FindMin's step 6 is undefined when no subrange answers 1. We repeat the iteration in that case.

  • Lemma 2's "worst case O(log n) time and messages" should be O(|T_x| log n).

  • Lemma 3's phase count adds up to 24c/C + 16c/C², not 40c/C. Only constants change. Not formalized.

  • Claim 1 of Lemma 3: "lg n / lg(C/2)" has a negative logarithm, and the number of trees drops only by a factor 1 − C/4. This is still O(log n) phases. Not formalized.

  • Insert misses the case where u and v are in different trees (not formalized).

  • Section 4.2's "(at most one) cycle": one cycle forms per merged component, possibly of length 2. Not formalized.

  • The odd hash of [33] is not 1/8-odd for w = 1 (thorup_not_odd_one), a parameter the paper does not use.

Typos

  • Lemma 4's chain of inequalities ends with "= 1/16" but in fact gives > 1/8.

  • |X|, W \ {x}, TestHigh, {u′, v}, "TestOut" in FindAny's step 5, "an edge no leaving" in Lemma 5, and O(log n/ log n log n) in the proof of Lemma 2.

Proof gaps in true statements (1)Lemma 5, proof

🔧 Lemma 5, proof (the 1/16 bound)

The proof of Lemma 5 only derives 1/16 − 1/(2n^c) for FindAny-C, not the stated 1/16.

Impact. Statement true, read with the fix of r above: the exact bound in Lemma 4 is ≥ 3/16 (lemma4_strong), and the stated 1/16 for FindAny-C follows from it (lemma5_findAnyC).

Full list of differences (including modelling choices and minor imprecisions): differencies.md.

The formalizationmodel, Lean names of the results, trust

Model.

  • No execution model. Each broadcast-and-echo is represented by the value it aggregates: sums over the nodes of T of local parities, local XORs or local fingerprint products. Message, time and memory bounds are out of scope. The paper's own model is synchronous CONGEST for construction and asynchronous for repair; KT1, distinct IDs, n known.

  • Graph. A finite SimpleGraph and a vertex set T (the marked tree T_x). cut G T is the set of edges with exactly one endpoint in T. Edge weights are distinct, and FindMin works on the set of leaving weights.

  • Randomness. Finite uniform sample spaces (Cslib.Probability.uprob). The point α ∈ ℤ_p of HP-TestOut, the seed of an arbitrary ε-odd or pairwise independent hash family, independent repetitions as Fin K → Ω, and independence of α and the hash as a product space.

  • Adversarial parts. FindMin's TestOut answers are an arbitrary function, assumed only to be one-sided. Its HP-TestOuts are exact, which is the w.h.p. event. Probabilities of TestOut successes (Chernoff/Markov) are not formalized.

  • Edge numbers. These are abstract keys with an injective encoding into a characteristic-2 group (XOR of bit vectors). The counterexamples use the paper's concrete concatenation numbers.
